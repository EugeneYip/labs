# Interview guide: how reading tutors check oral reading fluency

Stage 0 of Winner Validation for #016 Fluency Check. Prepared 2026-10-11. Internal; not for the website. Fluency Check is on **HOLD**: these interviews decide whether it deserves a real usage pilot. Don't assume the answer is yes.

**The one question these interviews answer:** do independent reading tutors check oral reading fluency often enough, and find enough friction in how they do it now, that Fluency Check deserves a pilot?

**What counts most:** what people describe doing *before* they see the page. Reactions after the reveal are useful color, not evidence of a problem.

## Before each interview (2 minutes)

- **Have ready:** a blank scorecard (INTERVIEW_SCORECARD.md), and the participant ID (T01, T02, …).
- **Open the page in a tab, but don't share or mention it until Part 4:** https://labs.eugeneyip.net/fluency-check/
- **Keep names and IDs apart.** Keep the list linking names to IDs somewhere private, never in this repository.
- **Format:** phone or video, about 20 minutes. Written answers are a fallback (see the end).

## Ground rules for the interviewer

- **Ask about the last real time,** then "what happened next?". Concrete recent examples beat opinions.
- **After asking about annoyances, stay quiet for a few seconds.** People fill silence with the real answer.
- **Don't suggest problems.** Don't say "that must be slow". Don't finish their sentences.
- **Don't mention the page, or that you made anything, before Part 4.** If asked why you're interested, say: "I'm looking at how people do this in practice; I'll explain more at the end."
- **Write down friction in their exact words.** Note whether they raised it themselves (including in answer to an open question like Q8) or you named that specific issue first.
- **Protect students.** If a student's name or identifying details come up, say "No need for names, 'a student' is fine", and don't write them down.
- **Notes only, no recording,** unless the participant asks for a recording and clearly agrees to it.
- **Never ask for** files, assessment reports, screenshots with student data, IEP information, or any logins. If they offer to share a screen showing student data, decline politely.

## Opening (about 1 minute; read it aloud)

> Thanks for making time. I'm doing some short research on how reading tutors check oral reading fluency: what you actually do, step by step. There are no right or wrong answers, and I'm not selling anything. Please don't mention any student by name or with details that could identify them; "a student" is fine. I'll take written notes and won't record. You can skip any question. It should take about 20 minutes. Shall we start?

## Part 1: your work (about 2 minutes)

1. "To start, tell me a little about your tutoring work."
   - Listen for: independent, in private practice, part of a small tutoring business, school-based, or other.
   - Listen for training, such as Orton-Gillingham, Wilson, or another structured-literacy method.
2. "Roughly how many students are you working with at the moment?" A number is enough.
3. "What ages or grades, broadly?" (K–2, 3–5, 6–8, 9–12, adults.)
4. "Do you meet students in person, online, or a mix?"

## Part 2: the last fluency check (about 8 minutes)

5. "Do you ever have a student read aloud so you can measure how fluently or how quickly they read?"
   - **If no:** "How do you keep track of how a student's reading is coming along?" Note the answer, then go to question 12. Score this person as not meeting the frequency bar.
   - **If yes:** continue.
6. "Think about the last time you did that. When was it?" Then: "Walk me through it from the start. What did you do first?"

   Let them talk. Use the probes below only for parts they don't cover, one at a time.

   | Topic | Probe |
   |---|---|
   | Materials | "What did you have open or in front of you?" |
   | Passage | "Where did the passage come from?" |
   | Timing | "How did you time it?" |
   | Mistakes | "While they were reading, how did you keep track of mistakes?" |
   | Self-corrections | "What do you do when a student corrects themselves?" |
   | After the reading | "What happened after the student finished reading?" |
   | The number | "How do you get to the number you write down?" |
   | Accuracy | "Do you work out accuracy as well? How?" |
   | Recording | "Where did the result go?" |
   | History | "Do you keep a history or a chart for each student? How?" |
   | Other systems | "Does the result go anywhere else afterward, such as parents, a school, or another system?" |
   | Online sessions (if they work online) | "When you're online, how does the student see the passage, and how do you mark it?" |

7. "How often do you do a check like that, and for how many of your students?"
   - Then ground it: "Can you think of the last two or three times? Roughly when were they?"

## Part 3: what gets in the way (about 4 minutes)

8. "Which part of that, if any, is annoying or slow?" Then pause.
9. "Has anything ever gone wrong when you've done one?" Don't offer examples.
10. "Have you tried another way of doing this, such as a different sheet, an app, a website, or a spreadsheet?"
    - If yes: "How did that go?" and "What made you stop, or keep using it?"
11. "What do you wish were easier?"
12. "Overall, how well does the way you do it now work for you?"

**Before moving on,** jot down the three judgments on the scorecard while they're fresh:
- frequency bar;
- unprompted relevant friction;
- already satisfied with a digital tool.

From here on, nothing they say changes those three.

## Part 4: the reveal (about 5 minutes)

**Say:**

> Thanks, that's really helpful. One last thing: I've made a free web page for fluency checks, and I'd like your honest first reaction. It's not a pitch; criticism is the most useful thing you can give me.

**Then:**
- Share your screen, or send the link: https://labs.eugeneyip.net/fluency-check/
- Let them look for about 20 seconds before saying anything.
- If they seem unsure what it is, say only this:

  > You paste or pick a passage and start the timer. You tap the words the student misreads, and tap the last word when time's up. It works out the score and keeps results only in that browser.

**Ask, in order:**

- R1. "What do you think this is for?"
- R2. "Where, if anywhere, would this fit into the workflow you just described?"
- R3. "What would stop you from using this during your next fluency check?"
- R4. "What's missing that would make it impractical?"
- R5. "What part seems unnecessary?"
- R6. "Would this replace anything you already do, or would it just add another step?"
- For online tutors: "How would this work in an online session?"

**Close:**

> Is there anyone else who checks reading fluency with students who you think I should talk to? Thanks so much for your time.

If they ask to try the page, they're welcome to: it's public. Note it as unprompted interest. Don't ask them to use it, report back, or join anything; a pilot isn't authorized.

## Questions not to ask, and what to ask instead

| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that did this?" | Predictions about future behavior are unreliable, and people are polite | "What did you do last time?" |
| "Would this save you time?" | Leading | "Which part, if any, is slow?" |
| "Do you care about privacy?" | Everyone says yes | Note whether they raise it on their own, for example in Q10 or Q12 |
| "Would you pay for this?" | Out of scope; it's free | Nothing |
| "Would you use Fluency Check?" | Leading, and names the product | R2, R3 and R6 |
| "Isn't counting the words tedious?" | Suggests the problem | "What happened after the student finished reading?" |
| "Do you use an app for it?" (early on) | Suggests the answer | "What did you have open or in front of you?" |

## Right after the interview (about 5 minutes)

**Fill in the rest of the scorecard.** Keep it to three things:
- the counts and methods;
- one to three friction quotes;
- your confidence.

**Don't revise the three pre-reveal judgments** because of how the reveal went.

**Keep completed scorecards out of this repository:**
- store them in `interviews/completed/`, which git ignores; or
- keep them on paper.

## If someone prefers written answers

**Send part one only**, then the link once they've replied:

1. What kind of tutoring do you do, roughly how many students do you have, at what ages or grades, and in person, online, or both?
2. Do you have students read aloud so you can measure fluency or reading rate? If not, how do you track their reading progress?
3. Describe the last time you did a fluency check, step by step: the materials, how you timed it, how you marked mistakes and self-corrections, how you got to the score, and where the result went.
4. How often do you do this, and for how many students?
5. Which part of it, if any, is annoying, slow, or error-prone?
6. Have you tried another way of doing it, such as an app, a website, or a spreadsheet? How did it go?
7. Overall, how well does your current way work for you?

**After they reply, send:**
- the link;
- R1, R3 and R6.

Written answers count the same way. Mark them "written" on the scorecard.
