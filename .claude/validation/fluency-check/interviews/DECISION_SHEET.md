## 2. Decide, in this order

**Step 1: clear failures, which mean STOP.** Recommend STOP if any of these is clearly true:

- a. F ≤ 2: two or fewer meet the frequency bar.
- b. Most participants (more than half of N) say their current method is easy enough. That's the scorecard's stop signal, with no relevant friction.
- c. Most participants already have a digital tool they prefer (S > N/2).
- d. For most participants, the real pain is finding passages or content, not the scoring workflow.
- e. Most of those who meet the frequency bar say Fluency Check would add a step instead of replacing one.
- f. Most of those who meet the frequency bar work online and say they can't practically fit it into their sessions.
- g. Most of those who meet the frequency bar need infrastructure outside the current product before they could use it: rosters, sync across devices, accounts, or school-system integration.

**Step 2: PROCEED TO PILOT.** Proceed only if all three are true:

1. **F ≥ 4:** at least 4 participants check oral reading fluency at least every two weeks for at least three students.
2. **R ≥ 3:** at least 3 participants, before seeing Fluency Check, described on their own meaningful friction that Fluency Check actually removes.
3. **S ≤ 2:** no more than 2 participants already use a digital solution they're satisfied with.

PROCEED means the pilot may be proposed. It still needs the owner's explicit authorization to start (VALIDATION_PLAN.md §3–4).

**Step 3: HOLD.** Only for one of these reasons:

- **The sample is clearly unrepresentative.** For example, more than 3 came through one introducer or organization (the cap in RECRUITMENT_SHORTLIST.md), or all are school-based rather than independent.
  - Next step: count only the first 3 interviews from any one source, taken in the order they happened, whatever they said.
  - Fill the remaining places, up to 8 in all, from other sources with the same guide. Then decide.
- **One major question is unresolved.** For example, most qualifiers work online and nobody could judge whether an online session works.
  - Next step: 2 short follow-up conversations with qualifying online tutors on that question alone. These don't change F, R or S.
- **Genuinely mixed near the threshold.** Exactly one of the three conditions in Step 2 is missed by exactly one participant, and none of Step 1 applies.
  - If N < 8: do the remaining interviews up to 8 with the same guide, then decide.
  - If N = 8: STOP. The approved range is used up.

Don't use HOLD to put off a STOP. Otherwise the outcome is **STOP**.

## 3. What each outcome means

- **PROCEED TO PILOT:** prepare the six-week pilot in VALIDATION_PLAN.md §3–4 for the owner's decision. Nothing starts without an explicit yes.
- **STOP FOCUSED VALIDATION:**
  - Fluency Check stays a maintained Labs experiment, unchanged.
  - It isn't a product failure: it means focused standalone investment stops, which saves the owner's time.
  - Record the outcome in VALIDATION_BRIEF.md and FACTORY_STATE.md.
- **HOLD:** run exactly the one step named above, then come back to this sheet and decide.
