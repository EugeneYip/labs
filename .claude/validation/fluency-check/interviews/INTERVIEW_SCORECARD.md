**Does it overlap with what Fluency Check removes?** Tick what applies.

Fluency Check removes or simplifies:
- ☐ juggling a separate timer
- ☐ marking errors on a paper copy
- ☐ counting words and finding the last word read
- ☐ working out words correct per minute or accuracy, including self-corrections
- ☐ writing results down
- ☐ charting a student's progress
- ☐ making a marked record to keep or share
- ☐ tools that only work with their own passages, or need an account
- ☐ worries about recording or uploading

It doesn't touch:
- ☐ finding or choosing passages
- ☐ benchmarks or norms
- ☐ school or district reporting
- ☐ rosters, or sync across devices
- ☐ showing the passage to an online student
- ☐ the reading time itself, or scheduling

## The three judgments (decide before the reveal; don't change them afterward)

**QUALIFIES FOR FREQUENCY BAR: ☐ YES ☐ NO**

They check oral reading fluency at least every two weeks, for at least three students. Base it on what they actually did recently, not on intentions.

**UNPROMPTED RELEVANT FRICTION: ☐ YES ☐ NO**

Before seeing Fluency Check, they described, on their own, meaningful friction in a part of the workflow that Fluency Check removes or materially simplifies (the first list above).
- A shrug ("it takes a minute, it's fine") is NO.
- Friction only in the second list is NO.

**ALREADY SATISFIED WITH DIGITAL SOLUTION: ☐ YES ☐ NO**

They already score or record fluency checks with a digital tool and are happy with it, or don't want to change.

## Stop signals for the decision sheet

Tick any that clearly apply.

- ☐ Their real pain is finding passages or content, not scoring.
- ☐ It would add a step rather than replace one.
- ☐ They work online and can't fit it into sessions.
- ☐ They'd need rosters, sync, accounts, or school-system integration before using it.
- ☐ They said their current method is easy enough.
