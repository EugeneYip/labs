# Outreach messages

Stage 0, prepared 2026-10-11. **Nothing here has been sent.** The owner sends these personally, one at a time. Claude doesn't send, post, or schedule anything.

**Ground rules:**
- Don't mention Fluency Check, an app, a product, a startup, or customers before the interview. The page is shown only at the end (INTERVIEW_GUIDE.md, Part 4).
- Don't send to lists, and don't BCC groups. Send one personal message at a time.
- If someone says no, or doesn't reply after one gentle follow-up, leave it there.

## A. To an introducer

For someone you know who trains reading tutors, runs a tutoring business, works in structured literacy, or knows independent reading tutors.

**Subject:** A small favor: reading tutors I could talk to?

> Hi [Name],
>
> I hope you're doing well. I'm doing a small piece of research on how reading tutors check students' oral reading fluency in practice: what they actually do, step by step, and which parts are fiddly.
>
> Would you know two or three independent reading tutors who might be willing to talk with me for about 20 minutes? It's purely research, not sales. We'd talk about their own way of working, so no student information comes up, and there's nothing to prepare.
>
> If anyone comes to mind, I'd be grateful for an introduction, or feel free to forward this note. And no worries at all if not.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your name]

**Shorter version for a text or chat:**

> Hi [Name]! Quick favor: I'm doing some research on how reading tutors check students' reading fluency. Do you know any independent reading tutors who'd chat with me for 20 minutes? It's about their own workflow, so no student info, no prep, and I'm not selling anything. Happy to send a short note you could forward.

## B. Directly to an independent tutor (no introducer)

For a tutor whose professional contact details are published (a practice website, or a professional directory listing).

**Subject:** 20 minutes on how you check reading fluency?

> Hi [Name],
>
> I came across your [practice / listing in the ______ directory] and hope you don't mind a short note. I'm researching how reading tutors check students' oral reading fluency in practice: what they actually do, step by step, and which parts are fiddly.
>
> Would you be open to a 20-minute call about your own way of doing it? Written answers are fine too, if that's easier. No student information comes up, there's nothing to prepare, and I'm not selling anything.
>
> Thank you for considering it either way.
> [Your name]
> [One honest line about you, for example "I'm based in the Boston area."]

**Optional thank-you, the owner's choice:** a small, equal thank-you for a stranger's time, such as a $20–25 gift card.
- If you offer one, add this line: "As a thank-you for your time, I'd like to send a $25 gift card."
- Never tie it to what they say.
- It may raise replies from strangers. It isn't needed for introductions.

## C. After an introducer has connected you

Reply to the introduction email, moving the introducer to BCC.

> Thanks, [Introducer]! (Moving you to BCC.)
>
> Hi [Tutor], thanks for being willing to talk. It's about 20 minutes on how you check reading fluency at the moment: no student details, nothing to prepare. Would any of these work? [Two or three times, with time zone], by phone or video, whichever you prefer.
>
> [Your name]

## Agreeing on a time

> Great, thank you! Let's say [day, date] at [time, time zone], by [phone at the number you shared / video: link]. It'll take about 20 minutes. If anything changes, just reply and we'll find another time.

## One gentle follow-up (only if there's no reply after a week)

> Hi [Name], just bringing this back to the top of your inbox in case it's useful: 20 minutes on how you check reading fluency, no prep. Completely fine if now isn't a good time.

## Thank-you afterward (optional)

> Thank you again for your time today. It was really helpful to hear how you do it.

Don't ask them to try or review anything. A pilot isn't authorized.
