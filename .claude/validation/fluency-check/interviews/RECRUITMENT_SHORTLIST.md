# Recruitment shortlist

Stage 0, researched 2026-10-11. **Nobody has been contacted.** This is a short list of routes to 6–8 independent reading tutors, not a contact database.

**What it lists:** organizations and directories only. No individual's details were collected. A listed organization doesn't endorse this research.

**Labels:**
- **[V]**: read on the source.
- **[S]**: search snippet or secondary page only (the site blocked automated reading or redirected).
- **[U]**: inference.

## The recommended order

1. **One introducer from the owner's own circle** (best). One trusted person who knows several tutors beats any directory. It also keeps every ask personal, and introduced tutors say yes far more often [U].
2. **One professional organization**, if no introducer turns up: the Massachusetts branch of the International Dyslexia Association, or a Boston-area training program with many practicing alumni.
3. **One or two directories**, as a fallback with no introducer: Wilson Language Training's credentialed-practitioner directory, then CERI's or ALTA's.

**Mix the sample.** No more than three participants from any one introducer or source, so one circle's habits don't decide the outcome.

## 1. Introducers: who to look for in the owner's circle

| Introducer type | Why | Where the owner might already know one [U] |
|---|---|---|
| **A structured-literacy trainer** (Orton-Gillingham, Wilson, or a certified academic language therapist who trains others) | Trainers supervise practicum tutoring and keep in touch with many practitioners, often in private practice | Teachers or speech-language pathologists in the owner's circle; Northeastern colleagues (see section 4) |
| **The owner of a small tutoring business or center** | Employs or knows several tutors who choose their own tools | A parent who has hired a reading tutor; local businesses |
| **A reading specialist or literacy coach** | Often knows the independent tutors families hire | School contacts, friends who teach |
| **A speech-language pathologist who works on literacy** | Works alongside reading tutors and dyslexia specialists | Northeastern's speech-language pathology community (section 4) |
| **A parent whose child has a private reading tutor** | A one-step introduction to a tutor | Friends and family |

**The single best type** is a structured-literacy trainer or the owner of a small tutoring business: one person who can name several tutors.

## 3. Directories (fallback, when there's no introducer)

These directories exist so families can find services. A research request is an unsolicited ask.

**How to use them:**
- Pick at most 8–10 Massachusetts listings that describe private practice or tutoring, not schools.
- Send message B from OUTREACH.md one at a time over about a week.
- Don't copy listings into a spreadsheet or database.
- Before using any directory, read its terms for rules on solicitation; they weren't confirmed in this research.
- **Expected replies:** a low reply rate is normal for cold research requests, perhaps 1 or 2 in 10 [U]. Introductions do far better.

## 4. Boston and Northeastern channels (plausible, not established)

This doesn't assume the owner knows anyone in these departments. These are only places where a warm introduction is plausible.

- **Northeastern's Department of Communication Sciences and Disorders** (Bouvé College) [V]:
  - lists literacy among its areas of excellence;
  - its Speech-Language and Hearing Center treats literacy disorders (phonological awareness, decoding, reading comprehension);
  - speech-language pathology faculty and clinicians often know reading tutors and dyslexia specialists [U].
- **Northeastern's Graduate School of Education** [V]:
  - its Master of Education in Special Education includes literacy courses (developmental literacy assessment and instruction, a multisensory tiered reading program, dyslexia screening);
  - instructors and students are licensed teachers, some of whom tutor privately [U].
- **No reading-specialist program** was found at Northeastern, so it isn't a direct source of reading tutors [S].
- **Best use:** if the owner knows anyone in these departments, or classmates who teach, send them message A. Otherwise skip this route.
- Boston-area training programs (section 2) are the stronger local route.

## Not recommended

| Route | Why not |
|---|---|
| Reddit, Facebook groups, association forums | No posting is authorized; promotion rules apply; it biases the sample toward enthusiasts |
| Tutor marketplaces (Wyzant, Outschool, Care.com) | Their messaging exists for booking lessons. Using it for research is against its purpose and possibly against their terms [U] |
| Mass email, scraping, contact lists | Unauthorized and inappropriate |
| National programs (Reading Corps) and school districts | A different segment that uses district-mandated tools |
| Close friends as participants | They overstate value. Use them as introducers instead |
