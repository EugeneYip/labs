# Comparative decision: where the owner's next hour of human validation should go

Winner Validation, Comparative Research (2026-10-11). Internal; not for the website. Research only: nobody was contacted, nothing was posted, and no public product, feature or infrastructure was changed.

**Candidates.** The four products classified PROMOTE in `.claude/PORTFOLIO_RESOLUTION.md`:
- #016 Fluency Check (Phase 1 decision: HOLD);
- #020 Custody Calendar;
- #022 Day Clock;
- #017 Frost Dates.

**Method.** Each package was written to falsify its opportunity, not to confirm it. The old Labs technical scores were **not** used.

**Ranking criterion.** The **expected value of the owner's next hour of human validation effort**.

**Evidence.** The packages in `custody-calendar/`, `day-clock/`, `frost-dates/` and `fluency-check/`, and their `notes/`.

## Decisions

| Product | Decision | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| **#016 Fluency Check** | **HOLD** (Phase 1, unchanged): **the primary candidate** | A narrow beachhead the product fits as built (independent reading tutors), and a cheap, decisive, low-risk human test already designed |
| **#020 Custody Calendar** | **STOP FOCUSED VALIDATION** | The market leader gives professionals the same job free; the pre-agreement job is episodic; after agreement the need is sync and records; legal and safety sensitivity |

All three STOP products remain maintained Labs tools. **No backup candidate is named:** the evidence isn't close (see "Ranking").

## Expected value of the owner's next hour

**Expected value** ≈ the chance the hour produces decision-changing evidence × the chance that evidence is positive × what a positive result unlocks, minus the hour's cost and risk. These are judgements, not measurements.

## Ranking

1. **Fluency Check: primary candidate.** The only one where the deciding question is human-answerable, cheap and low-risk, and where a positive answer leads straight to a pilot with the product as built.
2. **Custody Calendar.** Its remaining question is human-answerable, but the prior is poor:
   - the leader's builder is free for professionals;
   - a positive result runs straight into legal-output and sync pressure.

   It isn't close enough to be a backup.
3. **Frost Dates.** Its unknowns are engineering and search questions, not human ones. Desk checks already answered the decisive one (accuracy at the beachhead) negatively.
4. **Day Clock.** The core is free elsewhere (including under the same name), and the main unmet need can't be built within Labs.

**No backup.** The brief allows one "if evidence is close". It isn't: each STOP rests on at least one verified, decisive counter-fact.

## Fluency Check's position after comparison

**HOLD stands**, and Fluency Check becomes the recommended primary candidate.
- The comparison adds no new evidence about tutors, so it doesn't justify PROCEED.
- But it shows the other three are weaker on the criteria that matter for the next hour.
- The Phase 1 rule is unchanged: no interviews or pilot without the owner's explicit authorization. Nothing has started.

## Strongest counter-evidence, by candidate

- **Fluency Check.**
  - The same tap-and-score workflow exists inside McGraw Hill's Wonders and the district platforms, free in Microsoft 365, and free with an account in ReadingFluency.app.
  - Teacher-built apps doing exactly this have almost no users.
  - Paper with running word counts is quick.
- **Custody Calendar.**
  - OurFamilyWizard's Custody Schedule Builder is free for professionals, with templates, live overnight counts and percentages, and print-ready PDFs.
  - Parents leaving paid platforms go to general calendars.
  - Courts order specific platforms for the most conflicted families.
- **Day Clock.**
  - Technology4Seniors' free "Day Clock" iPad app has part-of-day words with editable periods and speaks the time, on iPadOS 15+ (older than Day Clock supports).
  - Calendar Clock is free with remote control and 20,000+ daily users.
- **Frost Dates.**
  - Against Canberra Airport (1995–2010) and Tuggeranong (1996–2026) station records, its cautious last-frost date is about 5 weeks early (Oct 4 against Nov 11; Oct 1 against Nov 3), with half the frost nights. The folk rule matches the stations.
  - Its calendar puts tomatoes outside in Dublin on Mar 12–19 and in London on Apr 11–18.

## Why the owner's time should go to Fluency Check

1. **It's the only candidate where an hour of conversation can change the decision.** The resolution rule is written: proceed if at least 4 tutors check at least every two weeks for 3+ students, at least 3 name friction unprompted, and no more than 2 are happy with a digital tool.
2. **A yes leads somewhere immediately.** The product already fits the beachhead: no account, nothing uploaded, any passage, offline. A pilot needs no new features or infrastructure.
3. **A no is also valuable and cheap.** Six to eight interviews would close the last open PROMOTE question and free the owner from all four.
4. **Low risk and no dependencies.** Tutors are professionals, no student information is collected, and nothing relies on an outside service.
5. **The others would spend the hour poorly:**
   - Custody's and Day Clock's likely answer is "the free incumbent is fine", in sensitive settings.
   - Frost Dates' real questions can't be answered by interviews.

## Corrections made during this research

- **Frost Dates' quota:**
  - One lookup costs about **83** weighted calls, and the free tier allows about **120 lookups a day per visitor IP**. I had earlier said about 830 calls and about 12 lookups a day.
  - Verified in Open-Meteo's server code and observed directly: the ninth lookup in a minute was refused.
  - `.claude/PORTFOLIO_RESOLUTION.md` is corrected.
- **Safari storage wording** (internal documents):
  - WebKit's tracking prevention may remove script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without interaction with the site.
  - Home Screen web apps are exempt from that cap, according to WebKit.
  - The public pages say Safari "can" clear data after "about a week without a visit". That was judged not materially misleading and left unchanged.
- **`models=era5_land` for Frost Dates:** suggested in the data notes for consistency. Tested: it doesn't improve accuracy, and at Canberra makes it marginally worse.

## Limits

- **No real users.** Nobody was contacted for any product, so every finding about users is indirect: reviews, forums, guidance, columns. That is the main reason none received PROCEED.
- **Blocked sources:**
  - Reddit couldn't be read by any tool, and several key sites blocked automated reading (Frost Dates: garden.org, PlantMaps, BoM; Day Clock: retail reviews).
  - The research search tool uses a US-only index, and there's no search-volume data.
- **Scholarly sources:** LibKey wasn't available, so they are named but not resolved or read.
- **The accuracy check is narrow:** two stations in one city, plus five US cities checked earlier. Part of the Canberra gap is screen height (1.2 m against 2 m).
- **The ratings and expected-value estimates are judgements from desk evidence.** They rank options; they don't measure them.
