# Competitive landscape: Custody Calendar

Comparative Research (2026-10-11). Internal. Full evidence: `notes/market-notes.md` §3–8. Labels: **[V]** read on the source; **[S]** snippet; **[U]** inference; **[V, rechecked]** confirmed directly during synthesis.

## Verdict

Visualization is given away everywhere: by the market leader to professionals, by free apps to parents, by law firms as lead magnets, and by general calendars for daily use. Money is in records, messaging, and expenses, which need accounts and servers. Custody Calendar's combination (free, no account, any pattern, dated year, overnight shares, calendar file, share link) has no exact match on the web, but that's a gap in form, not evidence of demand.

## Search

Pattern queries ("2-2-5-5 custody schedule", "50/50 custody schedule calendar") are served by co-parenting vendors' blogs and law firms; overnight calculators come from law firms and calculator sites; generic "custody calendar maker free" results in the research tool's index were spam [S]. No free, no-account, any-pattern web visualizer surfaced. Volumes unknown.

## Wedge hypotheses, tested

| Hypothesis | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Free | **Fails** | Free apps, Google Calendar, and the leader's free professional builder |
| No account | **Holds as a fact; value unproven** | Parents leaving paid apps didn't seek no-account tools; they used general calendars, which have accounts [V] |
| Any pattern plus a dated year plus overnight shares on the web | **Holds as a format gap** | Not found as a free, no-account web page; found in apps, paid templates, and professional tools [V] |
| Privacy before cooperation | **Plausible, untested** | Privacy is a selling point for some new apps (CoNest, Blended) [V]; no parent evidence |
| Useful to mediators | **Contested** | The leader's builder is free for them and makes PDFs [V, rechecked] |
| Useful after agreement | **Fails** | The job becomes live sync and records [V] |

## Monetization norms

Per-parent subscriptions ($7–$32 a month, $110–$300 a year), per-family pricing, one-time apps ($0.99–$6.99), freemium with analytics or history paywalled, documentation subscriptions ($80 a year), and free professional access to drive referrals [V]. Nobody charges for visualization alone.
