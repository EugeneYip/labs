# Thread C: land, woodland, farms, gardens, outdoor space

Batch 10 discovery, research thread C. One bounded purpose: real, recurring jobs in land, woodland, farms, gardens and outdoor space where a recognised method or official rule creates the work, and a static, no-account, offline-capable browser tool could do it materially better than today's tools.

Budget: at most 10 web searches, 8 page reads. Evidence labels: *verified* (seen on an official or primary page), *source claim* (a page says so), *inference* (judgment).

## Summary (ranked)

Status: DONE 2026-10-14. 10 of 10 searches, 8 of 8 page reads. Full gates under "Leads, gated" at the end.

1. **Felling-licence exemption record (England and Wales): lead, borderline.** Log each tree felled, sort out the ones that don't count, estimate the rest from what you can measure, and total against 5 m3 (2 m3 sold) per calendar quarter, offline, with a dated record. For: the only official aid is a 2005 log-volume table that needs the diameter halfway up the trunk, which you can't measure on a standing tree (*verified*, R2); no tool turned up in four searches or on the FC's new online service (*verified*, R8); and the FC says you must be able to prove an exemption or "may be liable to prosecution" (*verified*). Against: the Act defines no way of measuring volume (*verified*, R5), so the tool can only estimate. The people asking treat the stakes as low, and without the record it's a formula plus a sum. The main session needs to check four things: S.I. 1979/792; where the tariff equations come from and their licence; whether branches count; and whether owners lack the record or only the arithmetic.
2. **Tariffing a stand for licence applications or standing sales (agents): weak.** The method is real, but the users are trained and already equipped, and the Woodland Carbon Code's free spreadsheet already runs the DBH-to-volume chain (R6).
3. **Herbivore (deer) impact assessments: weak.** It is a grant condition in England (WS1, *verified* R7) and Scotland, and no app was found. But the rating is subjective with little to compute, the audience is small, and the official sheets already match what gets submitted.
4. **Stocking and beat-up surveys of new planting: REJECT.** The Woodland Carbon Code's free templates calculate stocking automatically and are what verifiers receive (*verified*, R6).
5. **BNG habitat condition assessments: REJECT.** The FC has a free Woodland Condition Assessment app, and Coreo's app covers every habitat offline (S9).

Skipped on earlier records: the hedgerow test (Batch 09, weak) and allotment crop rotation (Batch 07, REJECT).

- **R1** (read) GOV.UK "Tree felling licence: when you need to apply", published 8 July 2026 (*verified*): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tree-felling-licence-when-you-need-to-apply
  - Links one tool: "Timber Volume Calculator" (PDF, 69 KB, 1 page), https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e4ca6895317eadc3545c4/TimberVolumeCalculator.pdf, which "can help you estimate the volume of timber you wish to fell"; no other method is given.

- **R2** (read) the FC "Timber Volume Calculator" PDF linked from GOV.UK (*verified*; WebFetch saved the PDF and its compressed text was inflated locally to read it): https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4e4ca6895317eadc3545c4/TimberVolumeCalculator.pdf
  - Its title is "Rough guide to calculating timber volumes"; the file is dated 16 March 2005 (PDF metadata, author "Forestry Commission"). One A4 page, two printed tables, no form fields.
  - Table 1, "How many trees make 5 cubic metres?": tree length 2, 4, 6, 10, 15, 20 m against tree mid-diameter 8, 10, 15, 20, 25, 40, 60 cm (e.g. 8 cm by 2 m: 500 trees; 10 cm: 312, 161, 106, 63; 20 cm: 79, 40, 27, 16, 11, 8).
  - Table 2, "Timber Volume calculations": volume of one tree in m3 by length and mid-diameter (e.g. 20 m at 20 cm 0.63, 25 cm 0.98, 30 cm 1.41, 40 cm 2.51; 10 m at 10 cm 0.079, 20 cm 0.314; 6 m at 8 cm 0.03).
  - Checked here: every value is Huber's log formula, V = pi/4 x (mid-diameter)^2 x length (10 m at 10 cm: 0.0785; 20 m at 20 cm: 0.628; 6 m at 8 cm: 0.0302), and Table 1 is 5 / V (8 cm by 2 m: 497). So the official aid is a 2005 log-volume table (*verified* by recomputation).
  - The mismatch (*inference*): the licence threshold is the diameter at 1.3 m on a standing tree, but the table needs the diameter halfway up the stem and its length, which an owner can't measure on a tree still standing. No form factor, no species, no bark, no top diameter, no branches.

- **R5** (read) Forestry Act 1967, section 9, legislation.gov.uk (*verified*): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1967/10/section/9
  - s.9(3)(b): the licence requirement "does not apply to the felling by any person of trees on land in his occupation" (or occupied by his tenant) where "the aggregate cubic content of the trees which are felled in the relevant territory by that person without a licence" doesn't exceed 5 m3 in any quarter, and of those, the trees "sold by that person whether before or after the felling" don't exceed 2 m3 in any quarter (or a larger quantity the forestry authority allows in a particular case). So the allowance is per person, across all land they occupy in England (or in Wales), not per wood.
  - s.9(6): "quarter" means the three months beginning 1 January, 1 April, 1 July or 1 October. Diameter means over bark at 1.3 m.
  - The Act gives **no method for measuring cubic content** and says nothing about branches or lop and top. So there is no legal measuring rule to implement: any tool's figure is an estimate (*verified* absence in s.9; the regulations, S.I. 1979/792, not read).
  - Other exemptions in s.9 a tool would have to sort trees by: diameter not over 8 cm (15 cm for coppice or underwood); not over 10 cm when thinning to help other trees grow (s.9(3)(a)); fruit trees, or trees in an orchard, garden, churchyard or public open space; topping, lopping, hedge trimming or laying; danger or nuisance; statutory obligations; electricity lines; development with planning permission. Scotland's s.9 was repealed in 2019 (Forestry and Land Management (Scotland) Act 2018), so England and Wales only.

- **R6** (read) Woodland Carbon Code, *Survey protocol for Woodland Carbon Code projects*, version 3.0.1, July 2026 (*verified*; WebFetch saved the PDF and its text was inflated locally to read it): https://www.woodlandcarboncode.org.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/SurveyProtocol_Version3.01_July2026.pdf
  - Steps: stratify the site; choose plot size from planting density (Table 2: e.g. 1,006-1,999 trees/ha gives 0.02 ha, an 8.0 m radius circle, "at least 20 locations per plot"; 2,000-3,966 gives 0.01 ha, 5.6 m); choose the minimum number of plots (Table 3: by stratum net area and whether it is uniform or variable, e.g. 2-10 ha: 8 uniform, 12-16 variable; up to 30 per stratum improves accuracy); locate plots randomly (systematic grid nodes or random points, not within 5 m of an edge, grid references recorded before going to site, plots not moved on site); slope correction radius x 1/cos(angle) (Annex 1).
  - "Get your survey plan checked by your chosen verification body" before surveying, or it may have to be redone. Then "Enter data into spreadsheet to work out planting density (year 5) or carbon stock (year 15+)".
  - **The incumbent is the code's own templates:** a survey plan template "with a calculator" for average spacing and density, and Excel monitoring reports whose "stratum summary sheet automatically calculates the stocking density and overall tree health/growth"; data sheets "can be printed or completed using a suitable device", e.g. a Toughbook, and "If you have a suitable device, you can enter data into the excel plot tabs in the field". Results are read from named cells ("Cells AA2 to AE5"). The verifier rates the project green, amber or red; amber or red needs a remedial plan (template available).
  - Year 15+: DBH of every living tree of 7 cm or more in each plot, plus DBH and height of the two trees of each species nearest the centre (timber height for broadleaves, total height for conifers); the year 15+ monitoring report "will semi-automatically" compute carbon by the carbon assessment protocol's methods B and C. So there is a free official spreadsheet that runs the tariff-style chain from DBH and sample heights, for carbon (*verified* that it computes; that it uses tariff equations is *inference*). This weakens Lead 1's "no free tariff tool" for professionals.
  - Version history: 1.0 March 2016, 2.0 March 2018, 2.2.1 April 2024 (year 15 added), 3.0 August 2025, 3.0.1 July 2026. Licence not stated in the extracted text; the disclaimer names Scottish Forestry, the Forestry Commission, Welsh Government and NI Forest Service.
  - Verdict on the stocking-survey lead: **REJECT**. The rule-making standard ships free templates that calculate automatically and are what the verifier receives; the planning step needs the site's GIS strata; outside the code, stocking and beat-up surveys are done by estate managers and contractors (S5), and the plot arithmetic is a division.

## Leads, gated

### Lead 1. Staying within the felling-licence exemption: a tree-by-tree felling record with quarterly totals (England and Wales) — thread verdict: lead (borderline)

- **A. Audience.** People who fell a few trees at a time on land they occupy, mostly for firewood, thinning or clearing: owners of small woods (woodlands.co.uk sells and advises them), farmers taking out hedgerow and field trees, and the arborists and firewood cutters they hire. England and Wales, where s.9 of the Forestry Act 1967 still applies (Scotland's was repealed in 2019); Northern Ireland has a similar 5 m3 exemption under its own Act (*verified* R5; NI *source claim* S4).
- **B. Immediate job.** Before: standing in the wood with a tape, deciding which trees to take this winter, or adding up what was cut last weekend. For each tree: does it count at all (under 8 cm at 1.3 m, thinning under 10 cm, coppice under 15 cm, garden, orchard, churchyard, fruit tree, danger, planning permission), and if it counts, roughly how many cubic metres, and was or will it be sold? After: a running total for the calendar quarter against 5 m3, and against 2 m3 sold, and a dated record of what was felled and why it didn't need a licence, to show the FC if asked.
- **C. Why it matters.** The rule creates the work and the record: felling without a licence when one was needed is an offence (R1), with an unlimited fine in England since 1 January 2023 (*source claim*, S7); the FC's own service says that to claim an exception "you need to prove that they apply", and "If you cannot produce records to prove your claim, you may be liable to prosecution" (*verified*, R8). The arithmetic is where people go wrong: the only official aid is a 2005 one-page table of log volumes by mid-diameter and length (Huber's formula, *verified* R2), which can't be read off a standing tree; the threshold itself is measured at 1.3 m; the Act defines no method of measuring cubic content (*verified* R5); practitioners disagree whether branches count and call the table "more for plantation conifers than open grown broadleaves" (*source claim*, R4); the rules of thumb owners are given vary by a factor of several (S1, S10).
- **D. Current alternatives.** The FC's rough guide PDF (official, free, 2005); FC Booklets 39 and 49 with tariff charts and tables (free, archived, professional, very likely OGL: R3, S2); Welsh Farming Connect's calculator tab (*source claim*, not read, S4); asking the local woodland officer (free advice, suggested by GOV.UK and Arbtalk); rules of thumb (builders' bags, "40 cm is about a cubic metre"); a notebook or spreadsheet; generic or US calculators (board feet, cords); applying for a free felling licence instead. No dedicated app, calculator or tracker for the exemption in four searches (S2, S3, S4, S10), and none on the FC's own new service (*verified*, R8).
- **E. Strongest incumbent.** The FC's own rough guide plus a call to the woodland officer, and, for anyone near the limit, simply applying for the free licence. For a professional, the Blue Book tariff tables.
- **F. Labs wedge.** Offline in a wood with no signal, on a phone: each tree logged with its measurements (DBH by tape or girth; height by stick or clinometer; or a felled log's length and mid-diameter), the exemption it falls under or its estimated volume with a stated range, sold or kept; totals per calendar quarter per person across all their woods (and per wood), warned well before 5 and 2 m3; a printable dated record. That joins three manual steps (sorting by exemption, estimating from what can be measured, the quarterly tally) and produces the record the FC says you need. No account; nothing leaves the browser.
- **G. Technical fit.** Static and local; saving through `useOtherTabs` and `NotSaving` under a `labs:<slug>` key; a file export so a year of records doesn't live only in browser storage; print. Estimation: Huber for felled logs (matches the FC table exactly, R2); for standing trees, tariff equations from the FC booklets (OGL very likely, R3) or the WCC protocol (licence unchecked), or a stated form factor, tested against the Blue Book check values in S10. No server, no keyed service.
- **H. Strongest reason not to build** (searched for). (1) There is no legal measuring method, so the tool can only estimate, and a generous estimate near a threshold carrying an unlimited fine gives false comfort; it must say "estimate" everywhere and lean conservative. (2) The people asking treat the stakes as low ("dont expect anyone to be enforcing the rules", R4), and enforcement is rare (fewer than 1% of reported cases prosecuted, *source claim* S7), so demand may be thin; the evidence is a handful of forum threads, not a measured need. (3) Strip away the record and it is a formula and a sum, the "nice little utility" the batch rejects. (4) Two official texts frame the unit differently (per person in the Act, "from your property" on the FC service, R5 vs R8), and whether branches count is unsettled, so the tool has to show uncertainty where users want a yes or no. (5) The FC is rebuilding this exact journey (beta to 27 October 2026, R8) and ships free apps (Woodland Condition Assessment, S9), so it could add a calculator.
- **Deciding evidence:** for: the official aid doesn't fit standing trees (verified), no tool exists in four searches (source claims) or on the FC's new service (verified), and the FC says you must be able to prove the exemption (verified). Against: no legal method (verified), low perceived stakes, simple arithmetic.
- **Before any BUILD, the main session would need:** S.I. 1979/792 read for any measuring rule or further exceptions; the tariff equations' source and licence read in the primary PDF (Booklet 39 or the WCC protocol Part 2); a sourced answer on branches; and a sense of whether the record, not the calculation, is what owners lack.
