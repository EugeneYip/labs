### 1. Menus against the School Food Standards (England): REJECT for this batch
- **A. Audience:** providers of council-funded HAF holiday clubs in England (community groups, sports clubs, churches, childcare providers), school breakfast clubs, in-house school kitchens and small caterers; on the other side, councils' HAF teams and nutritionists reviewing menus (searches 1 and 3).
- **B. Job:** before: drafting a programme's menu (a week or a cycle of meals and snacks) for a HAF application, or a term's menu cycle. After: a menu shown to meet every daily, weekly and three-weekly rule across every food occasion, with each failure named, to submit or publish.
- **C. Why it matters:** statutory (the Requirements for School Food Regulations 2014; HAF's grant conditions); menus with choices and snacks make the weekly counts fiddly; councils won't consider HAF applications without full menus (Middlesbrough, *source claim*); a paid check costs £600–£700 a menu (LACA, *source claim*).

- **F. Wedge (as it would have been):** a free, local checker that counts every rule over the cycle from the user's own labels for each dish (deep-fried, pastry, oily fish, processed meat and so on), names each failure, and prints the check.
- **G. Fit:** static and offline; the rules are public (legislation.gov.uk, OGL).
- **H. Against, and deciding** (*verified*, read 1): the 2014 rules end in September 2027; the new regulations aren't made yet (to be laid "October 2026", subject to approval); the definitions a checker turns on (pastry products, breadcrumb-coated foods, processed meats, sweetened baked products) await DfE guidance; DfE promises tools to "manage weekly frequency limits" and a national monitoring system with the FSA. ### 2. Glider pilots' recency under Part-SFCL (UK and EU): REJECT
- **A. Audience:** glider pilots holding an SPL, and the CFIs and duty instructors who check them.
- **B. Job:** before a flying day or the club's annual check, the pilot needs to know whether they're legally recent: 5 hours as PIC including 15 launches, plus 2 training flights with an FI(S), in the rolling 24 months; recency for the launch method (5 launches in 24 months for winch, aerotow or self-launch); 3 launches in 90 days to carry a passenger. On top sits the club's currency barometer. After: yes or no, the date each item lapses, and what would restore it (search 6, *source claim*).
- **C. Why it matters:** a legal requirement for SPL privileges, new in the UK with the SPL (Swindon: "last year", *verified*); rolling windows and per-method counts kept in paper logbooks; the BGA publishes a guide to reading a logbook (October 2025, *source claim*), and many clubs keep an annual check on top.
- **D. Alternatives:** paper logbooks and clubs' annual-check forms (Scottish Gliding Centre, *source claim*); club management apps that show recency (Swindon, *verified*); e-logbooks with launch-method recency (capzlog, *source claim*), LogStar Glider and the Swiss FOCA's dLog (*source claim*); a free, configurable "Recency" app for iPhone (*verified*); myFlightbook covers FAA rules only (*source claim*).
- **E. Strongest incumbent:** the club's own system, which logs every launch anyway and, at Swindon, already shows recency to the pilot and the CFI.
- **F. Wedge:** free, no install, on any phone, offline at the airfield, with SFCL built in; mainly for pilots with paper logbooks who fly at more than one club.
- **G. Fit:** static and local.
- **H. Against, and deciding:** the data is already in the logbook or the club's log, so a page means retyping 24 months of flights; the tools are spreading (a club app in 2025, a free app in 2026); a small audience (UK glider pilots; an English-only page for the EU); active pilots clear the thresholds easily; a wrong "recent" has legal and safety exposure.
- **Verdict: REJECT.**

### Read 1 (the government's consultation response, September 2026)
- https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aa7af366d3764db1690455b/school-food-standards-updating-the-legislative-framework-government-consultation-response.pdf (DfE with OHID, "Government consultation response, September 2026", 36 pages, OGL). WebFetch returned the PDF as binary and saved a copy in the session's tool-results folder; the text was read from a scratchpad copy with macOS PDFKit, as thread F did (no second fetch, nothing saved in the repo). *verified* (the government's own document); quotes kept short.
- **Timing:** "The government intends to lay regulations in October 2026 which will apply from September 2027 subject to Parliamentary approval"; schools ready earlier "will be encouraged" to adopt them; some secondary requirements (healthier drinks, cheese, pulses) from September 2028, with a transition rule (secondary schools may serve up to three portions of foods containing cheese a week from September 2027 to August 2028, then two).
