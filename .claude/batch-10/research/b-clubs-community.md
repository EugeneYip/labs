## Log of searches and reads

**Read 1** (Gift Aid, HMRC's schedule guidance): https://www.gov.uk/guidance/schedule-spreadsheet-to-claim-back-tax-on-gift-aid-donations (*verified*, read 2026-10-14):
- Fields: title (max 4 characters), name (max 35; use a space, not a hyphen, in double-barrelled surnames), house name or number, postcode in capitals with a space, aggregated donations, sponsored event, donation date as DD/MM/YY (or the latest date in a series), amount with no £ and 2 decimals; over-claims entered as the tax over-claimed.
- 1,000 lines per schedule; "lines beyond that aren't attached"; more than one claim allowed, but one online claim must be submitted before starting the next.
- Aggregated lines: donations of £20 or less from different donors, a line's total at most £1,000, no donations linked to admission to visitor attractions, a description of at most 35 characters, the last donation's date, same accounting period only.
- One donor's regular gifts may go on one line with the most recent date, per accounting period.
- Sponsored events: "yes", the date received, the participant's total; any sponsor giving over £500 on their own line, not marked "yes".
- Non-residents: full address and country; no postcode for the Isle of Man or Channel Islands.
- **Format, the deciding fact:** the schedule is HMRC's own OpenDocument file, in an Excel version and a LibreOffice version ("don't convert between" them), its tab name (R68GAD_V1_00_0_EN) and .ods suffix unchanged, attached inside Charities Online. Its own troubleshooting: for "ASM" errors "check you haven't copied and pasted into the spreadsheet"; for other errors, check for spaces typed before or after donor details.
- *Inference:* (1) a Labs page can't safely hand over rows to paste, since HMRC itself names pasting as a cause of rejection; (2) the alternative, writing HMRC's .ods file directly, could only be proven by attaching it in Charities Online, which needs the charity's HMRC login (an account and credentials: outside Labs' limits and this batch). The page says nothing about GASDS.

**Search 1** (Gift Aid incumbents and problems; "free Gift Aid claim software small charity church treasurer Charities Online schedule spreadsheet problems"):
- Free: HMRC's own schedule (above); Comic Relief's GAIL (Gift Aid Interface Link), free and open source but needing a technologist to integrate and without GASDS (*source claim*, search summary of https://www.civilsociety.co.uk/news/hmrc-gives-more-details-on-charities-online.html, an older article); the Charity Tax Group's free online guide to completing the schedules (https://www.charitytaxgroup.org.uk/?p=7297, *source claim*).

- Problems listed are all about the file: don't rename the .ods suffix or the tab, use the right version for your software, other ODF programs may fail to attach, Mac users may need English GB settings (*source claim*, the same summary, consistent with read 1).

### Seed 1 verdict: club bar stocktake — REJECT (Batch 08's screen stands)

No new evidence. The clubs that take stock seriously pay an independent stocktaker, and what they buy is independence (the GCMA commentary's point: the people running the bar can make the figures look right); a self-run page can't give that. The arithmetic (opening + deliveries − closing, yields, GP) is what stocktakers, EPOS stock modules and spreadsheets already do, and no struggle with it was found; the hard part is counting honestly. Still small hospitality rather than volunteer admin. *inference* on the independence point, built on the source claims above.

## Own lead 1: wholefood buying groups splitting cases (unscreened in Batches 07–10)

**Search 3** ("wholefood buying group splitting cases between members spreadsheet coordinator Suma order food co-op"):
- The job exists: members split packs so no one ends up with too much (a Suffolk Suma group, https://www.eastsuffolk.gov.uk/assets/Environment/Green-Issues/How-to-set-up-a-food-group-with-Suma.pdf, *source claim*); "when splitting boxes between you, you'll have to do the maths yourself", and Suma has been willing to take several group orders and invoice each one separately to one address (https://selfsufficientish.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=117187, *source claim*, old forum); a coordinator's blog: a new spreadsheet "to log all the orders and start tallying up amounts", and "it took a while to set the whole sheet up with all the formulas" (Sustain food co-ops blog, https://www.sustainweb.org/foodcoops/philis_blog6/, *source claim*).
- Rules of the trade: Suma's free delivery over £250 and weekly cut-offs (*source claim*, same pages); freight split equally, by percentage, or by weight (*source claim*).
- Incumbents named: Google Sheets and Excel, the usual tool (an Australian buying-group FAQ, https://goodness.com.au/buying-groups-faqs/, *source claim*); Goodness's group platform has a "Split" feature (https://support.group.goodness.com.au/portal/en/kb/articles/what-is-a-split, *source claim*); Equal Exchange tells US clubs to tally orders into full cases or use buying-club software that "can handle split cases" (https://www.info.equalexchange.coop/articles/groups-sharing-cases-of-equal-exchange-products, *source claim*); an Open Food Network forum thread on "Order consolidation and split sheets" says it would be "way simpler" if the club didn't split cases (https://community.openfoodnetwork.org/t/order-consolidation-and-split-sheets/849, *source claim*).
- Known to me, not checked here: Foodsoft (free, open-source, hosted for German-speaking and Dutch food co-ops at foodcoops.net) lets members give a "tolerance" so part-cases fill (*inference*, unverified).

**Read 2** (Open Food Network forum, "Order consolidation and split sheets", March 2017): https://community.openfoodnetwork.org/t/order-consolidation-and-split-sheets/849 (*verified* as practitioners' posts; old):
- A buying co-op of about 30 members and often 30 products an order: members order, the manager consolidates into a wholesale order, and splits each case by decision (a 10-apple case, members asked for 3 and 5, given 4 and 6); its own "split sheets" record those decisions and make each member's packing list.
- Short and spoiled deliveries are common, so "the typical buying club invoices after delivery"; a wholesaler crediting 80% of a line means adjusting many members' orders; deleting members' items for short stock is a "pain to do"; refunds and re-sent invoices after edits are manual.
- OFN (free platform) covers it only partly (min-max ordering to help fill cases, bulk totals reports); the thread agrees splitting cases into members' orders isn't fully supported. Foodsoft is cited as allocating automatically.
- Other posters: a Canadian running buying groups on OFN, a French buying-club organiser.

**Search 4** (any free tool for the coordinator's half; "bulk buying club split case calculator members packing list short delivery invoice tool free 2025"):

### Own lead 1 verdict: buying-group case splitting — weak

- A: the volunteer coordinator of a wholefood buying group or buying club (UK groups ordering from Suma and similar wholesalers; US clubs; French and Canadian clubs on OFN).
- B: before, members' orders collated (a shared sheet, a form, an OFN report), the wholesale order placed, the delivery in; after, each case split between members, a packing list for each member's box, and each member's bill for what actually came, with the delivery charge shared.
- C: recurring (every order round), fiddly after short or spoiled deliveries (read 2), and done in a home-made spreadsheet that "took a while" to build (search 3).
- D and E: spreadsheets (the real incumbent); Foodsoft (free, open source, allocates automatically, a hosted service with member accounts, mainly German-speaking and Dutch co-ops, *inference* plus read 2's mention); OFN (free, partial: min-max and bulk reports); Goodness's group platform (Australia, has splits); wholesalers invoicing sub-orders separately (Suma, *source claim*).
- F: the coordinator's half only, after delivery: whole cases and splits decided, shortfalls re-allocated by a stated rule, member box lists to print, bills with delivery shared, nothing uploaded, works offline in the hall on sorting day.
- G: fits technically (a single coordinator's device). But the valuable version is members placing their own orders, which needs shared state; without it the orders must be pasted from the group's sheet (the parsing pattern) or retyped.
- H, against: the practitioner evidence is one 2017 thread and old blogs; the audience is small and scattered; once built, the group's spreadsheet is reused every round; splitting is a judgement the manager makes ("by the manager's decision", read 2); and it sits next to cost splitting (#003 Fair Share). Not worth more budget unless the main session wants a groups tool.

## Own lead 2: community shares interest and withdrawals (unscreened) — REJECT

**Search 6** ("community shares annual share interest calculation pro rata share register spreadsheet society treasurer withdrawals Community Shares Unit"):
- The job: a community benefit society's treasurer (community pubs, shops, energy) pays discretionary interest on withdrawable shares within the maximum declared in its rules or offer, often credited to members' share accounts, and handles withdrawals at the board's discretion (Co-operatives UK, Community Shares Handbook 6.1 and Finance Guide 3.1, https://www.uk.coop/resources/community-shares-handbook/6-share-interest-and-use-profit-or-surplus/61-interest-share and https://www.uk.coop/resources/community-shares-finance-guide/3-managing-members-financial-interests/31-interest, *source claim* via the search summary).
- **Deciding fact:** Co-operatives UK's own **AirRegister**, "a free, customisable tool hosted on Airtable" to manage the member register, interest payments, share withdrawals and new share applications, a DIY alternative to paid registry services (https://www.uk.coop/blog/airregister-maintaining-your-societys-membership-register, *source claim*, not opened).
- Verdict **REJECT**: the sector body already gives societies a free tool for exactly this; the register is a statutory record that must outlive a browser and be shared by the board (the settled storage objection); interest is once a year and a pro-rata formula.
