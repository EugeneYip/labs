## Ranked summary

1. **Lead: a visitor levy return worksheet for small accommodation providers (Scotland).** Edinburgh's levy (5% of the accommodation-only price net of VAT, first five nights, stays from 24 July 2026) is live; Glasgow follows (5%, no cap, 25 January 2027: *verified* on visitorlevy.scot), then Aberdeen (7%, April 2027) and Stirling (3%, first 7 nights, June 2027) (*source claim*). Every quarter, each provider must give, for each month, the accommodation-only total (no levy in it), revenue from qualifying pre-October-2025 bookings, and revenue from nights after the fifth, and must "evidence their calculations" on request, keeping records five years (*verified*, Edinburgh's guide v2.1). The official platform's calculator starts from those three totals (*verified*); the council endorses no calculator, its platform's included (*verified*), and its training won't cover the calculation (*source claim*); Airbnb neither remits nor reports and many hosts' prices include the levy (*verified*); FreeToBook's post covers only the guest's price (*verified*). Wedge: from a pasted booking list to the monthly boxes, with each booking's working printed, judgments left to the user, nothing uploaded. Against: mostly one multiplication, so a spreadsheet is the default; legal stakes; rules moving and differing by council; repeats #036's shape; Scottish audience.
2. **Weak: FRS 102's new lease accounting** (periods from 1 January 2026, small companies and charities included): a real new yearly calculation, but done by accountants whose accounts-production software is adding it; spreadsheets handle a few leases; professional-reporting cluster.
3. **REJECT: the HFSS nutrient profiling score** (promotion rules October 2025, advertising January 2026): free calculators already, including the University of Leeds one that councils recommend.

## Scan log

### Search 1 (Scotland's visitor levy, Edinburgh's rules and returns)
- Query: Edinburgh visitor levy accommodation provider guidance, quarterly return, 5%, first five nights, VAT, apportionment, bookings before 1 October 2025.
- Council press release (*source claim* via search snippet, https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/news/article/14141/edinburgh-declares-scotlands-first-visitor-levy): 5%, for a maximum of five consecutive nights, year-round, the whole council area; applies to advance bookings made from 1 October 2025 for stays on or after 24 July 2026.
- Industry and law-firm summaries (*source claim*: DWF, https://dwfgroup.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/edinburgh-are-you-ready-for-the-visitor-levy and its February 2026 update; Roommaster, https://www.roommaster.com/hotel-tax/edinburgh; the Scotsman Hotel, https://scotsmanhotel.co.uk/edinburgh-visitor-levy/): the provider collects the levy and submits quarterly returns through a "national visitor levy platform"; the first return covers 24 July to 30 September 2026 and is due in October 2026 (now); records kept five years after each stay; providers may keep 2% of the levy to offset admin (still "to be confirmed" in February 2026); the levy is 5% of the accommodation cost per room or unit before VAT and before extras (breakfast, parking, meals, transport); VAT then applies to accommodation plus levy (Scottish Financial News commentary says the levy itself bears 20% VAT). Wording of the booking-date rule differs between sources: "made", "paid", "paid in part or full" on or after 1 October 2025.
- So far (*inference*): every booking needs a levy figure from a few mechanical rules (stay dates against 24 July 2026, booking or payment date against 1 October 2025, a five-night cap, accommodation net of VAT and extras), and every quarter needs a return. Open: what the national platform asks for and computes, whether booking platforms collect, the official wording.

### Search 3 (official provider guidance: Edinburgh, Glasgow, gov.scot; restricted to those domains)
- Query: visitor levy guidance for accommodation providers, how to calculate, cancellations, extras, records, return platform, register.
- Official documents found (*source claim*, search summaries of the councils' own guides): Edinburgh "Visitor Levy Scheme: information for accommodation providers", Version 1 August 2025 (https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/downloads/file/37836/edinburgh-visitor-levy-scheme-information-for-accommodation-providers) and Version 2 August 2026 (https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/visitorlevyinformation); "Visitor Levy: Information for businesses" (https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/business/information-businesses); Glasgow's "Visitor Levy Scheme User Guide for Accommodation Providers" (https://www.glasgow.gov.uk/media/22017/The-User-Guide-for-Accommodation-Providers/pdf/Glasgow_Visitor_Levy_User_Guide.pdf); gov.scot policy page (https://www.gov.scot/policies/taxes/local-visitor-levy/).
- The per-booking rules (*source claim*, Edinburgh's guide as summarised): the levy is "5% of the accommodation only portion of the transaction, net of VAT"; leave out meals or drinks, parking, laundry, entertainment, transport to and from the accommodation; standard cleaning stays in (no option to book uncleaned); only the first 5 nights are charged; a third party's purchase is levied on the initial contract price agreed with the provider; a cancelled prepaid booking has its levy refunded to the visitor and not remitted; no levy is due if nothing was paid before a cancellation or no-show; Version 2 (August 2026): only stays that start on or after 24 July 2026 are liable.
- The return (*source claim*): registration, returns and payments all go through the national platform (visitorlevy.scot); Glasgow's guide: quarterly returns "reporting the total revenue received from accommodation charges, for each month in the period"; Edinburgh also asks whether the property had full, part or no availability in the quarter; retention 2% of the levy due in Edinburgh, 1.5% in Glasgow; the Act was amended in 2026 so returns can be amended after submission; records kept five years (s.28; Edinburgh: five years after the stay) covering each chargeable transaction and its payments and receipts.
- What this means (*inference*): the platform takes monthly totals and does the 5% and the retention; it can't apply a per-booking five-night cap, the extras and VAT exclusions, or cancellations, so those fall to the provider before totalling. That per-booking step, a month-by-month total for the return, and the five-year record are the job a tool could do. Each council's rules differ (Edinburgh 5% capped at 5 nights and 2% retention; Glasgow 1.5% retention; Stirling 3%).

### Read 1 (Edinburgh's provider guide, Version 2.1, September 2026)
- https://www.edinburgh.gov.uk/visitorlevyinformation (a 15-page PDF). WebFetch returned it as binary and saved a copy in the session's tool-results folder; the text was read from that copy with macOS PDFKit (no second fetch, nothing saved in the repo). *verified* (the council's own document); quotes kept short.
- **The rule per booking:** 5% "of the accommodation only portion of the transaction, net of VAT"; out: meals or drinks, parking, laundry, entertainment, transport, and any extra the visitor can opt out of (paid linen, towels, opt-out cleaning); in: standard cleaning. The provider deducts what it "deem[s] to be reasonably attributable" to non-accommodation costs (a judgment left to the provider). The levy counts as turnover and bears VAT for VAT-registered providers. Commission is in the levy base when it's part of the initial transaction (an OTA acting as agent), out when the provider pays it, and a wholesaler's resale is a subsequent transaction (worked examples: £250 with a 25% OTA commission gives £12.50; £200 with a £15 a night commission paid by the hotel gives £10; £175 x 3 to a wholesaler gives £26.25).
- **The five-night cap:** payable for at most 5 consecutive nights per chargeable transaction; "the actual value attributable to the first five nights" is used, "rather than the average cost of five nights across a stay" (10 nights at £500 then £1,000: levy 5% of £500); per-night cleaning counts only for the first five nights, a flat cleaning fee counts in full; scenarios: a 4-night stay plus a later 2-night stay in the same room are both fully liable; one company booking of 14 nights shared by two employees is one transaction capped at 5; "Accommodation providers are required to evidence their calculations if instructed to do so by the Council."
- **Which stays:** stays that start on or after 24 July 2026 (a stay that began before is not liable even if it ends after); bookings paid in part or full before 1 October 2025 are exempt only if the contract has specific dates, a precise number of rooms, a stated nightly price, and the payment is tangible, attributable to rooms and non-refundable.
- **Cancellations and refunds:** the levy becomes payable when the visitor takes entry; no entry (cancellation, no-show), nothing in the return; a refund after entry doesn't remove the levy. Exempt individuals pay up front and reclaim from the council (the provider charges everyone). Also covered: gift cards and points (levied at GBP value), complimentary stays (no transaction), paid upgrades (in).
- **The return** (through visitorlevy.scot, quarterly in arrears; first return 1-30 October 2026 for 24 July-30 September, payment by 13 November 2026; a stay crossing quarters goes in the quarter of check-out): quarterly availability (full, part or none, with a reason); and, **for each month**, three figures: "Total paid by customers for accommodation only" (no levy), "Revenue from overnight stays booked before 1 Oct 2025", "Revenue from overnight stays beyond 5 nights" (6th night onwards). On request: monthly total availability, occupied nights (units x nights), nights booked before 1 October 2025, number of bookings longer than 5 nights, nights beyond 5. Optional evidence upload. The platform applies the 2% retention.
- **Records:** five years from each submission (s.28): each stay, each chargeable transaction, and the payments, receipts and arrangements behind them.
- **Incumbents named by the council:** "a number of calculators available online" and OTAs' own levy calculation, none endorsed; visitorlevy.scot has "A Visitor Levy Calculator" (link https://visitorlevy.scot/calculate-levy, not yet read), also unendorsed, "used at the user's own risk".
- **Reading** (*inference*): the platform asks for monthly sums the provider must build booking by booking: accommodation-only (net of VAT and extras), the part after the fifth night at actual nightly values, pre-October-2025 bookings, cancellations out, each stay placed by check-out. That's a mechanical, repeatable split with judgments (what's an extra, what's the initial transaction) clearly the provider's. The council also expects the working to be shown on request.

### Search 8 (a second lead: FRS 102's new lease accounting for small companies and charities)
- Query: FRS 102 lease accounting changes 1 January 2026, small companies, Section 1A, charities, lease liability, right-of-use, calculator, spreadsheet, free, obtainable borrowing rate, transition.
