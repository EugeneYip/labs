## Notes, in order

### Lead 1. Language sample analysis (SLTs / SLPs)

- **Search 1** (language sample analysis, MLU calculator, SUGAR, SALT cost, free tools):
  - SALT (Systematic Analysis of Language Transcripts): a single-user licence "about $295" (*source claim*, a vendor-written comparison guide, https://conductscience.com/resources/salt-vs-free-alternatives); an older TalkBank/ASHA 2018 comparison table lists SALT at $179 and CLAN/KidEval as free, with some coding still by hand (*source claim*, https://talkbank.org/fluency/resources/ASHA18.pdf).
  - SUGAR (Pavelko and Owens 2017) is a free protocol (50 utterances, norms from n = 1,576), not software (*source claim*, same guide).
  - **A free in-browser incumbent already exists:** ConductScience's MLU calculator "implements Brown (1973) morpheme counting rules and runs entirely in your browser" (*source claim*, search summary, https://conductscience.com/tools/mlu-calculator), plus a SUGAR norms lookup (https://conductscience.com/tools/sugar-norms-lookup) and per-metric pages (https://conductscience.com/speech-therapy/metrics/mlu-w). They come from the team behind a commercial product, ConductSpeech (*source claim*). Simpler free calculators (enter total morphemes and utterances) exist too (https://everycalculators.com/mlu-calculator.html), and a page of AI prompts for computing MLU (https://aiforeducation.io/prompts/calculate-mean-length-of-utterance); many "free MLU calculator" results are spam clones (*inference*).
  - *Inference:* the counting step is already served free and locally; the page read next checks how far that tool goes (transcript in, morphemes counted, which measures, privacy).
- **Read 1** (https://conductscience.com/tools/mlu-calculator, read 2026-10-14): the incumbent already does the counting step free and locally (*verified* as the page's own description):
  - Paste a transcript, "One utterance per line"; morphemes counted automatically by Brown (1973) rules (contractions 2, regular -s/-ed/-ing 2, irregular past and plural 1, catenatives and diminutives 1, hyphenated compounds 1); the page calls its counter "heuristic" and says to check "contested tokens by hand"; no per-word override described.
  - Outputs MLU in morphemes and in words, totals, Brown's stage, a per-utterance breakdown; separate free tools for lexical diversity (TTR, NDW), percent grammatical utterances, and the SUGAR norms lookup.
  - "Data never leaves your browser", "No install, no sign-up, no upload"; free. Its paid sibling ConductSpeech "adds normative comparison, error categorisation, and a parent-ready report" (no price on the page).
  - *Inference:* Labs' usual wedge (local, no account, free, transcript in, measures out) is taken. What's left is either the transcribing itself or a better artifact than a paid report; the next search tests whether transcription is the real bottleneck.
- **Search 2** (surveys of SLPs' use of LSA and its barriers):
  - Nationwide US survey of school-based SLPs (Pavelko, Owens, Ireland and Hahs-Vaughn 2016, LSHSS): a third didn't use LSA in 2012-13; most analysed fewer than 10 samples a year and **transcribed in real time**; the most cited barrier was that LSA is "too time-consuming" (*source claim*, search summary of the abstract, https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2016_LSHSS-15-0044).
  - Dutch focus group and training study (Klatte et al. 2022): barriers were knowledge and skills, time, confidence, role beliefs, and no reimbursement; after CLAN training LSA was **not** done more often; the authors suggest user-friendly software co-designed with SLPs (*source claim*, https://www.internationalhu.com/research/publications/language-sample-analysis-in-clinical-practice-speechlanguage-pathologists-barriers-facilitators-and ; open access at https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2021_LSHSS-21-00026).
  - A 2026 LSHSS implementation study: most school SLPs say system-level barriers limit formal LSA (*source claim*, https://pubs.asha.org/doi/10.1044/2026_LSHSS-25-00188). A 2024 ATSU student survey: time and training (*source claim*, https://scholarworks.atsu.edu/slp-researchprojects/25).
  - (Citations taken from search results; not resolved through a library link resolver in this thread.)
- **Lead 1 assessment:**
  - A: SLTs/SLPs assessing children's expressive language (schools, private practice, NHS community clinics). B: just recorded or live-noted a 50-utterance conversation or narrative; need MLU, NDW, words and clauses per sentence, percent grammatical utterances against norms for a report or eligibility decision. C: recurring and rule-constrained (Brown's or SUGAR's counting rules), child data is sensitive. D: SALT (about $179-$295 a licence, *source claim*), CLAN/KidEval (free desktop), SUGAR protocol and norms (free), ConductScience's free in-browser MLU, lexical-diversity, PGU and SUGAR-norms tools, by-hand worksheets, AI prompts. **E: the strongest incumbent is ConductScience's free tools** (local, no sign-up, Brown's rules automated, *verified*), with SALT for full analysis.
  - F: no wedge left on counting. The barrier the evidence names is time spent collecting and transcribing (real-time transcription is the common practice), and training in free software didn't change behaviour. A static page can't remove transcription except by on-device speech recognition, and verbatim transcription of a young or language-impaired child's speech (errors kept, not corrected) is exactly what general speech recognisers do worst (*inference*, not searched).
  - G: technically easy (text in, counts out). H: the deciding reason not to build: a free, local, no-account tool already does the counting, and the bottleneck is elsewhere.
  - **Verdict: REJECT.** Deciding evidence: ConductScience's in-browser MLU calculator (*verified*) plus the surveys naming time and transcription, not arithmetic (*source claim*).

### Lead 2. Holiday pay for variable pay or irregular hours (UK micro-employers)

- **Search 3** (free 52-week holiday pay calculators; weeks with no pay; 104 weeks):
  - The rule as Acas states it (*source claim*, search summary, https://www.acas.org.uk/irregular-hours-and-part-year-workers/calculating-holiday-pay and https://www.acas.org.uk/calculating-holiday-pay): average pay over the previous 52 weeks; weeks with no pay skipped, looking back up to 104 weeks and no further (fewer than 52 weeks used if that's all there is); weeks of SSP-only sickness or statutory leave replaced by earlier weeks; regular overtime and commission included.
  - gov.uk publishes worked guidance, "Holiday pay: calculate your average weekly pay if it varied" (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/holiday-pay-calculate-your-average-weekly-pay-if-it-varied/holiday-pay-calculate-your-average-weekly-pay-if-it-varied), aimed at workers checking their pay (*inference* from the title; read next).
  - Free calculators exist: QX Accounting's "holiday pay overtime calculator" for accountants, covering the 52-week average and 12.07% accrual (*source claim*, https://qxaccounting.com/uk/tools-for-accountants/holiday-pay-overtime-calculator/). Payroll and HR vendors sell it as a feature: Employment Hero "How to easily calculate 52 week average for holiday pay" (https://employmenthero.com/uk/solutions/holiday-pay-calculation/), Staffology's guide (https://staffology.co.uk/?p=3315), LeaveWizard, Peninsula (advice service), and CIPP guidance for payroll professionals (https://www.cipp.org.uk/resources/news/calculating-holiday-pay-52-week-reference-period.html).
- **Read 2** (the gov.uk page above, read 2026-10-14): it is the Insolvency Service's guidance for **former employees claiming from an insolvent employer** (published 6 April 2021), not employer guidance (*verified*). Method: gross pay for each of the 52 weeks before dismissal, leaving out weeks of sickness, maternity or paternity leave, or no work, then divide by 52 or by the weeks available; an hourly-rate variant averages hours; worked examples (Sam £13,000 / 52 = £250, Robin 26.5 hours = £265). No calculator, nothing on 104 weeks, overtime, 12.07% or rolled-up pay; it points to separate gov.uk guidance on workers without fixed hours or pay (*verified*). Spent a read on the wrong page; the employer rules are Acas's (above, *source claim*).
- **Read 3** (https://qxaccounting.com/uk/tools-for-accountants/holiday-pay-overtime-calculator/, read 2026-10-14): a **summary-figure** calculator, not a reference-period builder (*verified* as the page's description): hourly base rate, contracted and standard weekly hours, leave hours taken, "Reference weeks actually paid (max 52)", "Exclude any weeks with no earnings", overtime lines with a multiplier and "Include as Regular?"; no week-by-week entry, no 104-week look-back, no replacement of sick or statutory-leave weeks, nothing on commission or a day's pay for varying days; a CSV breakdown; "a guidance tool, not as a substitute for formal payroll or legal advice", results "estimates", not "legally binding". Cost, sign-up and data handling not stated.
  - *Inference:* a free tool that takes each week's gross pay, skips unpaid weeks back to 104, swaps SSP-only and statutory-leave weeks for earlier ones, and shows which weeks it used, was not found in this search; the paid payroll packages do it from the pay history they already hold (Employment Hero's page above sells exactly that).

### Lead 4 (own). A lifetime gifts record for inheritance tax (UK households)

- **Search 8** (gift records, normal expenditure out of income, IHT403, annual exemption carry-forward):
  - The job is real and documented: executors claim the exemptions on HMRC's IHT403 after a death; for normal expenditure out of income they need each year's net income, normal spending and the gifts (to whom, how much), plus evidence the pattern was intended to be regular (a standing order, a letter); missing records don't kill the claim but make it "a lot harder" for the personal representatives (*source claim*, adviser guides: https://adviser.royallondon.com/technical-central/protection-guidance/inheritance-tax-and-related-manuals/inheritance-tax-and-normal-expenditure-out-of-income/ , https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/normal-expenditure-out-of-income-exemption-how-does-it-work/ , https://www.investcentre.co.uk/literature/iht-planning-gifts-out-income).
  - The annual exemption rule is mechanical: £3,000 a year, this year's used first, then last year's unused part, never older; gifts in a tax year taken in date order (*source claim*, https://professionalparaplanner.co.uk/technicalzone/inheritance-tax-annual-exemption-what-you-need-to-know/ ; HMRC's manual IHTM14144 on the roll-over, https://gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14144, not read).
  - Incumbents: **Hargreaves Lansdown's Gift Record Form** (PDF, https://www.hl.co.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0012/15179556/Gift-Record-Form.pdf), Standard Life's IHTS3 (https://www.standardlife.co.uk/dam/jcr:248958d9-fc0d-4128-9c12-45f204567529/ihts3.pdf), Quilter's and other advisers' gifting diaries, a free web calculator at sum.money (https://sum.money/uk/inheritance-tax-gifts-calculator/), a 2026/27 guide "How to Record Lifetime Gifts for Inheritance Tax: What Executors Will Need" (https://salarytax.uk/guides/how-to-record-gifts-for-inheritance-tax), and estate-record services (gofile.co.uk). No free spreadsheet that follows IHT403's headings surfaced (*source claim*, search summary).
- **Read 5** (https://sum.money/uk/inheritance-tax-gifts-calculator/, read 2026-10-14): a **single-gift estimator**, not a record (*verified* as the page describes itself): one gift amount and "Years since gift" (0 to 7+); annual exemption as yes/no toggles for this year and last, not allocated across dated gifts; small gifts as a count of recipients times £250; wedding gift optional; normal expenditure out of income only in the FAQ; IHT now and a taper table; no register, no dates, no IHT403 table; inputs "encoded in the URL", "No registration · No cookies", "Not financial advice", tax year 2025/26.
- **Lead 4 assessment:**
  - A: people in the UK making lifetime gifts (often retired), and adult children helping them; their executors later. B: before, a gift is made or a regular standing order set up; after, the gift recorded with the exemption it uses, the date it falls out of the seven years, and for regular gifts a per-tax-year table of net income, normal spending and gifts under IHT403's headings, printed and kept with the will. C: a rule and a form (IHT403) with 40% tax at stake; personal finances, so local processing matters; recurring each gift and each tax year.
  - D/E: Hargreaves Lansdown's Gift Record Form and other providers' gifting diaries (PDF), spreadsheets, advisers and solicitors; sum.money for a single-gift estimate. **Strongest incumbent: a provider's printable gift record form plus the donor's own spreadsheet** (and, for anyone with real exposure, an adviser).
  - F: the date-order allocation of the annual exemption with one-year carry-forward, small-gift and wedding limits per recipient, seven-year and taper dates per gift, the income-and-spending table per tax year, a printout and a saved file; nothing uploaded.
  - G: static fits, but the record has to last seven to fourteen years and be found by executors: a browser's storage is the wrong home, so it would live in an exported file and a printout (*inference*).
  - H (against): (1) **tax stakes**: whether regular gifts really are normal expenditure out of income (a pattern, from income, standard of living kept) is a judgment HMRC tests after death; a page that labels gifts "exempt" could mislead, and only the annual-exemption and small-gift arithmetic is mechanical; (2) no first-hand friction found (not searched further); (3) long-lived records suit paper and spreadsheets; (4) a minority of estates pay IHT today (*inference*; the share is expected to rise when unused pensions enter estates from April 2027, *inference*, not checked).
  - **Verdict: weak.** Deciding evidence: the gap is real (no free tool keeps a dated register or IHT403's table, *verified* for sum.money), but the valuable judgment is tax advice, the record outlives a browser, and providers already hand out printable forms.
### Lead 2, continued (read 6, searches 9 and 10, reads 7 and 8, in the order they were done)

- **Read 6** (Acas, "Holiday pay", irregular-hours and part-year workers, last updated 22 April 2026, https://www.acas.org.uk/calculating-holiday-pay, read 2026-10-14). *Verified* as the page states it:
  - Holiday pay is "based on their average pay over the previous 52 weeks", whenever the leave year starts. A week off sick on SSP only, a week of statutory leave on reduced pay (statutory maternity pay, for example), or a week with no pay: the employer "should use an earlier week in its place", counting back only as far as needed to reach 52, up to 104 weeks "but no further"; with fewer full weeks employed, use those (a 26-week example).
  - Must be included, for the full 5.6 weeks (for these workers): payments linked to tasks the contract requires (commission), payments for professional or personal status (length of service, seniority, qualifications), and other payments such as overtime "if regularly paid during the last year".
  - Not on this page: the exact end of the reference period, a day's pay when days vary, 12.07% and rolled-up pay (separate pages: https://www.acas.org.uk/irregular-hours-and-part-year-workers and https://www.acas.org.uk/irregular-hours-and-part-year-workers/rolled-up-holiday-pay), records, claims.

### Lead 2, verdict

- **E, restated without weakening it:** for employers who want it automated, a paid cloud payroll or HR suite that claims to do the 52-week average (Employment Hero's page sells exactly that, *source claim*); for everyone on Xero, Sage 50, BrightPay, Moneysoft or HMRC's Basic PAYE Tools, the incumbent is the payroll's own report (Sage's 52-week report, Moneysoft's average-pay report, Xero's Gross to Net) plus a spreadsheet, or a bookkeeper who keeps one. Acas has no calculator on its page (*verified*); gov.uk's calculator covers entitlement, not pay (*inference*, not re-read in this thread).
- **What decides it for:** a dated, current, vendor-acknowledged gap in the payroll products small employers use (Xero declined it on 27 November 2025 with 93 votes, *verified*; Moneysoft says it can't do it from the data it holds, *source claim*; BrightPay's notes list nothing, *verified* absence), a mechanical official selection rule (Acas, *verified*), vendor guides getting it wrong (*source claim*), and a new duty from 6 April 2026 to keep records that show holiday pay was right, for six years (*source claim*, several law firms; enacted text unchecked). Payroll data is personal, so nothing uploaded is a real advantage over a vendor's web form.
- **What decides it against (to weigh in the main session):** (1) **employment-law liability**: the employer's labels (worker type: irregular-hours or part-year or not; which weeks were SSP-only or reduced statutory pay; which elements count; whether overtime is "regular") carry the legal judgment, and an underpayment is an unlawful-deductions claim at an employment tribunal; a worker using the page to check their own pay could build a claim on a wrong figure. The page could only do the selection and arithmetic exactly, show every week used, skipped and replaced, and say plainly that the classification is theirs. (2) **Cluster:** its realistic input is a payroll report export, close to the export-to-report pattern of #034 and #035; keeping the input to pasted or typed weekly amounts reduces that but doesn't remove it. (3) **Rule detail still to verify** before any build: the end of the reference period (the last complete week before leave; "week" ending Saturday unless paid weekly), monthly-paid workers, the different bases for the 4 weeks and the 1.6 weeks for workers with normal hours, the regulation number and text of the records duty (legislation.gov.uk). (4) The irregular-hours case can be avoided with rolled-up pay at 12.07%, so the core audience is variable-pay staff with normal hours and irregular-hours staff not on rolled-up pay.
- **Thread verdict: lead** (the strongest of this thread). Deciding evidence: Xero's declined request and its commenters' spreadsheets (*verified*), payroll vendors leaving the figure to the user, and the April 2026 records duty; the liability and cluster risks above are the main session's to weigh.
