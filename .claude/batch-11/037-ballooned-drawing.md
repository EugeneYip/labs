# #037 Ballooned Drawing

Dossier for Batch 11's first experiment. Chosen 2026-10-15 (`CANDIDATES.md`, "Round 1: the weighing and the full gate"); built on `wip/037-ballooned-drawing`.

## The job

A small machine shop, fabricator, or contract manufacturer gets a purchase order for a new part, or a revision, with a first article inspection (FAI) required. Before measuring, the inspector numbers every characteristic on the customer's drawing (every dimension, tolerance, GD&T callout, and note, on every sheet) with a "balloon", and builds a table with one row per balloon: the characteristic number, where it is on the drawing (sheet and zone), the requirement as drawn, and the measured results. Under AS9102 that table is Form 3, and the ballooned print goes to the customer with the report, its numbers matching Form 3's rows (GKN's supplier guidance; Boeing adopts AS9102). Automotive PPAP dimensional results and medical device first articles ask for the same thing.

Today it's done with stamps in a paid Acrobat or hexagons drawn on paper, then every requirement typed again into a Form 3 template, or with paid software. Werk24's free Werkflow Studio does it in the cloud, but export-controlled and covered defence drawings can't lawfully go to a server that decrypts them (ITAR 22 CFR 120.54(a)(5) and (b)(1); DFARS 252.204-7012 (b)(2)(ii)(D), flowed down by (m)), and many commercial drawings carry proprietary legends their holders won't upload. The free offline tools are desktop installs or programs built from source; the one browser-local tool costs $360 a user a year. The page is for that gap: free, nothing to install, no account, and the drawing never leaves the computer, which anyone can check by turning the network off.

## Scope (first version)

- **Open a drawing:** a PDF of one or more sheets, chosen or dropped; each page is a sheet. Zoom (fit width, in, out) and scroll; sheets by tab or Page Up/Down.
- **Place balloons:** click or tap the drawing and a balloon with the next number appears there; drag to move; delete; insert as a chosen number (later ones move up); renumber by position on the drawing; undo and redo. From the keyboard: a crosshair moved with the arrow keys, Enter to place.
- **The requirement:** where the PDF has a text layer, the line of text nearest the click fills the requirement (two stacked numbers are read as limits); otherwise, or to correct it, typed. The drawing's text can be wrong (CAD symbol fonts, text drawn as outlines, scans), so it's always shown for checking.
- **Limits, read from the requirement** (the rules below), or from the title block's general tolerances by decimal places and for angles when the requirement has none, or typed in as lower and upper limits. Reference and basic dimensions, threads, fits without stated limits, and notes are recognised and treated as attributes or not measured, never given invented limits.
- **Results:** one to five pieces; each result is one or more values (for "4X" features), or pass, fail, or not applicable for attributes. Pass or fail is worked out exactly (no floating point), and a result exactly at a limit passes.
- **Reference location:** the sheet, and the zone from a grid set per sheet (the drawing frame, columns and rows, the corner where zone 1 and A start, I and O skipped as both ASME Y14.1 and ISO 5457 do; to be confirmed), or the sheet alone.
- **Checks, always visible:** balloons per sheet; requirements missing; limits not read; results missing; failures, and failures without a nonconformance number.
- **Out:** the ballooned PDF (balloons drawn onto the original vector pages; a file that is protected or that the writer can't read gets a copy rebuilt from page images, and says so); the Form 3 table as a spreadsheet file (CSV), columns in Form 3's order plus the limits and each piece's result; a printed Form 3 table with its header (part number, part name, serial number, FAIR identifier); the work saved as a file to open again.
- **Kept:** the drawing and the work in this browser (`labs:ballooned-drawing`), so a reload loses nothing; another open tab's changes taken up (`useOtherTabs`); `NotSaving` when the browser won't keep it; a control to clear it. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **Left out:** reading scans (OCR); ballooning every callout automatically; Forms 1 and 2 (typed records of the part, materials, and processes, which a shop's own template holds); importing CMM reports; ISO 286 fit tables and ISO 2768 general tolerance tables (the limits are typed, or the title block's own tolerances are entered); bonus tolerance from material condition modifiers (noted, not added); leader lines; 3D model-based definitions; drawings as images or DXF/DWG.

## How requirements become limits

All arithmetic is on exact decimals (and exact sixtieths for angles in degrees, minutes, and seconds). The rules, in the order tried; anything that doesn't fit one is "limits not read", and the page asks for them rather than guessing:

1. **Clean-up:** ⌀, Ø, and ø read as the diameter sign; `+/-` and `+-` as ±; the minus sign and dashes as a minus where they're deviations; ° and º as degrees; `DEG` as degrees; a decimal comma only when the user chooses decimal commas.
2. **A count first** ("4X", "4 PLCS", "4 PLACES"): kept as the count; the rest is read as below.
3. **Reference** (the whole value in brackets, or "REF"): not measured. **Basic** (boxed, `[25.4]`, or "BSC", "BASIC"): not measured on its own.
4. **GD&T** (a symbol such as ⌖ ⏥ ⌓ ⌒ ⟂ ∥ ∠ ○ ⌭ ↗ ⌰ ◎ ⌯ ⏤, or the words POS, FLAT, PROFILE, PERP, PARALLEL, ANGULARITY, CIRCULARITY, CYLINDRICITY, RUNOUT, CONCENTRICITY, SYMMETRY, STRAIGHTNESS): the first number (after an optional Ø) is the tolerance; the result is the measured deviation, from 0 to the tolerance. Material condition modifiers are noted as "bonus not added".
5. **Surface finish** ("Ra 1.6", "1.6 Ra", "Rz 6.3"): the value is the upper limit.
6. **Threads** ("M8x1.25-6H", "1/4-20 UNC-2B", "#10-32 UNF") and **fits without stated limits** ("Ø12 H7"): attributes. A fit followed by its limits ("Ø12 H7 (12.000/12.018)") uses those limits.
7. **MAX or MIN** after or before a value: the upper or lower limit only.
8. **Nominal ± tolerance:** nominal minus to nominal plus.
9. **Nominal with two deviations** ("+0.10/-0.00", "+0.1 -0", "-0.02/-0.05", "+0.05/+0.02"): nominal plus the lower to nominal plus the higher.
10. **Two limits** ("12.05/11.95", "11.95 / 12.05", "11.95 to 12.05", and two stacked numbers from the drawing): the smaller to the larger. A hyphen between two numbers with no spaces is refused as ambiguous.
11. **A lone value** (with Ø, R, SR, and similar prefixes, units, and words like TYP or THRU): the title block's tolerance for its number of decimal places (or for angles), if set; otherwise limits not read.

## Principal failure modes (the pre-build risk review), and how each is handled and checked

1. **Balloons in the wrong place.** Rotated pages, crop boxes smaller than the media box, a media box not at the origin, `UserUnit`, zoom, device pixel ratio. Positions are kept in the PDF's own user space and converted through pdf.js's viewport both ways. *Check:* fixtures with each quirk, made independently (Python's reportlab and a raw PDF writer); balloons placed by clicking in Chromium and WebKit; the exported file read back with pypdf (the circle's centre in the appended content) and re-rendered (the pixels under the click).
2. **Wrong limits, or limits invented.** Every notation above, the refusals, decimal commas, inches, leading dots, angles in degrees, minutes, and seconds. *Check:* a Python generator writes requirements in many notations from known limits, and the parser must give them back exactly; a table of awkward and ambiguous cases that must be refused; an independent Python reading of the same rules over the same cases.
3. **Wrong pass or fail.** Floating point (12.00 + 0.05 must equal 12.05 exactly), boundaries, several values, several pieces, attributes, results that aren't numbers. *Check:* exact rationals throughout; random values at, just inside, and just outside each limit against Python's `fractions`.
4. **Wrong zone.** The frame, the counts, the start corner, I and O, zone boundaries, points outside the frame, rotated sheets (zones belong to the sheet as viewed). *Check:* a brute-force Python version that builds every zone's rectangle and tests containment, against the model, over random grids and points.
5. **Numbers that don't match rows.** Deleting, inserting, moving, renumbering, undoing, and redoing must keep the numbers 1 to N with no gaps or repeats, each row with its balloon. *Check:* random sequences of operations against a plain reference in the test, with the invariants after every step.
6. **The text layer misread.** Callouts split into several pieces, stacked limits, vertical text, CAD symbol fonts that come out as letters, no text at all. *Check:* fixtures with known text, clicking near each callout; the page always shows what it read.
7. **Hostile, broken, or huge files.** Encrypted, truncated, not a PDF, 100 sheets, an A0 sheet at high zoom (canvas size limits, iPhones' especially), a 50 MB file, 2,000 balloons. pdf.js runs with JavaScript evaluation off. *Check:* each in both engines; render size capped by pixels; timings recorded; the page never locks up and says what it couldn't do.
8. **Stale state under fast work.** Clicking balloons quickly, typing results and pressing Enter at once, zooming or changing sheet while a page renders, opening a second drawing while the first loads. Functional state updates, render tasks cancelled, late results ignored by sequence, focus moved at once (the Comp Desk and Holiday Pay lessons). *Check:* fast automation with real key presses and clicks.
9. **Lost work.** A reload, two tabs, storage refused or full (a big drawing in IndexedDB), damaged saved data, a work file from another drawing. *Check:* reload, two tabs, refused and damaged storage, a quota-exceeded drawing, opening a mismatched work file.
10. **Exports that are wrong or unsafe.** The ballooned PDF missing a sheet or a balloon, numbers unreadable on rotated pages, the CSV mangled by Excel (encoding, quotes, line breaks, cells starting with = + - @). *Check:* pypdf and pdf.js read the export back; Python's `csv` reads the table back; formula-looking cells defused.
11. **Claims the page can't back.** "Never leaves your computer" is checked by watching the network (only the site's own files, no request carrying the drawing) and by working offline after one visit; nothing about ITAR or any customer's acceptance is claimed. *Check:* the network log in both engines; offline on the built site.
12. **An inaccessible workflow.** A visual task: the crosshair for placing by keyboard, the characteristic list as one tab stop with the arrow keys, an editor for the chosen row, announcements for placing, moving, and deleting, 200% text, phone width, light and dark, a printable table. *Check:* the keyboard flow, axe at each state, the Tab count with 500 balloons.
