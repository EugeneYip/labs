### R8 (read 8 of 8, the last): 26 CFR 1.471-3, inventories at cost (the official basis for landed cost)
- URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.471-3
- *Verified* (the regulation's text on LII): for merchandise purchased during the year, cost is "the invoice price less trade or other discounts" (strictly cash discounts aside), plus **"transportation or other necessary charges"** incurred in acquiring possession of the goods; it points to §§ 1.263A-1 and 1.263A-3 for further amounts. Import duties and customs aren't named. Last amended by T.D. 9843, 20 November 2018.
- *Inference, not checked here:* small business taxpayers under IRC § 471(c) may use simpler inventory methods, which softens this for the smallest US sellers; for UK and IFRS accounts, IAS 2 and FRS 102 Section 13 list import duties and transport in the cost of purchase (general knowledge; the main session should read the text before relying on it).

## Stage B: landed cost per shipment (the only survivor)

- **The workflow** (*inference* from R5–R7 and general knowledge): a shipment arrives with the supplier's commercial invoice (10 to 200+ lines: SKU, quantity, unit price, often in USD, EUR or CNY, sometimes weights and carton sizes), a forwarder's bill (freight, origin and destination charges), the broker's customs entry (duty per commodity-code line; import VAT or GST is recoverable, so not a cost), insurance and delivery. Someone converts currencies, picks a basis for each cost (freight by weight or volume, insurance by value, duty per commodity line and then by value within it, fees by value or quantity), works out each item's landed unit cost, and types it into the inventory or accounts system, keeping the sheet for the accounts.
- **Recurrence and stakes:** every inbound shipment, weekly to quarterly for a small importer (*inference*). Unit costs drive prices and margins, and stock value and cost of sales in the accounts; US tax rules put "transportation or other necessary charges" into purchased inventory's cost (R8, *verified*); IAS 2 and FRS 102 add import duties (*inference*, unchecked). A wrong basis shifts margin between products (a heavy, cheap item carried by value looks more profitable than it is).
- **Strongest incumbents (not weakened):** the spreadsheet: free, flexible, already the bookkeeper's tool, with free templates widely offered (*inference*; not searched for landed cost specifically). Built-in landed cost: Zoho Inventory (value or quantity, per-item hand edits; R6, *verified*); Odoo, open source, whose landed costs split by quantity, current cost, weight or volume (*inference* from general knowledge, unverified; needs Odoo's inventory valuation and accounting); paid inventory systems (Cin7 Core, Unleashed, Katana, inFlow) and Shopify apps such as Sumtracker (S2, *source claim*). Duty calculators (Freightos, SimplyDuty, Zonos) estimate duty per product but don't spread a real shipment's costs (*inference*).
- **The Labs wedge:** one shipment at a time: paste or open the invoice lines; add each cost from its document with its own basis, including duty per commodity-code line; convert at the documents' rates; allocate to the penny so each cost's parts sum exactly (largest-remainder rounding); flag missing weights or codes, invoice codes absent from the entry, and items with outsized uplift; export per-item landed unit cost as CSV (for Zoho's per-item entry, a Xero bill import, Shopify's cost per item) plus a printable allocation statement. Supplier prices and margins never leave the computer; free; no account. Against Zoho specifically: the weight, volume and commodity-line bases it lacks.
- **Technical feasibility:** the user brings a CSV or pasted lines (or XLSX, by copying #028's zip reader or promoting it to `src/components/`, R6 note), and types the costs from PDFs (invoices and customs entries vary too much to parse; scans are common). A worksheet kept through `useOtherTabs` and exported per shipment, not a system of record, so the storage objection doesn't apply (*inference*).
- **The strongest reason not to build:** formula depth. Each cost pool is one SUMPRODUCT in a spreadsheet the bookkeeper already has, and the evidence of people stuck is thin: two undated Zoho posts, no high-vote request read, no first-hand account of hours spent. Importers with enough volume to feel it usually buy an inventory system that does it, and the result still has to be keyed into the user's own system, so little time is saved. It also sits next to the money-rules family, though it isn't UK-specific or legal.
- **Verdict:** **weak. Not recommended for the full gate without new evidence.** Checks that could change that: a landed-cost request on Xero's ideas portal or Shopify's community with many votes and a "not planned" status; Intuit's own pages on QuickBooks Online's lack of landed cost; bookkeepers' accounts of the spreadsheet taking hours a shipment; whether free templates already split duty by commodity line; Odoo's split methods and edition, from its documentation.
