# Thread D: official and association worksheets that make people compute, merge, cross-check, schedule, or allocate across many rows by hand

**Purpose (one, bounded):** a discovery method, not a domain. Find jobs created by an official or association procedure (regulators' and agencies' templates and worked examples, professional bodies' practice guides, inspection and maintenance procedures, certification and accreditation procedures, extension-service worksheets, volunteer handbooks) whose published worksheet, spreadsheet template, printable form, or manual makes people do many-row work by hand. The question is what the person repeatedly has to produce, check, reconcile, allocate, transform, or calculate, and whether a browser tool would remove real effort or errors. Directions Labs hasn't shipped in come first (facilities and building operations; planned maintenance across many assets; physical inventory and inspection; conservation beyond environmental logging, such as condition surveys, IPM trap records, salvage priorities; small-scale agriculture and horticulture; outdoor organisations; logistics and equipment for small organisations; print production; physical-space planning; specialist volunteer work), but evidence outranks domain.

**Budget:** at most 9 web searches and 8 page reads (WebFetch). Counted below as S1..S9 and R1..R8.

**Labels:** *verified* (read on an official or primary page; a vendor's own page is primary for what the vendor says about its product), *source claim* (a page or a search snippet says so), *inference* (this thread's judgment). Short quotes only (under 15 words).

**Stage A verdicts:** REJECT with the deciding reason (no real audience; not substantial; an obvious free tool; needs a backend, account, or API), or survives. Stage B for the one to three strongest survivors: workflow, recurrence and stakes, strongest incumbent (free ones included, never weakened), manual alternative, Labs wedge, technical feasibility, rule volatility, strongest reason not to build.

### S1 (search 1 of 9): FSC chain-of-custody volume summaries and credit accounts

Query: `FSC chain of custody "volume summary" annual audit printer spreadsheet template credit account conversion factor`

- Canfor's documented control system keeps FSC deliveries and the FSC Mix Credit Account in Excel, kept by a multi-site coordinator per sawmill and species group, with sales and procurement summaries ready for the annual audit (*source claim*, https://oldwww.canfor.com/documents/environmental/3%29-chain-of-custody-documented-control-system.pdf, search summary).
- Preferred by Nature (a certification body) lists an "FSC COC annual summary sample" for tracking annual volume (*source claim*, https://www.preferredbynature.org/pl/search?page=76, listing only): the certification bodies hand clients a template.
- SafetyCulture's FSC group audit checklist covers material accounting, conversion factors, and volume summaries (*source claim*, https://safetyculture.com/library/manufacturing/fsc-group-audit-v4lya88tqb8nrunq): a checklist, not a calculation.
- Group manuals (University of Kentucky CFWC, Indiana DNR) describe volume control, the percentage system, and the credit system for group members (*source claim*, https://cfwc.ca.uky.edu/sites/cfwc.ca.uky.edu/files/COCSection_OperationsManual.pdf, https://secure.in.gov/dnr/forestry/files/fo-FSC-chain-of-custody-group-member-procedures.pdf).
- *Inference:* the procedure is real and spreadsheet-kept, but the visible keepers are sawmills and group managers with a coordinator, not the small printer; printers mostly use the transfer system (each output takes the lowest input claim), where the yearly summary is a short in/out tally that the print MIS or one sheet covers. No pain voice surfaced. **Stage A: weak, parked** (not substantial for the smallest certificate holders; the hard credit-system cases sit with firms that have coordinators and MIS). Not spending more budget unless a later search shows small holders struggling.

### R1 (read 1 of 8): https://museumpests.net/monitoring-record-keeping-and-reporting/ (the IPM Working Group's own page)

- The guidance is general: pick a system "sustainable with your institution's resources", from a simple list or spreadsheet to a custom database or GIS; tailor reports to the audience; use visualisations; map pest activity; GIS to see trends and problem areas (*verified*, read 2026-10-15; no page date, uploads dated February-March 2022, cited talks 2002-2011).
- Tools it names (*verified*): a downloadable monitoring spreadsheet template (LibreOffice Calc or Excel; cost and author not stated, not opened); **ZPest Tracker** (zpesttracker.com), "follows the MPWG recommendations for logging and reporting pest data", replaces the older Zpest and Collection Pests programs, "runs cross platform on most devices", and hosts the MuseumPests site itself (cost and account not stated on this page); an IPM-WG database field list (mandatory, suggested, optional fields); institutional examples (NHM London's KE EMu pest mapping, 2011; AMNH/NMAI's Access "Pest Manager Database", 2003; Riverside Municipal Museum's GIS work, 2004; Texas Tech's GIS paper, 2002).

### S3 (search 3 of 9): sample-based condition surveys (the British Library's Preservation Assessment Survey)

Query: `"Preservation Assessment Survey" random sample 400 items library archive condition survey spreadsheet tool still available`

- The procedure (*source claim*, IFLA 2009 paper by Walker and Foster, https://cf-www.ifla.org/past-wlic/2009/92-walker-en.pdf; LIBER Quarterly, https://liberquarterly.eu/article/download/10336/10820?inline=1; search summaries): a sample of about 400 items gives ±5% at 95% confidence; simple random, systematic, or stratified sampling, systematic (a fixed interval through the collection) the most common; 15 preservation questions per item with weighted scores; the total splits into five priority bands. Run as a National Preservation Office service from 2001 and used in over 200 UK and Irish libraries, archives, and museums.
- Status: a 2012 British Library PAS workshop on the Conservation DistList (https://cool.culturalheritage.org/byform/mailing-lists/cdl/2012/0857.html) and a Kent special collections blog crediting the BL with "the tools for the job" (https://blogs.kent.ac.uk/specialcollections/?p=1746, undated in results) are the latest signs found; no evidence it is offered now (*source claim*, absence in results; not proof).
- *Inference (from memory, unverified here):* US equivalents exist as free online tools (the Preservation Self-Assessment Program from Illinois; CALIPR from California), so a condition-survey kit would not face an empty field.
- **Stage A: REJECT for now, not substantial in recurrence** (*inference*): a sample survey is a project every five to ten years per institution, the fieldwork (handling 400 items) is the real cost, and the arithmetic once the answers are in is a weighted sum and five bands that a spreadsheet does. The one interesting technical gap (proper estimates and intervals when shelves are sampled first and items second, which naive percentages get wrong) serves a small, infrequent audience. Not spending more budget.

### S4 (search 4 of 9): organic certification mass-balance and traceback audits

Query: `organic certification "mass balance" worksheet template farm OR handler annual inspection prepare certifier "traceback" OR "trace back" spreadsheet`

- The procedure: under the USDA National Organic Program, inspectors must do at least one traceback and one mass-balance exercise at every annual inspection (*source claim*, Oregon Tilth's Fall 2022 NOSB comments, https://tilth.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Oregon-Tilth-Fall-2022-NOSB-Meeting-Comments.pdf). The inspector picks the product on site and traces it from sales back to ingredients, "like a mock product recall" (*source claim*, CCOF, https://ccof.org/news/recordkeeping-tools-maintaining-detailed-records/; CCOF farm and handler inspection prep checklists, May 2025, https://ccof.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Handler_Inspection_Prep_Checklist.pdf).
- Certifiers publish their own workbooks (*source claim*, search summaries): QCS, an Excel supplement to its Organic Handler Plan (products, ingredients and suppliers, multi-ingredient recipes; https://qcsinfo.org/2022/01/06/new-organic-product-profile-released/); QAI's Handler Profile Workbook, which calculates organic percentages (removing water and salt) and "streamlines" the audit trail and mass-balance parts of the inspection (https://qai-inc.com/certification-resources/learning-resources/new-simpler-handler-profile-workbook-tutorial.php; QAI "In/Out Balance" explainer, https://qai-inc-prod.nsf.org/certification-resources/learning-resources/in-out-balance-organic-certification.php); Oregon Tilth's auto-calculating dry-matter-intake sheet for livestock and farm record templates; CCOF audit-trail forms.
- Errors happen and the regulator notices: NOP accreditation reviews of certifiers found exercises lacking units of measure, projected yield, purchased feed, beginning and ending inventory, the time frame, and quantity sold (*source claim*, search summary of NOP audit reports such as https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/NOP%20OEFFA.pdf and https://www.ams.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media/NOP%20MOCA.pdf).
- The NOP asked (docket AMS-NOP-22-0042) whether certifiers should share universal mass-balance and traceback worksheets; Oregon Tilth backed uniform forms (inspectors report "inefficiencies, frustrations and confusion" from inconsistent forms); IOIA, the inspectors' association, opposed mandating them but agreed spreadsheets can do the maths (*source claim*, https://www.ioia.net/media/rbvdgdpf/ams-nop-22-0042_attachment.pdf; to read).
- *Inference:* this is exactly this thread's pattern (an official procedure, many rows, a hand-built worksheet, documented errors), but the person who computes is the inspector, on the day, on the certifier's own form, and the operator's part is keeping records. That narrows the audience to inspectors (a few hundred, each on dozens of inspections a season) and operators preparing for a chosen product. Deciding facts: what the docket says about the burden and who does the arithmetic.

### R2 (read 2 of 8): IOIA's comment on docket AMS-NOP-22-0042, 29 September 2022 (https://www.ioia.net/media/rbvdgdpf/ams-nop-22-0042_attachment.pdf)

WebFetch could not parse the PDF's compressed text; the bytes it saved in this session's tool-results folder were copied to the scratchpad and their text extracted locally with macOS PDFKit (no second fetch; counted as this one read). Primary document: the International Organic Inspectors Association's letter to the NOSB on the CACS discussion document "Oversight improvements to deter fraud: Minimum Reporting Requirements", signed by its executive director (*verified*).

- **Who and how many:** IOIA has "nearly 250 inspector members in over a dozen countries"; inspectors are the people at every annual inspection (*verified*).
- **The burden is acknowledged:** IOIA thanks the NOSB and NOP for recognising "the complex math and process" in the audit exercises (*verified*).
- **Spreadsheets are the tool, and inspectors edit the formulas:** IOIA's earlier comment, quoted here, asked for templates because "Spreadsheets can do some of the more complex math"; it proposes a course that teaches "how to create and change formulas in excel mass balance forms" (*verified*). So the arithmetic is done today in Excel forms that inspectors adapt by hand.
- **What is missing:** a funded toolkit of forms and templates inspectors could download, with links to NASS yield data, "common conversion calculators", and standard weights of common measures, called "an incredible and much-needed support" (*verified*). Examples of the hard parts: reading SAP reports for a mass balance, how Brix changes the weight of a gallon, whether a settlement statement fits the yield (*verified*); even experienced inspectors struggle with losses from spillage, evaporation, moisture, and cleaning, and want guidance on "reasonable" discrepancies (*verified*); a snapshot of raw-material inventory on the start date is often hard to get (*verified*).
- **The counter-argument, which matters for any tool:** IOIA opposes mandated universal worksheets because no single form fits (a coffee roaster blending five origins with rework against a roadside produce stand; a seven-ingredient soup against cuts from a steer's live weight), rigid forms push operations to bend their records, and a standard template "gives such fraudsters the answers to the test" (*verified*). It supports templates that "MAY be used" plus training (*verified*).
- *Inference:* this is the strongest evidence in this thread so far of an official procedure (an exercise required at every inspection) whose many-row arithmetic is done in hand-built spreadsheets, with the practitioners' own body asking for conversion tools and templates. The wedge would be an optional, flexible worksheet (stages, units, conversion factors, loss allowances, variance flags, a printable record), not a universal form; local and offline matter because inspectors work on farms and plants with operators' confidential records (*inference*, to check against inspectors' confidentiality terms). The audience is narrow: a few hundred to perhaps a thousand inspectors and reviewers, plus operators' compliance staff.

### R3 (read 3 of 8): https://zpesttracker.com/ (the vendor's own page)

- **ZPest Tracker** is Leon Zak's online pest-tracking tool for museums and libraries, the successor to his Windows XP-era Zpests program, following the IPM Working Group's guidelines (*verified*, read 2026-10-15).
- **Paid, online, login:** "Zpest Tracker is $99 paid yearly" (the demo page also gives $15 a month); a 60-day free trial on the home page, 30 days on the demo page; a Login link; "an online tool with ongoing expenses" (*verified*). Runs in any browser; a phone app "in development"; one-click CSV backup export (*verified*).
- **What it does:** logs observations by count and type; "over 14 reports in 5 styles" filterable by location, trap ID, pest name, to show trends (*verified*). No floor plans, maps, or alerts mentioned (*verified*: absent from the page).
- **The IPM field, as now known:** ZPest Tracker (paid, $99 a year, hosted, login); Conserv Essentials (free, hosted, login, "IPM up to 50 traps", Batch 09 *verified*); Axiell EMu's IPM module with floor-plan maps (paid collections system, *source claim*); the IPM-WG's own spreadsheet template (free); home-made Excel and Airtable bases (Auckland, *source claim*).
- *Inference:* a free, accountless, local trap-log analyser (import the museum's own check sheets, normalise species and trap IDs, per-trap and per-room trends, pests-of-concern flags, the traps placed on the museum's own floor plan, a printed report) would have a real if modest wedge: free against $99 a year, no login against Conserv, plans that stay on the computer (store layouts are security-sensitive), and a map that the cheaper tools don't advertise. It **survives Stage A**; Stage B below.

### S5 (search 5 of 9): free tools for the organic in/out balance

Query: `organic inspector "mass balance" OR "in/out balance" spreadsheet calculator template excel IOIA OR "Organic Integrity Learning Center" yield conversion audit exercise tool`

- No free, general mass-balance calculator from IOIA, the NOP, or the Organic Integrity Learning Center surfaced; OILC is free USDA online training (*source claim*, https://hub.ofrf.org/resource/the-national-organic-program-organic-integrity-learning-center; absence in results, not proof).
- QAI defines the in/out balance as the inspectors' check that a company had enough of an organic ingredient to make its organic finished products (*source claim*, https://qai-inc-prod.nsf.org/certification-resources/learning-resources/in-out-balance-organic-certification.php).
- The same exercise appears elsewhere: EU organic control rules have the competent authority check input against output on a documented template (*source claim*, a German law-collection page, https://www.lexaris.de/book/version/documentflat/head/2063703); Horticulture New Zealand publishes "Guidance for Carrying out Mass Balance Calculations" (https://www.hortnz.co.nz/assets/Compliance/Mass-Balance-Guidelines.pdf, not read); an IFSQN (food safety professionals') forum thread has a member sharing a home-built mass-balance worksheet for food traceability (https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/47853-food-traceability-mass-balance-documentation, *source claim*).
- *Inference:* the arithmetic recurs well beyond US organic: food-safety certification schemes ask factories for a yearly traceability test with a quantity reconciliation (from memory, unverified here: BRCGS Food Safety Issue 9 clause 3.9.2, a test at least yearly including a mass balance, within four hours). If so, the audience for "trace a lot and balance the quantities" is every certified food site's technical or QA staff, not only a few hundred organic inspectors. That needs one check before it carries weight.
