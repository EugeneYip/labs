# Thread D: official and association worksheets that make people compute, merge, cross-check, schedule, or allocate across many rows by hand

**Purpose (one, bounded):** a discovery method, not a domain. Find jobs created by an official or association procedure (regulators' and agencies' templates and worked examples, professional bodies' practice guides, inspection and maintenance procedures, certification and accreditation procedures, extension-service worksheets, volunteer handbooks) whose published worksheet, spreadsheet template, printable form, or manual makes people do many-row work by hand. The question is what the person repeatedly has to produce, check, reconcile, allocate, transform, or calculate, and whether a browser tool would remove real effort or errors. Directions Labs hasn't shipped in come first (facilities and building operations; planned maintenance across many assets; physical inventory and inspection; conservation beyond environmental logging, such as condition surveys, IPM trap records, salvage priorities; small-scale agriculture and horticulture; outdoor organisations; logistics and equipment for small organisations; print production; physical-space planning; specialist volunteer work), but evidence outranks domain.

**Budget:** at most 9 web searches and 8 page reads (WebFetch). Counted below as S1..S9 and R1..R8.

**Labels:** *verified* (read on an official or primary page; a vendor's own page is primary for what the vendor says about its product), *source claim* (a page or a search snippet says so), *inference* (this thread's judgment). Short quotes only (under 15 words).

**Stage A verdicts:** REJECT with the deciding reason (no real audience; not substantial; an obvious free tool; needs a backend, account, or API), or survives. Stage B for the one to three strongest survivors: workflow, recurrence and stakes, strongest incumbent (free ones included, never weakened), manual alternative, Labs wedge, technical feasibility, rule volatility, strongest reason not to build.

### S1 (search 1 of 9): FSC chain-of-custody volume summaries and credit accounts

Query: `FSC chain of custody "volume summary" annual audit printer spreadsheet template credit account conversion factor`

- Canfor's documented control system keeps FSC deliveries and the FSC Mix Credit Account in Excel, kept by a multi-site coordinator per sawmill and species group, with sales and procurement summaries ready for the annual audit (*source claim*, https://oldwww.canfor.com/documents/environmental/3%29-chain-of-custody-documented-control-system.pdf, search summary).
- Preferred by Nature (a certification body) lists an "FSC COC annual summary sample" for tracking annual volume (*source claim*, https://www.preferredbynature.org/pl/search?page=76, listing only): the certification bodies hand clients a template.
- SafetyCulture's FSC group audit checklist covers material accounting, conversion factors, and volume summaries (*source claim*, https://safetyculture.com/library/manufacturing/fsc-group-audit-v4lya88tqb8nrunq): a checklist, not a calculation.
- Group manuals (University of Kentucky CFWC, Indiana DNR) describe volume control, the percentage system, and the credit system for group members (*source claim*, https://cfwc.ca.uky.edu/sites/cfwc.ca.uky.edu/files/COCSection_OperationsManual.pdf, https://secure.in.gov/dnr/forestry/files/fo-FSC-chain-of-custody-group-member-procedures.pdf).
- *Inference:* the procedure is real and spreadsheet-kept, but the visible keepers are sawmills and group managers with a coordinator, not the small printer; printers mostly use the transfer system (each output takes the lowest input claim), where the yearly summary is a short in/out tally that the print MIS or one sheet covers. No pain voice surfaced. **Stage A: weak, parked** (not substantial for the smallest certificate holders; the hard credit-system cases sit with firms that have coordinators and MIS). Not spending more budget unless a later search shows small holders struggling.

### S2 (search 2 of 9): museum IPM trap records, the association's method and the tools

Query: `museum integrated pest management trap monitoring results spreadsheet template analysis blunder traps floor plan trend report MuseumPests`

- The association: the Integrated Pest Management Working Group (IPM-WG) runs MuseumPests.net. Its "Monitoring: record keeping and reporting" page lists a spreadsheet template (LibreOffice Calc or Excel) and **ZPest Tracker**, an online tool said to follow the working group's recommendations; it warns that one year of monitoring makes a lot of data (*source claim*, https://museumpests.net/monitoring-record-keeping-and-reporting/, search summary; to read). A "MonitoringDataAnalysis.pdf" sits on the same site (https://es.museumpests.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/03/MonitoringDataAnalysis.pdf, not read). A 2008 IPM-WG procedures template covers how monitoring data are collected, analysed, and presented (*source claim*).
- Practice at a big museum: Auckland Museum checks sticky traps monthly and records finds in a custom Excel workbook that grew automated functions, visual summaries, and a **Pest Occurrence Index (POI)**; it moved to Airtable in 2021 for multi-site, multi-team collection and reporting (*source claim*, https://papahou.arphahub.com/article_preview.php?id=165429, search summary).
- Commercial: Axiell EMu has an IPM module with pest data shown on interactive maps and floor plans (*source claim*, https://help.emu.axiell.com/latest/en/Topics/EMu/Archive/Integrated%20Pest%20Management%20(IPM).htm).
- Already known (Batch 09, *verified* there): Conserv Essentials, free, cloud with login, "IPM up to 50 traps".
- *Inference:* the job is real and many-row (traps x checks x species), and big institutions build their own workbooks or databases, which says the analysis is wanted. But the free field is not empty: an association template, ZPest Tracker, and Conserv's free tier. Deciding facts: what ZPest Tracker costs and needs (account?), and what analysis the working group asks for (counts per trap per period only, or indices and maps).

### R1 (read 1 of 8): https://museumpests.net/monitoring-record-keeping-and-reporting/ (the IPM Working Group's own page)

- The guidance is general: pick a system "sustainable with your institution's resources", from a simple list or spreadsheet to a custom database or GIS; tailor reports to the audience; use visualisations; map pest activity; GIS to see trends and problem areas (*verified*, read 2026-10-15; no page date, uploads dated February-March 2022, cited talks 2002-2011).
- Tools it names (*verified*): a downloadable monitoring spreadsheet template (LibreOffice Calc or Excel; cost and author not stated, not opened); **ZPest Tracker** (zpesttracker.com), "follows the MPWG recommendations for logging and reporting pest data", replaces the older Zpest and Collection Pests programs, "runs cross platform on most devices", and hosts the MuseumPests site itself (cost and account not stated on this page); an IPM-WG database field list (mandatory, suggested, optional fields); institutional examples (NHM London's KE EMu pest mapping, 2011; AMNH/NMAI's Access "Pest Manager Database", 2003; Riverside Municipal Museum's GIS work, 2004; Texas Tech's GIS paper, 2002).
- The working group asks for no fixed computation (no required index, threshold, or per-period table) (*verified*: none named). *Inference:* the many-row job here is collation and mapping, not a procedure's arithmetic, so the "official worksheet makes people compute" test is only half met; the strongest free incumbent is the working group's own recommended tool (ZPest Tracker), whose terms decide the lead.

### S3 (search 3 of 9): sample-based condition surveys (the British Library's Preservation Assessment Survey)

Query: `"Preservation Assessment Survey" random sample 400 items library archive condition survey spreadsheet tool still available`

- The procedure (*source claim*, IFLA 2009 paper by Walker and Foster, https://cf-www.ifla.org/past-wlic/2009/92-walker-en.pdf; LIBER Quarterly, https://liberquarterly.eu/article/download/10336/10820?inline=1; search summaries): a sample of about 400 items gives ±5% at 95% confidence; simple random, systematic, or stratified sampling, systematic (a fixed interval through the collection) the most common; 15 preservation questions per item with weighted scores; the total splits into five priority bands. Run as a National Preservation Office service from 2001 and used in over 200 UK and Irish libraries, archives, and museums.
- Status: a 2012 British Library PAS workshop on the Conservation DistList (https://cool.culturalheritage.org/byform/mailing-lists/cdl/2012/0857.html) and a Kent special collections blog crediting the BL with "the tools for the job" (https://blogs.kent.ac.uk/specialcollections/?p=1746, undated in results) are the latest signs found; no evidence it is offered now (*source claim*, absence in results; not proof).
- *Inference (from memory, unverified here):* US equivalents exist as free online tools (the Preservation Self-Assessment Program from Illinois; CALIPR from California), so a condition-survey kit would not face an empty field.
- **Stage A: REJECT for now, not substantial in recurrence** (*inference*): a sample survey is a project every five to ten years per institution, the fieldwork (handling 400 items) is the real cost, and the arithmetic once the answers are in is a weighted sum and five bands that a spreadsheet does. The one interesting technical gap (proper estimates and intervals when shelves are sampled first and items second, which naive percentages get wrong) serves a small, infrequent audience. Not spending more budget.
