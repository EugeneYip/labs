### S1 (search 1 of 8, extended): laytime and demurrage from a statement of facts (marine operations)
- Query: `laytime demurrage calculation from statement of facts free laytime calculator Excel spreadsheet charterers operators errors disputes tool`.
- **The job, as the sources give it** (*source claim*, search summary): read the charter party's laytime clauses; take the statement of facts (SOF) from the port agent; work out the laytime allowed (for example 60,000 t at 10,000 t per weather working day is 6 days); start laytime at notice of readiness plus turn time; deduct the interruptions the charter excepts (weather, Sundays and holidays, shifting); demurrage for time over, despatch (often half the rate) for time under. Disputes come from vague SOF entries ("operations slow due to rain" can't be counted) and from clause readings (reversible or not, once on demurrage always on demurrage) (globallawexperts.com; voyagerportal.com's guide written for commodity traders).
- **Incumbents found** (*source claim*): paid modules: System 5's Laytime Calculation (a time sheet from the SOF, https://www.system5.de/laytime/), Danaos's calculator tied to the vessel's own time sheet (https://www.danaoscy.eu/laytime-demurrage-calculator/), Burmester-Vogel's BV Laytime with a "Discrepancy Discoverer" that compares a laytime statement with the counterparty's or with the SOF (https://burmester-vogel.com/products/bv-laytime/), Enqlare, Voyager Portal (traders). Free: a 2009 blog post on building the sheet in Excel or LibreOffice (http://maritimedomain.blogspot.com/2009/09/laytime-calculations.html); unprovenanced "Lloyd's laytime" Excel templates; one article names Heisenberg Shipping's free online laytime calculator (SHINC, SHEX, weather) (unconfirmed).
- **Lead L1 opened: a laytime statement built from the SOF and checked against the counterparty's.** *Inference:* real workflow shape (SOF text in, intervals classified by the charter's exceptions, statement out, a counterparty's statement reconciled) and money stakes on every port call; but it's a professional B2B job with paid modules aimed straight at it, including the reconciliation step, the deciding judgement is the reading of each charter's clauses and case law, and a free online calculator may exist. ### R4 (read 4 of 8): the Merchant Shipping (Maritime Labour Convention) (Hours of Work) Regulations 2018, SI 2018/58 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/58/body/made, read 2026-10-16)

### S8 (search 8 of 8, extended; the last): how laytime claims go wrong (L1's stakes and struggle)
- Query: `demurrage claims laytime statement disputes how common time-barred missing documents P&I club article charterers owners laytime calculation errors checking counter calculation`.
- **Stakes, from case law and clubs** (*source claim*, summaries of law-firm and P&I pages): demurrage claims usually must be presented within a short time bar, typically 90 days after discharge, some 60, "missed by even one day" and the claim fails; in *Tricon Energy v MTM Trading* [2020] EWHC 700 (Comm) the owners' whole claim was barred for leaving out the bills of lading, though the SOF held the figures (https://www.shiplawlog.com/2020/04/22/tricon-energy-ltd-v-mtm-trading-llc-2020-ewhc-700-comm-queens-bench-division-commercial-court-mr-justice-robin-knowles-cbe-23-march-2020/; CMS, https://cms-lawnow.com/en/ealerts/2020/01/oil-gas-shipping-time-bars-and-supporting-documents-in-demurrage-claims); Skuld calls the reading "strict on owners" (https://www.skuld.com/topics/legal/pi-and-defence/timebar-for-demurrage-claims/). The usual document set: invoice, time sheet, NOR, SOF, sometimes letters of protest and pumping logs.
- **Where the calculations go wrong** (*source claim*, guides, mostly vendors'): ambiguous SOF entries, an NOR tendered before the ship was ready, excepted periods miscategorised, the wrong commencement or cargo quantity, shifting time, rain and holidays, calendar rules (assuming Sundays don't count); the advice is an independent calculation compared with the other side's, and one guide notes despatch overpaid when the charterer's own calculation wasn't checked (Shipfinex, HandyBulk, Voyager Portal, danil-hristich.com). No figure for how often, and no first-hand practitioner complaint.
- **More incumbents** (*source claim*): Supa Maritime's laytime and demurrage software (https://supamaritime.com/blog/laytime-demurrage-management-software); the Shipowners' Club's "Laytime & Demurrage" publication (https://www.shipownersclub.com/latest-updates/publications/laytime-and-demurrage/); ICS Ireland's "A Layman's guide to Laytime" (2007, training).

## Leads and Stage A verdicts

- **L1. A laytime statement built from the agent's statement of facts, checked line by line against the counterparty's (marine operations): survives Stage A.** No obvious free tool: the nearest, Heisenberg Shipping's browser calculator, is single-port with typed SOF rows and PDF output, links a log-in and states no price (R2, R5, *verified*). Not formula depth only: the work is a timeline (NOR, turn time, every interruption classified by the charter's exceptions, reversible ports) and a reconciliation of two statements. Recurs on every voyage-charter port call with money and a time bar at stake (R6, *verified*). Not a settled cluster (0 matches). Stage B below.
- **L2. Walk sheets and a polling-day knock-up list from the full electoral register (elections): REJECT at Stage A** (*inference* on S2's *source claims*). Most candidates stand for a party whose own system they get free; independents have UK-specific paid tools, one with an offline web app, teller packs and knock-up tracking (Campaign Desk), another at £1,097 a year (canvassing.info); each candidate fights every four years or so; and the local reason is weaker than it looks: nothing found in the rules bars a campaign from using a processor, and the ICO calls the register's use compatible with data protection law when that law is followed (S2). What a page could do alone is a sorted, grouped print of the register (next to the settled label and mail-merge family) and a single-device committee room. No first-hand struggle found.
- **L3. Stage B below.
- **L4. ## Stage B

### L1. Laytime statements from the SOF, checked against the counterparty's: survives Stage B narrowly; weak to moderate; not recommended for the full gate without first-hand evidence
- **Job and who:** the post-fixture analyst or operator at a shipowner, charterer, commodity trader or ship operator (and brokers and claims handlers), for every port call under a voyage charter: take the agent's SOF (often scanned, often by email), start laytime at NOR plus turn time, classify every interval by the charter's terms (weather working days, SHINC or SHEX, shifting, waiting for berth, once on demurrage always on demurrage, reversible ports), compute demurrage or despatch, present the claim with its documents inside the time bar, and check the other side's statement against one's own (S1, S8, R8, *source claim*; R7, R8 vendors' pages *verified* as their claims).
- **Evidence (labelled):**
  - *Verified*, a P&I club: laytime and demurrage carry "a large number of pit falls"; NOR validity and exceptions are read strictly; missing documents bar claims (Shipowners' Club, August 2023, R6).
  - *Source claim*, case law: *Tricon Energy v MTM Trading* [2020] EWHC 700 (Comm), a whole demurrage claim time-barred for want of the bills of lading (S8); time bars typically 90 days, some 60 (S8).
  - *Verified as vendors' claims*: "Manual SOF re-keying costs 4-6 hours per claim" (BV Laytime, R7); spreadsheets worked from scanned SOFs, the model "breaking down", claims missed in inboxes (Supa Maritime, June 2026, R8).
  - *Source claim*, guides: the usual errors are an early NOR, excepted periods miscategorised, the wrong commencement or quantity, shifting, rain, holidays and calendar rules; advice is an independent calculation reconciled with the other side's (S8).
  - **Missing:** any first-hand account by a practitioner of the time it takes or of a dispute lost to a calculation error; any figure for how often.
- **Incumbents:** paid modules aimed straight at it: BV Laytime (SaaS plus an offline desktop application; automatic SOF extraction; a "Discrepancy Discoverer" that aligns two statements and cites the clause, R7, *verified*), Supa Maritime's Merchant module (R8), System 5, Danaos, Enqlare, Voyager Portal for traders, Netpas's estimator (S1, S6, *source claim*), and voyage-management suites (Veson IMOS, from memory); free or nearly: Heisenberg Shipping's single-port browser calculator (R2, R5, *verified*; price and log-in unclear) and Excel templates (S1).
- **Wedge (*inference*):** free, no account, nothing uploaded (charter terms and rates are commercially confidential): paste or open the SOF (its text layer read locally with the pdf.js engine #037 already ships, typed entry for scans), confirm each event's time and class, laytime for one port or reversible across several, the claim's deadline and document checklist from the charter's own clause, then load the counterparty's statement and see every interval the two count differently; export a statement as PDF and a spreadsheet.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the hard part is legal (whether the NOR was valid, how an exception reads, which documents the clause demands), not the arithmetic, and a public page can only apply the user's reading; the volume segment already buys platforms that read scanned SOFs automatically, which a static page can't match; the long tail lives in Excel templates and has at least one free browser calculator; and no practitioner's own voice was found, so the pain rests on vendors' claims. A professional B2B audience like #037's, but without #037's legal bar on the cloud tools (confidentiality is a preference, not a rule).
- **Checks that could change the verdict:** whether Heisenberg's calculator is free and account-free, and whether its promised multi-port version compares statements (either would be a REJECT); first-hand accounts from small owners, brokers or traders' operations staff reconciling statements by hand (shipping forums, ICS student boards, trade-press letters); published worked examples to test against (BIMCO's *Laytime Definitions for Charter Parties 2013*, ICS course material, HandyBulk's examples), checked for licence; how often agents' SOFs carry a usable text layer.

### L3. Monthly hours-of-rest records with rolling checks: weak at Stage B; REJECT recommended
- **Job and who:** the master (or someone authorised), heads of department and each seafarer on a commercial vessel: record daily hours of rest, check at least 10 hours in any 24 and 77 in any 7 days, no more than two periods with one of at least 6 hours (STCW adds at most 14 hours between rest periods, S3; the UK regulations keep that only for agreements, R4), keep the monthly record in the flag's format, signed by both, a copy to the seafarer (R4, *verified* for UK ships; S3, *source claim*, for the STCW and MLC baseline).
- **Evidence (labelled):** records of hours of rest are "the most reported inspection deficiency" in an STCW inspection study; the Isle of Man registry's surveyor puts the most trouble on yachts under 500 GT; 64.3% of surveyed seafarers reported adjusting their records (S3, *source claim*); failures are offences and ships can be detained (R4, *verified*).
- **Incumbents:** ISF Watchkeeper 4 (the industry's own; cloud, mobile, works offline, a Yachts edition; price on request; S5, *source claim*); Voly Crew (free, account, iPhone and iPad, offline, MLC monitoring; R1, *verified*); IDEA Yacht, Manage My Vessel (monthly report, PDF or Excel export, counter-signing), YMS360 (S5, S7, *source claim*); flags' and managers' paper and spreadsheet forms (S3).
- **Wedge (*inference*):** no account, any device including Android, offline, the flag's form printed, each non-conformity explained, a planned watch schedule tested before the voyage.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** at least five products already do the checks, including the industry's own tool offline and a free app; the record is a daily log kept all month on board (close to the storage objection, "years of records in one browser"); and the failure the evidence points to is adjusted records, which a checker can't fix and might even help to tidy.
- **Checks that could change the verdict:** a seafarer's need for a private record of their own, kept apart from the employer's system (the Nautilus app of 2018 couldn't be read, R3); ISF Watchkeeper's price for a single small vessel; whether fishing vessels under ILO C188 or small workboats lack any of the five.
