# Thread G: jobs finished today by chaining tools or rebuilding the same spreadsheet (discovery round 2)

**Purpose (one, bounded):** a discovery method, not a domain. Find recurring jobs that practitioners finish today by chaining several tools (export from one program, convert or clean in a second, check or merge by hand in a spreadsheet, paste into a third), or by rebuilding the same spreadsheet each time, in directions Labs hasn't shipped in: workshops and fabrication (beyond first article ballooning), physical inspection and testing, maintenance across many assets, print production, logistics and stores for small organisations, equipment planning, field work and surveying, specialist volunteer operations, small-scale horticulture operations, niche professional practices. The question for each lead: what the person repeatedly has to produce, check, reconcile, allocate, transform, or calculate, and whether one browser page would replace the chain.

**Budget:** at most 8 web searches and 8 page reads (WebFetch), counted below as S1..S8 and R1..R8. Read-only: no sign-ups, log-ins, posts, forms, or deliberate downloads.

**Labels:** *verified* (read on the page itself), *source claim* (a search snippet or a secondary page says so), *inference* (this thread's judgment). Short quotes only (under 15 words). Findings are advisory; the main session decides and re-checks deciding claims.

1. **L5. A county bird report's tables from this year's merged record exports: survives Stage A and B, moderate; the thread's strongest lead** (Stage B in full below).
   - *Job and who:* each recording area's bird recorder (about a hundred across Britain and Ireland, R8, *verified*) with the report's editors and species-account writers, volunteers (Berkshire's report says so, S6, *source claim*). Once a year, with quarterly downloads in between, they merge the county's records from BirdTrack, eBird, iRecord, observers' spreadsheets and emails, then turn them into per-species tables for the annual report.
   - *Evidence:* BTO lists the editors' own work as verifying, integrating sources, taxonomies, rarities and site names; Cambridgeshire alone had over 200,000 BirdTrack records in 2020 (R4, *verified*).    - *Incumbents:* each county's own system (MapMate, Recorder 6, bespoke Access, an Excel master file, Berkshire's program; *source claim*); BTO's per-county BirdTrack metrics, in development (R4, *verified*); BirdTrack's export summary sheet; eBird's region reports; the R package auk for programmers (*source claim*). - **Free templates already do the per-CML arithmetic:** Atlantis NDT's free editable Excel "API 570 Piping Inspection Record", 30 CMLs with previous and current thickness, short- and long-term corrosion rate, remaining life (https://atlantisndt.com/resources/api-570-piping-inspection-record, *source claim*); Maptrack's free UT thickness report template (https://www.maptrack.com/templates/ultrasonic-thickness-testing-report-template, *source claim*); a free online corrosion-rate calculator (https://midstreamcalculator.com/calculators/pipeline-ops/corrosion-rate.html, *source claim*). - Method as the snippets give it: LT rate = (t_initial − t_actual) / years; ST rate = (t_previous − t_actual) / years; remaining life = (t_actual − t_required) / the larger rate (a 16-year-old CR4 post, https://cr4.globalspec.com/thread/55150/Corrosion-Allowance-for-Service-Pipe-Line, *source claim*).
- **Lead L1 opened and weakened at once:** thickness surveys to corrosion rates and next-inspection dates. *Inference:* the per-CML arithmetic is formula depth already served by free templates; the hard part (keeping CML histories across many surveys and circuits) is what plants buy an inspection data management system for, which is a system of record (the storage objection). ## Stage B (the strongest survivors)

### L5. A county bird report's tables from this year's merged record exports (strongest in this thread)
- **The actual workflow.** *Before:* the recorder holds the county's BirdTrack download (at least quarterly in Shropshire, R6), eBird records placed by latitude and longitude, iRecord records, a club's yearly Excel recording sheet from observers (Northants, R5), emails and paper, and the records committee's decisions on description species (R6). *Steps:* combine, match species names across lists and site names to the county's own, convert eBird's latitude and longitude to grid references, find the same sighting sent by two routes (R6 names the duplication), hold or drop unaccepted rarities, keep rare breeding sites out of anything published (R6), then build per-species tables (records, sites, first and last dates with place, monthly and site maxima, peak counts with place and date) for the species-account writers (Berkshire's Excel program, Sussex's and Argyll's report contents, S6, *source claim*). *After:* tables the writers and the editor can trust, and a merged file for the county database.
- **Recurrence and stakes.** Yearly per recording area, with quarterly downloads and an "end-of-year rush" (R6, *verified*); Cambridgeshire alone had over 200,000 BirdTrack records in 2020 (R4, *verified*), Norfolk 1.3 million from all routes in 2024 (S7, *source claim*, source page unidentified). The records feed population trends and environmental impact studies for planning applications (R5, *verified*); double-counted flocks or misplaced sites distort both (*inference*). Reports run late (Berkshire's 2015-2017 reports came out in 2018-2021, S6, *source claim* from titles).
- **The strongest incumbents (not weakened).** The county's own system, whatever it is: MapMate (paid; common; duplication warnings), Recorder 6 (free desktop; not checked here), a bespoke Access database (Gloucestershire), an Excel master file (Northants), Berkshire's Excel program (S7, S6, *source claim*). BTO's per-county derived metrics from BirdTrack (effort maps, weekly Complete Lists, species charts, reporting rates, 10-km maps), in development with selected recorders (R4, *verified*). BirdTrack's export summary sheet (species, records, places, first date and place; S6, S8, *source claim*). eBird's region reports and the R package auk for programmers (S8, *source claim*). No dedicated free tool for the tables surfaced (S8, *source claim*), and BTO's public pages describe none (R8, *verified*).
- **The current manual alternative.** Spreadsheet merges with lookups, pivot tables or MINIFS/MAXIFS per species, by-hand de-duplication, and site-name clean-up, every year (*inference* from S6-S8 and R6).
- **The Labs wedge.** One page, nothing uploaded, that opens this year's exports in their own shapes, maps the columns once (remembered per county), matches names and sites against lists the recorder keeps, converts latitude and longitude to grid references and tetrads locally, flags likely cross-route duplicates for a decision rather than deleting them, holds description species until accepted, leaves sensitive species and sites out of every output, and writes the per-species tables and a clean merged CSV for the county database. Local processing matters because rare breeding sites are kept confidential (R6, *verified*) (*inference* for the wedge as a whole).
- **Technical feasibility** (*inference*). CSV import is Labs' own (#005); XLSX needs a reader (#028 already parses workbook XML). Hundreds of thousands of rows are fine in a worker; a million-plus (Norfolk) needs column-wise storage and care. WGS84 to British National Grid by the OS's published Helmert approximation is accurate to a few metres, ample for tetrads (Irish Grid needs its own). The name crosswalk between the British list and eBird's taxonomy is a data table whose licence must be checked, or a list the recorder keeps, with scientific names matched first. Settings (column maps, gazetteer, name map, sensitive list) fit `useOtherTabs` storage; the records themselves stay per session, so the storage objection doesn't apply.
- **The strongest reason not to build.** A small, expert, once-a-year audience (about a hundred recording areas, R8) whose counties already run systems holding decades of records and their own conventions; once merged, the tables are pivot tables; the hard parts of the merge (which duplicates are the same birds, site gazetteers, taxonomy changes) are county-specific judgment; BTO may ship summaries for BirdTrack, its largest source; and it sits next to Batch 09's REJECT of wildlife records into iRecord and county formats, so the main session must judge whether compiling the report is a distinct enough job.
- **Checks that could change the verdict.** (1) Whether recorders already get per-species summaries from BTO behind a login, or from MapMate or Recorder 6 reports (any one makes it a REJECT for those counties). (2) The column layout of BirdTrack's county download and of iRecord's export, from public guides. (3) First-hand accounts of the time the merge and tables take (BirdForum was refused; the introductions of county reports such as Berkshire's 2016 report, and the source of Norfolk's 1.3 million, are the next places). (4) The licence of any species-name crosswalk Labs would ship. (5) Whether BirdTrack's data terms for recorders restrict passing records to online tools (strengthens the local wedge if so).

### L4. Volunteer geophysics grids to a processed, georeferenced composite (second)
- **The actual workflow** (S5, *source claim*, societies' guides). Survey 20 m grids with an earth-resistance meter (RM85, RM15) or a gradiometer, download with the meter's software, assemble and process in Snuffler (despike, destagger for zigzag traverses, edge-matching between blocks, interpolation), export a PNG composite, georeference it in QGIS by hand, write it up for the group's report and the HER.
- **Recurrence and stakes.** Survey seasons, several days of grids each; the plot decides where a group digs and goes into reports (*inference*). No official process for volunteers was checked (Historic England's guidance is for professionals).
- **The strongest incumbents (not weakened).** Snuffler: free, built for exactly this community, taught in free HLF-funded courses (S5, *source claim*); TerraSurveyor and Geoplot (paid); the Archaeological Geophysics Toolbox plugins for QGIS (free); R or Python for those who code (R3, *verified* as a 2016 list). Every package listed in 2016 was Windows only (R3, *verified*); Snuffler's latest listed version is 1.32 of July 2021 with Windows installers and PNG-only export (S5, *source claim*; its own page refused the connection, R2).
- **The Labs wedge** (*inference*). Any device (Mac, iPad, Chromebook as well as Windows), offline in the field to check the day's grids before leaving, grid assembly with the traverse pattern and start corner shown so a mis-set grid is caught at once, minimal processing kept visible, and a georeferenced export (PNG plus a world file from the grid corners' coordinates) that drops straight into QGIS; nothing uploaded, which suits sites kept quiet because of illegal detecting (*inference*).
- **Technical feasibility** (*inference*). Canvas image processing and world-file export are straightforward; the risk is the meters' file formats (some binary or proprietary) and validating against real files without downloading any (sample data would have to be built from documented formats).
- **The strongest reason not to build.** Snuffler already does the processing free, for exactly these people, with training behind it; the audience is small (one processor per group); processing is interpretive, and a new tool that smooths differently could mislead; and the platform gap rests on a 2016 list and a 2021 version number, both unverified for 2026.
- **Checks that could change the verdict.** Snuffler's current version and platforms; whether a free cross-platform tool now exists (Python packages for archaeological geophysics, for example, unchecked here); public documentation of the RM85, RM15 and gradiometer export formats; first-hand complaints from groups about Windows-only processing or hand georeferencing.
