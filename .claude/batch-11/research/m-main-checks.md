## First article inspection ballooning: can a static page open a drawing? (2026-10-15)

- **The libraries** (*verified*, `npm view`, 2026-10-15): pdfjs-dist 6.4.299 (Apache-2.0, published 2026-10-03; `pdf.min.mjs` 452 KB and `pdf.worker.min.mjs` 1.2 MB before compression) renders and reads PDFs; pdf-lib 1.17.1 (MIT, last published 2022-05) writes them. Labs has no runtime package beyond React today (`package.json`), so this would be the first, loaded only by this experiment's own page.
- **The spike** (scratchpad, not the repository): a synthetic A3 drawing made with pdf-lib (a border with zone labels, an outline, five dimension strings, a title block), opened by pdf.js from bytes in the page, rendered to a canvas at 1.5×, and its text read with positions, through Playwright's routing (a local server isn't reachable from the browsers here; the established `route-preload.cjs` approach).
  - **Brave (Chromium):** rendered (19,991 dark pixels), 29 text items with page coordinates ("120.00 +/-0.05" at 450, 510; "M8x1.25-6H THRU" at 540, 440; the title block), 155 ms.
  - **WebKit 27.2:** the same 29 items at the same coordinates, 195 ms.
- **What this doesn't settle:** real drawings. CAD exports often draw text as outlines (AutoCAD's SHX fonts, some SolidWorks settings) or arrive as scans, so a page can't rely on reading the requirement from the drawing; typing it in has to be the normal path, with the text read only when it's there.
- **Writing balloons back into the original PDF** (*verified*, the same spike): a four-page fixture whose pages are rotated 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270° (landscape drawings are often stored rotated); a "click" at 30% across and 40% down each rendered page mapped to PDF space with pdf.js's `convertToPdfPoint`; pdf-lib draws a numbered circle there on the original page and saves; re-rendered, every circle sits under the click (159–177 red pixels within 12 px of it, none 60 px away), in Brave and WebKit alike. The original pages stay vector; the file grew from about 1.5 KB to 3.1 KB. One detail for a build: the number's text is drawn in page space, so on a rotated page it must be counter-rotated to read upright.

## First article inspection ballooning: do export-control and defence-contract rules keep drawings off cloud services? (2026-10-15)

Read for the main session by a page reader from the primary texts (Cornell LII's copies of 22 CFR 120.50, 120.54, 120.56 and subpart C; acquisition.gov's DFARS 252.204-7012, MAY 2024, page showing DFARS Change 5/7/2026). eCFR redirected to a bot check, which wasn't followed.

- **ITAR, 22 CFR 120.54(a)(5)** (*verified*): sending, taking, or storing unclassified technical data is not an export only if it is secured with end-to-end encryption (FIPS 140-2 modules or successors, or strength at least comparable to AES-128), not intentionally sent to or stored in a §126.1 proscribed country, and, under §120.54(b)(1), never unencrypted between the originator and the intended recipient, with "The means of decryption are not provided to any third party."
- **What a release is** (*verified*): an export includes a transmission out of the United States and a release to a foreign person in the United States (§120.50(a)(1)–(2)); a release includes causing technical data outside the United States to be in unencrypted form (§120.56(a)(3)–(4)).
- **So** (*inference*, from the text): a cloud service that decrypts a drawing on its own servers to read it (automatic ballooning) holds the means of decryption, so the encryption carve-out doesn't apply; decryption on servers abroad, or access by foreign-person staff, is a release. Werk24's Werkflow processes drawings on its servers with EU residency by default (thread A, *verified* there), which is exactly what an ITAR shop must avoid without a licence.
- **DFARS 252.204-7012** (*verified*): a contractor using "an external cloud service provider to store, process, or transmit any covered defense information" must require and ensure the provider meets security requirements equivalent to the FedRAMP Moderate baseline ((b)(2)(ii)(D)); the clause flows down to subcontracts whose performance involves covered defense information ((m)(1)); covered defense information includes controlled technical information, whose examples expressly include "engineering drawings" ((a)).
- **Caveat:** Cornell's §120.54 page flags an amendment, 91 FR 55460 (28 August 2026), and a correction, 91 FR 58610, not yet in its text; not read. The carve-out's core condition (no third party with the means of decryption) is the deciding one, and a build would claim nothing about ITAR anyway, only that the page never sends the drawing anywhere.
- **Consequence for the gate:** for export-controlled and covered defence drawings, a free cloud service is off the table for most small suppliers without the right hosting; customers' proprietary-information legends and NDAs add commercial drawings that their holders won't upload to a third party (*inference*). The audience the wedge needs exists.
