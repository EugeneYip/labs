### R2 (read 2 of 8): Zmartly's stock book worked example
- URL: https://zmartly.co.uk/news-insights/vat-margin-scheme-stock-book
- *Verified* (what the page says): Zmartly is a London accountancy firm (monthly plans at £129, £250, £499), **not a software product**; it names no stock-book tool and says a stock book can be kept "on paper or digitally (a spreadsheet is fine)".

### R3 (read 3 of 8): HMRC VAT Notice 718 (the 2020 text)
- URL: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-718-the-vat-margin-scheme-and-global-accounting/vat-notice-718-the-vat-margin-scheme-and-global-accounting (first 100,000 of about 107,700 characters read).
- **Caveat, *verified*:** this text was last updated 31 December 2020 and **withdrawn on 23 December 2021, replaced by updated guidance** that this thread did not read. Everything below is *verified* against the withdrawn text only; the main session must re-check it against the current GOV.UK margin-scheme guidance before relying on it (*inference*: the scheme's mechanics are long-standing and were restructured into guidance pages rather than changed, but that is unchecked).
- Stock book, para 5.2: per item, a stock number in numerical sequence, purchase date, purchase invoice number (unless self-billed), purchase price, seller's name, description; sale date, sales invoice number, selling price or method of disposal, buyer's name; margin and VAT due (margin × 1/6), "nil" when there is no margin.
- Invoices, para 5.3: purchase invoices show both parties, number, date, description and total price, with a margin-scheme statement from a VAT-registered seller, and an item with VAT shown separately is not eligible; sales invoices show the dealer's VAT number, the buyer, a stock book cross-reference, the total with no separate VAT, and a margin-scheme statement.
- If the conditions aren't met, or HMRC can't check margins from the records, VAT is due on the full selling price (paras 2.5, 5.1).
- Purchase price includes the supplier's incidental charges (commission, packaging, transport, insurance) but not repairs, refurbishment or overheads (paras 2.11, 3.2). A loss-making sale has no VAT and can't be offset against other items (5.2). Part-exchange: the allowance counts in the sale price and becomes the purchase price of the item taken in (13.6).
- Global Accounting, section 14: any item bought for over £500 is excluded; so are aircraft, boats and outboard motors, caravans, horses and ponies, and motor vehicles including motorcycles (except vehicles broken up for scrap); period margin = sales less (opening stock + purchases); a negative margin carries forward to the next period only; on leaving, closing stock's purchase value is added to the final period's sales unless the VAT on it is £1,000 or less (15.7). Records "do not have to be kept in any set way" (15.1).
- VAT return, para 5.4: Box 1 output tax on margin sales; Box 6 the full selling price less the margin VAT; **Box 7 the full purchase price of eligible goods bought in the period**; Boxes 8 and 9 not needed.
- *Inference:* Box 7 takes purchases in the period while Box 1 takes VAT on items sold, so the return needs both the period's buys and the period's sells matched to their own buys; that is the stock book's job and why it can't be a one-item calculator.

## Stage B

### Lead 1. Margin-scheme stock book

- **The workflow.** *Buying:* each item bought (from a private seller, an auction, a house clearance, or another dealer's margin-scheme invoice) gets the next stock number and a purchase line: date, invoice number (or the dealer's own purchase invoice), price paid, seller, description (*verified*, R7). The price includes the supplier's incidental charges but not repairs or refurbishment (*verified*, R3, withdrawn text). *Selling:* the sale line (date, invoice number, selling price or disposal, buyer), the margin, and VAT at one-sixth, "nil" for a loss; a margin-scheme invoice with no VAT shown, the stock number and the scheme wording (*verified*, R7; wording *source claim*, S8). *Each quarter:* Box 1 the margin VAT; Box 6 the selling prices less that VAT; Box 7 the period's purchases (*verified*, R3, withdrawn text). Low-value goods may instead use Global Accounting: the period's eligible sales less purchases, a negative margin carried forward, with vehicles, boats, aircraft, caravans, horses and anything bought for over £500 excluded (*verified*, R3, withdrawn text). *After:* six years' retention, longer for unsold old stock (*verified*, R7).
- **Recurrence and stakes.** Every item and every VAT quarter, for years. HMRC visits can inspect the stockbook (*source claim*, S2). If the records can't support the margins, VAT is due on the full selling price (*verified* in the withdrawn notice, R3; *source claim* for the current internal manual, S8). Worked example (*inference*, arithmetic): £100,000 of sales at a 30% margin owes about £5,000 under the scheme against about £16,700 on full prices.
- **Strongest incumbent (not weakened).** A spreadsheet: free, accepted ("a spreadsheet is fine", R2), two formula columns for margin and VAT, usually kept or checked by the accountant, who also makes the Xero adjustment (R1, S3). Paid: Odoo's add-on (S2, Odoo users only), dealer systems for vehicles (MotorDesk, a listing shows a starter plan at 79 a month, S3), and accountants' bookkeeping (Zmartly's plans from £129 a month, R2). The connectors (A2X, Link My Books) route marketplace sales into Xero, but no evidence found that they work out per-item margins (S3, S9; *inference* from A2X's 0%-rate advice).
- **The Labs wedge.** Open the purchase log and the marketplace sales exports; each sale matched to its purchase by stock number or SKU; the quarter's Box 1, 6 and 7 figures; the stockbook, unsold stock at cost, and the exceptions as a spreadsheet; nothing uploaded. The checks a spreadsheet doesn't make by itself: sales with no purchase line (the full-price risk), skipped or repeated stock numbers (the numerical-sequence condition), losses shown nil and not netted, purchases over £500 kept out of Global Accounting, the quarter's purchases versus the quarter's sales, refunds across quarters, and Global Accounting's carried-forward negative margin (*inference*). The interaction model is the one #034 and #036 use: open your own downloads, see the rules applied, take a spreadsheet away.
- **Technical feasibility.** Static and local. The user brings their purchase log (CSV or XLSX; the stockbook they already keep, or a template the page gives them) and sales exports (eBay order reports, Etsy and Shopify order CSVs; whether Vinted and Depop export sales with a SKU is unverified). The join depends on a stock number in the SKU or custom-label field. The records stay in the user's files; only column mappings would be remembered (`labs:<slug>`), because a six-year record doesn't belong in browser storage (*inference*).
- **Strongest reason not to build.** Global Accounting already turns the high-volume, low-value case (most clothing resellers) into period totals that a spreadsheet adds up, so per-item matching is needed mainly for higher-value items, where volumes are lower and a spreadsheet copes. And the audience is VAT-registered dealers only (above the registration threshold, £90,000 from April 2024 by memory, not checked here, or registered voluntarily), many with an accountant who already owns the spreadsheet. Also: UK tax rules (Ireland and the Netherlands run their own versions, S9), the rule-based cluster #034 and #036 already sit in, a modest 81 votes mostly from accountants, and no small reseller's own complaint about the spreadsheet read (only "tedious" at volume).
- **For the main session to check:** the current GOV.UK Global Accounting page (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/using-the-global-accounting-vat-margin-scheme) and VAT-return page (https://www.gov.uk/vat-margin-schemes/vat-return), since R3's loss, Global Accounting and box rules come from the withdrawn notice; whether any reseller tool computes per-item margin VAT; how eBay, Vinted, Depop and Etsy exports carry a SKU; and Making Tax Digital's digital-links rule for a spreadsheet imported into bridging software (from memory, a file import counts; *inference*).
