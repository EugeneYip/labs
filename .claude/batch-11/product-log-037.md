## 2026-10-16 · #037 Ballooned Drawing

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/ballooned-drawing](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/ballooned-drawing/). For the inspector or quality lead preparing a first article inspection (an AS9102 report, PPAP dimensional results, or any customer asking for a ballooned print with the measurements). Open the customer's drawing (a PDF of one or more sheets, as CAD exports it, or a scan) and click or tap beside each characteristic: a numbered balloon appears beside it, never on top of its text, and where the PDF has a text layer the requirement is read from the drawing (a callout in several pieces, stacked deviations, stacked limits, vertical text, and a feature control frame's cells). The page reads the limits from the requirement by stated rules (nominal ±, two deviations, two limits, MAX and MIN, angles in degrees and minutes, GD&T tolerances, surface finish, the title block's tolerances by decimal places), refuses what it can't read safely (11.95-12.05, a single deviation, two values too far apart to be one dimension's limits, an inch fraction, numbers run together, a chamfer, a frame whose symbol didn't come through, two frames in one), and treats threads, fits without limits, notes, reference, and basic dimensions as what they are. Enter each result (one to five pieces, a value for each feature of a "4X") and it's passed or failed exactly; a sheet's zones give each balloon its reference location (C4). Numbers always run from 1 with no gaps, through deleting, inserting, renumbering, reading order, undo, and redo. Checks list what's missing or failed. The ballooned drawing downloads as a PDF (balloons drawn onto the original pages; a protected file is rebuilt from pictures of its pages, and says so; a balloon under the drawing's own comments or stamps is named, since readers draw those on top), the table as a CSV in Form 3's column order with the limits and pass or fail (with semicolons between cells when decimal commas are on), and as a printed table; the work saves as a file to open again. It's kept in this browser as it goes; nothing is uploaded; once a drawing has been opened, it works offline.
- **Problem it tests:** Every new part, revision, or process change at a small machine shop supplying aerospace, defence, automotive, or medical customers means numbering every characteristic on the customer's drawing and building a matching table: hours by hand for a complex part, with Acrobat stamps or hexagons on paper, and every requirement typed again. The free cloud tool that does it (Werk24's Werkflow Studio) uploads the drawing to its servers, which export-controlled and covered defence drawings can't lawfully go to without the right hosting (22 CFR 120.54; DFARS 252.204-7012), and the free offline tools are desktop programs to install or build; the one browser-local tool costs $360 a user a year. Does a free page that needs no install or account, and never sends the drawing anywhere, make ballooning and the Form 3 table a quick, checkable job?
- **Scope decisions:** The page reads and numbers; the inspector decides. Requirements read from the drawing are always shown for checking (CAD symbol fonts and text drawn as lines defeat any reader), limits from the title block say so and ask that boxed basic dimensions be marked, and anything ambiguous is refused rather than guessed. Arithmetic is exact (fractions), so a result exactly at a limit passes, and a "4X" stays open until each feature has a value. A balloon's number is its place in the list, so the table's numbers and the drawing's can't disagree. GD&T results are deviations from 0 to the tolerance; bonus tolerance from material condition modifiers is noted, not added.
- **Limitations:** No OCR (scans and outlined text get typed requirements), no automatic ballooning of every callout, no inch fractions or composite frames read (type their limits, or balloon each segment), no Forms 1 and 2, no CMM import, no ISO 286 or ISO 2768 tables (type the limits, or the title block's tolerances), no leader lines, PDF drawings only (not images, DXF, DWG, or 3D models). The table follows Form 3's column order but isn't the SAE or IAQG form, and nothing on the page says whether a customer will accept a report. Checked with drawings made independently (rotated, cropped, offset, scanned in CCITT G4 and JPEG 2000, protected, CJK fonts, TrueType GD&T symbols, 100 sheets, A0, 2,000 balloons), its exports read back by pypdf and drawn by macOS's own renderer, a Python generator's 1,598 requirements, an inspector's flow in Chromium and WebKit, and an independent audit whose 32 findings were all fixed; not yet used by an inspector.
- **Revisit if:** inspectors use it. Likely asks are automatic ballooning from the text layer, leader lines, ISO fits and general tolerances, Forms 1 and 2, CMM imports, sub-numbered characteristics (12.1, 12.2), and weld maps for fabricators.
