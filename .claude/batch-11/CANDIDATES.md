### Thread A: first article inspection ballooning (finished 2026-10-15; 10 searches, 8 reads)

- **Ballooning a customer drawing and building the FAI's Form 3: survives Stage B narrowly; the full gate turns on whether uploads are really barred** (`research/a-shop-floor-fai.md`). The job is real and recurring: every drawing note and characteristic goes on Form 3, numbered to match the ballooned print, and an FAI is redone after design, process, location, tooling, or NC-program changes or a two-year lapse (GKN's 2022 supplier guidance, *verified* by the thread); Boeing adopts AS9102 and names no platform (*verified* by the thread); vendors claim 250–500 characteristics on a complex part and 2–8 hours to balloon by hand (*source claim*). **The field is crowded:** Werk24's Werkflow Studio is free ("Paid Studio subscriptions are not currently offered"; the first report needs no account), with AI ballooning, results with pass/fail, Form 3, PPAP, and Excel export, processed in the cloud (EU residency, US on request, no ITAR statement) (*verified* by the thread, vendor page); The Bubbler (GPL, desktop, "works fully offline", 19 commits) balloons by click, fills nominal and tolerance including ISO 2768 and 286 fits, flags results, and exports a ballooned PDF and a spreadsheet (*verified* by the thread); `balloon` (MIT, built from Go source) auto-balloons from the text layer and writes Form 3 (*verified* by the thread); Balloonist sells the browser-local version ("never uploaded", AWS GovCloud, Form 3 Rev C) at $360 a user a year (*verified* by the thread). **The wedge** is the only option found that is free, install-free, account-free, and upload-free at once: it matters where drawings may not leave the machine (ITAR, CUI, NDAs) on locked-down PCs (*inference*, to settle). **Against it:** each free tool lacks only one of those properties, $360 a year is trivial for a business, and a Labs page would be a manual, less capable relative of them. Feasibility is settled in the main session's spike (`research/m-main-checks.md`): pdf.js renders and reads text, and pdf-lib places balloons exactly, on rotated pages, in both engines; scans and outlined text have no text layer, so typed entry is the normal path.

### Full gate: ballooning a drawing for a first article inspection

- **A. Audience:** the inspector or quality lead at a small machine shop, fabricator, or contract manufacturer who must number every characteristic on a customer's drawing and report each one: aerospace and defence suppliers under AS9102 and their customers' flow-downs, automotive suppliers' PPAP dimensional results, medical device first articles; also anyone asked for "a ballooned print with the measurements", and people learning inspection.
- **B. Trigger:** a purchase order for a new part number or revision that calls for an FAI, or a change that calls for a full or partial one: a design change affecting fit, form, or function; a change of source, process, inspection method, location, tooling, materials, or NC program; corrective action; a two-year lapse in production (GKN's guidance and Boeing's page, *verified* by thread A; Lockheed's and GD-OTS's flow-downs, *source claim*).
- **C. Input:** the customer's drawing as a PDF of one or more sheets, usually a vector export from CAD, sometimes with text turned to outlines or a scan; the title block's general tolerances; the shop's measurements from hand gauges and the CMM report.
- **D. Output:** a ballooned drawing whose numbers match a Form 3-style table (characteristic number; reference location, the sheet and zone; designator; the requirement as drawn; results as values, not "OK"; tooling; nonconformance number; remarks), each result passed or failed against its limits, and a check that every balloon has a row and the numbers run without gaps or repeats; for the customer's or the IAQG's template and the FAIR's support data ("Ballooned Print(s) ... with the noted corresponding Characteristic Numbers", GKN, *verified* by thread A).
- **E. Frequency and stakes:** every new part, revision, or qualifying change, weekly to monthly in a job shop (*inference*); complex parts carry 100–500 characteristics and ballooning by hand takes hours (vendors, *source claim*); a missed characteristic is a missing row, and a prime writes guidance "so that first time acceptance can be achieved and schedule delays are reduced" (GKN, *verified* by thread A).
- **F. Current workflow:** Acrobat stamps or comments (a paid Acrobat; a quality technician on Adobe's forum couldn't get the number inside the oval), or hexagons drawn by hand on a paper print, then every requirement typed again into the IAQG's Form 3 template or a shop spreadsheet and the count checked by eye (*source claim*, thread A); or a paid suite; or Werkflow where uploading is allowed.
- **G. Strongest incumbents (none weakened):** Werk24's Werkflow Studio, free, with AI ballooning, results with pass/fail, Form 3, PPAP, and Excel export, processed in the cloud with EU residency by default, for drawings that may be uploaded; Balloonist, browser-local, $360 a user a year, for drawings that may not; The Bubbler, a free GPL desktop program that works offline (click to balloon with ISO 2768 and 286 fill, results, a ballooned PDF and a spreadsheet), for shops that may install software; `balloon`, an MIT Go program built from source that auto-balloons from the text layer and writes Form 3; and the paid suites (Ideagen Quality Control, DISCUS, High QA, 1factory, Net-Inspect, SolidWorks Inspection).
- **H. Labs wedge:** the only option found that is free, needs no installation and no account, and never uploads the drawing, and that can show it (it keeps working with the network off once opened). That combination is a requirement, not a taste, for export-controlled and covered defence drawings: ITAR's encryption carve-out fails once a third party holds the means of decryption, and DFARS 252.204-7012 requires FedRAMP Moderate-equivalent clouds, flows down to subcontractors, and names engineering drawings as controlled technical information (*verified*, `research/m-main-checks.md`). It also covers drawings under customers' proprietary legends, and shop PCs where installing software needs IT's approval (*inference*). Against the manual method, numbering, the table, the completeness check, pass and fail, and the ballooned PDF all come from the same clicks.
- **I. Technical fit:** shown by the main session's spike: pdf.js renders a drawing and reads its text layer, and pdf-lib draws balloons onto the original vector pages exactly at the click, on pages rotated 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°, in Brave and WebKit. Limits to state: no OCR (scans and outlined text get typed requirements); an encrypted file's ballooned copy has to be rebuilt from page images; the two libraries would be the site's first runtime packages beyond React (about 2.2 MB minified, loaded only by this page); model-based definitions (3D models with PMI) are out of scope.
- **J. Falsification, investigated:**
  1. *The free tools each miss only one property, and $360 a year is small for a business* (thread A's strongest point). Investigated: the property a cloud tool misses is the one the rules make decisive for controlled drawings (above), and no free hosted, local tool exists (`m-main-checks.md`, one more search: only Balloonist, paid). The wedge is narrow but real: drawings that mustn't leave the machine, on computers where installing isn't allowed, for shops that won't buy a seat or can't get one approved quickly.
  2. *A public lab's page may not earn a quality manager's trust for controlled drawings* (*inference*). Answered in the product: an offline mode anyone can check, and a plain statement of what is kept, where, and that nothing is sent.
  3. *Automatic ballooning is where the paid field is going.* True, but automatic ballooning "fails silently" (GroundControl's 2026 buyer's guide, *source claim*), so a page-by-page completeness check is needed either way; the text layer can still fill the requirement where there is one.
  4. *The build is larger than most, and pdf.js parses untrusted files.* Accepted as maintenance debt: pdf.js kept current and its JavaScript evaluation switched off; the page lazy-loads both libraries.
  5. *A desk job, and Labs must work on a phone.* Viewing, placing balloons, and entering results work on a phone; placing hundreds is computer work, and the page says so.
- **The deciding incumbent claims re-checked in the main session** (a page reader on the vendors' and repositories' own pages, 2026-10-15, all six loaded): Werkflow Studio is "free to start" and "Paid Studio subscriptions are not currently offered", the first report needs no account, you "Upload your own drawing", "Encrypted in transit and at rest; zero retention optional", "EU residency, US on request", run by W24 Service GmbH in Munich, with nothing about ITAR, EAR, export control, or CUI; The Bubbler is a downloaded desktop binary (macOS "unsigned"), GPLv3+, latest release v0.3.1 on 29 September, with no AS9102 layout, zones, or sheets; Balloonist is $360 a user a year with a 7-day trial and "Drawings never leave your browser"; `balloon` has no hosted version (`go build`, then a localhost server) and writes Form 3 with Results "left empty for the inspector" (*verified*).
- **Verdict: BUILD, #037.** The thesis survives: the job is official, recurring, and consequential; its strongest free incumbent can't lawfully take a large class of the drawings; nothing free is install-free, account-free, and upload-free at once; and the workflow (open → balloon → record → decide → export) is the depth the brief prefers. A new direction for Labs (manufacturing quality, workshops). Provisional name **Ballooned Drawing** (`ballooned-drawing`), listed under School & work. The wedge is narrow, which the page and the Product Log say plainly; who would use it is a question for human validation (parked).

### Full gate: inbreeding and mate choice from a breeder's own records

- **A. Audience:** breeders of rabbits, goats, sheep, pigs, poultry, cats, and dogs who keep their own records (a spreadsheet of animals with sire and dam), especially of unregistered animals or breeds whose registries offer no mating tool.
- **B. Trigger:** choosing a mate before each breeding season, and buying in or keeping back stock.
- **C. Input:** the breeder's list: ID or name, sire, dam, sex, and birth date, as CSV or XLSX.
- **D. Output:**
  - each animal's inbreeding over all known generations, with how complete its pedigree is;
  - every possible mate for a chosen animal, ranked by the offspring's inbreeding;
  - the pedigree's errors (an animal its own ancestor, a sire recorded as female, a parent younger than its offspring, duplicates);
  - a printed pedigree.
- **E. Frequency and stakes:** seasonal, and the stakes are real but slow: inbreeding depression and recessive disease.
- **F. Current workflow:** spreadsheets with no inbreeding (Rabbit Talk, 2016 to 2022, breeders asking how to add it, unanswered; *verified* by thread H); paid apps; registries' tools for members.
- **G. Strongest incumbents (re-checked in the main session, 2026-10-16):**
  - **MiniInbred**, from the University of Minnesota's Department of Animal Science (Garbe and Da, user manual version 2.1, 2014): it "calculates the inbreeding coefficients resulting from all potential matings" of the current breeding animals and identifies the lowest (*source claim*: the search engine's summary of its record; the record's page is behind a firewall check, which wasn't bypassed). It is a free download, format unconfirmed.
  - **PMx:** free; a Windows program; mate suitability ranking (*verified* by thread H from its manual).
  - **optiSel** (R): plans matings from a kinship matrix. **PEDIG** (INRAE). **pawlineR** (R).
  - **QuickPed:** a free hosted web app that computes inbreeding and kinship from a pedigree file, built for small human pedigrees.
  - **Accounts and paid apps:** Pedigree Database Online (an account; up to 10-generation inbreeding with CSV import); PigeonDB; Farmbrite (paid, COI per mating); paid breeding apps.
  - **Registries on full breed records:** the Kennel Club's Mate Select; ADGA's planning tool; PawPeds' test matings; the Kunekune society's Stud Adviser.
- **H. Labs wedge:** in the browser, with nothing installed, no account, and nothing uploaded, on the breeder's whole list. That's the same kind of narrowness as #037's, but **without #037's decisive reason**. No law or customer bars a breeder's records from a cloud service or a desktop program; privacy here is a preference.
- **I. Technical fit:** good. The tabular method or Meuwissen and Luo's algorithm for thousands of animals; an independent check by path counting in Python; generated pedigrees with known answers.
- **J. Falsification:**
  - *Free tools already rank every mating:* MiniInbred (by its record's description) and PMx do it. They are desktop programs, but no reason in this job rules them out the way #037's rules out the cloud.
  - *Partial records read low and could reassure wrongly:* a breeder's own three or four generations give a figure below the truth. A completeness index can say so, but the number most likely to be quoted is still the low one.
  - *Thin first-hand demand:* one forum thread across two goes, unanswered, and other species' evidence by source claim only.
  - *The breeders with most at stake are served:* registries' tools work on the whole breed's records, which a breeder's own list can't match.
- **Verdict: REJECT.** The workflow is deep and buildable, but the thesis rests on "in a browser" alone. Free tools already compute and rank the matings for anyone who can run a program, the breeders with most at stake have their registries' figures on full records, and demand is thin. It would score about 39 on the portfolio framework, far below #037's 51 and the portfolio's middle.
