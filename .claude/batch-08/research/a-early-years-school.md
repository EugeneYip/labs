## A1. Ratio planning for childminders and nurseries (England, EYFS)

### What the rules are (as of 2026-10-13)
- Version in force: the September 2026 editions of the two frameworks (childminders; group and school-based providers), applying from 1 September 2026. (verified) https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/early-years-foundation-stage-framework--2
- That page's change history (verified):
  - Framework applying from 4 Sept 2023: England minimum ratio for 2-year-olds 1:4 to 1:5; childminders may care for more than three young children when caring for siblings of children already in their care or their own child; "adequate supervision" at meals means within sight and hearing.
  - 16 Oct 2024: clarified which level 6 qualifications count in group ratios; confirmed the childminder framework applies with or without domestic premises.
  - 1 Sept 2025 editions took effect (details not read; one secondary summary says the changes were mostly safeguarding, attendance and staffing, source claim https://schoolgovernors.thekeysupport.com/news/need-to-know-early-years-foundation-stage-eyfs-statutory-framework-updated-for-september-2025/).
  - 13 July 2026 notice: Sept 2026 editions add safer sleep, screen use, banned dog breeds and other safeguarding points. No ratio-number change is listed for 2026.
- Childminders (source claim, Coram PACEY page dated 27 Sept 2024, read in full; KinderStart and West Sussex say the same): at most 6 children under 8 per adult, own children included; at most 3 of them "young children" (young child = until 1 September following the 5th birthday); only 1 under 1. Exceptions only if the childminder can show parents and Ofsted that each child's individual needs are met: siblings, own child, continuity of care, and 3-5 year olds attending only before/after a normal school day or in school holidays (may be cared for alongside three other young children). https://www.corampacey.org.uk/?p=365604
- The cap of 6 under 8 per adult holds under every exception. (verified, DfE blog) https://earlyyears.blog.gov.uk/2023/04/20/how-staff-to-child-ratios-work/
- Assistants and second childminders: each adult gets their own set of limits (source claim, PACEY). A summary of the 2026 text says an assistant only counts in ratios once cleared as suitable via Ofsted or the childminder agency (source claim, search summary of the 2026 PDF). Registered-premises or condition-of-registration caps may bite as well (inference, not checked).
- UNVERIFIED: the "only 1 under 1" limit and the exact exception wording could not be confirmed in readable official text (the PDF would not extract; the DfE blog does not state the under-1 rule). A 2018 practitioner thread treats an under-1 limit as real (verified thread). Paragraph numbers drift between editions (3.42 in 2018, 3.53 per the DfE blog after Sept 2025, 3.50-3.51 per a summary of the 2026 text), so any tool should cite by edition.
- Group providers (source claim, Twinkl summary of the framework in force from 4 Jan 2024): under 2s 1:3; 2s 1:5; 3+ 1:8, or 1:13 where a qualified teacher or level 6 member of staff is present in some settings (qualification conditions not checked).
- Ratios apply to the whole provision, not room by room; inspectors look at ratios only if concerns arise and do not do constant headcounts. (verified, DfE blog above)
- Other UK nations differ (e.g., Wales: 10 under 12, six under 8, four under 5, three under 2, per the PACEY page; source claim). Any tool is England-only.

### E1 Audience
- Registered childminders in England: sole traders, typically a handful of children, bookings handled by hand. And nursery/pre-school managers: 27,500 non-domestic childcare providers at 31 Aug 2025, of 60,000 registered providers in all, which fell 1,270 (2%) in a year, mostly through childminders (source claim) https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/childcare-providers-and-inspections-as-at-31-august-2025/main-findings-childcare-providers-and-inspections-as-at-31-august-2025
- The childminder half is a specific person; the nursery half (managers with rotas and room moves) is a different job and needs multi-person state.

### E2 Exact job
- Before: a parent asks for a new booking (or the childminder plans a term or the school holidays). They hold each child's date of birth and session pattern (before school, after school, full day, holiday) and, if any, an assistant's hours.
- After: a yes/no for the new booking, with the exact day and time slots where under-8, young-child or under-1 limits break, and which slots would need a documented exception.

### E3 Evidence of friction
- Governed by a statutory rule: yes. (verified) gov.uk page above.
- Mistakes: a January 2019 Ofsted childminder report found the childminder at times looked after more than six under-8s, "did not understand the ages and ratios", and rated her inadequate with a Welfare Requirements Notice to follow (source claim, search summary of the Ofsted PDF; PDF unreadable here) https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50053975
- Ofsted compliance notices from 2025-26 record ratio notifications by nurseries; the causes given are mostly real-time (headcounts when children move rooms, children out of sight and hearing), and most providers stay registered (source claim, search summaries; PDFs unreadable) e.g. https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50292944 and https://files.ofsted.gov.uk/v1/file/50307199
- Confusion over exceptions: a childminder's Dec 2018 thread asks whether a sibling-swap needs two under-1s; colleagues read the exceptions paragraph differently; Ofsted refers her back to the EYFS and the local authority may not give a clear answer (verified, thread read) https://eyfs.info/forums/topic/50994-variations-to-ratios/
- Confusion over mixed-age counting: a Feb 2015 manager thread (verified, read) shows Excel and Word tables, disagreement on how to count 2s against 3s, "ratios shift when a 2-year-old turns 3", conflicting advice from inspectors and advisers https://eyfs.info/forums/topic/42623-monitoring-numbers/ . Both threads are old; I found no recent or quantified evidence.
- Other listings (source claim, titles only): Mumsnet "rising 5" https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/childcare/575717-rising-5 and "Possible childminder over numbers" https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/childcare/2241295-Possible-childminder-over-numbers ; 30-hours wraparound exception https://www.nurseryworld.co.uk/content/news/ratios-made-more-flexible-for-childminders-with-30-hours-children
- A paid document exists just to record ratio variations (source claim) https://childmindinguk.com/products/variation-form-record-and-permission-to-vary-ratios
- Recurring and time-sensitive: every booking; every September (children turning 3 or 5; the 1 September young-child rule); school holidays. (inference)

### E4 Current alternatives (searched by job words, not product names)
- Paid childminder software: MinderHQ, 19.99 GBP/month or 199.90 GBP/year, first month free, account required, no free tier. Its register has a ratio check that warns near or over the limit and its Today board has live ratio meters; no pre-booking capacity check is described, and the vacancy badge is updated by hand. (verified, vendor page) https://minderhq.co.uk/
- Paid nursery software: Famly (Foundation about 79 GBP/month with no rotas; rota tier roughly 167-219 GBP/month, sources conflict; per site; age-specific room ratios, ratio alerts, plan staff hours by ratio need), Nursery in a Box (from about 149 GBP/month), Ovivio (required vs expected staff by room). (source claim, search summaries) https://famly.co/pricing , https://www.famly.co/platform/efficient-staffing . Blossom, Kinderly, Parenta, iConnect, Tapestry: not checked (budget).
- Free web calculator: Twinkl staff:child ratio calculator (enter children, get educators required, mixed ages, qualification-dependent); a single moment in time, not a week (source claim, page not opened) https://www.twinkl.co.uk/l/10smo2
- Template: Twinkl "Childminder Ratio Planner Template" (ratios and floor space); whether free or gated could not be checked (page would not load) https://www.twinkl.com.mt/resource/t-c-1139-childminding-ratio-grid
- Home-made Excel/Word tables (verified, 2015 thread above); local authority and PACEY guidance sheets; paper grids.
- Free tool that checks a whole week of bookings by time slot against the childminder limits: none found across three targeted searches (inference; absence of evidence only).

### E5 Strongest incumbent
- Childminders: MinderHQ, cheap (about 20 GBP/month) and aimed squarely at solo childminders, with live ratio meters on the day. It does not appear to offer a forward "what if I accept this child" check across the week. If a childminder already pays for it, a free checker adds little.
- Nurseries: Famly-class software, paid, already does ratio-based staff planning.
- No free tool does the week-ahead check adequately as far as I could find.

### E6 Labs wedge
- Real but narrow: a free, no-account, local "what if I accept this booking" simulation over a week of time slots, computing the young-child status from each date of birth on each date, flagging under-1 and exception cases, with an assistant on/off. Children's names and birth dates stay on the device (relevant to a childminder's data-protection duties). (inference)
- Against: the arithmetic is a few lines (6 / 3 / 1 per adult); a spreadsheet does it; MinderHQ already shows ratio meters.

### E7 Labs fit
- Childminder-only version fits cleanly: single user, public rules, local storage, phone-friendly.
- Nursery version needs shared rotas, room moves, qualifications and attendance actuals, which is multi-person state and a management product: fails the fit.
- Regulatory content risk: rules changed in 2023, 2025 and 2026; the under-1 rule is unconfirmed; a wrong answer could lead a childminder into a breach, so it would need a visible "check the current framework" notice. England only.

### E8 Falsification (strongest reasons not to build)
1. The hard part is judgement on exceptions, not arithmetic; practitioners read the paragraph differently and Ofsted refers them back to the text (verified thread). A tool cannot settle that.
2. The ratio problems Ofsted publishes are mostly live events (headcounts, transitions); DfE says inspectors do not do constant headcounts and look only on concern (verified blog). A weekly planner would not prevent most of them.
3. The only childminder enforcement example found is from 2019, and it was a misunderstanding of the rules rather than a planning failure.
4. The audience is shrinking (childminders fell again in 2025, source claim).
5. A cheap incumbent already shows ratio meters (verified), and free guidance and templates exist.
6. Demand evidence is dated (2015, 2018) and unquantified.

Recommended outcome: HOLD.
Deciding facts:
- No free week-by-slot checker found, and MinderHQ (19.99 GBP/month) covers day-of meters but no pre-booking simulation. https://minderhq.co.uk/
- The arithmetic is small and the hard cases are exceptions needing judgement. https://eyfs.info/forums/topic/50994-variations-to-ratios/
- Current rule wording (especially the under-1 limit) was not confirmed from the framework; rules change most Septembers. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/early-years-foundation-stage-framework--2
- Nursery half fails the fit test (shared rotas).
To upgrade to BUILD-candidate: (1) a person reads the Sept 2026 childminder PDF and confirms the limits and exceptions; (2) one practising childminder confirms the pre-booking check is a real pain MinderHQ and spreadsheets don't cover; (3) scope to childminders only.

## A2. Does it generalize to US family child care homes? (kept short)

- Rules vary by state, and by licence type within a state, in ways that change the model (source claim, regulation text via search summaries):
  - California: small family child care home capped at 8 including own children under 10; "infant" means under 24 months; the mix depends on infants (4 infants only; or 6 children with at most 3 infants; or 7-8 only if Health and Safety Code criteria are met). Large home: 12 with an assistant (at most 4 infants), up to 14 with extra criteria. https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-22/division-12/chapter-3/article-6/section-102416-5 , https://blog.wonderschool.com/california-family-child-care-licensing-types-of-licenses
  - Texas: licensed child-care home up to 12 including the caregiver's own related children, with age-based mixes (table not confirmed); registered home up to 12 with at most six unrelated; separate "listed" home for 1-3 unrelated children. https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/26-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-747-1605 , https://blog.wonderschool.com/child-care-provider-resources/texas-family-child-care-licensing-types-of-licenses
- Different definitions (infant age, own-children age, assistants, school-age children) mean one rule set per state, probably 50 rule sets to keep right (inference). Licensing bodies already publish their own capacity worksheets, e.g. a California county's: https://kern.org/community-connection-child-care/wp-content/uploads/sites/15/2026/05/Capacity-Regulations-Worksheet-COLOR.pdf
- Per the brief, A2 only mattered if A1 looked promising; A1 is HOLD.

Recommended outcome: REJECT.
Deciding facts: (1) state-by-state definitions and licence types differ, https://regulations.justia.com/states/california/title-22/division-12/chapter-3/article-6/section-102416-5 vs https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/26-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-747-1605 ; (2) regulators publish their own worksheets; (3) A1's core is not yet proven.

## A3. Primary school class formation for next year

### E1 Audience
- Class teachers, year-group leads and senior leaders in primary schools with more than one class per year group, mixing classes for September in the summer term. (inference on who; the process is described in school policies)
- Context for import: Arbor serves 44% and Bromcom 16% of English schools by one count; ScholarPack is being folded into Arbor (source claim) https://www.tes.com/magazine/news/general/ess-sims-loses-market-dominance-school-information-systems-mis

### E2 Exact job
- Before: per-pupil data (gender, date of birth, attainment band, SEN or other needs, friendship nominations such as "name five children", keep-apart and keep-together pairs) and a decided number of classes.
- After: a draft list per class, balanced and honouring the constraints, which staff adjust and senior leaders sign off before children and parents hear in July.

### E3 Evidence of friction
- Recurring and time-boxed: yearly, observation then teacher meetings then senior-leader review then head approval, shared with children and parents in July (source claim) https://www.stfrancis.surrey.sch.uk/_site/data/files/documents/policies/189778A51C3849F7CE48C6EE423589DE.pdf ; Castle Hill letter, early June, announces lists at end of June https://files.schudio.com/castle-hill-primary-school/files/documents/06.06.25_-_Class_lists.pdf
- Rule-governed by school policy, not statute: criteria listed as date of birth, gender, ability, special educational, social and personal needs, friendships; some policies promise each child at least one of five named friends (source claim) https://lionhearttrust.org.uk/media/acynesno/let-mixed-classes-policy-2025-2027.pdf , https://files.schudio.com/strathmore-infant-and-nursery-school/files/documents/Class_Mixing_Policy__(4).pdf
- Error-prone: schools describe cascading "domino" effects when one pupil moves (source claim, school letters in Canada and Australia, UK policies similar); parent complaints about mixing (source claim) https://www.teachwire.net/news/seating-plans-mixing-classes/
- Workload: the vendor page says list creation saves leaders "dozens of hours" (verified that the page says it; the figure is a vendor claim) https://wonde.com/uk/application/class-creator/overview ; an Alberta division says its process takes several weeks each spring (source claim) https://ces.westwind.ab.ca/home/news/post/110944 . No independent time measurement found.

### E4 Current alternatives
- Cloud products: Class Creator (UK schools via Wonde; survey-driven; separations and pairings; "ST4S Assessed" badge; base price not published, optional premium support 295 USD/year) (verified Wonde page; price source claim) https://wonde.com/uk/application/class-creator/overview , https://www.classcreator.io/pricing . Class Composer (US only listing, starting price about 699 USD, free trial; "Classroom Management Solution of the Year" at the 2026 EdTech Breakthrough Awards) (source claim) https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1010906/classcomposer , https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/10/3309868/0/en/class-composer-named-classroom-management-solution-of-the-year-in-2026-edtech-breakthrough-awards-program.html
- Local desktop: Class Maker (Australian; whole-school licences from about 299 USD/year; student data stays local; keep together or apart, gender ratios, average ability, friend placement, manual edits) (source claim) https://www.top4.com.au/business/class-maker-156086
- Cheap spreadsheets: a Tes "class mixing sheet" for Google Sheets only, with an Automix that gives every child a friend, avoids pairs, balances ability and teacher workload (3 keep-apart names and 5 friends per pupil); price not captured (source claim) https://www.tes.com/en-ca/teaching-resource/class-mixing-sheet-class-list-organiser-13054287 . A "Sorting Wizard" Excel file written by an engineer for his primary-teacher wife; availability unclear (source claim) https://teachmag.com/a-new-way-to-create-class-lists-introducing-the-sorting-wizard/
- Free but manual: rank within boy and girl piles, deal out, then fix pairs (source claim) https://www.kindergartenworks.com/teacher-tools/end-of-year-class-sort/
- MIS built-ins: no evidence found that SIMS, Arbor, Bromcom or ScholarPack ship a balanced-class builder (searched; documentation not checked, so absence of evidence only). A South African MIS (D6) has a class-grouping module, so such features exist elsewhere (source claim) https://help.d6.co.za/portal/en/kb/articles/class-grouping
- Cards or sticky notes on a table: the traditional manual method (inference; the policies found describe meetings and observation but I found no source naming sticky notes).
- Free, no-sign-up web balancer: none found in two targeted searches. Keamk balances teams but needs an account; Ohmyluck keeps data in the browser but is a random picker (source claim).

### E5 Strongest incumbent
- Class Maker, for the data-stays-local angle (paid, install). Class Creator, for a full UK product linked to school data and carrying a school-safety badge (paid). The Tes Google Sheets file is the cheapest. None is free, and none runs with no install and no account. Whether a paid 299 USD/year licence stops a UK primary school is unknown (inference).

### E6 Labs wedge
- Free, no account, no install, nothing uploaded: pupil attainment and SEN data never leave the device, which cloud tools and Google Sheets cannot match and Class Maker matches only with a licence. (inference)
- Plus: paste a spreadsheet export, an automatic first draft, live balance meters per class, pin and swap, and a copyable list, so teachers adjust rather than start from nothing.
- "Cleaner UI" is not the wedge; free + local + no install is, and it only holds if the first draft is good.

### E7 Labs fit
- Technically fits: local optimizer, data in the browser, no keyed services. Mobile: dragging 60-90 pupils on a phone is awkward, so use tap-to-move (design constraint, not a blocker).
- Sensitive data: SEN and behaviour notes in localStorage are riskier than most Labs data; prefer session-only with export and import by file (design choice).
- Versus #029 Places by Choice: genuinely a different job. #029 allocates people to capacity-limited activities by ranked preference. Class formation partitions a cohort into k classes balanced on several attributes with hard keep-apart and keep-together pairs. The "at least one of five named friends" rule is the one place it overlaps with ranked-choice logic. It is adjacent to the already-settled group makers and seating charts, which share the keep-apart primitive, but it is a heavier job than either. (inference)

### E8 Falsification
1. The meeting is the point: staff knowledge about pupils lives in conversation, not in data columns, so the tool yields a first draft at best; some teachers will distrust algorithmic placement or balk at entering 60-90 pupils. (inference)
2. At least four incumbents already cover it and one is local-only; Class Composer's 2026 award suggests an active market. (source claim)
3. Once a year, and only for schools with several classes per year group. (inference)
4. Schools may still refuse an unvetted free tool for pupil data even if local; the UK incumbent advertises a school-safety badge. (verified badge on the Wonde page; the refusal risk is inference)
5. Overlap with settled items and #029 could make it look like a repeat.
6. Workload evidence is mostly vendor marketing.

Recommended outcome: BUILD-candidate (conditional).
Deciding facts:
- No free, no-sign-up balancer found; every real incumbent is paid. https://www.top4.com.au/business/class-maker-156086 , https://wonde.com/uk/application/class-creator/overview
- Local-only already exists but only as paid desktop software (Class Maker), so the wedge is free + no install, not privacy alone.
- Different job from #029 (balance and pair constraints, not ranked preference).
- Workload ("dozens of hours") is a vendor claim; have one primary teacher confirm before building. https://wonde.com/uk/application/class-creator/overview
Suggested small first version if built: paste a table (name, gender, band, SEN flag, keep-apart and keep-together names, optional five friends), pick number of classes, get a balanced draft, tap to swap, copy or print.
