## C1. Child performer hours in England (Children (Performances and Activities) (England) Regulations 2014, SI 2014/3309)

Counters when C1 was written: 9 searches, 7 fetches used.

### The actual limits (verified on legislation.gov.uk)
Note: the hours limits sit in Part 4 (regs 19-24) and Part 5 (regs 25-29) of the Regulations, not in the Schedules (Schedule 2 = licence application information, Schedule 3 = records).
Sources: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/3309/contents , https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/3309/part/4 , https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2014/3309/made

| Age | Earliest / latest at the place (reg 21) | Max hours at the place per day (reg 22) | Max performing+rehearsing per day | Longest continuous spell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| birth to under 5 | 07:00 / 22:00 | 5 | 2 | 30 min |
| 5 to under 9 | 07:00 / 23:00 | 8 | 3 | 2.5 h |
| 9 to school-leaving age | 07:00 / 23:00 | 9.5 | 5 | 2.5 h |

- Reg 21 does not apply where the place is where the child lives or is educated. Required education time counts toward hours at the place (reg 22).
- "School leaving age" is defined by reference to s.8(3) of the Education Act 1996 (reg 2). (inference) In practice that is the last Friday of June in the school year the child turns 16, so a checker needs the date of birth and the performance date, not just an age; after that date the person is no longer a "child" under these rules.
- Breaks (reg 23): under 5: breaks of at least 15 min, and one must be a 45-min meal break once the child has been present 4+ consecutive hours. Age 5+: present over 4 but under 8 consecutive hours = one 45-min meal break plus one other 15-min break; 8+ hours = plus one more 15-min break. Two performances in one day: at least 1.5 h between (cut to 45 min if the earlier one is under 1 h and the next is at the same place or needs no travel).
- Overnight (reg 24): at least 12 h between attendances (subject to reg 28).
- Reg 20: no other employment on the day of a performance or the day after.
- Licensed performances only (Part 5, reg 25): max 6 consecutive days of performing/rehearsing (reg 26); after 6 consecutive days for 8 consecutive weeks, 14 days off (reg 27, not where the licence is under 60 days); night-work outside reg 21 times only if the licensing authority allows, then 16 h before any other performance or rehearsal, and no further night-work for 7 days if used on two consecutive days (reg 28); chaperone discretion up to 1 extra hour past the latest time if the reg 22 limits still hold, welfare is not prejudiced and the cause was outside the licence holder's control, with notice to the licensing and host authorities by the next day (reg 29).
- Education (reg 13): at least 3 h on each school day (deemed met by 6 h a week per four-week period, max 5 h a day; spells under 30 min do not count). Chaperone: max 12 children each (reg 15).
- Records (reg 11, Schedule 3 Part 1): per child per day: date, arrival and departure times, each performance/rehearsal period, each rest interval, each meal interval, any night-work; plus tutoring, injuries, the reg 27 break dates, earnings. Keep 6 months after the last performance. I could not confirm from the text whether records must be signed or kept at the venue.
- DfE departmental advice, Feb 2015: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/child-performance-and-activities-licensing-legislation . The PDF would not read through the fetch tool (compressed streams), so I relied on the Regulations and the 1963 Act text plus council guidance. Not read: the DfE advice itself.

### Do BOPA groups follow the same limits?
- (verified, s.37 of the 1963 Act, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1963/37/section/37 , read through the fetch summary) The "four days in six months" exemption is s.37(3)(a); school-arranged performances and bodies approved by the local authority/Secretary of State (BOPA) are s.37(3)(b). The statutory power to set hours, rest and meals for exempt performances covers (a) only. Reg 19 extends Part 4 (hours, breaks, earliest/latest, overnight) to s.37(3)(a) performances; Part 5 is licensed only (reg 25).
- (inference) So the hours table does not bind a BOPA or a school-arranged show by statute. (source claim) Councils bind BOPA groups to it as a condition of approval: West Sussex, North Yorkshire, Redbridge and Hackney guidance say rehearsals and shows must comply with the 2014 Regulations on days, hours and breaks; the sanction is revocation. City of London says the law does not require council-approved chaperones under a BOPA but makes them a policy condition. Some councils add rules (one rehearsal or performance on a school day; no school absence). Records kept 6 months (West Sussex).
- Sources: https://www.westsussex.gov.uk/media/15180/bopa_guidance.pdf , https://www.northyorks.gov.uk/licensing-and-registration/child-employment-licences/body-persons-guidance-notes , https://www.cityoflondon.gov.uk/assets/Services-DCCS/BOPA-Policy-FINAL-Policy.pdf , https://education.hackney.gov.uk/content/children-work-entertainment-and-chaperone-licences . Surrey's BOPA page (read) is silent on hours and says only: no payment, no school absence, approved chaperones expected, 21 days' notice, per-child performing-day counts for annual approvals: https://www.surreycc.gov.uk/children/support-and-advice/young-people-and-their-families/guidance-on-children-and-young-people-in-employment-or-entertainment/body-of-persons-exemption
- (inference) A school show arranged by the school is outside the licence and, by the statute's structure, outside the hours regulations; dance-school shows are not covered by the school exemption (Portsmouth guidance, source claim: https://www.portsmouth.gov.uk/services/schools-and-learning/schools/school-attendance/child-performances/performance-licence-and-exemption ).

### Gate

For one show a day with a few children they are adequate. Absence of an app in my searches is weak evidence (search tool is US-only, thin on niche UK sites).
- E6 Wedge: a checker that takes each child's date of birth and the day's timeline and flags which limit is broken, by regulation number, before the day; plus children's names and birth dates never leave the device (no account, no upload), which matters for data on minors; plus a printable day record in the Schedule 3 fields. (inference)
- E7 Fit: passes. Public law table, no backend, no keys, local storage only.
- E8 Falsification: (1) no evidence of breaches or complaints; (2) the paperwork burden councils describe is licences, BOPA applications, DBS, child-protection policies and rotas, not hour arithmetic; (3) hours limits do not bind BOPA/school shows by statute, and councils vary their own add-ons; (4) England only, Scotland/Wales/NI differ (below); (5) licences can carry extra conditions and chaperones have discretion, so a "pass" can mislead: liability and disclaimer burden on a safeguarding rule; (6) seasonal use.

## C2. Stage manager line notes (free browser tool: paste script, tap to mark, per-actor notes)

Counters when C2 was written: 13 searches, 10 fetches used (one fetch was a redirect notice that I count as used).

- E1 Audience: stage managers and ASMs running line runs (plays and musicals) in professional, university, school and community theatre; the notes go to the actors.
- E2 Exact job: before: a script (paper or PDF), cast off book, a line run starting. During: follow the script and mark each error: dropped or skipped ("d" or "j" for jumped), paraphrased ("p"), added, called for line ("l"), wrong cue. After: a list per actor (page, line, error type, the correct line), sent or handed out as soon after rehearsal as possible.
- E3 Friction (all source claims; no rule or deadline governs this):
  - SMNetwork forum threads on the job are heavily read (read counts shown in the thread titles in search results; threads not opened): "Rehearsals: Giving line notes - a real novice question" 502,900 reads, "PROMPT SCRIPT: Creating a Digital Calling Script" 50,658, "Line Note - POST ITS!" 30,679, "Taking notes" 9,265. https://smnetwork.org/forum/students-and-novice-stage-managers/rehearsals-giving-line-notes-a-real-novice-question/30/ , https://smnetwork.org/forum/tools-of-the-trade/line-note-post-its!/ , https://smnetwork.org/forum/students-and-novice-stage-managers/taking-notes/ , https://smnetwork.org/forum/stage-management-plays-musicals/creating-a-digital-calling-script/
  - Advice found (via search summaries of forum and university wiki pages): the ideal is one SM on book and another taking line notes, but teams often lack the staff; do not try to catch everything; mark errors with a letter code (d dropped, p paraphrased, j jumped); share notes right after rehearsal so actors get instant feedback.
  - Reviews of the paid app say five note types is too few, want margin callouts so notes do not cover the script text, Google Drive import; one reviewer said the email-everyone button did not work (App Store page below).
  - Honest limit: I found no official guidance (for example from the Stage Management Association) and no measure of how often notes are missed or late. The threads are mostly about technique and etiquette, not tool pain.
- E4 Alternatives (searched by job words):
  - Paper: pencil or dry-erase marks in the script with a letter key; post-its; printable "line notes" sheets with a key of marks (the copies I saw sit on spam-looking domains, not trusted); UW-La Crosse stage management forms library lists blocking keys and contact sheets; an Etsy "Stage Management Line Notes Excel Template" is listed (paid; not opened).
  - Apps: Line-It (UrbanByte): USD 24.99, iPad and Mac (and Vision), no iPhone, no in-app purchases, 4.4 from 7 ratings, circle a missed or misread line and pick a note, email each cast member their notes, iOS Share Sheet in v3.1.0, Dropbox picker (verified App Store page: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/line-it/id1090772813 ). Line Notes for Actors and Crew (iOS; only a listing seen: https://appfollow.io/ios/line-notes-for-actors-crew/1353841711?country=us ). StagePro (free on iPad, also a website per a search summary; rehearsal notes on any line; scripts must be text, not image PDFs; no per-actor send mentioned; free tier's line-note features not confirmed): https://apps.apple.com/am/app/stagepro/id1207932011 . Scriptigo Pro, Scriptation (PDF script annotation; https://scriptation.com/blog/stage-managers-webinar ), ProductionPro (used by a Broadway SM; https://www.loopinsight.com/2018/11/11/broadway-ditches-age-old-processes-to-run-shows-with-ipads/ ).
  - Stage Write (web and iPad; verified home page https://www.stagewritesoftware.com/ ): digital script with notes, blocking and light cues, spacing charts, shared editing, 30-day free trial, log-in; the page does not mention line notes, per-actor notes, script breakdown or scheduling.
  - No free browser tool that takes a pasted script, lets you tap lines or words, and produces per-actor notes turned up in three searches by job words (absence in a US-only search tool; weak evidence).
- E5 Strongest incumbent: Line-It does the job as described, adequately, for an iPad or Mac user who will pay about USD 25. Its weaknesses are price, Apple-only, five note types, and a tiny review base (7 US ratings, which also hints the paid market is small (inference)). StagePro is the unverified free rival.
- E6 Wedge: free; works on any device with a browser (Chromebooks, Android, Windows, phones); no account; the cast list and notes stay on the device; notes can carry the correct line text and be grouped by actor with copy, print or mail-to; a tally of repeat errors across runs saved locally. (inference)
- E7 Fit: passes the hard limits (static, local, no keys); sending is copy, print or mailto, not real email. Reliability risks: pasted text from PDFs loses page numbers, and actors and SMs refer to "page 23, third line" in the physical script; speaker-label parsing is fine for plays but weaker for musicals with lyrics. Overlap: shares the paste-and-parse base with #018 Off Book, but the user (SM, not actor) and the job (record errors, not self-test) differ. Shared code would have to live in src/components, since experiments may not import each other.
- E8 Falsification: (1) SMs follow the calling script on paper in the prompt book; adding a screen mid-run splits attention, and paper is fast; (2) nothing forces the job: no rule, no deadline, low stakes; (3) page references would not match the actors' physical scripts; (4) StagePro may already be a free, web-capable answer (not checked); (5) phone-width tapping at line-run speed is hard; (6) licensed scripts must be pasted by the user, and many licensed scripts are only distributed as PDFs.

Recommended outcome: HOLD.
Deciding facts: (a) a paid incumbent does the exact job but is Apple-only at USD 24.99 with 7 US ratings: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/line-it/id1090772813 ; (b) no free browser tool found, but StagePro (free, iPad app and site) not checked for line notes: https://apps.apple.com/am/app/stagepro/id1207932011 ; (c) forum interest is high but about technique, and paper with a letter key is the norm: https://smnetwork.org/forum/tools-of-the-trade/line-note-post-its!/ ; (d) page-number loss from pasted text is the structural weakness (inference). To lift the HOLD: confirm StagePro's free tier, and find SMs who say paper line notes cost them real time.

## C3. Scene breakdown and rehearsal planning for amateur and school theatre (earlier HOLD)

Counters when C3 was written: 18 searches and 12 fetches used (budget spent). Three fetches returned nothing usable: the DfE PDF would not read, one site redirected, one had a certificate error.

- E1 Audience: directors, stage managers and drama teachers of school, youth and community shows with many characters and scenes, deciding whom to call to each rehearsal.
- E2 Exact job: before: has the script text, the cast list, and either everyone's conflicts or tonight's attendance. After: a character-by-scene chart, then either a calendar of scenes, dates and called actors, or tonight's list of scenes that can run.
- E3 Friction:
  - (source claim) Calling by scene from a chart is already normal practice. A school rehearsal calendar says calls are by scene, not by individual, and tells students to use the French scene chart to decide whether to attend: https://sites.google.com/fcps.org/robinhoodbhs/rehearsal-calendar . Another school schedule says different sets of scenes are practised each time: https://bonneville.alpineschools.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2015/01/Tom-Sawyer-Rehearsal-Schedule-2015.pdf
  - (source claim) The pain directors write about is conflicts: blank conflict sheets followed by trips and shifts revealed at tech, and late changes to long rehearsals; the problem is surprises and collecting data from many people, not grid arithmetic: https://www.onstageblog.com/editorials/2025/4/29/dont-lie-about-your-conflicts-just-dont , https://www.onstageblog.com/editorials/2023/4/13/lying-about-your-conflicts-is-one-of-the-worst-things-you-can-do-to-your-show . Youth-theatre parents also post about rehearsal dilemmas: https://forums.welltrainedmind.com/topic/602757-youth-theater-rehersal-dilemma/
  - No rule governs the job, and I found no measure of time lost building the chart.
- E4 Alternatives (searched by job words):
  - By hand: a grid of act, scene, page, location, characters with X marks (LibreTexts technical theatre text: https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Theater_Film_and_Storytelling/Technical_Theatre_Practicum_(Boltz)/01%3A_Chapters/1.04%3A_The_Actor_Scene_Breakdown ); a free Word rehearsal-schedule template (listing: https://whdl.org/en/node/22951 ); a paid Google Sheets template on TeachShare with characters by scene plus a second sheet for building calls (https://www.teachshare.com/browse/drama/graphic-organizers/price_asc ); conflict forms on Jotform and PDF (https://form.jotform.com/220876988277174 ); Google Calendar; Notion templates. A university-project database tool called Rehearsal Pro was mentioned in a result (availability unconfirmed, source not opened).
  - Cast98: casting, performer conflict calendars and a rehearsal schedule builder for community and school theatre; needs accounts; the developer says casting is free, other features are pay-per-show or an annual studio plan, and a recent post by the developer floats 100 auditionees and 50 cast as "small" for a free tier; a school program's policy page shows conflicts submitted through it. Its own site was not read (certificate error). https://forum.broadwayworld.com/thread-off/Any-directors-want-to-try-an-online-auditions-rehearsal-scheduling-tool , https://www.controlbooth.com/goto/post?id=366575 , https://chca-oh.org/portal/7fq2x9lm4b8k3r1c/spongebob/
  - Professional and film tools: Propared (live-entertainment production management; not opened); Production.pro breakdown view with a Characters table (https://support.production.pro/en/articles/7914929-learn-more-about-the-breakdown-view ); Arc Studio Scenes and Cast reports list speaking characters per scene and export CSV (https://help.arcstudiopro.com/guides/script-reports ); I found nothing specific on WriterDuet or Final Draft reports. Stage Write's home page lists no breakdown or scheduling (verified, https://www.stagewritesoftware.com/ ).
- E5 Strongest incumbent: two halves. The chart: a hand-built sheet or a cheap template, adequate. Conflicts and schedules: Cast98, which carries the shared-state job a Labs page cannot (source claim; its free tier is the developer's own description). No tool surfaced that builds the chart from pasted stage-play text and answers "who is here tonight, what can we run" (weak evidence of absence).
- E6 Wedge: paste text, get a speaking-role grid in seconds (then edit for silent and ensemble roles); tick tonight's attendance to see scenes that can run and scenes one person short; printable call sheet by scene; no account, and nothing about students leaves the device. (inference) Narrow.
- E7 Fit: the valuable half (everyone's conflicts, a shared calendar, notifying the cast) needs shared live state or accounts, so it fails; the local half passes. Parsing limits: speaker labels give speaking roles only; silent characters appear only in stage directions such as Enter and Exit lines, which can be matched against known names but not discovered; French scenes (a new scene at each entrance or exit) are rarely marked in printed text; scene headings vary; text pasted from PDFs breaks lines; musicals add lyrics and ensemble labels. The commercial Arc Studio Scenes report has the same speaking-only limit (source claim). Expect "right for most speaking roles, needs review" (inference). Overlap: shares the paste-and-parse base with #018 Off Book; I did not examine Off Book's parser (outside this thread's remit).
- E8 Falsification: (1) the pain directors cite is conflicts, which need shared data a local page cannot collect; (2) the chart is a known, cheap manual artifact with free and cheap templates, and a free-for-small-shows service covers scheduling; (3) parse errors (silent roles, doubling, ensemble) mean the director must review, which shrinks the time saved; (4) no evidence that anyone is stuck on "who can we run tonight", only that schools publish scene-called schedules; (5) screenplay reports exist but assume film formats.

Recommended outcome: REJECT as framed (rehearsal planning). Only a much smaller idea survives (chart from pasted text plus "who is here tonight"), with no evidence of pain; revive it only if a director or drama teacher shows the chart-building time is real, and test the parser on a handful of public-domain plays first.
Deciding facts:
1. The pain directors describe is conflicts, which need shared state (fails the Labs limits): https://www.onstageblog.com/editorials/2025/4/29/dont-lie-about-your-conflicts-just-dont
2. An account-based incumbent already schedules around conflicts and is free for small shows (developer's claim): https://www.controlbooth.com/goto/post?id=366575
3. Parsing yields speaking roles only, the same limit as commercial software: https://help.arcstudiopro.com/guides/script-reports
4. The chart is routinely built by hand in Docs and Sheets and published as a scene-called calendar: https://sites.google.com/fcps.org/robinhoodbhs/rehearsal-calendar

## Summary (for the batch)

Budget used: 18 of 18 searches, 12 of 12 fetches (three fetches returned nothing usable, one more was thin).

- C1 Child performer hours (England): BUILD-candidate, narrow, with a demand caveat. Strongest of the three. Limits verified from the 2014 Regulations (regs 21-24 for hours, breaks, earliest/latest, overnight; regs 26-29 for licensed performances only); legal record-keeping duty; councils supply paper grids that record but do not check; no checker found. Weak: no breaches, prosecutions or complaints found; BOPA and school shows follow the table by council condition only (s.37(3)(b)); England only; liability wording.
- C2 Stage manager line notes: HOLD. Line-It does the job but costs USD 24.99, Apple-only, 7 US ratings; StagePro (free) unchecked; paper with a letter key is the norm; pasted text loses page numbers.
- C3 Scene breakdown and rehearsal planning: REJECT as framed. Real pain is conflicts (shared state; Cast98 covers it free for small shows); parsing gives speaking roles only; local remainder has no pain evidence.

Not verified: the DfE advice PDF itself (unreadable through the fetch tool); Welsh and Northern Irish numbers; StagePro's free tier; Cast98's own site; Off Book's parser.
