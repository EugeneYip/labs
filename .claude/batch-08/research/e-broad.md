# Thread E: broad need-first scan for new leads (2026-10-13)

Budget used: 20 of 20 searches, 10 of 10 fetches. Read-only; nobody contacted, nothing downloaded or submitted. No lead has been taken through a gate.

Each lead gives audience, job (before and after), evidence, alternatives and a first impression. "Evidence" says whether I opened the page (fetched) or only saw the search snippet (snippet). Quotes are short or paraphrased.

Order is by first impression, best first. Leads 1-8 deserve a second look; 9-12 are weaker or sit close to settled ideas; "Also noticed" lists things that look already solved or too thin.

## 1. Pitch marking plan (grassroots football, rugby, hockey, school sports day)
- Audience: the volunteer who lines out the pitch for a youth club or school with string, a tape and a marking trolley.
- Job, before: look up the right size for the age group and format in a governing-body table (the FA's gives 9v9 as 73 x 46 m), then peg a baseline and square the corners with a 3-4-5 triangle, by hand and mostly in feet. Pitchcare's how-to walks through it with a 100 m tape: peg the 30 ft point, hold the 120 ft mark at the corner, peg the 80 ft mark (all sums done in your head), and says a bigger triangle is more accurate. After: pick sport and format (or type your own space) and get a one-page plan in metres or feet: every corner and line length, the triangle multiples and which tape marks to hold and peg, string lengths, circle radii, and a tick list. Works offline on a phone in a field.
- Evidence: Pitchcare "How To series - initial line marking using 3:4:5 triangle" (fetched; https://pitchcare.com/blogs/news/how-to-series-initial-line-marking-using-3-4-5-triangle). Football Foundation line-marking guide names the 3-4-5 method, a 10 cm line and a plus or minus 5 cm tolerance, but gives no steps and no sizes by age (fetched; https://footballfoundation.org.uk/support-article/football-pitch-line-marking-guide-accurate-lines-made-simple). FA size table (snippet; https://thefa.com/-/media/cfa/cheshirefa/files/facilities/pitch-size-metric.ashx). Football Queensland "Line Marking Guide" PDF (snippet). One setting-out guide also says to compare both diagonals (snippet only; not in the pages I opened).
- Alternatives: governing-body PDF tables and diagrams, Pitchcare how-tos, a generic Pythagoras calculator, a 3-4-5 triangle product. I found no tool that outputs a whole marking plan.
- First impression: PROMISING. Specific person, recurring (every season and every re-line), outdoors on a phone, small geometry, printable. Watch-outs: sizes differ by body and country (one snippet says sources disagree on the 9v9 penalty-spot distance), so ship editable presets and cite each number; the guidance exists so the pain is implied, not directly shown.

## 2. Rotating lesson timetable (peripatetic music teachers)
- Audience: visiting instrumental and singing teachers in schools, and the school music office, who rotate pupils' lesson slots so nobody always misses the same class lesson.
- Job, before: build the term grid by hand or buy a locked Excel timetabler. After: enter pupils, slots, term weeks and half-terms; get the week-by-week rotation, a teacher's day sheet, a printable per-pupil dates list; fixed (static) pupils and rotating pupils mixed.
- Evidence: Tes listing is a paid Excel workbook, 5 pounds, up to 13 weeks and 20 groups, lessons 15 to 60 minutes, sheets locked, Office 365 only; one buyer reported being asked for a password and a missing Read Me; the seller's pitch is "no longer do you need to manually calculate times" (fetched; https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/rotating-peripatetic-music-timetable-13121486). A Hong Kong school page says the rotation stops pupils missing the same lesson (snippet; https://explorer.discovery.edu.hk/2017/09/peripatetic-music-programme-starts-next-week). Music Monitor help "rotating and fortnightly lesson times" (snippet; https://music-monitor.groovehq.com/help/rotating-and-fortnightly-lesson-times). Clipboard post on term timetables with rotating schedules (snippet; https://clipboard.app/post/create-your-terms-music-lesson-timetable-using-rotating-schedules).
- Alternatives: the paid Tes workbook, Music Monitor and Clipboard (paid school systems), a plain spreadsheet with a MOD formula.
- First impression: PROMISING. Strongest market signal of the set (someone sells it, with a buyer complaint). Free, no sign-in, Excel-free and printable is a clear gap. Watch-out: sits near the settled rota, shift-calendar and meeting-time-rotation family; the object here (pupils cycling through lesson slots across weeks, minus holidays) is different. Audience is mostly UK, Australian and Hong Kong school music staff.

## 3. Group-photo numbered key (local-history and family-history volunteers)
- Audience: heritage-society volunteers, club or school archivists, family historians trying to name everyone in a group photo.
- Job, before: copy the scan into an editor, stamp a number on each face, type a key by row and position, post it on Facebook and ask for names. After: open the image locally, tap each face to drop a numbered pin, type names ("?" for unknown, who identified them), export an annotated copy plus a caption such as "front row, left to right" and the key list; the original is never touched; nothing uploaded.
- Evidence: Dana Leeds blog: she made a faded copy in Photoshop Elements, numbered 30 people, posted both on Facebook and got 25 named; she notes Elements is dated and suggests Procreate (fetched; https://www.danaleeds.com/labeling-people-in-group-photo/). Whiting-Robertsdale Historical Society asks the public to match names to numbers on its photos (snippet; https://www.wrhistoricalsociety.com/identify-photos). Tropy forum thread asking how to tag people in group pictures (snippet; https://forums.tropy.org/t/recognize-and-tag-persons-on-group-pictures/2141).
- Alternatives: Photoshop, Canva or PowerPoint shapes; Facebook tagging (account); Tropy (desktop research tool); cloud photo managers with face grouping (history societies advise against publishing unverified names).
- First impression: PROMISING. Phone-friendly tapping, local only, no keyed service, one screen. Shares mechanics with the settled image-redaction idea, but the job and audience differ. Keep it manual: no face detection.

## 4. Sprinkler catch-can audit (the "tuna can test")
- Audience: homeowners, community-garden and allotment managers, small grounds teams following a water utility's or extension service's advice.
- Job, before: scatter 10 to 20 cans, run each zone for a timed period, measure depths with a ruler, average, multiply by 60 over the minutes to get a rate per hour, judge evenness, then work out run time for the depth wanted (and seasonal percentages from a local table). After: enter each can's reading on a simple layout, minutes and target depth; get rate, evenness, run minutes by season per zone; printable sheet; inches or mm.
- Evidence: a Rachio forum thread where a homeowner cannot work out how to run the test with scattered, overlapping heads, finds the conversion from cup volume to inches per hour and efficiency unexplained, and gets "use a grid, multiply" answers plus a link to Rachio's support article; no spreadsheet or app is suggested (fetched; https://community.rachio.com/t/how-do-i-do-a-catch-can-cup-test-on-this-yard/23663). Utility guidance asking homeowners to do the test: Leander TX (https://www.leandertx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/1589/Measuring-How-Fast-Your-Sprinkler-System-Applies-Water-PDF; fetch returned 403, snippet only), QWEL Irrigation Audit (https://qwel.net/files/QWEL_Irrigation_Audit.pdf; snippet). Rachio support says it offers a Google Sheet that does the math (snippet; page body would not load; https://support.rachio.com/en_us/how-do-i-perform-a-catch-cup-test-BJi9vLkYD).
- Alternatives: utility PDF worksheets, Rachio's Google Sheet (needs a Google account), calculator.
- First impression: PROMISING-ISH. Official guidance asks individuals to do it; a real person is stuck; arithmetic is small and explainable. Weaknesses: done a few times a year, mostly US and Australian water-restricted areas; the "which cups belong to which zone" problem would need a thoughtful layout.

## 5. Camera-club competition runner and season scores
- Audience: the competition secretary of a camera club or photographic society.
- Job, before: collect entry files (file names carry title and author), build a slideshow, hide authors, record the judge's marks on paper or on hardware keypads, then tally per member and per season in a spreadsheet. After: load the entry files, show them full-screen in a fair order with titles only, key in scores (one judge or several), get class results, member totals and a season table as CSV or print.
- Evidence: TalkPhotography thread (May 2020) where a club secretary wants an app-based multi-judge scoring system to replace keypads; one reply recommends Photo Comp, Photo EX and DiCentra, and says another club wrote its own web voting tool; only one reply (fetched; https://www.talkphotography.co.uk/threads/photography-club-scoring-system.711486/). MyPhotoClub support points to free DJS Judging, which needs USB keypads and heavy set-up (snippet; https://support.myphotoclub.com.au/?p=2010326). PhotoEntry is a subscription service (snippet; https://photoentry.uk/). "Photo Competition Manager" is a WordPress plugin (snippet).
- Alternatives: Photo Comp, Photo EX, DiCentra, DJS Judging plus keypads, PhotoEntry (paid), the WordPress plugin, spreadsheets with RANK and VLOOKUP, PowerPoint.
- First impression: PROMISING-ISH, but evidence is thin and commercial tools exist, so check that small clubs find them too costly or fiddly. Sits near settled quiz scoring and dance scrutineering; the image display and fair-order step is the difference. One operator at a laptop, so no shared live state.

## 6. Stream biotic-index score card (volunteer water monitors, school and scout field days)
- Audience: volunteer stream monitors and teachers following state programmes (Iowa DNR, West Virginia Save Our Streams, New Hampshire VBAP, Wisconsin WAV).
- Job, before: tally animals by group on a field card, multiply each count by a tolerance value, sum, divide by the total, work out EPT richness and percent EPT, convert to points and a rating. After: tap counters per group with the chosen programme's values built in; get the index, rating and a printable card.
- Evidence (all snippet; my fetch of the Iowa PDF returned only unreadable file data): the Iowa DNR form defines the index as the sum of count times tolerance value over total organisms (https://www.iowadnr.gov/media/5295/download); West Virginia "BioScore" sheet turns metrics into points and a rating (https://dep.wv.gov/WWE/getinvolved/sos/Documents/Surveys/BioScore.pdf); New Hampshire volunteer protocol includes a calculation worksheet (https://www.wildlife.nh.gov/sites/g/files/ehbemt746/files/inline-documents/sonh/vbap-protocol-2008.pdf).
- Alternatives: paper forms plus a calculator, programme spreadsheets, programme data portals (account-based).
- First impression: PROMISING BUT NICHE. Official method, hand arithmetic, volunteers and classes. But each programme has its own tolerance lists and cut-offs, so a wrong table would mislead; would need to choose one programme and cite it.

## 7. Duplex card sheets from a list (teachers, game designers, tutors)
- Audience: teachers and tutors making matching, flash or task cards; board-game designers prototyping; language learners.
- Job, before: Word mail-merge or Excel tricks, then fiddling margins so fronts line up with backs, or Windows-only nanDECK. After: paste a two-column list or CSV, choose card size, get a print-ready PDF with backs mirrored for the printer's flip edge plus an alignment test page.
- Evidence: BoardGameDesign Forum thread where a designer wants a quick way to lay out prototype cards from spreadsheet data and gets nanDECK (scripting), Xara, a Python library and an Inkscape CSV extension, with complaints that manual editing is slow, scripting is hard and InDesign is too dear (fetched, dated 2012; https://bgdf.com/forum/game-creation/prototyping/card-layout-tools). Two-sided alignment trouble: OpenOffice forum threads on two-sided cards (https://forum.openoffice.org/en/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4367) and a guide to aligning double-sided cards with Excel (https://smallbusiness.chron.com/align-doublesided-business-cards-excel-2007-47010.html) (both snippet). Tes "double sided flash card maker" is a free Excel workbook (snippet; https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/double-sided-flash-card-maker-12321742).
- Alternatives: nanDECK (Windows), command-line scripts (Proxy Printer, cards.py), Excel and Word workbooks, a Google Workspace flashcard add-on.
- First impression: REAL NEED, HIGH NEAR-DUPLICATE RISK. Overlaps settled booklet imposition (duplex page logic) and tent cards/badges (templated printing from a list). Treat as weak if those count as the same family.

## 8. Home fire escape plan and drill (families, classes in fire-safety week)
- Audience: parents and carers; teachers and child-minders running a home-safety lesson.
- Job, before: draw a map of the home on NFPA's printable grid, mark every door and window, two ways out of each room, alarms and an outside meeting place, then practise twice a year. After: drag rooms and doors on a grid, tap to mark exits, alarms and meeting spot, print one page; optional drill mode that times each person out.
- Evidence (snippet): NFPA/Sparky "Home Fire Escape Plan Grid" (https://sparky.org/assets/uploads/Home-Fire-Escape-Plan-Grid.pdf), republished by many fire departments; the guidance says practise at least twice a year; one source cites an NFPA survey in which 71% have a plan and 47% of those have practised.
- Alternatives: printable grids (pen and paper), floor-plan apps (accounts), generic drawing tools.
- First impression: MODERATE. Public-interest and family-friendly; the official ask is explicit. Needs careful wording (a drawing aid, never a safety certificate); the editor is most of the work and is close to the existing gallery-wall layout mechanics.

## 9. Choir riser planner
- Audience: school, church and community choir directors.
- Job, before: place singers by voice part and height on risers using spreadsheets or paid Google Slides templates. After: drag names onto risers colour-coded by part, see balance by row and section, print.
- Evidence (snippet): Chorus Connection "Introducing Riser Charts", a paid software feature (https://blog.chorusconnection.com/blog/2016/5/13/introducing-riser-charts); a paid Teachers Pay Teachers pack of riser Google Slides templates; Choirplace articles on mixing by height and blend.
- Alternatives: Chorus Connection (paid), TpT packs (paid), spreadsheets.
- First impression: MODERATE-WEAK. Near the settled classroom seating charts idea; the voice-part balance check is the only new piece.

## 10. Field-event cards: high jump and pole vault countback
- Audience: volunteer field-event officials and scorers at club and school meets.
- Job, before: paper score sheets of O, X and pass marks, then work out places by countback (fewest failures at the tie height, then fewest overall). After: enter heights and attempts per athlete; get highest cleared, failures, ranking with countback and flagged jump-offs, and a printable result.
- Evidence (snippet): many circulated "High Jump Score Sheet" and "Pole Vault Score Sheet" PDFs; UIL tie-breaker rules (https://uiltexas.org/files/athletics/tf-tiebreaker.pdf); countback rule summary (https://kwx.liehendr.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2024-01/A/Countback).
- Alternatives: meet-management software (paid), paper cards.
- First impression: MODERATE, NICHE. Hand cards exist but I saw no sign that anyone finds them painful. Sits beside settled cross-country scoring and sports-day points.

## 11. Homeschool high-school transcript
- Audience: homeschooling parents (mostly US) preparing transcripts for college applications.
- Job, before: fill an Excel or Word template with grade-point lookups and credit-weighted GPA by formula. After: course list, credits, grade scale, weighted and unweighted GPA, printable one-page transcript.
- Evidence (snippet): National Center for Home Education guide and Excel template (https://www.nche.com/helps/high-school/transcript/); Time4Learning PDF template (https://media.time4learning.com/uploads/homeschool-high-school-transcript-template.pdf); Liberty University template instructions (https://www.liberty.edu/residential/wp-content/uploads/Homeschool-Transcript-Template-Instructions.pdf).
- Alternatives: several free Excel and PDF templates, paid generators.
- First impression: MODERATE. Real annual job, but crowded with free templates; value would be the layout and GPA logic without a spreadsheet.

## 12. Craft-fair or fete floor plan
- Audience: organisers of craft fairs, village fetes and school fairs.
- Job, before: measure the ground, draw pitches to scale in Excel or Canva, keep 1 m aisles and access routes, number pitches for stallholders. After: draw the site, drop standard pitches, auto-check aisles, number and export for stallholders.
- Evidence (snippet): The Craft Map "how to create a craft fair floor plan" (https://www.thecraftmap.com/blog/how-to-create-a-craft-fair-floor-plan); stallholder packs (Rushmoor Council, https://www.rushmoor.gov.uk/media/jtyl0gy3/frost-stallholder-info-pack-2023-accessible.pdf) say plans follow bookings; one fair requires 1 m clear between pitches.
- Alternatives: Excel grids, Canva, account-based event-layout software.
- First impression: WEAK-MODERATE. Same mechanics as the existing gallery-wall layout and trip board.
