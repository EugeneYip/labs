Budget used: 18 searches (one Reddit-restricted query was refused by the tool and is counted) and 12 fetches (two PDFs, the PTA UK step-by-step PDF and the Southern Nevada staffing plan, came back unreadable; one council page returned 403; the Home Office release page held no figures in text).

## D1. Silent auction run on paper bid sheets: winners, per-bidder totals, checkout, receipts

**E1 Audience.** The volunteer organiser of a PTA/PTO, church, charity or club auction (roughly 20 to 100 donated lots, held once or twice a year, mostly bid on paper at the event). (inference, matches the setting in the PTA UK guide)

**E2 Exact job.** Before: collect donated lots, print one bid sheet per lot (number, description, retail value, start bid, increments, bidder-number/bid columns), register bidders with numbers. After bidding closes: read the highest bid on each sheet, name the winner per lot, add up each bidder's lots, hand over a slip, take payment, release the prize only once paid, chase the unpaid, keep a record or receipt. (inference, assembled from the PTA UK case study below)

**E3 Evidence of friction.**
- Time-sensitive and end-loaded (verified, PTA UK guide https://pta.co.uk/events/ideas/a-guide-to-silent-auctions/): its case study closed bidding half an hour before the end, the compere announced winners, most paid cash on the day (BACS also taken), and prize envelopes were released only after payment. The guide says winning bids must be paid within a set timeframe. It does not mention queues or spreadsheets.
- Pain claims are almost all vendor marketing (source claim): BiddingOwl says phone checkout removes the long checkout queue (https://www.biddingowl.com/en-us/learning-center/silent-auction-checkout-best-practices); a mobile-bidding vendor's promotional story of a PTA whose paper sheets were chaotic, with only winning bids typed into Excel (https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/kicking-school-year-how-mobile-bidding-helped-pta); a school auction plan with a checkout chair and 2-3 runners (search-result summary, not fetched). No independent forum or Reddit thread on checkout pain turned up in two targeted searches. Pain evidence is therefore thin and vendor-authored.
- Governed by rule (verified): PTA UK says no licence is needed for a silent auction, the Sale of Goods Act applies to how items and values are described, and paper records of names and contact details should be destroyed after the event. HMRC Gift Aid guidance s3.40 (https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-detailed-guidance-notes/chapter-3-gift-aid) says a payment for an item at a charity auction is a purchase, not a gift. A bidder who deliberately pays above the item's worth may Gift Aid the excess only if the item is commercially available and the bidder knew its retail price when bidding; promises and celebrity-enhanced lots cannot. So the brief's "Gift Aid can't apply" is right for the lot price, with that narrow split-payment exception. Gambling: the PTA guide does not mention it (verified); raffles are covered on separate PTA pages (https://pta.co.uk/events/planning/licensing-guide-all-you-need-to-know/, source claim). I found no official page saying "an auction is not gambling" in so many words, only the no-licence statement (inference: no gambling issue for a plain bid-the-price auction).

**E4 Current alternatives (searched by the job's words).**
- Free printable bid sheets: 32auctions (says no signup, https://www.32auctions.com/silent-auction-bid-sheet), RallyUp, Donorbox, Zeffy, many more (source claim).
- Spreadsheets: free Excel/Google Sheets receipt template (https://spreadsheetdaddy.com/templates/silent-auction-receipt) and many bid-sheet templates. A single free workbook doing sheets + winners + per-bidder totals was not found in one search (inference: easy to build with a SUMIF, no evidence a polished one is widely used).
- Free-tier platforms (all need an organiser account): Auctria Explorer is $0/year, up to 250 guests per event and 3 team members, events tracking under $10K a year, 1% Auctria card fee plus processor, sign-up button (verified, https://www.auctria.com/pricing; the page does not say whether paper bid sheets or winner reports are on that tier). BiddingOwl: auction manager "free", no limit on items or users, 5% on winning online/mobile bids, supports paper bid sheets (source claim: G2 listing and BiddingOwl's own comparison https://www.biddingowl.com/en-us/learning-center/biddingowl-vs-auctria; the BiddingOwl page I fetched was promotional and confirmed none of this). 32auctions: free up to 20 items, ads, online bidding (source claim, https://www.32auctions.com/silent-auction-software).
- Paid: Silent Auction Pro, the paper-bid-sheet specialist (Bidsheet Basic $99 prints bid sheets and certificates; Plus $348 plus 2% of gross; another comparison lists $449-$899/yr plus 2%); ClickBid ($795-$2,295/yr claims), Handbid (from about $1,396 per event), GiveSmart and OneCause (quote), RallyUp (about 4.9% on auctions) (source claim, mostly competitor-authored: https://rallyup.com/blog/silent-auction-software-tools/). UK: PTA UK names Donate, JumbleBee and PTA Events (percentage of proceeds or annual fee) and Superstars, a managed service taking a percentage (verified, PTA UK guide). Also Facebook comment-bidding (verified, same guide) and a sealed-bid box.

**E5 Strongest incumbent.** For a committee happy to make an account: BiddingOwl's free manager (claimed to cover paper sheets) or Auctria Explorer (free, verified). For no-account: a free spreadsheet and printable sheets. Plainly: free tools already do this job adequately for most PTA-sized events. I found no free, no-account page that prints sheets, names winners and produces per-bidder checkout slips.

**E6 Labs wedge.** (inference) The only real edges: no account, nothing uploaded, works offline in a hall with no wifi; bidder names and phone numbers never leave the device and one "clear all" button matches PTA UK's advice to destroy paper records; bid sheets that print the retail value (which is also the HMRC split-payment condition); after the event, type lot / bidder number / price once and get per-bidder pick-up slips and a bidder-sorted lookup list. Weak: someone running one event a year can simply make a free account, and a spreadsheet adds up a bidder's lots.

**E7 Labs fit.** Single-device, paper-sheet back office fits (static, localStorage). Fails or degrades: phone bidding (shared live state), card payment (keyed paid processor), several checkout desks working at once (shared state). A Gift Aid declaration for the excess needs donor name and address and edges into tax advice. (inference)

**E8 Falsification.** Strongest reasons not to build: (1) free incumbents exist and are adequate, one verified; (2) the arithmetic is trivial, the end-of-night queue is a people-flow problem that vendor guides fix with runners, pre-sorted envelopes and phone checkout, not a calculator; (3) all the friction evidence is vendor marketing, none independent; (4) UK schools are visibly moving to online platforms (PTA UK lists three plus a managed service), so paper is the shrinking case; (5) once-a-year use means little search demand and no habit; (6) people hand over real money at a charity event and will not trust an unfamiliar free page for "what you owe". (inference unless marked)

**Recommended outcome: REJECT** (revisit only if independent evidence appears that volunteers refuse accounts or hold auctions where there is no signal, or that no free tool handles paper sheets).
Deciding facts: free tiers exist: https://www.auctria.com/pricing (verified) and https://www.biddingowl.com/en-us/learning-center/biddingowl-vs-auctria (claim); the official UK guide does not mention checkout queues or spreadsheets and points organisers to platforms: https://pta.co.uk/events/ideas/a-guide-to-silent-auctions/ ; friction evidence is vendor-authored: https://www.builtinchicago.org/articles/kicking-school-year-how-mobile-bidding-helped-pta ; Gift Aid cannot be claimed on the lot price (verified): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-detailed-guidance-notes/chapter-3-gift-aid

## D2. Lifeguard rotation planning: a day's rotation of guards across stands, with breaks

**E1 Audience.** The head lifeguard, pool supervisor or duty manager at a leisure-centre, school, hotel or council pool or water park who sets each session's rotation of lifeguards across stands, other duties and breaks. (inference; job postings below)

**E2 Exact job.** Before: knows who is on shift (the staff rota is built elsewhere), the pool's stand list and session times, and which guards are trained for which stand (slides, wave pool). After: a time grid (per guard, per slot: which stand or break) posted poolside that keeps within the published limits and hands over cleanly, plus something to cue each rotation. (inference)

**E3 Evidence of friction.**
- Governed by guidance (verified, RLSS UK https://www.rlss.org.uk/hsg179, updated 17 July 2024): rotate every 15, 20 or 30 minutes; no more than 30 minutes in one static position; no more than 60 minutes at the poolside (90 in exceptional circumstances in the pool hall); cited to the NPLQ Gen 10 manual, section 1, page 84; these are RLSS UK's recommendations, not stated as HSE's; the page says nothing about breaks. It also says the old "10:20" scanning rule was replaced by "Natural Scan : 20" in July 2023. HSG179 itself I could not read; the "paragraphs 93-94" references come from an Edge Hill risk assessment seen via search summary (source claim), which also calls HSG179 guidance rather than law. UK employment law separately gives a 20-minute rest break on shifts over six hours (source claim). Elsewhere: Lifesaving Society (Ontario) asks for a 15-minute break after at most two hours of direct supervision; Royal Life Saving (Australia) summarising RLSS UK research says rotate ideally every 20 minutes (range 5-30) with a break about every 90 minutes (source claims, search summaries).
- Recurring (source claim, search-summarised postings): head-lifeguard job descriptions list building daily rotations as a duty, for example St Mary's County MD (creating and managing daily rotations and duty schedules is an essential duty, https://www.stmaryscountymd.gov/docs/jobheadlifeguard.pdf), Concord NH, a Chattanooga posting (assign daily rotations per supervision guidance), Hutchinson MN. Inference: a daily task at every pool with lifeguards.
- Practice varies, no single standard (verified, DigiQuatics Q&A https://blog.digiquatics.com/aquatics-faqs/how-to-structure-lifeguard-pool-rotations-78rs3): 30-minute stands with at least a 30-minute break; three 20-minute stands then a 20-minute break; 40 minutes up, 20 down (40 and 40 on long shifts); one site moving from three rotations of six stands to at most four stands with 15-minute breaks and a 30-minute meal break. Nobody on that page says how the schedule is built or complains that it is hard.
- Not found: any complaint about the time or errors in building rotations, any enforcement or incident report blaming a faulty rotation plan. I did not search for incident cases specifically. So there is evidence of a rule and a daily task, but no evidence of pain.

**E4 Current alternatives (searched by the job's words, three searches).** Paper charts and whiteboards; free spreadsheet grids (Southern Nevada Health District staffing plan, Section 4 (Rotations) grid with an X for breaks, https://media.southernnevadahealthdistrict.org/download/aquatics/snhd-lifeguard-staffing-plan.pdf, PDF not readable by my fetch, so source claim via search summary; University of Georgia aquatics SOP, 20-minute rotations with hand-offs at :17, :37, :57 and a "down guard", source claim; Vertex42-style rotation templates, source claim). Generic shift-pattern tools that do not do stand rotation (ShiftScheduleUp, free, no signup, 2-2-3 / Pitman / DuPont patterns; Autorota, old Windows freeware) (source claim). RationalGo, an AI app-builder template for lifeguard post rotation compliance on a credit-based platform (source claim). DigiQuatics (commercial aquatics operations, price not found), Lynxight (camera-based occupancy analytics, enterprise) (source claim). Staff-scheduling suites such as Planday and Shiftbase plan who works when, not who stands where (inference). One real generator: Rotation Board (below).

**E5 Strongest incumbent.** Rotation Board, an iPhone/iPad app by Oleksandr Savchuk: plans daily team rotations across stations, generates automatically from a skills matrix, balances hard stations and avoids repeats, handles absences, with manual override; data stays on the device, no account; free tier is one board, 7 members, 5 stations, 3 periods a day; Pro is $9.99 a month or $89.99 a year (unlimited, PDF export); the listing never mentions lifeguards or pools; Mac "not verified"; too few ratings to show (verified, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6757284204). Plainly: it does the generic job, but is iOS-only, capped (a pool with more than 5 stands or 7 guards must pay) and paywalls PDF. I found no dedicated free lifeguard rotation generator; spreadsheets and the capped app are adequate for a small pool.

**E6 Labs wedge.** (inference) Works on any phone or desktop with no install or caps; free print and PDF; takes the number of guards and stands, session length, rotation length and break pattern, then builds the grid and checks it against the RLSS-style limits (stand no more than 30 minutes, poolside no more than 60, break rules the user sets), flags gaps and unfair loads on hard stands, shows hand-over marks; a full-screen "now / next" countdown for a deck tablet. Staff names are personal data and stay in the browser. Weak spot: the algorithm is a simple shift-by-one pattern a supervisor can draw by hand.

**E7 Labs fit.** Fits well: static, deterministic, localStorage for the pool set-up, printed output instead of shared state. Caveat: safety-critical, guidance varies by operator and country; must present as a planning aid, cite the numbers, never as compliance. (inference)

**E8 Falsification.** (1) No pain evidence anywhere: the practitioner thread is silent on planning difficulty. (2) Rotation is commonly a fixed pattern per pool reused daily, so "generate" is occasional, and the harder job (who works when) belongs to other software. (3) A copyable spreadsheet grid and a paid-but-small app already exist. (4) Liability and trust: an unofficial safety page; the right limits differ between UK, US and Canada and between operators' own procedures. (5) Small, niche audience; a countdown or horn at the pool matters more than a chart. (inference)

**Recommended outcome: HOLD** (technical fit is excellent, evidence of pain is missing; build only if a pool supervisor or forum shows the daily rotation is genuinely a time sink or error source).
Deciding facts: limits are published and specific (RLSS UK): https://www.rlss.org.uk/hsg179 ; practice varies and nobody reports difficulty: https://blog.digiquatics.com/aquatics-faqs/how-to-structure-lifeguard-pool-rotations-78rs3 ; closest generator is generic, iOS-only and capped: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6757284204 ; daily-duty evidence is job postings only: https://www.stmaryscountymd.gov/docs/jobheadlifeguard.pdf

## D3. Three other temporary-event workflows screened

**D3a. Sponsored walk or run: per-lap pledges, amount owed per sponsor, collection.** Audience: school PTA or club organiser running a sponsored walk, swim or run. Evidence: many school forms already carry columns for pledge per lap, maximum pledge, flat donation, laps completed, amount owed and amount collected (source claim, e.g. https://www.scaschools.org/uploads/9/6/5/4/9654115/walkathon_sponsor_registration_sheet.pdf, https://www.lapalcanal.co.uk/sponsorship/sponsorship-information/; search summaries). Hosted pledge platforms already compute per-lap totals online (inference, not checked). Evidence of collection pain from independent sources: none found. Recommended outcome: REJECT (no friction evidence; online platforms and printed forms cover it; the money chase is a people problem).

**D3b. Village flower, produce and craft show: entries by class, results, cup points, prize money.** Audience: show secretary of a village horticultural or flower show. Evidence: real shows publish entry forms by section and class, results PDFs and trophy lists, with an overall cup for most points and section cups, some barred to past winners (source claim, search summaries of e.g. https://www.haddenham.net/lib/trophy-winners-2025-F980199.pdf, https://www.laceygreen.com/Sections/Horticultural_Society/2024-RESULTS.pdf). Commercial cloud show software exists for larger agricultural shows (ShowBiz, SuperShow; prices not found); no free software aimed at village secretaries turned up; spreadsheets (SUMIFS) are the obvious DIY. Evidence of friction: none found. Recommended outcome: HOLD at best, leaning REJECT (shows exist and a niche is plausible, but nothing shows the secretary's tally is painful).

**D3c. Temporary Event Notice (TEN) planner, England and Wales: is a TEN needed, am I inside the limits, what is the last day to give notice.**
- E1 Audience: organiser of a one-off event where alcohol is sold or entertainment provided at a site without a licence: village hall committee, charity, PTA, club, community group, pop-up. (inference)
- E2 Exact job: before, has a date, venue, expected numbers and plans to sell alcohol; after, knows the notice type (standard or late), the last day to give notice counted in clear working days, whether the limits allow it, who gets a copy and the fee. (inference)
- E3 Evidence. Rules are consistent across about twenty council pages seen via search summaries (source claim; Licensing Act 2003 itself not read; e.g. https://www.arun.gov.uk/temporary-event-notice, https://elmbridge.gov.uk/licensing/alcohol-and-entertainment-licences-0/temporary-event-notice-ten/types-and-limits-tens, https://www.pembrokeshire.gov.uk/temporary-event-notices/how-much-notice-do-i-need-to-give): at most 499 people at one time including staff; at most 168 hours; a premises at most 15 TENs or 21 days a calendar year; a person without a personal licence at most 5 a year (2 of them late), with one 50 (10 late); 24 hours between TENs; standard notice at least 10 clear working days, late notice 5 to 9; clear working days exclude the day notice is given, the event day, weekends and bank holidays, and some councils also exclude the first day or treat after-hours receipt as next working day. Cost of mistakes (source claim, council pages via search summary, e.g. https://www.pembrokeshire.gov.uk/temporary-event-notices/how-much-notice-do-i-need-to-give, https://www.watford.gov.uk/licensing-3/temporary-events-notice): errors can make a notice invalid, fees are not refunded, a late notice has no appeal and fails on a police or environmental-health objection; Pembrokeshire asks for 28 days even though the minimum is 10. Time-sensitive and rule-governed: yes. Volume: the Home Office publishes TEN counts (Alcohol licensing, England and Wales, April 2025 to March 2026, released 1 October 2026, https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/alcohol-licensing-england-and-wales-april-2025-to-march-2026, verified that it covers TENs) but the numbers are in a spreadsheet I could not open, so the audience size is unquantified.
- E4 Alternatives: each council's page and online form (authoritative, local counting rules); council checklists (e.g. Central Bedfordshire's temporary event checklist PDF, source claim); legal and ticketing blogs (Woods Whur, tickts.co.uk, source claim); a spreadsheet WORKDAY with a holiday list (inference). No dedicated TEN deadline calculator turned up in two searches.
- E5 Strongest incumbent: the council's own page and form. Plainly: it is authoritative and a side calculator would never replace it.
- E6 Wedge: one page that takes the event date and returns the last standard-notice day and the late window with England and Wales bank holidays, checks 499 / 168 hours / 24-hour gap, counts the year's TENs against the 5 and 15 limits from a list kept in the browser, and says "give 28 days where you can". (inference)
- E7 Fit: static and keyless (bank-holiday data bundled or from gov.uk's public list); England and Wales only (Scotland and Northern Ireland use different permissions); no accounts, no live state. (inference)
- E8 Falsification: (1) a wrong date has real consequences (invalid notice, no refund; selling alcohol without authority is an offence, from my own knowledge of the Act, not checked here), so an unofficial calculator invites blame; (2) councils themselves advise leaving far more than the legal minimum, which undercuts the value of an exact last-day figure; (3) council online forms probably flag short notice themselves (not checked); (4) counting rules differ by council and nation, and limits have changed recently (Arun mentions a temporary 2022-23 increase); (5) volume per organiser is a few TENs a year. (inference unless marked)
- **Recommended outcome: HOLD.** Deciding facts: rule-governed, time-sensitive, costly mistakes, no calculator found (councils' pages above); volume unread (https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/alcohol-licensing-england-and-wales-april-2025-to-march-2026); the council form is the authority and advises 28 days (https://www.pembrokeshire.gov.uk/temporary-event-notices/how-much-notice-do-i-need-to-give); legal-adjacent liability.

## Ranking and what would change it

1. D2 lifeguard rotation (HOLD): best fit and a real gap, but the missing piece is evidence that supervisors find building rotations slow or error-prone. One head-guard or pool-manager statement to that effect would move it to BUILD-candidate.
2. D3c TEN planner (HOLD): strongest rule-and-deadline case; needs the national TEN count and evidence that organisers miss deadlines.
3. D3b village show (HOLD, leaning REJECT), D1 silent auction (REJECT), D3a sponsored laps (REJECT).
