## Scan log, searches 1-2 (Easy Read)
- Easy Read made in-house: guides from councils and charities (Scottish Government service manual, Norfolk CC checklist, AbilityNet, Self Advocacy Info layout PDF, Innovate Trust for volunteers) all describe the same method, a two-column Word table (picture left, words right), Arial 14-16 pt, 1.5 spacing, one idea per sentence, 15 pages or fewer, same picture for a repeated idea (source claim, search summaries). Norfolk says only trained staff should make it, and providers should do complex documents (source claim).
- Cost: "around £120 per page" and weeks for traditional production, said by a UK government demo of an AI tool, "Simply Readable", on the Cabinet Office's NDX AWS walkthroughs (source claim, aws.try.ndx.digital.cabinet-office.gov.uk/walkthroughs/simply-readable/step-3); a provider's price sheet adds £125 per document for checking by people with learning disabilities (source claim).
- Incumbents moving to AI: Photosymbols (the paid picture library everyone names) now sells EasyMaker, AI conversion "in minutes" with generated images, PDF, audio, web (source claim); KAIST's AI Easy Read tool; an "Easy Read AI" iOS app (source claim). First verdict: REJECT-leaning. The hard parts (rewriting, picture choice, testing with readers) are human or now AI-served; a free layout-and-check page would be a Word table with a sentence checker, little more than a cleaner UI, and pictures need a licensed library (ARASAAC is CC BY-NC-SA).

## Scan log, searches 6-7, fetch 1 (captions for live performances by small companies)
- New lead from the first screen's "accessibility work by small organisations": open captions for a live show (amateur and fringe theatre, school plays, community opera and choirs), the script shown on a screen and advanced by an operator. Practice found: Stagetext (the UK's main theatre captioning charity) has a trained captioner format the script in its software, add speaker names and sound descriptions, and cue it live; it published "Captioning Display Standards" (2022) for theatres that produce their own captions (source claim, stagetext.org search results). Access Project (Brighton) keeps scripts electronically and formats them into PowerPoint shown on TVs and a big screen; a fringe commentary calls PowerPoint and a projector the cheapest route, about three lines at a time, with timing the main difficulty (source claim, search summaries incl. devotedanddisgruntled.com/blog/deaf-access, brightonfringe.org stagetext.pdf).
- Alternatives by the job's words (source claim, AlternativeTo listings unless marked): Qstit (GPL-3.0, desktop Win/Mac/Linux, manual live control, reads .txt or .srt, so the script must be converted first); Subtivals (open source, live subtitles); Glypheo (free, Mac only, imports Word, OSC/MIDI, Syphon out); Captitles (free download, AI turns an imported script into surtitles and splits long lines; "All changes are stored in cloud", shared "same way as in Google Docs"; live auto-detection still in development; pricing page not read) (verified, captitles.com, fetch 1); paid: Frieze, Spectitular (browser service or offline app), QLab text cues (Mac). First verdict: worth a gate. Free desktop tools exist, but each needs an install and a hand-converted script, and the AI one stores the script in its cloud.
- Search 8, fetch 2 (Stagetext's standards): launched 11 Nov 2022 for "theatres and production companies providing captioned performances", because of "more and more venues providing captions in-house"; "not a technical manual for captioning"; Theatre Captioner Network captioners train for up to a year (verified, stagetext.org/?p=5299). Stagetext captions 460+ performances a year with 140+ theatres "and supports theatres to caption an additional 500 themselves" (source claim, search summary of stagetext.org); a May 2026 Stagetext tender for theatre captioning screens (source claim). The standards PDF sits in stagetext.org/for-venues/resources/ (not read).

## Captions gate, searches 13-14, fetches 5-8
- Captitles reference page (verified that the page says so, captitles.com/captitles-data): desktop software for macOS and Windows, audience app for iOS and Android; "stores surtitles in the cloud"; AI can "identify dialogue and divide it into individual surtitles"; used by "more than 600" organisations (source claim, vendor's figure). ## Gate 1: caption cues for a live show (open captions run by the company itself)
- E1 Audience: whoever prepares and operates open captions for a captioned performance in a fringe, amateur, school or community company (stage manager, volunteer, access lead), for d/Deaf and hard-of-hearing audiences; then small venues captioning in-house (Stagetext supports about 500 such performances a year, source claim). The Fringe addresses its artists directly (verified).
- E2 Job: before, the script is retyped or pasted into PowerPoint, Keynote or Impress a caption at a time, two or three lines per slide, the speaker's name on each line, song titles and sound effects added, blank slides for blackouts, then clicked through on the night and re-edited after every script change. After, paste or open the script, get caption-sized chunks with names (stage directions dropped, sound cues kept in brackets), fix any by hand, then run it: an operator view on the laptop (current, next, previous, search or jump to a scene, blank) and the audience view in a second window on the projector or TV, offline; a printed cue script and an .srt or .txt export for Qstit or QLab.
- E3 Friction: an official festival page prescribes the slide-by-slide layout for a whole script and says preparation is where the cost lies (verified, Fringe); a practitioner's 2012 thread calls the cue building tedious (verified); "1,000 slides" for a play (source claim, vendor). A two-hour play of about 15,000-20,000 words would come to roughly 1,200-1,600 captions of two lines (inference). NEGATIVE: three searches found no first-hand complaint from a present-day amateur group, and no templates for sale.
- E4 Alternatives (by the job's words): PowerPoint, Keynote, Impress (the Fringe's DIY route); QLab text cues (Mac, existing users); Glypheo (free, Mac only); Qstit (GPL desktop, needs a hand-made .txt or .srt); Subtivals; FreeShow and OpenLP (free church lyric software, not dialogue-aware); Captitles (free plan for "amateur and community theatres with a single show", desktop install, account, cloud, AI splitting, verified); paid Spectitular, Frieze; free AI live captions in PowerPoint and Windows, which the Fringe warns mishear theatre (verified); Stagetext and the Difference Engine as services. No free browser-based runner found (two searches).
- E5 Strongest incumbent: PowerPoint, which every group has; Captitles Free, made for exactly this group and doing the splitting with AI.
- E6 Wedge: nothing to install and no account (school and council laptops, Chromebooks, inference); the script never leaves the device (Captitles keeps it in its cloud, verified); offline in a hall without Wi-Fi; unlimited shows; deterministic splitting by the Fringe's stated rules, every chunk editable, rather than AI; an operator view and audience window built for a live show (blank screen, jump, resume after a reload).
- E7 Fit: static, local, keyboard-first; a second window moved to the projector; preparation and a one-screen tap-to-advance mode work on a phone or tablet mirrored to a TV (inference). New domain for Labs (access for deaf audiences); the parser and second-screen ideas are known from #018 and #032 (no shared code).
- E8 Against: (1) Captitles Free already serves one amateur show at no cost, with AI splitting; (2) no present-day first-hand pain, and a group captions perhaps one performance a run (inference); (3) Stagetext treats captioning as skilled work (captioners train up to a year, verified), so easy DIY risks poor access unless the page enforces the formatting rules; (4) script formats vary and parsing is brittle (Off Book's experience); (5) a crash mid-show is very visible, so state must survive a reload; (6) a third theatre-adjacent tool after #018 and #030; (7) licensors' terms on projecting script text are unchecked (inference).

## Gate 2 (short): a carer's week of caring tasks, summed for an assessment
- E1 Audience: unpaid carers in the UK preparing for a carer's assessment, or helping with the cared-for person's PIP or Attendance Allowance claim.
- E2 Job: before, a paper diary for a week or two (tasks, time each takes, nights, worst day); after, quick entries and totals by kind of task, day and night, printed for the assessor.
- E3 Friction: guidance asks for it everywhere (Ceredigion, Bristol, Parkinson's UK FS70, SMA UK, source claim); no complaint that the diary is hard found.
- E4 Alternatives (search 14, source claim): the PIP assessor's own printable Carer's Diary (Capita, January 2024), MND Association's care summary grid (task, how often, how long), Parkinson's UK's guide, Citizens Advice and MS Society PIP diaries in Word and PDF; Purpl PIP & ADP Tracker (free daily diary app for claimants); Jointly by Carers UK (£2.99 once, free through many councils; shared notes, logs, tasks, no hours tracking).
- E5 Strongest incumbent: the free paper grids, including the assessor's own form. E6 Wedge: totals and a print, nothing uploaded; that's all. E7 Fit: fine (a week or two in one browser).
- E8 Against: a log with sums, the tracker class Batch 07 rejected; official forms already ask the right questions; carer eligibility turns on the impact on wellbeing, not an hours total (inference), so the totals add little.

## Verdicts
- **Caption cues for a live show: BUILD-worthy, narrowly.** Deciding for: an official festival page tells companies to lay out a whole script by hand, two or three lines per slide with names and sound effects (verified), and no free tool found does it in a browser without an install, an account or uploading the script. Deciding against, to weigh before numbering: Captitles' free plan for "amateur and community theatres with a single show" (verified), and no present-day first-hand complaint. Two checks the main session can do itself before numbering: split a public-domain play (for example a Gutenberg Shakespeare) to show the scale and the parser's accuracy, and confirm that Captitles needs a sign-up and an install (its download page). If either fails, HOLD.
- **Carer's diary summary: REJECT.** Free official and charity diaries (the PIP assessor's own among them) and a free claimant app already cover it; the only extra is adding up.
- **Screened out in the scan: REJECT** Easy Read layout (AI tools from the main picture library, Photosymbols EasyMaker; the hard part is writing and testing with readers); visual stories (JeffCAN's free template tool, Canva); care-home activity records (they belong in digital care records); wheelchair-basketball line-ups (adding five numbers; Playing Time's territory).
- **Not searched (budget spent), first-screen inference only:** access riders for disabled freelancers (free templates are known to exist, unchecked here); accessible-path surveys by volunteers (overlaps thread N's outdoors lane, and a wrong gradient grade could send a wheelchair user onto an unsafe path).
