Steel City Striders: winner's time / runner's time, best 5 of 12, plus a separate WMA age-graded table, published as a shared spreadsheet (source claim: steelcitystriders.co.uk/road-race-championship/road-championship-2025/).   - S3 (incumbents): no championship-standings product surfaced; clubs run their own spreadsheets or websites. So the arithmetic itself has versions and edge rules a club must pin down (inference).
  - S4 (data): official WMA factor files are downloadable (Australian Masters Athletics lists men's and women's factor .xls; WMA 2023 Appendix B PDF; Howard Grubb's site offers Excel files "with all the age-grading factors and formulae") (source claim, snippets). The npm "age-grade-tables" package (fetched, unpkg README) holds only Ironman 2025 factors, MIT for code, no data licence: irrelevant.   - S6 (incumbents, second pass): Lymm Runners' championship counts each member's best six results automatically, with age-group awards by age on 1 July (source claim, lymmrunners.org.uk/members/awards/club-championships-2/); an IMRA forum post says Ireland's mountain-running site wanted "automatic club championship calculation" like Scottish Hill Runners' site, but it was cut from the 2008 rebuild (source claim, imra.ie/forum/topic/id/4541); MyGameDay (paid sports-admin platform) combines competitions into a weighted "Club Championship" ladder (source claim, support.mygameday.app).   - S9 + fetch (data licence, the deciding E7 question): Alan Jones's Age-Grade-Tables repository is **CC0-1.0** (verified, github.com/AlanLyttonJones/Age-Grade-Tables, sidebar licence, read 2026-10-14): road tables 2010 and 2015 (approved by WMA and USATF), 2020 road standards (USATF MLDR, 20 May 2020), 2025 road standards (USATF MLDR, approved 2025-01-10; 5 km, 10 km, half and marathon, other distances interpolated), plus 2004/2006 WMA tables; spreadsheets, no CSV. ## Gate 1: club season championship standings (running clubs first)

- **E1 Audience.** The volunteer who keeps a club's championship (often "the statistician") in running clubs, especially UK and Irish clubs whose championships count other organisers' races: Steel City Striders, Lymm Runners, Didcot Runners, York Knavesmire, Ipswich Jaffa (source claim); Serpentine and Swansea Harriers (verified). Cycling clubs keep the same best-N table with VTTA standards (source claim). US "grand prix" series often run inside RunSignUp (source claim; its series scoring verified). How many clubs keep one by hand: unknown (inference: most affiliated clubs run some championship).
- **E2 Job.** Before: after each championship race, find the members in that race's results (or in members' emails), work out each score by the club's rule (age-grade % typed into a single-result calculator, winner's time / runner's time, or position points), update the spreadsheet, apply best N, minimum races and age-on-a-fixed-date categories, publish an update (Steel City posts 12 a season, source claim). After: paste or open each race's results, members matched to the club list with unmatched names flagged, scores by the stated rule and pinned table, the season table and categories updated, a file kept and a table to publish (inference).
- **E3 Friction.** Moderate and indirect. Snippets only: "time consuming and prone to errors", typing each runner into a calculator, a year of doing it "via a manual method", "a very large spreadsheet with a lot of code" (source claim; the forum is closed). Swansea: missing names, delays, members asked to chase (verified). IMRA wanted automatic championships and cut it (source claim). No organiser was found saying the job takes hours; the pain reads as a willing volunteer's chore (inference).
- **E4 Alternatives** (searched as "club championship", "age graded", "grand prix series", "league table", "template"): club-built spreadsheets, with Howard Grubb's Excel factor files or the Australian Masters factor sheets (source claim); single-result calculators (HMRRC, Runner's World, Hampstead, Fetch; source claim); big clubs' own databases (Serpentine, Scottish Hill Runners; source claim); RunSignUp Series Scoring (verified: account, races on RunSignUp, no age grading mentioned); OpenTrack for competitions on OpenTrack (source claim); RunScore (paid; source claim); MyGameDay (paid; source claim). No free template or no-account tool found in seven searches (S1, S3, S4, S6, S10, S13, S14).
- **E5 Strongest incumbent.** The club's own spreadsheet: free, already built, and it encodes that club's rule mix. For US series, RunSignUp.
- **E6 Wedge** (inference). No spreadsheet to build or inherit when the volunteer changes; rules chosen from presets with tie-breaks shown (age grade % from pinned CC0 road tables 2010, 2015, 2020 or 2025 by race date; winner's time ratio; position points; best N of M; minimum races; age on a fixed date; categories); paste a results page or CSV and see members matched, non-members and near matches flagged; dates of birth never uploaded; a season file and a table to print or paste into the club site.
- **E7 Fit.** Static, local, offline. Open road data verified (CC0, Alan Jones). Limits (inference): no WMA 2023 track factors or Grubb's road adjustments, so clubs like Serpentine wouldn't match; a season of entries must live in a file, since browser storage can be cleared; copied results pages come from dozens of timing sites, so parsing pasted text is the fragile part.
- **E8 Strongest reason not to build.** The friction is collecting results and each club's own rule mix, not arithmetic. Rules sprawl (training and participation points at Swansea, volunteer bonuses in the US, Serpentine's average of five, Steel City's winner ratio with a five-race minimum), so presets miss many clubs, while the incumbent spreadsheet already fits its club exactly. A season-long job sits badly with browser storage. Table versions differ between clubs, so numbers that disagree with members' own calculators invite disputes. And it would be a second rule-based standings tool straight after #032 Comp Desk.
- **Verdict: HOLD.** Deciding: real, recurring and unserved by any free tool (seven searches), with open data available; but the evidence of pain is indirect (snippets from a closed forum), the incumbent spreadsheet already fits each club, the hard parts (collecting results, club-specific rules) don't generalise, and it repeats Comp Desk's shape now. Reopen with two desk checks: about 10 UK club championship rule pages, to see whether most fit four presets; and pasted results from five timing sites (RaceResult, Sporthive, Webscorer, SiEntries, Run Britain), to see whether members can be picked out reliably.
