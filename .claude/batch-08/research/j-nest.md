## Log, searches 5-8, fetches 1-4
- BTO "Planning your visits to the nest" (verified, fetched https://www.bto.org/get-involved/volunteer/projects/nrs/guidance-and-training/planning-your-visits-nest): one visit in laying (date + accurate egg count lets the first-egg date be worked out), one in incubation (full clutch), one just after hatching, two in the nestling stage (half and three-quarters grown), one just after the young are expected to fly, approaching carefully to avoid premature fledging. Periods from BirdFacts "Biology"; example given as ranges (Blackbird incubation about 10-15 days, Sparrowhawk 30-36). No worked calculation; no calculator, app or spreadsheet named. No numeric chick-age cut-off.
- NestWatch FAQ on estimating dates (verified, fetched): backdate first egg at one egg a day (worked example: 2 eggs on 10 May, 4 on 13 May = first egg 9 May); some groups lay every other day or less, "which makes estimating very difficult"; do NOT backdate hatch date from nestling age (growth varies); record fledge date only if witnessed or well estimated; volunteers may skip dates they can't estimate. Names no period source and no tool.
- NestWatch Code of Conduct (verified, fetched https://nestwatch.org/learn/how-to-nestwatch/code-of-conduct/): check every 3-4 days (weekly still useful), 5-10 visits per nest, songbird nests last about 30 days; avoid early morning, dusk, bad weather, first days of incubation; "Do not approach nests when young are close to fledging"; judged by appearance ("fully feathered and very alert"), no numeric cut-off, no prediction asked for.
- BTO Ringing & NRS Info app (verified, App Store listing v2.0.3, Sept 2024, free): ring sizes and recording codes only; listing mentions no nesting periods, no date calculator, no visit planning. DemOn (web) is where visits are entered; BTO pages found say nothing of it estimating dates or scheduling visits (source claim, not confirmed inside DemOn, which needs an account). No separate BTO nest field-recording app found in search.
- NestWatch size (source claim, NestWatch Digest PDFs via search summary): 42,357 nest attempts from 45 countries in 2024 (+17.5%), 36,035 in 2023, 32,077 in 2022; >3,000 people submitted in 2011; open dataset >860,000 attempts. No recent participant count found.

## Log, searches 9-12, fetch 5

Paper growth charts exist (Waterman Bird Club EABL chart) for ageing chicks.

## Answers

**E1 Audience.** UK: almost 750 NRS participants, 35,000+ nests a year (source claim, bto.org/nrs). North America: NestWatch received 42,357 nest attempts from 45 countries in 2024 (source claim, NestWatch Digest); latest participant count found is >3,000 people in 2011 (source claim). Bluebird trails: many local programmes of tens to hundreds of boxes and volunteers (e.g. 92 weekly volunteers, North Park PA; 58 trails, Lake County IL) (source claim); no national count found. Also Purple Martin landlords (PMCA sells a date wheel for them) and casual nest-box owners asking when chicks will leave (source claim). (inference) Formal recorders number in the low thousands to perhaps ten thousand across schemes; a niche but committed, recurring (every spring) audience.

**E2 Job.** (verified, BTO and NestWatch pages) After each visit the monitor turns "N eggs on date D" or "chicks of about age A" into an estimated first-egg date (back-count one egg a day for most small birds; every other day or slower for herons, gulls, owls, geese, doves etc.), then adds the species' incubation and nestling periods to get hatch and fledge windows, and spaces visits by stage: BTO wants one visit in laying, one in incubation, one just after hatching, two in the nestling stage (half and three-quarters grown), one after expected fledging; NestWatch wants checks every 3-4 days, 5-10 visits a nest. Both say never approach when young are close to fledging; bluebird guidance turns that into "do not open after day 12-13" (source claim, NABS factsheet as reproduced). Result needed: first-egg/hatch/fledge dates for the record (NestWatch's nest summary asks for them, observed or estimated) and dates for the next visits plus a last safe check date.

**E3 Friction.** Moderate, mostly indirect. (verified) NestWatch's FAQ admits a full chronology is "difficult", fledge dates are hard to estimate, slow-laying groups make it "very difficult", and lets volunteers skip dates. (verified) The 2006 Sialis spec calls notebook tracking "very cumbersome" and someone built a free Prognosticator spreadsheet for it; (source claim) PMCA still sells a $12.95 wheel for exactly this sum; USGS built a spreadsheet for shorebird researchers; NestWatch added overdue-check reminders in 2018. (source claim) Casual owners ask forums when chicks will fledge. Not found: any recorder saying the arithmetic is hard; (inference) experienced recorders do it mentally, so the gain is largest for newcomers, bluebird trail monitors juggling many boxes, and edge cases (incubation starting before clutch completion, slow layers).

**E4 Alternatives** (searched by the job's words, 16 searches). BTO Ringing & NRS Info app: ring sizes and codes only, no periods, no date or visit features (verified, App Store listing, v2.0.3 Sept 2024). DemOn (web, account needed): no public page says it estimates dates or schedules visits (source claim; not checkable without an account). NestWatch app: logging, GPS, offline, optional reminders after 5 and 10 days without a check; no date estimation, the FAQ tells users to estimate by hand (source claim). Bluebird: Sialis Bluebird Prognosticator spreadsheet (2006, projected hatch/fledge and stop-checking date; verified page, download unchecked); paper growth charts; weekly data sheets with date fields. Purple Martins: PMCA Prognosticator wheel, $12.95 (source claim). Researchers: USGS Arctic shorebird spreadsheet (source claim). Regional: NatureCounts Nesting Calendar Query Tool (season, not per nest). Generic "hatch date calculators" are poultry-only (21/28 days). No current free web or app tool that takes species + observation + date and returns dates and a visit plan was found. Not checked: BTO's printed "A Field Guide to Monitoring Nests" (2011), which (inference) likely gives per-species timings.

**E5 Strongest incumbent, plainly.** Doing it by hand: a calendar plus BirdFacts figures (UK) or a bluebird guide's day-12/13 rule (US), recorded in the NestWatch app (with its overdue reminders) or DemOn. The only purpose-built tools are single-purpose and dated: PMCA's cardboard wheel (one species, paid) and the 2006 Sialis spreadsheet.

**E8 Strongest reason not to build.** (1) Data rights: the obvious species tables can't simply be republished: BTO's terms forbid copying or redistributing site material without prior written consent (verified); Cornell's species figures come from Birds of the World (subscription; NestWatch pages credit it, verified) and its terms page could not be read. The tool's real value, a vetted species table with laying interval, incubation start and period ranges, is exactly the constrained part. (2) The core sum is simple (count back, add about 14, add about 18 days) and the formal UK audience is about 750 people, so the wedge over a calendar is thin for experienced recorders. (3) False precision risk: observed periods are wide (Blue Tit fledging 15-22.5 days, verified BirdFacts); NestWatch discourages backdating hatch from nestling age and relies on appearance near fledging; a wrong "safe" date could cause premature fledging. Mitigable with windows and conservative cut-offs. (4) Double entry: records live in NestWatch or DemOn. (5) Timing (inference): the northern season starts in March, so no users until spring.

**Data.** BTO BirdFacts gives per-species typical ranges plus means and observed ranges (incubation, fledging, clutch, first-clutch laying date), no laying interval, (c) BTO, terms as above (verified). BirdFacts asks users to cite the original sources linked for specific values (source claim). Cornell: All About Birds life-history pages (Nesting Facts as ranges, from memory, inference) returned 403; NestWatch house pages carry no periods (verified). Open path: Storchova & Horak 2018, Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.n6k3n, 499 European species incl. incubation period and clutch size (source claim); Dryad's default is CC0 (source claim); a fledging field and the record's licence need one check. (inference, unverified) Myhrvold et al. 2015 amniote life-history database (incubation and fledging ages, global) may be a CC0 source for North American species. (inference, legal, not advice) facts aren't copyrightable in the US, but UK database right covers extracting a substantial part of a database such as BirdFacts; a small, self-compiled table cited to primary or CC0 sources avoids that; asking BTO's permission means contacting someone, an owner decision.

## Verdict
HOLD, leaning BUILD. The job is verified on both schemes' official pages, people keep building single-purpose tools for it (2006 spreadsheet, a $12.95 wheel, a USGS sheet), and no current free tool does species + observation -> dates + visit plan; it suits Labs (offline in the field, nests kept locally). Deciding unsettled fact: an openly licensed per-species source for incubation and nestling ranges (plus a laying-interval rule) covering the common nest-box and NRS species. Check the Dryad record (licence, fledging field) and the Myhrvold licence: if they hold, BUILD with windows and conservative last-safe-check dates; if not, REJECT rather than ship a bare date-adder with user-typed periods. Secondary weights: thin direct friction evidence, small formal audience, out of season until March.
