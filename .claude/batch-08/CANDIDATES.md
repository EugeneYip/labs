### Direct checks (round 1)

- **Room-by-room heat-loss estimates for sizing a heat pump: REJECT.** Free tools already do it for homeowners: Heatpunk (free, by Midsummer), Gas Engineer Software's free room-by-room calculator (watts and BTU/h, saved as you go), and Trystan Lea's open-source room-by-room tool (OpenEnergyMonitor); Elmhurst's needs contact (*source claim*, search results 2026-10-13). The hard part is U-values and air changes, which no page can supply.
- **Printable visual schedules for autistic children: REJECT.** Free, no-account web tools exist: Special Learning's Visual Schedule Maker (templates, reorder, print), Pictoagenda (ARASAAC pictograms, printable), Picto4Me; PrAACtical AAC keeps a list of free resources (*source claim*, search results 2026-10-13).
- **Compare two lists of names: REJECT (was a Batch 07 HOLD).** Its question is answered: free browser tools already show who is missing from each side, locally. raw.org's Compare Two Lists gives "A only", "B only", and the intersection, and "runs locally in your browser" (*verified*, raw.org/tool/compare-two-lists, read 2026-10-13); Bushe's list tool and CompareLists.org say the same (*source claim*); Kaystons Forge adds optional Levenshtein matching (*source claim*). The job is real (a 700-name master list against 175 meeting attendees, BetterSheets, *source claim*), but what's left (nicknames, "Smith, Robert", initials) is a refinement, in the lists-and-spreadsheets domain this batch deprioritizes.
- **Martyn's Law scope and tier for small venues: REJECT.** A one-off decision per venue that turns on legal interpretation (hirers, events, places of worship), not in force until about April 2027 (Royal Assent 3 April 2025, 24-month implementation), and already walked through by free advice notes from ACRE, NALC, county associations, the Evangelical Alliance, and ProtectUK's guidance (*source claim*, search results 2026-10-13).
- **Silent-auction bid sheets and checkout (thread D): REJECT.** Free tiers exist (Auctria Explorer $0 for small events, *verified* auctria.com/pricing; BiddingOwl's free manager with paper sheets, *source claim*); PTA UK's guide points to platforms and doesn't mention checkout pain (*verified*); the pain evidence found was vendor-written. Raw notes: `research/d-events-leisure.md`.

### Decision for #030 (2026-10-13)

- **BUILD → #030 Show Order** (`show-order`, homepage category `groups`). Checked here before numbering:
  - **E1 Audience:** dance-studio owners and teachers planning the yearly recital or showcase (20–80 routines, dancers in several each); then school concerts, variety shows, and performing-arts showcases where performers appear in more than one act.
  - **E2 Job:** before, the routines and their dancers are set (from the studio's class lists) and the order is laid out on index cards or a spreadsheet, each dancer's gaps checked by eye. After, a running order where nobody is in back-to-back numbers and quick changes are as few as possible, with each dancer's numbers and gaps listed for the dressing room, and a printable order.
  - **E3 Friction:** every recital; tedious (hundreds of dancers × several routines) and error-prone. A studio owner "determine[s] all the quick changes beforehand" and sets up a changing booth for any dancer with fewer than three numbers between dances (Dance Teacher, *source claim*); Recital Conflict Manager was written by a parent after watching staff lay out 3×5 index cards (Dance Teacher review, about 2012, *source claim*). People keep building and selling tools for it, and studio suites include it.
  - **Real data, checked here:** a studio's published 2026 spring recital order (27 routines, 92 dancers, 70 in two or more) was read and every dancer's gaps measured: nobody back to back, but 40 changes with only two numbers between (under Dance Teacher's threshold of three). A plain local search over the same routines, keeping the opener first, found orders with **none** under three in each of five runs; with a rule of four between, 29 against the studio's 74 (*verified*, scratchpad scripts, 2026-10-13). A careful hand order leaves quick changes a search removes, though the studio may have had reasons the data doesn't show (ages, flow), which is why the page keeps the planner's order where asked.
  - **E4 Alternatives** (*source claim* unless marked):
    - index cards; spreadsheets; static quick-change templates (Rundown Studio, costume-change trackers);
    - Running Order Planner ($17, The Whole Studio on Payhip): a downloadable offline HTML file with drag-and-drop, flags for "no time to change" and a tight one-routine gap, and print; it doesn't reorder by itself (*verified*, payhip.com/b/x5Gah);
    - ShowSort (DJ Roze LLC, Apple and Windows, usage-based from US$2.49, no free trial, no reviews on Capterra): optimizes the order to minimize quick changes, locks routines, exports each performer's schedule;
    - Recital Conflict Manager ($19.99, Windows with Excel, up to 75 routines);
    - studio-management suites (subscriptions): Jackrabbit has drag-and-drop ordering, a conflict gap, and "Refresh Conflicts", with no suggested order (*verified*, help.jackrabbitclass.com); Class Manager has a gap threshold with conflict guidance; CompuDance has position numbers edited by hand;
    - Prosody Backstage (free iPhone app, 5 ratings, an account with name, email, and phone; release notes mention a "Quick Change flow"; no ordering described; its platform not found) (*verified* App Store page; what the flow does is unknown);
    - **no free tool found** that checks gaps, let alone suggests an order (seven searches including an extended one and the app stores; the only free thing is Stage Stubs' printable recital checklist).
  - **E5 Strongest incumbent:** ShowSort for an optimizer (paid, desktop, unknown uptake); Jackrabbit's module for studios already paying for Jackrabbit.
  - **E6 Wedge:** free, no account, no install (iPad, Chromebook, studio laptop); an order suggested within stated rules (numbers between, locked opener and finale, intermission) rather than only flags; live conflicts while reordering by hand; each dancer's schedule printable; any roster pasted or opened (CSV); children's names never uploaded.
  - **E7 Fit:** one person plans on one device; static; a local search over orders is fast in a browser for about 150 routines; the plan saved in the browser, with a file to keep.
  - **E8 Against, and why BUILD survives:** studios on Jackrabbit or Class Manager have conflict checks (but no suggested order, and many studios, schools, and community shows have no suite); ShowSort is cheap (but paid, desktop, unknown); a show's order is artistic (so the page fixes what the planner pins and moves as little as it must); once or twice a year per studio (but hours each time, for the studio's biggest event); a 15-routine show is easy by hand (the 27-routine real one wasn't). Much of the pain evidence is vendors' or old; the real-data check is the strongest evidence.
  - **Name:** "ShowOrder Plus" is a trade-show ordering app and "Running Order" an iOS festival set-times app; nothing called "Show Order" plans running orders (*source claim*, search 2026-10-13).
