### Direct checks (round 1)

- **Harris matrix for excavations (archaeology): REJECT.** The job is real and standard: the matrix is the accepted diagram of a site's stratigraphic sequence, and UK practitioners consulted by the AHRC Matrix project (Historic England with York and South Wales) said it was "most often" built in Excel, with phasing drawn by hand and large matrices "rigid and unwieldy" (May, Taylor and Binding, *Internet Archaeology* 61, 2023, *verified*). But that project built the tool: **Phaser**, a free, open-source single-page app on GitHub Pages with no server, no install, and no account, which reads the relationships as CSV, lays out the matrix, groups and phases contexts, runs the UK practice's validation checks, and compares the sequence with dating evidence (*verified*, stratigraphic.github.io/matrix and the paper's section 7). Its gaps (a prototype tested on desktop Chrome and Edge, PNG export only, no stated cycle or redundant-relation handling) are refinements, not a missing tool; iDig (free, iPad) and old free desktop tools (Stratify, ArchEd, the Harris Matrix Composer) add to it, and yEd is used by excavations for drawing (*source claim*). A Labs page would be a second browser tool behind the field's own.
- **League fixtures with shared grounds (sports operations): REJECT.** The constraint is real (two teams sharing a pitch can't both be at home), but the official free systems already handle it: the FA's Full-Time scheduler asks for "venue sharing pairs" and its advanced tool covers ground sharing across divisions (FA Grassroots Technology help, *source claim*); the ECB's Play-Cricket has a Shared Grounds function, across leagues too (*source claim*); Rundator (free) has "derby pairs" (*source claim*); PlayHQ and Playwaze cover Australian and subscription leagues.
- **Fixity checks against a manifest for small archives (digital preservation): weak.** Checking a transfer against its checksums (BagIt, md5 or sha files) is a standard step, and the DPC's handbook notes that strict IT controls hamper the email-linked Fixity Pro (*verified*, dpconline.org fixity demo), but the field's free desktop tools are its standard (Bagger, DART, DataAccessioner, Fixity, Exactly, TeraCopy; POWRR's workflow teaches them, *source claim*), PowerShell and `shasum` are built in, Web Crypto has no MD5 and no streaming digest (large video files), and the job doesn't suit a phone.
- **Checking the deputy report's accounts (lay deputies, Court of Protection, England and Wales): lead from thread B, checked here.** The rule, *verified* on the July 2025 OPG102 form and its OPG102A guidance (text read from the PDFs here): for each main bank account, the money paid in for the reporting period under 19 set rows (Attendance Allowance … State Pension, account interest, refunds, "Transfers in from client's other accounts", "Other money paid in"), and out under 20 (care fees, household bills, the deputy's security bond, OPG fees, the client's personal allowance, "Cash you have withdrawn", professional fees, deputy's expenses, gifts, "Transfers out to client's other accounts", major purchases …), with date-by-date detail lists for gifts, cash, fees and expenses, purchases, transfers (with the other account's last four digits), and other money; then boxes 1–6: opening balance + money in − money out against the closing balance on the statement, and "If the figures in box 5 and box 6 are different, tell us why"; the guidance: "If you don't explain why, we may not be able to accept your report", use the bank statements ("Don't rely on receipts or from memory"), the period is 12 months from the court order's date, "not the tax year", and a spreadsheet kept as you go helps, though OPG "can't accept a spreadsheet instead of your report". Scale: 64,456 deputyships at the end of 2025–26, up 5.6% in a year (OPG annual report 2025–26, *verified*). No free template or tool for this report found in two searches here (one by the thread); law firms sell lay-deputy support (*source claim*). Open: how the online service ("Complete the deputy report") takes money in and out (totals or each payment); its start page asks for "financial records relating to the client (for example bank statements)" (*verified*).
  - **More, checked here (same day):**
    - **The online report's own model,** *verified* from its public source code (github.com/ministryofjustice/opg-digideps, `main`, read 2026-10-14: `client/app/src/Entity/Report/MoneyTransaction.php` and the translation files): money in has 26 types in 8 groups (salary or wages; interest, dividends, property rental; private and State pension; 12 benefits plus "other benefits"; compensation; inheritance, cash gifts received, refunds, sales of assets, investments, property; anything else), money out 41 types in 11 groups (care and medical, household bills including food, accommodation, the client's personal expenses, fees including OPG's and the security bond, major purchases, tax and charges including bank charges, "moving money": cash withdrawn and payments to other accounts; anything else); "Enter a total amount or individual amounts for each type", "Make sure you only enter each payment in one place, so we do not count it twice". Gifts and deputy expenses have their own sections, and transfers between the client's accounts a section of their own (from, to, amount; "you can give us a total"; not needed with fewer than two accounts). The balance: the accounts' opening balance plus money in, less money out, against the accounts' closing balance; when they differ, "Accounts not balanced", the difference, a list of common causes (wrong balances, gifts or deputy expenses also entered under money out, interest), and a reason box; the report can still be submitted.
    - **DeputyDB,** the only purpose-built tool found: "a money management program dedicated to generating annual reports for people appointed by the Court of Protection", importing CSV and QIF into "COP reporting categories"; version 0.2 alpha, source code only (Qt, C++), last updated 2012-10-07, no reviews (*verified*, sourceforge.net/projects/deputydb). Not a live incumbent, but a deputy once built one.
    - **Bank downloads** come in pieces: Lloyds' CSV is capped at about 150 transactions an export, Santander's at 600 for a personal account (*source claim*, third-party guides and Santander's help page), so a year usually means several overlapping files: the double-counting the forum threads describe.
- **Combining judges' scores when each judge marks a different subset (awards, grant panels, science fairs, poster prizes, hackathons): open, a lead.** The problem is recognised by the people running such panels: HeroX's guide to standardizing judges' scores, Carrot's "level playing field" page (a judge scoring everything 1–2 against one scoring 4–5), Eventex Awards adopting normalization in 2023, an Oregon grants committee's 2025 process change to per-team z-scores, and a Lever for Change script for its competitions (*source claim*, search results 2026-10-14). The tools that do it are paid platforms (Evalato, Eventex, LiveJudge, awards and grant software) or scripts; Scorejudge.com is free with no logins but doesn't normalize; no free, no-sign-up page that takes a score sheet and adjusts for judges' severity was found (one search). To check: demand outside platforms, the method's soundness (z-scores assume each judge saw a typical set; an additive judge-plus-entry model uses the entries judges shared), and whether it repeats #024 and #032 (results from scores).
  - **More evidence (same day):** Penn State's Graduate School poster competition has over 200 posters and about 100 judges, five posters each, with no-shows and walk-ins so the judge count is known only on the day; the statisticians who wrote it up found a simple average unfair and a balanced design impossible (it would need about 2,010 judges), and propose assigning judges one at a time as they arrive in a connected, near-balanced design, then a model that adjusts for judges, which in simulation more often gives the prizes to the truly best posters (Penn State statisticians, "Near-Balanced Incomplete Block Designs with An Application to Poster Competitions", arXiv 1806.00034, *verified* via ar5iv). A small society's student contest (RMBS) publishes results where "the scores were normalized for each judge", each poster judged by six or more (*source claim*). A regional science fair judges each project three times and averages, then caucuses (UNM, *source claim*). The author of Gavel (MIT, an open-source pairwise judging server used at hackathons) argues z-scores still fail when judges see few entries (*verified*, anishathalye.com). Devpost's judging doesn't normalize; organizers export and do it themselves (*source claim*). No free, static tool found for either half (assigning judges, combining scores); the open-source ones are scripts (Lever for Change's, Python) or servers (Gavel).

  - **More (discovery for #035):** university research days and societies' student poster prizes assign each judge 4 to 6 posters by specialty and outside their own department, and handle conflicts (the student's adviser, committee members, co-authors) by recusal and reassignment (UMBC, Mount Sinai, Michigan, MSU, the Entomological Society of America's judges' training; EGU's own judging system tracks each judge's load; *source claim*, search results 2026-10-14); a 2026 hackathon write-up found 32 of 40 projects changed place after normalizing (*source claim*). **Status: a fallback, not yet BUILD.** The need and the gap are real, but it sits in the event and organisation cluster the brief asks Batch 09 to move away from (results from scores after #024 and #032; assignment beside #029 and #031), the job comes once a year per event, and its method is contested (z-scores, a judge-plus-entry model, or pairwise comparison as Gavel does). Decided after round 2.

### Decision for #035

- **BUILD → #035 Collection Climate** (`collection-climate`, homepage category `school`, for work): a climate report from temperature and humidity loggers' files, against a stated guideline. Checked here before numbering (A–H):
  - **A. Audience:** whoever looks after a small museum's, historic house's, archive's, library's, or church's collection and downloads its temperature and humidity loggers, often monthly: a lone curator, a collections officer, a volunteer; then lenders' and borrowers' registrars preparing facilities reports.
  - **B. The job:** before, an export from the logger's own software (CSV or text from Tinytag, HOBO, Lascar EasyLog, Hanwell, Elitech, and phone apps for Bluetooth loggers). After, for each logger or room: how much of the time it was inside the guideline's band, every 24-hour RH swing that broke the limit, the monthly minimum, maximum, and mean, a graph with the band shaded, several loggers side by side, and a page to print for trustees, a loan, or a sustainability decision.
  - **C. Frequency and importance:** monthly downloads, yearly reports, every loan; the guideline most cited is the Bizot Green Protocol's (2023): "a stable relative humidity (RH) is required in the range of 40 - 60% and a stable temperature in the range 16 - 25°C ... with fluctuations of no more than ±10% RH per 24 hours within this range" (NMDC and AIC, *source claim*), its 2015 version 45–55% RH with ±5% drift; museums widening their bands to save energy have to show what their rooms actually did.
  - **D. Alternatives:** the loggers' own software: graphs, daily minimum and maximum, alarm limits, a dew point tool, exports (Tinytag Explorer, *source claim*; no time-in-range or 24-hour swing figures found); Excel by hand (home-made templates at Duke and on the Conservation DistList, *source claim*); Conserv Essentials, free but in its cloud with a login, files up to 1 MB, "standard graphs & reports", preservation metrics only on Pro at $95 a month (*verified* by the thread); IPI's eClimateNotebook, about $120–180 a year, an account and uploads (*source claim*); the V&A's Climate Toolbox, an Excel workbook with VBA macros for up to 8,000 readings (Pretzel, *Heritage* 2023, CC BY, *source claim* via Semantic Scholar; whether the workbook is freely obtainable wasn't found); ConSciR (an R package); the Getty's Excel tools on request.
  - **E. Strongest incumbent:** the logger's own software plus Excel, which every site has; Conserv Essentials for free graphs in the cloud; eClimateNotebook for those who pay; the Climate Toolbox where it has been obtained.
  - **F. Labs wedge:** any logger's export opened on the device, nothing uploaded and no account; no macros (Microsoft blocks macros in files from the internet by default, and many organisations block them altogether), so Macs, Chromebooks, and locked-down PCs work; a year of readings at any interval (the Toolbox takes 8,000, fewer than a year of hourly readings); the guideline chosen and stated (Bizot 2023, Bizot 2015, a lender's band, or the site's own), with time in band, each 24-hour swing breach, monthly figures, a shaded graph, loggers side by side, and a printable page.
  - **G. Labs fit:** static; local; the charts drawn on the page; thousands to hundreds of thousands of readings handled in the browser; offline once opened.
  - **H. Against, and why BUILD survives:** free graphs already exist (but not the guideline's figures, which are what trustees and lenders ask for); the Climate Toolbox may do much of it (but needs desktop Excel with macros allowed and stops at 8,000 readings); the friction evidence is older mailing-list posts and the help schemes that analyse data for small sites, not recent complaints; the ±10% per 24 hours rule is read in more than one way (so the page offers both readings and says which it uses); export formats vary (the #034 reader's lessons: a forgiving reader, a preview, the columns changeable); a report could be taken for a conservator's judgment (so it reports figures against the band chosen and says that a conservator's evaluation decides, as the NMDC text itself says).
  - **Diversity:** a new domain for Labs (collections care) and a new interaction (a time series read, charted, and measured against a standard), in the School & work category; it reads files, like #034, but the job is environmental monitoring.

## Decision for #034 (2026-10-14)

- **BUILD → #034 Deputyship Accounts** (`deputyship-accounts`, homepage category `family`): the money section of a lay deputy's yearly report, prepared from the bank's own downloads. Checked here before numbering (the batch's gate, A–H):
  - **A. Audience:** lay property and affairs deputies in England and Wales: usually an adult child, spouse, or parent appointed by the Court of Protection to run the money of someone who lacks capacity, reporting each year under general supervision, online ("Complete the deputy report", the default for lay deputies) or on the OPG102 paper form. 64,456 deputyships at the end of 2025–26 (*verified*); the lay share isn't published in what was read.
  - **B. The job:** before, the reporting period ends (12 months from the court order's date, "not the tax year"), and the deputy has the statements or downloads of each of the client's accounts. After, the money in and out of each account under the report's types, gifts and deputy expenses apart, transfers between the client's accounts paired, the balance agreeing with the statements (or the difference found), the figures to type into the online report or copy onto the form, and a spreadsheet to send with it, which the OPG invites (*verified*).
  - **C. Frequency and importance:** every year for the life of the deputyship, with records kept through the year; the guidance warns that an unexplained imbalance may mean the report isn't accepted, and that a deputyship can be reviewed and a deputy removed over a report (*verified*, OPG102A); deputies describe "about 20 hours" and a day's work without balancing, caused by payments entered twice, interest entered twice, wrong opening balances, and missed bank charges (two Alzheimer's Society forum threads, 2024–26, *verified* by the thread); the data is a vulnerable person's bank account.
  - **D. Alternatives:** the OPG's online report (it computes the balance and says how far out it is, with a list of common causes, but not where); the paper form; the deputy's own spreadsheet; the OPG case manager; solicitors' paid lay-deputy services; general budgeting apps (not the report's types); DeputyDB (2012 alpha, source only). No free template or tool in the report's types found (four searches, two here and two by the thread).
  - **E. Strongest incumbent:** the online report plus the deputy's own spreadsheet. The online report is free, official, and enforces the balance; a careful deputy with downloads and Excel can do the job.
  - **F. Labs wedge:** the bank's own downloads read directly, several per account, with overlaps between exports removed exactly (same date, amount, description, and running balance) and look-alikes kept for the deputy to judge; the bank's running balance used to show the very line where an account stops adding up; transfers between the client's accounts paired across accounts; payee rules ("everything from SUNNYSIDE CARE is care fees") so a year is sorted in minutes, every line visible and the deputy deciding; the report's exact types (the online report's 67, from its source code; the paper form's rows mapped from them, the form's own "not listed above" rows taking the rest); gifts and deputy expenses kept out of money out, as the online report asks; nothing uploaded, no account, offline once opened.
