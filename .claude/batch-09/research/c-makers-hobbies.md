### 3. Regularity / TSD rallies
- Motorsport UK NCR 2026: scheduled historic road rallies are assessed by time penalty or a "fails" system, chosen in the Official Documents; at standard sections early arrival costs 1 min per minute early, late 15 s per minute late (*source claim*, https://ebooks.motorsportuk.org/motorsport-uk-ncr-2026-edition-4/0952123001742394230/p468; not read).

- Competitor-side apps exist: Rally Co-Pilot (free, regularity stages, target speed/time), OldTimer Rally Timer (iOS), hardware Rally Time Keeper (*source claim*). No organiser-side free results tool surfaced in one search (*source claim*, weak).

### 1. Club consignment auctions (continued, searches 7-8, fetch 1)

- Stamp clubs: the New Zealand Philatelic Federation's club guide says vendors give numbered lot lists, lots per member may be capped, allow about two lots a minute, and a "Bid Sale Manager" checks descriptions and reserves (*source claim*, https://nzpf.org.nz/resources/club-activities/club-buy-sell/during-club-meetings/; not read). Overlaps Batch 08's silent-auction REJECT and Stall Till (#025) in spirit (*inference*).

### 3. Regularity / TSD rallies (continued, search 6)
- BMW CCA Oktoberfest 2019 TSD rally instructions: 1 point per second early or late against perfect time, max 30 per control, lowest total wins, ties broken from the first control on; the same document mentions an Excel scoring worksheet built years ago by an Ohio rallyist that used lookups for "Perfect Time Adjustments" (*source claim*, https://ofest.bmwcca.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Oktoberfest-2019-Shell-TSD-Rally-General-Instructions-SC-FINAL.pdf; not read). Another club scores 1 point per minute early or late at checkpoints (*source claim*).
- Organiser-side hosted apps exist: Richta GPS checkpoint app (records arrival at about 50 timing controls automatically and shows the score; used at SCCA events), "Competitor" app (*source claim*, motorsportreg.com event listings). Driver-side: TSD Rally Computer, GPS Rally Computer, Rally Co-Pilot (*source claim*).
- **Thread view so far: weak to lead.** A rule creates the job (perfect times from the route's speeds and distances, penalties per control, caps, ties, throw-outs), it's done in spreadsheets, and the finish venue may be offline. But the computation is a results desk, the same family as Comp Desk (#032), events are a few a year per club, and GPS apps are taking over timing (*inference*).

### 10. Handmade toy safety paperwork (UK/EU crafters) (search 12, fetches 2-3)
- A maker's account (*verified* as what the page says, https://thebritishcrafthouse.co.uk/ukca-marking-for-soft-toys/, read 2026-10-14, undated): chemical testing by a lab ("very costly on a small scale") or materials with EN71-3/Oeko-tex certificates; physical tests done by the maker (crushing, clamping, weights, dropping, twisting, a filmed burn test to calculate burn rate, repeated after a cold soak; two samples per model because the burn test ruins one); a label with the UKCA mark, the maker's address and a batch number; a technical file (shape, size, exact materials, construction); a record of each buyer; a signed Declaration of Conformity kept for Trading Standards; everything kept ten years. Awareness among makers described as low.
- UKCA & CE Marking Handmade Toys Collective (*verified*, https://www.cemarking-handmadetoys.co.uk/, read 2026-10-14): a membership community (wiki, bootcamp, discussion groups, member directory); sells EN71-3 test results and a UKCA & CE guide; free intro guide and free entry-level Facebook group; membership price not shown; says "You can self-certify"; names EN71-1, -2, -3, the Toys (Safety) Regulations 2011 (GB) and the Toy Safety Directive (NI/EU); no mention of the new EU Toy Safety Regulation; no free templates or tools mentioned on the homepage.
- Search summary: crafter guides list DoC contents (product ID, maker's details, conformity statement, standards, signature) and traceability items; one guide's claim that UKCA became mandatory from 1 Jan 2023 is out of date (GB later extended CE recognition) (*source claim* plus *inference*: rule drift is a real risk).
- Business Companion's CE/UKCA marking guide (*verified*, https://www.businesscompanion.info/node/1471/printable/print, reviewed December 2025): Schedule 4 to the Toys (Safety) Regulations 2011 lists the technical file contents and Schedule 6 the conformity assessment; a DoC is required for either mark and goes in the technical file; the manufacturer documents conformity assessment including "test reports and risk assessments"; "most products can use either or both" marks (CE continues alongside UKCA). It's the general marking guide, not the toy guide: no DoC content list, no retention period, no template.
- Search 14: no free technical-file or batch-log template found; consultancies (Compliance Gate, EaseCert, EUverify) publish checklists and sell services; one guide says there is "no exact format or template" for toy technical documentation (*source claim*, compliancegate.com). No Etsy or forum complaint thread surfaced in two searches, so friction rests on one maker's article and the existence of a membership collective (*source claim*, weak).

### 10. Handmade toy safety paperwork (continued: the rule, and the general GPSR case; searches 15-16, fetches 5-6)
- **The rule** (*verified*, https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2011/1881/schedule/4, read 2026-10-14): Schedule 4 "Technical documentation (Annex IV to the Directive)", under regulation 17(3), requires (a) a detailed description of design and manufacture, with components, materials and chemical safety data sheets; (b) the safety assessment(s) under regulation 12; (c) the conformity assessment procedure followed; (d) a copy of the declaration of conformity; (e) addresses of places of manufacture and storage; (f) documents sent to an approved body, if any; (g) test reports and how conformity of production was ensured (internal production control, Module A); (h) type-examination items if Module B+C. Inserted 31 Dec 2020 by the EU Exit amendments; a pending change from SI 2026/40 to Schedule 7 is listed as not yet applied (rule drift is live). Retention not stated on that page (the maker's article says ten years; *source claim*).
- **General GPSR (EU 2023/988, all non-food consumer products sold into the EU/NI, including handmade):** Article 9 requires an internal risk analysis and technical documentation before sale (*source claim*, quoted in vendor guides: craftybase.com, erank, 24hour-ar.com, euverify.com, compliancegate.com, easecert.com). Etsy added per-listing and (April 2025) shop-wide responsible-person fields (*source claim*). The Commission's e-Academy slides reportedly include a model technical-documentation template (product identification, characteristics and composition, risk analysis and mitigation) (*source claim*; the Commission URL redirected to sorry.ec.europa.eu, so not read). Free vendor templates and checklists exist (24hour-ar "free risk assessment template", Compliance Gate "free EU GPSR checklist 2026", EaseCert template blog) alongside paid services (EaseCert technical file product, EU responsible-person services) (*source claim*). So the generic GPSR paperwork is crowded with free templates (*inference*); the toy-specific pieces (EN71-1/-2 home test log, batch traceability, Schedule 4 file, DoC) are where no free tool surfaced (*source claim*, negative result from two searches).
- Search 17: Conformance sells a self-certification pack for handmade soft toys, teepees and craft kits with editable paperwork (*source claim*); a BSI preview says BS EN 71-1:2026 supersedes EN 71-1:2014+A1:2018 (*source claim*: the standard itself changed this year, so test limits quoted by makers' guides may be stale); a crafters' Facebook group pooled money to send fabrics for lab tests (*source claim*). Test-house pages describe the torque, tension (seam) and pull tests (*source claim*, centexbel.be). EN 71 text is BSI/CEN copyright, so a tool can't reproduce its limits (*inference*).
- **Folksy forum thread "A different take on the CE question"** (*verified*, https://talk.folksy.com/t/a-different-take-on-the-ce-question/12304, posts 16 Sep to 9 Oct 2016, about nine participants): makers kept written due-diligence records, a technical file and risk assessments for ten years; rules felt unclear and Trading Standards advice varied; one paid for a Conformance pack ("I decided the cost (£25 not that much) was too much for sideline items", Tweedsnipsnbits); "The hardest part was finding fabric that conformed to EN71 standards" (BigBirdLittleBird); a Facebook group pooled £10 each for fabric lab tests. The pain named is materials and testing first, paperwork second.
- **Conformance** (*verified*, https://www.conformance.co.uk/handmade-or-homemade-toys, read 2026-10-14, © 2026): sells a pack for "handmade soft toys, teepees and craft kits" and says other handmade toys "are covered by a general toy pack"; prices and contents aren't on the page; says makers "don't need to notify or register with anyone".

## Leads (thread verdicts, strongest first)

### Lead 1. A handmade toy's safety file (GB, and NI/EU)
- **Audience:** a small handmade toy maker (soft, knitted or crocheted toys, toy bags, wooden toys, craft kits) selling on Etsy, Folksy or at craft fairs in Great Britain, Northern Ireland or the EU.
- **The job:** *Just before:* a new design is finished (or a new batch uses a new fabric lot); the maker has suppliers' EN71-3 / Oeko-tex certificates and has done the home physical tests (torque, seam tension, pull, small parts, drop, a filmed burn test). *Needed after:* a technical file holding the Schedule 4 items (design and manufacture with components and materials; the safety assessment; the conformity procedure, usually internal production control; a copy of the DoC; addresses of manufacture and storage; test reports and how production conformity is ensured), a signed DoC, a label with the mark, name, address and batch number, and a batch and buyer record; produced on request to Trading Standards; kept ten years (*verified* rule for the file's contents; retention *source claim*).
- **Frequency / importance:** per design and per batch; a legal duty with an artifact that must exist when asked for (*verified* rule).
- **Evidence of friction:** rules felt unclear and Trading Standards advice varied; £25 pack too much for sideline items (*verified*, Folksy 2016); lab testing "very costly on a small scale" and low awareness among makers (*verified*, British Craft House maker's article, undated); demand shown by a membership collective and paid packs (*verified*). **Gap in evidence:** no complaint found that the paperwork itself is slow or error-prone; the pain named is materials and testing.
- **Alternatives found:** Conformance packs (soft toys/teepees/craft kits; general toys; price not shown, £25 in 2016 per a forum post) (*verified* existence); UKCA & CE Marking Handmade Toys Collective (membership, wiki, bootcamp, paid guide and EN71-3 test results, free intro guide and Facebook group) (*verified*); consultancies and their checklists (Compliance Gate, EaseCert, EUverify) (*source claim*); Word; Business Companion's general marking guide (*verified*). No free tool or template for the toy file found in three searches (*source claim*, negative).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the Collective plus Conformance's packs: cheap, maker-specific, with community answers and shared material test results.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** a free, private, offline "toy file" per design: materials with certificate references, the maker's own test log (values typed against limits from the maker's own copy of EN 71), a batch register with a label preview, a Schedule 4 checklist that shows what's missing, a DoC assembled from the maker's entries, printed or saved as a file to keep; nothing uploaded.
- **Labs-fit problems:** legal wording (no "compliant" or "safe"); rule drift (EN 71-1:2026 just replaced the 2014 edition, *source claim*; SI 2026/40 amends the Regulations, *verified* listing; the EU's new Toy Safety Regulation not checked); EN 71 limits are copyrighted; GB vs NI/EU vs US (CPSIA, not researched) differ; ten-year keeping needs a saved file, not localStorage.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the binding problem is materials and testing, not the document; a cheap pack plus Word already produces the paperwork, and a stale or wrong field could give false reassurance about a legal duty.
- **Thread verdict:** **lead (narrow).** Worth the main session's checks: the packs' current price and contents, whether Collective members get templates, any evidence that makers lose time or fail checks over the paperwork, and the EU regulation's dates.

### Lead 2. Results desk for a regularity / TSD rally
- **Audience:** the rallymaster or results team of a car club's time-speed-distance or historic road rally (BMW CCA, Porsche Club regions, SCCA RoadRally, Rallysport Ontario; UK clubs under Motorsport UK).
- **The job:** *Just before:* the route is measured and written as instructions with distances, average-speed changes and pauses; cars hand in time cards (or checkpoint crews hand in sheets). *Needed after:* perfect times at each control, penalties under the event's own rules (per second or per minute, early vs late, caps per control, a fixed penalty for a missed control, throw-outs and perfect-time adjustments), ties broken at the first differing control, results posted at the finish venue.
- **Frequency / importance:** a few events a year per club, results the same evening (*inference*); the rules are written per event, under national rulebooks (*source claim*).
- **Evidence of friction:** "time consuming scoring at the finish" (*source claim*, PCA SDR summary; page 404); a rallymaster's Excel worksheet cut the scoring crews' workload (*source claim*, BMW CCA 2019).
- **Alternatives found:** Richta suite (commercial, GPS, licensing unknown); private Excel worksheets; driver-side apps (Rally Co-Pilot, OldTimer Rally Timer, TSD Rally Computer); professional timing for big European classics (*all source claim*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** Richta, which removes manned checkpoints and finish scoring altogether.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** a free, offline results desk: route legs in, perfect times computed, time cards typed in, configurable penalty rules, tie-breaks, a printed results sheet.
- **Labs-fit problems:** none structural; the penalty model must be configurable and perfect-time arithmetic must be right.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** GPS apps are replacing the manual workflow; low frequency per organiser; it's a results desk like Comp Desk (#032); every piece of evidence here is a search summary.
- **Thread verdict:** **weak** (closest to a lead after Lead 1).

### Lead 3. Settling a club's consignment auction (stamp, coin, horticultural societies)
- **Audience:** a society's auction or bid-sale manager. **Job:** members' numbered lot lists and reserves before; after, buyers' totals and each vendor's payout less the club's commission (*source claim*, NZPF club guide; APS column; Kawartha and ISWSC rules).
- **Strongest incumbent:** auction.fish, free and open source, in-person and online, bidder numbers, automatic invoices, Square or cash (*verified*). Silent auctions were already rejected in Batch 08; Stall Till (#025) is nearby.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** a free purpose-built incumbent exists, and no settlement complaint surfaced.
- **Thread verdict:** **weak.**
