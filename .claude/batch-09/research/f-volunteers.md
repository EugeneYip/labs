## Leads (strongest first)

### 1. The LOSAP year-end points list (volunteer fire companies and rescue squads, NY first)
- **Audience:** the LOSAP coordinator or secretary of a volunteer fire company or rescue squad (a volunteer), who turns a year of attendance into each member's points. About 600 NY sponsors (*verified*, F4); NJ, MD and VA counties run their own point systems (*source claim*, S2, S8).
- **The job:** before: a calendar year of sign-in sheets (drills, meetings, stand-bys), training course hours, the call log (company total and each member's responses, fire and ambulance separately), officer terms. After: each member's points by category, capped (NY: drills 20, meetings 20, training 25, responses 25 for meeting the volume-scaled share, etc., *verified*, F1), the 50-point year-of-service flag, then the list certified under oath to the sponsor by 31 March (NY, *source claim*, F8), approved and posted; members left off may appeal within 30 days of posting (*verified*, F1). NJ: 30-day sponsor review, resolution, 30-day posting (*source claim*, S8).
- **Frequency / importance:** yearly, tracked monthly (Plainsboro's monthly LOSAP report, *source claim*); it decides each volunteer's service-award credit (a pension-like benefit), so errors cost volunteers or the public money; audited by the State Comptroller (*verified*, F4).
- **Evidence of friction:** auditors', not users': the 2015 audit of 10 districts found caps exceeded, short drills counted, undetected software errors, first-year eligibility missed, and earned points never compared with the approved list (*verified*, F4); Jamesport: 322 points unsupported across 27 members; Seaford: none of 23 members' points accurate (*source claim*, S1); a NY administrator stresses calendar-year tracking and review because manual entry introduces errors (*source claim*, F5). No forum complaint read.
- **Alternatives found:** paid fire RMS and scheduling with LOSAP modules (Emergency Reporting, First Due, Fire Rescue Systems, one listing USD 5,000, FirePlan/Fireshield), third-party LOSAP administrators (Penflex, Firefly Admin, McNeil) (*source claim*, S2, S8); no free tool or official worksheet found in the two searches that looked (S2, S8) (*source claim*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the department's RMS where it has one (Emergency Reporting, First Due); otherwise spreadsheets and paper (*inference*).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** a page that starts from the sponsor's point schedule (NY GML 217 categories, caps, the two-hour drill minimum and the response-share rule prefilled; editable for a local 100-point scheme or a NJ/MD/VA chart), opens the year's activity log (CSV: date, member, activity, hours) and call counts, and gives each member's capped points by category, the 50-point flag, warnings (over-cap entries ignored, drills under two hours, dates outside the calendar year, duplicate sign-ins, members near 50), and a printable list for certification and posting. Members' names stay in the browser. Nothing like it in the portfolio (*inference*).
- **Labs-fit problems:** point systems differ by sponsor, so the schedule must be editable without becoming a rules engine; much of the input is paper; the response rule needs each member's responses and the company total; what counts as a drill or stand-by is the sponsor's call; US-only.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the evidence is auditors' findings, not coordinators asking for help, and departments with an RMS already get the list; the remaining audience may keep points on paper and still face the data entry (*inference*).
- **Thread verdict:** lead.

### 2. A rescue's intake and outcome statistics from its own animal spreadsheet (Virginia ACR, Colorado PACFA, SAC)
- **Audience:** the volunteer director or "data person" of a small, often foster-based rescue: required in Virginia (all releasing agencies, *verified*, F2) and Colorado (*source claim*, S10); voluntary monthly reporting to Shelter Animals Count anywhere (*source claim*, S3).
- **The job:** before: a spreadsheet with one row per animal (species, age or birth date, intake date and type, foster periods, outcome date and type). After: the counts in each body's categories: Virginia's yearly summary (intake by species and category including stray, seized, bite case, quarantine, owner surrender, transfers in from Virginia and out of state; dispositions; on hand 1 January and 31 December; foster animals still in custody, *source claim*, S4) typed into the PIN-protected state system in January (*verified*, F2); Colorado's yearly form (start in shelter and in foster, intakes, outcomes, end, juveniles up to 120 days, a start + in = out + end check, *source claim*, S10); SAC's monthly fixed-column CSV (juveniles up to 5 months; beginning and ending counts at month boundaries, *source claim*, S3, F9).
- **Frequency / importance:** yearly (state) or monthly (SAC); a legal filing in Virginia, with the underlying custody records open to public inspection for five years (*source claim*, S4); published figures are scrutinised by outsiders (NAIA's Shelter Project, *source claim*, S10).
- **Evidence of friction:** thin and indirect: a July 2026 Maddie's Fund thread (a student building a free Sheets toolkit for small rescues; a rescue founder: "Any tool that needs weekly hand-entry dies by month three", *verified*, F3); VDACS ships an FAQ and tutorial (*verified* that it exists, F2); Colorado's form carries a balance check (*source claim*). No first-hand complaint about compiling the counts found.
- **Alternatives found:** paid rescue software with SAC or Asilomar exports (Pawlytics, Rescue Workflow, *source claim*); a trial-based Ragic template; the state portals take totals only, with no worksheet (*verified* for Virginia). Shelterluv, PetPoint, Petstablished, RescueGroups and ASM were not checked (search cap): they are the likely strongest incumbents, and any free tier with these exports would weaken the lead (*inference*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** rescue management software's built-in SAC report (unknown which free tiers include it).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** open the rescue's own CSV, map its columns once (remembered in the browser), pick a report (Virginia year, Colorado year, SAC month), and get the counts with the balance check, a list of animals that don't fit (no outcome type, outcome before intake, missing age, adoption returns not entered as new intakes, animals still open), and the SAC upload file. Adopters' and fosters' details never leave the device. Same "records in, official summary out, with a balance check" shape as #034 (*inference*).
- **Labs-fit problems:** three definition sets (age cut-offs, category names) to keep right; messy free-form spreadsheets; foster counts need foster dates the sheet may not hold; the SAC CSV must match SAC's column template exactly (not read).
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the stated pain is keeping the spreadsheet up, not counting from it, and rescues on software already have the report.
- **Thread verdict:** lead, behind 1; first check free tiers of Petstablished, RescueGroups and PetPoint, the SAC template, and Virginia's form categories (acr-sample.pdf).

### 3. A food pantry's monthly service report (US partner agencies)
- **Audience:** a volunteer pantry coordinator reporting to a regional food bank.
- **The job:** before: the month's sign-in sheets and intake forms. After: days open, unduplicated households, total (repeat) households, new households (from the fiscal year start at some food banks), individuals by age band (duplicated at some food banks, once a month at others), unknown ages; due the 1st to the 9th through the food bank's portal (Agency Express at NTFB, *verified*, F6); TEFAP's own monthly report (*source claim*, S6).
- **Frequency / importance:** monthly; ties to food allocations and TEFAP compliance (*inference*).
- **Evidence of friction:** none first-hand; only that definitions differ between food banks (*verified* NTFB, *source claim* others).
- **Alternatives found:** the food bank's portal (Agency Express); client-intake systems food banks provide (Service Insights on MealConnect, Link2Feed, Oasis Insight, PantryTrak) not checked (*inference*).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** a one-device check-in list kept at the pantry that produces the month's numbers in the food bank's definitions; clients' details stay local.
- **Labs-fit problems:** several intake tables mean several devices (shared state); paper is the norm; food banks often mandate their own system.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the cost is data entry from paper, which a page doesn't remove.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.
