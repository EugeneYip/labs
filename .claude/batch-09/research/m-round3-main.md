# Round 3, the main session's own screening (2026-10-14, while #035's audit ran)

Searches by the main session, a few per area, to choose where bounded threads go. Read-only.

- **Plate layouts for lab work (96/384 wells, randomised, exported):** served free already: ConductScience's Plate Map Designer runs in the browser, exports CSV, and keeps data on the device (*source claim*, search result); Well Plate Maker (Bioconductor, R with a graphical interface, constraint-based placement, *source claim*); SAPL (Bath, an R Shiny tool for spatially balanced layouts, *source claim*); UWPR's Octopus (a browser app for randomised, balanced mass-spectrometry batches, local processing, *source claim*). **Reject:** the job is done free, in the browser, by several tools.
- **Labels for food prepacked for direct sale (Natasha's Law, UK, since October 2021):** the name and the full ingredients list with the 14 allergens emphasised, compound ingredients broken down in brackets; it applies to home bakers selling from home, market stalls, cafés (FSA's guidance for bakers and for mobile sellers, gov.uk, *source claim*). Tools: paid (Planglow's LabelLogic Live, a subscription with its labels; Nutritics bundles; Kafoodle; Gen-Label via Bunzl; foodlabelmaker.com), a wholesaler's 3-month trial for account customers (Thomas Ridley, 2021), and free static templates (Avery's, a Word template, Canva) that leave the ordering and emphasis to the baker (*source claim*). No forum evidence of the friction surfaced in two searches. **A lead for a thread:** the job (recipes from suppliers' labels, ingredients in descending weight, compound ingredients nested, allergens carried through and emphasised, printed on label sheets) fits a static page; against it, a missed allergen harms someone, so the page could only format what the baker marks, never detect allergens on its own, and the need is unevidenced.
- **Drivers' hours for minibus and community-transport drivers (GB domestic rules):** the rules are short (at most 10 hours' driving and 11 hours' duty a day, 10 hours' rest between working days or 8.5 up to three times a week, 24 hours off every two weeks; a light-use exemption under 4 hours a day; gov.uk, *source claim*), drivers keep a record book, and digital logs cost about £1 a driver a week after a free month (CheckedSafe, *source claim*). The assimilated (EU) rules are harder, but their vehicles have tachographs whose data goes to analysis software operators already use. **Weak:** a checker adds little to simple rules, and the hard case belongs to tachograph software.
- **Weeding a library's collection by the CREW method:** CREW (Texas State Library and Archives Commission, 2012) gives each Dewey range a formula such as 10/5/MUSTIE (years since the latest copyright, years without use, then judgment: Misleading, Ugly, Superseded, Trivial, Irrelevant, Elsewhere) (a library system's cheat sheet citing the manual's pages 47–48, *source claim*). The data comes from the library system's reports: Evergreen's weeding list by call-number range with last checkout dates, Symphony's spreadsheet output with a last-checkout field, OCLC WorldShare's circulation and publication-date reports joined by barcode, Clarivate's Decision Center low-circulation report (*source claim*, the vendors' and consortia's guides). Free tools: a 2014 Excel "Collection Manager" from an Oregon mailing list; no free tool found that applies the CREW formulas by Dewey range (*source claim*). **A lead for a thread:** before, a report export and a spreadsheet filtered by hand per section; after, the candidates per section by its formula, the collection's age by section, and a list to take to the shelves. Against: the library systems' own reports (and paid analytics) may do it, the formulas are a starting point for judgment, and the job sits near the school cluster (school librarians are a large part of the audience).
- **Clustering DNA matches by the Leeds method (genealogy):** done by hand in a spreadsheet: the closer matches listed with their shared centimorgans, each one's shared matches coloured into groups that point to the four grandparents' lines; "several hours" by hand (an Ontario Genealogical Society talk, January 2025, *source claim*). Automated clustering is paid (Genetic Affairs' AutoCluster, DNAGedcom, the Collins-Leeds tool through DNAGedcom's client) and, for those, AncestryDNA matches aren't supported; MyHeritage and FamilyTreeDNA export match lists as CSV; GEDmatch's free tools don't cluster; DNA Painter formats clusters made elsewhere; AncestryDNA, the largest database, has no export and no segment data, and the old free match-downloader extension is reported broken (2022) (*source claim*, search results). **A lead for a thread:** before, hours of copying and colouring; after, pasted match and shared-match lists grouped into clusters with a printable chart. Against: Ancestry's own paid "Pro Tools" may now cluster (unchecked), the data entry is still manual for Ancestry users, and other people's DNA match lists are personal data (kept on the device).
  - Follow-up: AncestryDNA added "Matches by Cluster" to its Pro Tools add-on (about $10 a month on top of a membership) on 1 July 2025, in beta and rolled out through December 2025, with custom clusters announced (Ancestry's help pages and genealogy societies' handouts, *source claim*). **Weak:** the largest database's users now have a cluster view from Ancestry itself, paid; what's left is a free version of a feature Ancestry sells, with every match typed or pasted by hand.
