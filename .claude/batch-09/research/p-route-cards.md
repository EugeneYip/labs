# Thread P: route cards for walking expeditions (DofE, Scouts, schools, walking groups), 2026-10-14; budget 10 web searches and 6 page reads

Read-only; nobody contacted, nothing downloaded. Tags: *verified* (page read), *source claim* (search snippet or secondary source), *inference*.

## Notes as the thread went

- Started. Questions: (1) what DofE and Scouts require on a route card; (2) how it's done today and where the friction is; (3) whether a static page can do it from a GPX (grid references, bearings, distance, height gain); (4) the strongest free incumbent and the strongest reason not to build.
- Search 1 (DofE route card form and columns): no single official DofE download page surfaced. DofE's own site hosts a filled-in "Land exercise route card" PDF (dofe.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Land-exercise-route-card.pdf, an example dated 05/05/2009) (*source claim*). Schools host editable PDF templates: Magdalen Court School's "Expedition Route Card Master Form" (one card per day; aim, group name, contacts, day/date, team names), Catmose College's "Expedition Route Card" with lettered columns (a)–(k), a "Setting out time" box, and a note that journeying must total at least half the minimum hours of planned activity (*source claim*). The columns, roughly: leg; place with grid reference; general direction or bearing; distance (km); height climbed (m); time allowed for journeying; time for exploring, rests or meals; total time for leg; ETA; brief route details (full details on the reverse); escape/notes (*source claim*). The form is a PDF, filled by hand or as a form.
- Search 1, the important lead: the search summary says the route card tool is built into **eDofE Mapping**, which gives access to OS maps (*source claim*, to check: eDofE is the DofE's own free online record system every participant has, so a built-in route card tool would be the incumbent).
- Search 2 (eDofE Mapping): DofE and Ordnance Survey made OS mapping, "through the OS Maps application", available for expedition planning in eDofE (OS news item "young people dofe get mapping boost os", ordnancesurvey.co.uk/news/..., *source claim*); for participants it sits under their Expedition section, with a user guide and FAQs (dofe.org/run/delivering-dofe/edofe/, *source claim*). Training materials say participants have OS maps for the whole UK "and a Route Card tool through eDofE Mapping" (a school's Expedition Training Framework; a DofE canoe course form, *source claim*). Cost to participants not stated in the snippets (to check). The Scouts' "mapping your adventure" page points to OS Maps' 7-day free trial for online route planning (scouts.org.uk/activities/mapping-your-adventure/, *source claim*).
- Read 1, OS news, 16 June 2022 (ordnancesurvey.co.uk/news/young-people-dofe-get-mapping-boost-os, *verified*): OS mapping from the OS Maps application is "available directly when logging into the eDofE web platform", Great Britain and Northern Ireland; young people plot their own routes and share them with fellow participants, Leaders and Assessors; standard and premium topographic mapping; import and export routes; unlimited printing up to A3. Aimed at the "hundreds of thousands of young people" doing DofE each year. The item doesn't mention a route card tool or the cost (*verified*: absent from the page). *Inference:* since mid-2022 every DofE participant has OS mapping, with GPX import and export, inside the DofE's own system.
- Search 3 (eDofE Mapping's route card tool, the user guide): nothing found that documents it; still only the training materials' claim from search 2 (*source claim*, unconfirmed). Other templates surfaced: the English School (Cyprus) "Adventurous Journey Route Card" with the same leg-by-leg columns and a supervisor contact field; Girlguiding's walking scheme route card (v1.1, November 2023) with a "Sunset" field, an escape-route column, and a separate bad-weather alternative plan; a Yorkshire walking group's "Route Cards Design and Use v2" (scrca.foscl.org.uk): a route usually broken into 5 to 30 legs (*source claim*). *Inference:* the format is shared across DofE, Girlguiding, Scouts and walking groups, with small differences (sunset, bad-weather plan, lettered columns).
