DofE's own site hosts a filled-in "Land exercise route card" PDF (dofe.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Land-exercise-route-card.pdf, an example dated 05/05/2009) (*source claim*). Schools host editable PDF templates: Magdalen Court School's "Expedition Route Card Master Form" (one card per day; aim, group name, contacts, day/date, team names), Catmose College's "Expedition Route Card" with lettered columns (a)–(k), a "Setting out time" box, and a note that journeying must total at least half the minimum hours of planned activity (*source claim*). The columns, roughly: leg; place with grid reference; general direction or bearing; distance (km); height climbed (m); time allowed for journeying; time for exploring, rests or meals; total time for leg; ETA; brief route details (full details on the reverse); escape/notes (*source claim*). The form is a PDF, filled by hand or as a form.
- Search 1, the important lead: the search summary says the route card tool is built into **eDofE Mapping**, which gives access to OS maps (*source claim*, to check: eDofE is the DofE's own free online record system every participant has, so a built-in route card tool would be the incumbent).
- Search 2 (eDofE Mapping): DofE and Ordnance Survey made OS mapping, "through the OS Maps application", available for expedition planning in eDofE (OS news item "young people dofe get mapping boost os", ordnancesurvey.co.uk/news/..., *source claim*); for participants it sits under their Expedition section, with a user guide and FAQs (dofe.org/run/delivering-dofe/edofe/, *source claim*). Training materials say participants have OS maps for the whole UK "and a Route Card tool through eDofE Mapping" (a school's Expedition Training Framework; a DofE canoe course form, *source claim*). Cost to participants not stated in the snippets (to check). Aimed at the "hundreds of thousands of young people" doing DofE each year. The item doesn't mention a route card tool or the cost (*verified*: absent from the page). *Inference:* since mid-2022 every DofE participant has OS mapping, with GPX import and export, inside the DofE's own system.
- Search 3 (eDofE Mapping's route card tool, the user guide): nothing found that documents it; still only the training materials' claim from search 2 (*source claim*, unconfirmed). Other templates surfaced: the English School (Cyprus) "Adventurous Journey Route Card" with the same leg-by-leg columns and a supervisor contact field; Girlguiding's walking scheme route card (v1.1, November 2023) with a "Sunset" field, an escape-route column, and a separate bad-weather alternative plan; a Yorkshire walking group's "Route Cards Design and Use v2" (scrca.foscl.org.uk): a route usually broken into 5 to 30 legs (*source claim*). *Inference:* the format is shared across DofE, Girlguiding, Scouts and walking groups, with small differences (sunset, bad-weather plan, lettered columns).

The 20 conditions' wording didn't surface. *Inference:* a route card is a hard deliverable for every qualifying DofE expedition, checked by the Assessor weeks ahead, and often revised after feedback, so it's made more than once per expedition.

What they say: the participant plots the route with checkpoints in **OS Explore** (OS's mapping, reached through eDofE), clicks "Get new maps" in eDofE, which pulls the maps over "and create[s] your route card"; adding or deleting checkpoints in OS Explore needs a route card refresh, and info already entered is kept unless its checkpoint was deleted; the route card can only be edited in eDofE, and the expedition level must be chosen before saving; "Download PDF" gives the route card as a PDF to save or print; the map prints with checkpoints ("Add route information to the print", "Generate PDF"); "Export GPX" after saving (on a Mac the file arrives as .GPX.XML) (*source claim*). *Inference:* every DofE participant (they all have eDofE accounts through their centre's enrolment) already has a free-at-use, OS-mapped route card generator whose output goes straight to the leader; which columns it fills itself (grid references, distances, height, timings) isn't confirmed by these snippets.

## Closed by the main session (2026-10-14)

- **The job (before → after):** before, a route card per expedition day, leg by leg (grid references, bearing, distance, height, timings, escape routes), handed to the supervisor or assessor weeks ahead and revised after feedback; after, the card filled from a route drawn on a map.
- **Alternatives found:** for DofE participants, **eDofE Mapping**: OS mapping inside the DofE's own system since June 2022 (*verified*, OS news and the DofE's eDofE page), with help articles on creating, editing, and printing a route card as a PDF and importing and exporting GPX (*source claim*, the help centre's titles and the search's summary; the pages refuse the fetcher); for Scouts, OS Maps (its free trial pointed to by the Scouts' own page, *source claim*); for everyone, the shared PDF templates (DofE, schools, Girlguiding, walking groups).
- **Strongest incumbent:** eDofE Mapping's route card tool, free to every DofE participant through their centre, on OS maps, with the card going straight to the leader.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the main audience already has a route card generator inside the system their leaders check, and route cards are taught and signed off as a navigation exercise; no evidence of friction surfaced (no forum threads, no time estimates).
