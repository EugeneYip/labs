Searches 1-4 (0 fetches): museum environmental monitoring (temperature and RH logger data)
- S1 (*source claim*, via search): a conservator on the Conservation DistList said logger suppliers' software "tends to be linear recording rather than providing true analysis" (cool.culturalheritage.org/byform/mailing-lists/cdl/2010/0992.html); others copy the data into Excel (same list); Duke's preservation blog describes a spreadsheet template with min/max/median for lux and temperature and a monthly download routine (blogs.library.duke.edu/preservation/tag/environmental-readings); a Drexel thesis says logger data is "typically converted from an excel spreadsheet to a basic line graph"; South East Museums' advice sheet (southeastmuseums.org, Monitoring-temperature-and-humidity.pdf) covers why and how to monitor. Gemini (Tinytag maker) pitches volunteer-run sites. No published "percent of time in range" method or volunteer template surfaced.
- S2 (*source claim*, IPI store and pricing PDFs via search): IPI's eClimateNotebook (eCNB) is the field's analysis service: CSV upload from most logger brands, account on eclimatenotebook.com; store listings show Basic USD 120 a year (5 users, 10 location datasets), Basic Plus 300, Professional 600, Professional Plus 900; relaunched February 2026 with a new pricing model and an Enterprise tier (rit.edu/ipi ... ecnb_pricing_comparison_2026_v5.pdf, not read); a 2012 description mentioned a free level; whether one survives in 2026 is unconfirmed. Onset (HOBO) partners with it.
- S3 (*source claim*, AIC Sustainability Committee blog on the 2023 Bizot refresh, community.culturalheritage.org): Bizot Green Protocol 2023 guideline: stable RH 40-60%, stable temperature 16-25 C, "fluctuations of no more than +/-10% RH per 24 hours within this range"; the 2015 version was 45-55% RH with +/-5% drift; AIM hosts the refreshed protocol (aim-museums.co.uk). No rule found that borrowers must send 12 months of data (lender-specific, *inference*).
- S4 (*source claim*, via search): TWPI (IPI's Time-Weighted Preservation Index) is offered in Conserv's paid cloud product (support.conserv.io/knowledge/understanding-twpi; Conserv recommends a year of data); the R package ConSciR computes dew point, mould risk and preservation index from logger spreadsheets (CRAN README; free but needs R); IPI's dew point calculator (dpcalc.org) does one reading at a time; Historic New England's guide still describes IPI's old Climate Notebook desktop tools. No free browser tool that takes a logger CSV and reports against Bizot surfaced.

Searches 5-8 (0 fetches): transcription files, weeding, archive catalogues, war memorials
- S5 FreeREG (*source claim*, freereg.org.uk transcriber pages via search): WinREG (Windows only) is "the best choice" and adds headers for you; Mac and Linux users are told to use a spreadsheet; spreadsheet users type the header block themselves; the new flexible format (# in A5, DEF in B5) is not available in WinREG at all, so it needs a spreadsheet; field names must match exactly ("variations are currently not allowed"); row 6 holds the field names; chapman_code mandatory, three upper-case letters; dates dd Mon yyyy; about 2,000 records per file, one place, church and register type per batch; if no "File Processed" email arrives, ask the coordinator. - S6 CREW weeding (*source claim*, SLJ, INFOhio, California DoE brochure, Shortgrass cheat sheet via search): CREW formula per Dewey class (e.g. 10/5/MUSTIE: years since copyright / years since last circulation / MUSTIE judgements); sources differ on "and" vs "or"; Follett Destiny has weeding reports and logs; Evergreen's reporter can find candidates; no ready CREW spreadsheet template surfaced. Not pursued further (*inference*: the big US school systems already report this; the judgement part is human).
- S7 archive catalogues (*source claim*, via search): Norfolk Archives' community-archives guidance says only 6 compulsory ISAD(G) fields are needed, information at a higher level applies to lower levels, reference codes like HS/1/1/2 (collection/series/file/item) (archives.norfolk.gov.uk/article/30984/Standard-descriptions); Gloucestershire Archives asks owners to list records before deposit "using a standard template we supply" (collections information policy PDF); a Staffordshire report says a trained depositor catalogued 140 files in a spreadsheet, which staff edited and imported, and that this is time-consuming for staff (moderngov.staffordshire.gov.uk, Item 8 Volunteers); West Sussex and Glamorgan volunteers box-list in Excel; AtoM publishes an ISAD(G) CSV import template (accesstomemory.org docs). No first-hand account of specific errors found yet.
- S8 war memorials (*source claim*, Great War Forum threads, Leatherhead and Oxford college projects via search): names fail to match CWGC through spelling variants, eligibility (died after discharge, after cut-off), and common names needing context such as next-of-kin address; one college found 42 names missing from its list. Matching is contextual research, not a mechanical join (*inference*): weak.

Searches 9-12 (0 fetches): condition reports, oral history summaries, audits, copyright
- S9 condition reports (*source claim*, App Store listings and vendor pages via search): Articheck (iPad, free download, about 200 paying organisations; offline; annotate photos; one review says it "crashed constantly"); Horus Condition Report (iOS; first report free, then Basic or Advanced versions; annotation, preset damage terms; Basic 1-6 photos; Pro for loans and exhibitions); free Word templates from the Joan Mitchell Foundation, Smithsonian TBMA, Te Papa PDF form; a 2010 open-source condition report project on the DistList. - S10 oral history summaries (*source claim*, via search): a timed summary (one line per 1-5 minute segment with a timecode such as [05:34]) is the accepted substitute for a transcript (Oral History Victoria module 3; OHA "Index (see also time log)"; Baylor guide); Norfolk Archives: verbatim transcription takes about 6 hours per recorded hour, a summary is often enough, and its example marks muted sensitive passages (archives.norfolk.gov.uk/article/31021/Summaries-and-transcripts); Reading Museum recruited volunteer "Oral History Summarisers" who write subheadings and summaries attached to recordings (readingmuseum.org.uk PDF); Leicester's guide lists consent/copyright form, deposit form, and summary or transcript as the three documents. OHMS (Nunn Center) indexes segments (title, synopsis, keywords, subjects) but is a hosted service with an account and online media; oTranscribe (free, in the browser, local audio, timestamps, autosave in the browser) already covers typing a timed log (*inference*, from prior knowledge, not re-checked in this thread).
- S11 audits (*source claim*, Collections Trust Spectrum pages via search): Spectrum separates Inventory (listing room by room, shelf by shelf, with location hierarchy) from Audit (do the records match the objects; individual, sample, or whole-location checks; findings into a written action plan; re-audit after fixing a faulty procedure) (collectionstrust.org.uk/resource/audit-suggested-procedure, inventory-suggested-procedure); Museum Development North runs collections audit training; Collector Systems describes a shelf with five objects instead of six. Barcodes or phone workflows not covered.
- S12 copyright of unpublished works (*source claim*, via search): the 2039 rule (unpublished works of known authors who died before 1969, created before 1 August 1989, except pre-June 1957 photographs) still stands after the 2014 consultation; The National Archives publishes Crown and non-Crown duration flowcharts; the Community Archives and Heritage Group (CAHG) has an accessible flowchart and a "Community Archives Copyright handbook" (communityarchives.org.uk, June 2026 upload); Collections Trust links both. Official, free flowcharts exist, and the stakes are legal (*inference*): reject as a stand-alone; a batch rights check of a digitisation list is possible but data such as death dates is rarely in the list.

## Leads (strongest first)

### 1. Climate report from logger files (temperature and RH, optionally light)
- **Audience:** the collections volunteer or lone curator of a small museum, historic house, or community archive who downloads one or more temperature and RH loggers each month and has to show trustees, an Accreditation return, a lender's facility report, or the GIS Environmental Adviser what the conditions were.
- **The job:** before: an export from the logger's own software (CSV or XLSX from Tinytag, HOBO, Lascar EasyLog, Hanwell, Elitech, or a phone app for Bluetooth loggers). After: the share of readings inside the target band, the 24-hour RH swings that broke the limit, monthly minimum, maximum and mean, a graph with the band shaded, and a one-page summary to file or send; optionally dew point, mould risk, a preservation index, and light dose (lux hours against an annual budget) (*inference* on the exact contents).
- **Frequency / importance:** monthly downloads, yearly reporting, and every incoming loan; the Bizot Green Protocol 2023 guideline is stable RH 40-60%, temperature 16-25 C, RH "fluctuations of no more than +/-10% RH per 24 hours" (*source claim*, AIC Sustainability Committee blog and NEMO, CIMAM news; primary PDF at the NMDC not read); lenders ask for environmental data in the facility report (*source claim*); GIS borrowers must manage environmental conditions, assessed by ACE's Environmental Adviser (*source claim*).
- **Evidence of friction:** logger software "tends to be linear recording rather than providing true analysis" (*source claim*, Conservation DistList 2010); logger data "typically converted from an excel spreadsheet to a basic line graph" (*source claim*, Drexel thesis); Duke's and a DistList conservator's home-made spreadsheets (*source claim*); MANL lends loggers and supplies "a comprehensive graph" of the results, and Conserv's training addresses those who feel monitoring is "too technical" (*source claim*), so analysis is the part small sites need help with (*inference*). No recent first-hand complaint found.
- **Alternatives found:** Conserv Essentials, free, cloud, log-in, "Import logger files up to 1MB", "Standard graphs & reports"; preservation metrics only on Pro at "$95 /month" (*verified*, conserv.io/pricing); IPI eClimateNotebook, subscription with account and CSV upload, Basic USD 120 a year in the store, about USD 180 under the February 2026 pricing (*source claim*); the heritage Climate Toolbox Excel/VBA workbook with in-range and RH-swing proportions (*source claim*; availability unknown, and VBA needs desktop Excel with macros allowed, *inference*); ConSciR (free R package: dew point, mould risk, preservation index; *source claim*); vendor software (graphs, alarms; *source claim*); IPI's dpcalc.org (one reading at a time).
- **Strongest incumbent:** Conserv Essentials for graphs, eClimateNotebook for preservation metrics; the Climate Toolbox if it turns out to be freely downloadable.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** open any logger's export on your own device (desktop or phone), pick a guideline (Bizot 2023, Bizot 2015's 45-55% +/-5, a lender's band, or your own), and get time in band, every breach of the 24-hour swing limit, a monthly table and a printable page; no account, no macros, no upload, several loggers side by side.
- **Labs-fit problems:** export formats vary widely (preamble lines, date formats and time zones, daylight saving gaps, C/F, decimal commas, XLSX); the 24-hour rule is read differently (+/-10 around a running mean or a 10-point max-min range, *inference*), so the tool must say which; preservation metrics must follow IPI's published method closely enough to be trusted; XLSX support needs a dependency.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** free graphs already exist (Conserv Essentials, vendor software), the Climate Toolbox may already do the in-range and swing analysis, and the friction evidence is old or inferred from help schemes rather than recent first-hand accounts.
- **Thread verdict:** lead. Checks for the main session: the Climate Toolbox's availability and licence; eCNB's 2026 entry price; whether Tinytag Explorer, EasyLog or HOBOware already report percent in range; the Bizot 2023 wording in the NMDC PDF.

### 2. Oral history interview log and deposit sheet
- **Audience:** the volunteer summariser or interviewer in a community oral history project (often lottery-funded) preparing each interview for deposit with a record office, museum or library.
- **The job:** before: the recording (WAV or MP3) and the signed recording agreement, with any closures. After: a timed summary (one line per segment with a timecode such as [05:34], names, places, subjects), each closed passage marked with start and end times, a reason and an end date, and a cover sheet with the interview's technical details and closures, deposited with the recording (*source claim*: Norfolk guidance, Newcastle policy).
- **Frequency / importance:** every interview, typically tens per project (*inference*); a summary replaces a transcript that takes about 6 hours per recorded hour (*source claim*, Norfolk); a missed closure exposes the archive and the interviewee (*inference*).
- **Evidence of friction:** Reading Museum recruited volunteer "Oral History Summarisers" (*source claim*); Norfolk's example marks muted sensitive passages (*source claim*, page 403); OHS: closures need "clear end-dates" and must be "realistically enforceable" (*verified*); Newcastle asks for closure details per interview on a cover sheet (*source claim*). No complaint about the summarising tools themselves found.
- **Alternatives found:** oTranscribe (*verified*: free, MIT, local audio, Ctrl+J timestamps, autosave, Markdown/text export, desktop only, no segments, keywords or closures); OHMS (Nunn Center) indexes segments with keywords but is a hosted service with an account and online media (*source claim* and *inference*); Word templates and archive cover sheets.
- **Strongest incumbent:** oTranscribe plus a Word template.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** play the file locally and stamp segments as you listen; closures as timed ranges with end dates and reasons; a deposit sheet generated with duration and format read from the file; a project-wide index of names and places; Word-friendly and CSV output; works on a phone for review; nothing uploaded (unpublished personal stories and closed passages).
- **Labs-fit problems:** fine technically (local playback, localStorage for the log); typing summaries on a phone while listening is awkward; each archive's cover sheet differs.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** oTranscribe already does the core (timestamps, local, free) and archives accept plain Word summaries, so the added structure may save little; adjacent to Labs' Family Stories and Transcript Pseudonyms.
- **Thread verdict:** lead, weaker than 1.

### 3. Catalogue check before deposit (box lists to ISAD(G))
- **Audience:** the volunteer cataloguer of a community, church, club or company archive who lists records in a spreadsheet before depositing them with a county record office.
- **The job:** before: rows with reference codes (HS/1/1/2), titles, dates, extent and level. After: a list the record office can import into its catalogue with little editing, and a printed handlist.
- **Frequency / importance:** usually one deposit per group, sometimes years of listing (*inference*); record offices ask depositors to list on their template (*source claim*, Gloucestershire).
- **Evidence of friction:** Staffordshire staff edit and import depositors' spreadsheets, which is time-consuming for them (*source claim*); volunteers box-list in Excel at West Sussex and for Glamorgan Archives (*source claim*); Norfolk teaches 6 compulsory ISAD(G) fields and inheritance between levels (*source claim*, page 403). No account of specific errors found.
- **Alternatives found:** record offices' Excel templates; AtoM's ISAD(G) CSV import template (needs an AtoM server); CALM, Epexio (record offices' paid systems). No free stand-alone checker surfaced (not searched for directly).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the record office's template and its staff.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** open the spreadsheet and check that every reference has a parent, levels match depth, references aren't repeated, required fields are filled where ISAD(G) needs them, and child dates fall within parent dates; show the catalogue as an indented tree to print; export clean CSV (AtoM-style parent links, *inference*).
- **Labs-fit problems:** XLSX needs a dependency; templates differ by record office.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** mostly one-off per group and the record office checks anyway; the friction is staff-side and indirect.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

### 4. Graveyard survey recorder (monumental inscriptions)
- **Audience:** a family or local history society team recording a churchyard's memorials.
- **The job:** before: a numbered plan (section, row, stone) and paper forms; after: a typed list of inscriptions with condition notes and photo references, and a surname index, often published as a booklet (*source claim* on forms and printed surveys; *inference* on booklets).
- **Evidence of friction:** paper forms are still the norm (Ballast Hills, Badsey, Moy) and BillionGraves warns of no signal on site (*source claim*); no offline form that exports to a spreadsheet surfaced in one search.
- **Alternatives found:** BillionGraves and Find a Grave (accounts, uploads to their sites); Historic Graves (Ireland, its own system); the Cemetery Surveyor app (academic, Android); FAIMS; KoboToolbox and ODK (free offline forms, not checked here).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** an offline phone form with MI conventions and the plan numbering, each volunteer's records merged later by file, CSV and printable output, kept with the society rather than given to a crowdsourcing site.
- **Labs-fit problems:** photos in browser storage are heavy; merging between devices only by file.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** generic offline-form tools probably cover it, and no complaint was found.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

### 5. FreeREG file check for spreadsheet transcribers
- **Audience:** FreeREG volunteers on Mac, Linux or Chromebook, and anyone using the flexible format, which WinREG doesn't support (*verified*).
- **The job:** before: a spreadsheet of baptisms, marriages or burials; after: a CSV that FreeREG accepts first time.
- **Evidence of friction:** FreeREG's own warnings: set cells to Text, turn off AutoCorrect, older Excel has "a known problem" with its CSVs, pre-1900 dates and leading zeros get mangled (*verified* in part, F3; rest *source claim*); exact field names, header block, split-year and UCF date rules (*verified*). No first-hand complaint or error rate found; how upload errors are reported not found.
- **Alternatives found:** WinREG (Windows), FreeREG's own processing emails and coordinators.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** check and repair the file before upload (dates Excel converted, lost leading zeros, misspelt field names, missing header rows).
- **Labs-fit problems:** places and churches can only be checked against FreeREG's own database; the format belongs to one organisation and can change.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** FreeREG's processing and coordinators already catch errors, for one organisation's volunteers.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

## Screened out
- **CREW/MUSTIE weeding from a catalogue export:** the formula is simple, the MUSTIE half is human judgement, and the systems school libraries use already report age and circulation (Destiny weeding reports, Evergreen reporter) (*source claim*, S6). Reject.
- **War memorial names against CWGC:** matching needs context (next of kin, unit, eligibility rules), not a join (*source claim*, Great War Forum, S8). Reject.
- **Condition reports with annotated photos:** Articheck, Horus Condition Report (cheap iOS) and free Word templates cover it; no volunteer friction found (*source claim*, S9). Reject.
- **Copyright duration (the 2039 rule):** official TNA flowcharts, CAHG's accessible version and handbook, Collections Trust links; legal stakes (*source claim*, S12). Reject.
- **Location audits and library stocktakes:** Spectrum defines the audit, but the tool is a generic list comparison and no friction was found (*source claim*, S11). Reject.
- **Not reached:** photo dating and captioning, Spectrum object entry and loans paperwork, exhibition labels, digitisation file naming, retention schedules (screened by judgement as forms, lookups, or covered by free desktop tools).
