## Findings

**The job (before → after).** Before: a household at a decision point (an EV, a battery or solar, a heat pump, a Tracker/Agile price swing, a bill or standing-charge shock) has half-hourly data somewhere (an Octopus dashboard CSV; n3rgy's 90-day zips; an app's export) and published prices somewhere else, and either lines them up by half-hour in a spreadsheet (regions, product versions, standing charges, BST/GMT, missing rows) or hands an app their Octopus API key. After: "over these 12 months you paid £Y; on Agile/Go/Flexible it would have been £X, standing charges included; your always-on load is N W, about £Z a year; you use most at 17:00–19:00", worked out on their own device, with the assumption (same usage, no shifting) stated. (*inference*, built from the sources above.) It's an occasional job, done at the decision point; enthusiasts track it continuously in apps (*inference*). Evidence of the job: people doing it by hand on MSE (Agile vs Tracker over 24 days), OpenEnergyMonitor (asking for a full-year Tracker comparison), Buildhub (an OVO customer pricing their data on Agile), and a steady supply of tools made in 2025–2026 (AgileBuddy's January 2026 sample, Octoprice ver 2025.08.21, the Octo-Aid review of April 2026, OEM's mid-2026 model) (*source claim* except where marked verified above). Reddit evidence couldn't be read (the tool refuses reddit.com).

**Alternatives found.** Free, browser-only, Octopus API key: Octoprice (*verified*). Freemium apps: Octopus Watch (Smart Compare; Tracker/Flux/Cosy only with a subscription; not with export), Octo-Aid, WattSaver for Octopus Energy. Account-based web: AgileBuddy (registration; Octopus range plus EDF FreePhase; Octopus referral) (*verified*). Demo-data modelling: OEM SolarMatching (*verified*). Data viewers and routes: n3rgy's consumer portal, Guy Lipman's server-side viewer (*verified* as a test bed), Loop pointing to n3rgy. DIY: spreadsheets, GitHub scripts, Home Assistant and emoncms apps. Other markets: Australia's SolarQuotes Battery Calculator takes an uploaded NEM12 file (*source claim*). No official Octopus "what would I have paid" feature surfaced (*source claim*, absence only).

**Strongest incumbent.** Octoprice: free, "does not collect personal information", keeps Octopus details in the browser only, compares the whole Octopus range with standing charges, VAT and export, and simulates a battery and solar ("Load Shift") (*verified*). Its one limit: it needs an Octopus API key and account number, so people with other suppliers get only a trial on sample data, and nobody can bring a file.

**Labs-fit problems.**
- Prices: Octopus's products API works from a static page with no key (*verified*: `access-control-allow-origin: *`), but per region (14) and per product version; Tracker and Intelligent Go need separate lookups; Intelligent Go's cheap hours depend on the car's charging slots, so past data can't price it exactly (*inference*). Other suppliers' tariffs have no public price feed (*inference*), so they'd be typed in. The page depends on Octopus keeping that API open to browsers.
- Input: Labs can't ask for an API key (the consumption API is a keyed service under the constitution), so the visitor must bring a file. That means several formats (Octopus CSV header variants with zero rows dropped; n3rgy zips of 90 days, one fuel each; app exports), gas in m³ vs kWh, and on a phone the file is the hard part (*inference*).
- Honesty: a past-year comparison assumes the household wouldn't have changed its habits (the reason Octopus Watch tries to separate movable from fixed load); EV and battery what-ifs need a modelled load or dispatch, and an overconfident "you'd save £X" can push a switch or a purchase (*inference*).

**Strongest reason not to build.** The people for whom the answer matters most, Octopus customers (the supplier whose smart tariffs and public prices this is about), already have a free, browser-only, no-account tool that pulls their data automatically and models batteries (Octoprice), plus several apps. What's left for Labs is a "bring your own file" page for people with other suppliers, who first have to get a half-hourly file (n3rgy's IHD-MAC sign-up and 90-day zips, or supplier exports that often aren't half-hourly), and whose demand is the least evidenced here.

**Thread verdict: weak.** Real, recurring job with active 2025–2026 tool-making, but the main audience is served free and in-browser by Octoprice; the unserved slice (non-Octopus households with a file, or a supplier-neutral always-on/hour-of-day reading of any half-hourly file) is feasible for Labs but thinly evidenced and would carry format and estimate-honesty risks.
