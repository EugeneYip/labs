Not legal advice.
  - **What a formula means** (p. 57, "The CREW Guidelines for Weeding Your Collection"): first figure = years since the latest copyright; second = maximum years without use, "assuming that the item has been in the library's collection for at least that period of time"; third = MUSTIE (Misleading, Ugly, Superseded, Trivial, Irrelevant, obtainable Elsewhere). "X" = that part doesn't apply. The worked example reads "8/3/MUSTIE" as consider for discard when copyright is more than 8 years old "**and/or**" last circulation **or in-house use** more than 3 years ago "and/or" any MUSTIE factor. The formulas are "rules of thumb".
    - *Inference*: three things a tool must get right: (a) the "and/or" — show both readings (old AND idle = strong candidates; old OR idle = review list), as #035 did with its two readings; (b) the idle test needs a date added/accession date, or new purchases get flagged; (c) in-house use and MUSTIE aren't in any export, so the output is a pull list to check at the shelf, never a discard list.
  - **The table**: guidelines by Dewey class on pp. 61–77 plus children's on pp. 81–82; my text tally finds about 60 formula strings: roughly 50 Dewey ranges (some with alternatives) plus six non-Dewey categories with formulas (Biography, F, graphic novels, E, JF, YA) (e.g. 200: 10/3 or 5/3 for fast-changing areas; 570: 7/3, but 5/2 for genetics). Examples: 004 Computers 3/X; 010 Bibliography 10/3; 030 Encyclopedias 5/X; 101 Philosophy 15/5; 133, 150, 160/170 10/3; 306 5/2; 310 Statistics/almanacs 2/X; 320 5/3; 330 Economics 3/3; 340 Law 5/2; 370 10/3; 398 Folklore X/3; 500 5/3; 520 Astronomy 5/3; 550 X/3; 560 5/2; 610 Medicine 5/3; 629 Auto repair X/2; 636 Pets and 640 Home Economics 5/2 and 5/3 (which goes with which is ambiguous in the text layer: the two-column layout runs them together, so the table needs a check against the page images); 709, 720, 740 X/3; 791 10/2; 800 X/3; 910 Travel 3/2 (narratives 5/2); 930–999 History 10/3; Biography X/3; F Fiction X/2; Graphic novels X/1; E X/2; JF X/2; YA fiction 3/2; J and YA nonfiction by the adult classes (*inference* from the heading, text not read); periodicals, government documents, nonprint, local history get prose, not formulas. The appendix "Overview Chart of CREW Formulas" (p. 105) is an image, so no machine-readable table exists in the PDF; anyone wanting the table in a spreadsheet has to type it.
  - **Workflow the manual itself prescribes** ("CREWing with Computers", pp. 55–56): ILS reports per Dewey area with latest checkout date, accession date, copyright date, copies; "check whether you can export the information into a better format ... into Excel", or "cut and paste" into Excel; points to Greiner & Cooper, *Analyzing Library Collection Use With Excel* (ALA 2007). Print only what you can weed "within a four to six week period"; "probably 80% of the items that have not circulated in three years can be discarded" but each still needs review; then a second pass over items that did circulate (condition, one-or-two-use items). Frequency (p. 16): "systematic and continuous" weeding; "Reports can be generated from the online catalog on a quarterly basis".
    - *Inference*: so the job is officially "export, then hand-build a spreadsheet per Dewey area against a table you type in yourself". That is exactly the gap a page could fill, and the manual is 14 years old and frozen.

## The job (before → after)

- **Before** (the manual's own prescribed workflow, *verified*, plus the consortium reports, *source claim*): run an ILS report per Dewey area (call number, title, copyright/publication date, date added, total and last checkout), export or paste it into Excel, find a copy of the CREW table (since early 2025 no longer downloadable from TSLAC; the PDF's overview chart is an image, so it has to be typed in), write per-range formulas that cope with "X", the "and/or" reading, the "owned at least that long" condition and call-number prefixes (J, YA, E, REF, B/92, FIC), sort, print a 4–6-week chunk, take it to the shelves, then do a second pass for worn items that do circulate. Repeated per section, ideally continuously or quarterly. Needs "Excel skills" (NLLS FAQ).
- **After**: drop the export into a page that reads it in the browser, map its columns once, choose the 2012 CREW numbers (editable; or a simple UK SLA age rule, or the library's own), and get per-range counts (old, idle, both, never circulated, too new to judge) and a shelf-order pull list to print or save as CSV, with found/missing/pull ticks kept in the browser for the shelf walk. MUSTIE, in-house use and condition stay the librarian's call at the shelf.

## Alternatives found

- **Excel by hand** (the de facto tool; the manual recommends it and points to Greiner & Cooper, *Analyzing Library Collection Use With Excel*, ALA 2007).
- **ILS and consortium reports**: Destiny Collection Analysis (Enriched Reports, US Destiny Cloud only), Alexandria "Weeding List" report, Koha saved reports to CSV, Evergreen weeding lists, Bibliovation (SCLS), Upper Hudson "Collection Weeding Report", Mid-Hudson "Not Circulated in 3 Years", Alma Analytics. They filter but don't apply a per-Dewey CREW table (none found that does).
- **Free vendor collection analyses for schools**: Follett TitleWise Analysis (free, Titlewave account, age by classification, aged-titles weeding list; no circulation, no CREW), Mackin, Bound to Stay Bound, Capstone (collection data only, no circulation; vendors "want your business").
- **Paid**: collectionHQ (Baker & Taylor; dead/grubby items with library-set thresholds; subscription, price unpublished); EBSCO Core Collections (title lists for selection and weeding).
- **Guides, not tools**: CREW 2012 (CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 US, archived by TSLAC in 2025), Vnuk's *The Weeding Handbook* (ALA), Baumbach & Miller's *Less Is More* (ALA 2006, school, 71 Dewey areas), dozens of state-agency cheat sheets that re-type CREW, UK SLA guidance.
- **No free page or script that takes a CSV export and applies CREW per Dewey range** surfaced in three targeted searches (spreadsheet template, GitHub/web app/plugin, built into small-library systems).

## Strongest incumbent

For the largest group (US school libraries, Follett-dominated), **Destiny's own reports plus Follett's free TitleWise Analysis**: one click from the catalogue, age by Dewey and an aged-titles list. It ignores circulation and CREW's per-class numbers and sits inside a bookseller's account, but it's free and already where those librarians work. For larger public libraries, collectionHQ. For everyone else, Excel.

## Labs-fit problems

- **Format variety**: every ILS names its columns differently; call numbers carry prefixes and Cutters; copyright dates come as "c2012", "[2012?]", "2012-2013"; a blank last checkout can mean "never" or "before the last system migration" (migrations often reset circulation history and date added), so the idle test is only as good as the history. Needs a column-mapping step, a "can't judge" bucket, and fixtures in several systems' column names (none read in this thread).
- **The table itself**: CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 US. Ship the numbers (facts) in the page's own words, attribute "CREW: A Weeding Manual for Modern Libraries © 2012 TSLAC", link the licence, let users edit; don't reproduce the manual's notes. The text layer is ambiguous in places (two-column layout), so the table must be checked line by line against the page images before shipping.
- **Political and legal sensitivity**: CREW is cited in *Little v. Llano County*; weeding is entangled with book removals. Output must be framed as a review list resting on the library's own policy, with only content-neutral criteria (age, use), never "what to discard".
- **Phone**: reading a file works on a phone, but the analysis is a desktop job; the shelf walk with ticks is the phone part. Large collections (tens of thousands of rows) are fine in memory, but saved ticks must be keyed by barcode, not by storing the file (localStorage size).
- **Privacy**: some report exports can include patron fields; a local-only page is the right shape, and it should say so.
- **Low frequency**: a few sessions a year per section; fine for Labs, but it's not a daily tool.

## Strongest reason not to build

The people with the most weeding to do already have a free or paid near-substitute where they work (US schools: Destiny reports and TitleWise; larger public libraries: collectionHQ; consortium members: ready-made "not circulated in 3 years" reports), and the parts of CREW that matter most (MUSTIE, in-house use, condition) can't come from a file. The page would save an Excel session a few times a year for the remainder (small independent public, church, special and non-Destiny school libraries), and the evidence of their pain is indirect: how-to guides that say "requires Excel skills", the manual's own Excel instructions, a Reddit thread asking where the manual went, a classroom-library app's feature request. No direct complaint about the spreadsheet step was found.

## Verdict

**lead (modest)**: a real, specific and recently widened gap (the official CREW source vanished in 2025, no free tool applies its per-Dewey age-and-use rule to an export, the export fields have converged), a clean static fit like #035; held back by indirect pain evidence and a free age-only incumbent for US schools.
