## Leads (strongest first)

### 1. California conservator's account: the GC-400 / GC-405 schedules and Summary of Account from the bank downloads
- **Audience:** a family member appointed conservator of the estate in California (not a professional fiduciary), often self-represented, preparing the first account or a biennial one.
- **The rule:** Probate Code 2620 and Rules of Court 7.575 (*verified*, courts.ca.gov rule 7.575): a standard accounting unless a simplified one is authorised; the Summary of Account (GC-400(SUM)/GC-405(SUM)) in every accounting; GC-405 Schedules A and C in every simplified one; for a standard accounting the other Judicial Council forms are optional if the same categories and information are given "in a similar general format"; all account statements lodged (original or a verified unaltered printout); signed under penalty of perjury. Timing: one year after appointment, then every two years, examined in detail by a probate examiner (*verified*, SF court page). Schedules: A receipts (dividends, interest, pensions and annuities, rent, Social Security and VA, other), B gains on sales, C disbursements (caregiver, residential care, fiduciary and attorney fees, living, medical, household, investment, property sale, other), D losses, E assets on hand at end, F changes in form of assets, G liabilities (*source claim*, selfhelp.courts.ca.gov form pages via search). The court's own aid is GC-400(A)(C), a 2008 adding sheet, not filed (*verified*).
- **The job:** before: the inventory and appraisal (the first account's opening position), every account statement for the period (including the one just before appointment), receipts. After: schedules whose totals agree with the Summary, a Summary that balances (opening estate plus receipts and gains equals disbursements, losses, distributions and property on hand at the end), statements lodged, a petition and a hearing.
- **Frequency / consequences:** first at one year, then at least every two years; deficient accounts are sent back for a supplement and the hearing continued; a non-compliant account was named as possible grounds for suspension or removal (*source claim*, Santa Barbara rulings via search).
- **Evidence of friction:** a first account whose Schedule C was "subtotaled" 6,122.94 above its own total, with three schedules missing and unexplained payments (*verified*, Santa Barbara ruling 23PR00445, F4); opening balances that "do not match" the inventory and missing statements in other courts' notes (*source claim*, S9); a free monthly accounting workshop in Orange County for self-represented filers (*source claim*, S15); a family conservator asking a tax forum for software in 2011 (*verified*, thin, F10). Whether the F4 conservator was a relative isn't stated.
- **Alternatives found:** the Judicial Council PDFs and GC-400(A)(C) (free, paper arithmetic); Thomson Reuters Form Builder's California accounting calculator (practitioner software, price not shown, *source claim*); single fillable schedules from form resellers (FormsWorkflow lists USD 13.99 a form, terms unclear, *source claim*); QuickBooks or Quicken (*verified*, as a forum suggestion); lawyers, accountants and professional fiduciaries paid from the estate (*inference*). No tool for family conservators found in two searches.
- **Strongest incumbent:** the lawyer or accountant who prepares it for a fee from the estate (*inference*).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** open the period's bank downloads, sort each line into the Judicial Council's Schedule A and C categories (moves between the conservatee's own accounts kept out), carry in the opening cash from the inventory, total every schedule, build the Summary's figures and check that it balances, and show where an account stops agreeing with its statement balance; print the schedules in the Council's categories (allowed for standard accounts by 7.575) and the figures to copy onto GC-400(SUM). This is Deputyship Accounts' method with a different jurisdiction, form and audience: either a second experiment or a California output of #034, the main session's call (*inference*). The constitution's "never imports from another experiment" means shared logic would have to move to `src/components/` or be copied (*inference*).
- **Labs-fit problems:** the schedules examiners most often find missing (gains and losses, non-cash assets on hand, changes in form of assets, escrow statements) come from investment and property records, not bank downloads, so the page covers the cash side only (*inference*); county local rules add requirements (Shasta, Santa Barbara, *source claim*); simplified-account eligibility not checked; filling the official PDF in the browser would mean bundling the form (*inference*).
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the failures examiners write about are mostly legal completeness (missing schedules, inventory mismatch, unexplained spending), which sorting doesn't fix; one state's family conservators, every two years, many with lawyers; and it may be only a feature of #034.
- **Thread verdict:** lead.

### 2. US representative payee: the yearly report's figures from the payee account
- **Audience:** an individual Social Security representative payee who isn't exempt: an adult child for a parent, a sibling, grandparent, other relative or friend (*inference* from the exemptions).
- **The rule:** SSA POMS GN 00605.001 (*verified*): a yearly Representative Payee Report (paper SSA-623/6230/6234, or online through iRPA) to "monitor how the payee spent or saved the benefits"; a face-to-face interview if the answers show improper use or the report isn't returned, and a change of payee after repeated non-response. Exempt since 2018: parents and legal guardians of a minor living with the child, parents of a disabled adult in the same household, spouses (*source claim*, POMS GN 00605.015 via search). The report asks how much went on food and housing, how much on other needs, how much was saved (*source claim*, The Arc of Northern Virginia via search; form wording not read first-hand). Totals under 90% of the Total Accountable Amount that the payee can't explain lead to an interview (*source claim*, POMS GN 00605.070 via search). Records kept two years plus the current one, not sent (*source claim*).
- **The job:** before: a year of the payee account's statements and receipts, with the shared household costs; after: three figures and where savings are held, on paper or online, and records ready for a site review.
- **Frequency / consequences:** yearly; interviews, payee change, and repaying misused funds (*inference* on liability).
- **Evidence of friction:** thin. A caregiver-forum payee keeps an Excel log with a column for the report's categories (*source claim*, S4); spam pages chasing "representative payee bookkeeping template" (*inference*: search demand); a vendor (True Link) sells payee cards with transaction logs (*source claim*). No first-hand complaint found.
- **Alternatives found:** SSA's online report (takes the totals, does no sorting, *inference*); True Link prepaid cards (price not checked); spreadsheets.
- **Strongest incumbent:** a spreadsheet, or the bank's own spending categories (*inference*).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** open the payee account's download for the report period (which needn't be a calendar year), mark each line food and housing, other needs, saved, or not benefit money, and show the three totals against the benefits received with the 90% check. The sorting is #034's engine; the output is three numbers.
- **Labs-fit problems:** the form's wording not verified first-hand; apportioning shared household costs is a judgment and could drift into advice.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the return is three numbers, SSA's online report exists, the most common family payees (parents, spouses) are exempt, and friction evidence is thin.
- **Thread verdict:** weak (the scale, millions of payees, is the only reason to look again).

### 3. US VA fiduciary's yearly accounting
- **Audience:** a non-spouse relative appointed VA fiduciary for a veteran or survivor, holding over USD 10,000 of VA funds.
- **The rule:** 38 CFR 13.280 (*source claim*, via search; eCFR blocked the fetch): yearly accounting where VA funds exceed USD 10,000, a fee is taken, the beneficiary is rated 100%, or the hub requires it; spouses exempt; due 30 days after the period; a corrected one within 14 days of a discrepancy notice; original statements for the whole period; filed through FAST (an AccessVA sign-in portal) instead of paper 21P-4706b. VA's own page says only that fiduciaries "may need to provide accountings or fund usage reviews" (*verified*, thin).
- **The job / frequency / consequences:** opening balance, income, expenses, closing balance matched to statements, yearly; late accounting can mean suspended benefits, a new fiduciary, or a misuse investigation (*source claim*).
- **Evidence of friction:** none gathered (search cap).
- **Alternatives found:** FAST (official; whether it totals or checks isn't known); the VA 21-4718 account book (*source claim*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** FAST.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** the same sorting and balance check as #034, for the 4706b's lines. Mostly a feature of a US accounts page.
- **Labs-fit problems:** the 4706b layout wasn't read.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** an official online tool exists, and no friction evidence.
- **Thread verdict:** weak (unresearched).

### 4. OPG Scotland financial guardian's annual account
- **Audience:** a relative who is a financial guardian under the Adults with Incapacity (Scotland) Act 2000.
- **The rule:** yearly account on the OPG's notice; simplified where the estate is under GBP 30,000, standard otherwise, schedules 1-6 and a summary; supporting information sent with it; a review fee from the estate; an approval certificate (*verified*, OPG Scotland "Annual account" page).
- **Alternatives found:** the OPG's own Excel workbook for the standard account ("2025-account-form.xlsx") and Word simplified form, a Nov 2025 guide, worked examples (*verified*).
- **Evidence of friction:** not searched.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** sort the year's bank downloads into the Scottish schedules (interest, income and pensions, other income, living expenses, other expenses) and give the totals to type into the OPG's workbook. **Only a feature of Deputyship Accounts.**
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the official body already supplies the spreadsheet; only the sorting is left.
- **Thread verdict:** weak as a new experiment; a possible #034 output.

### 5. England direct payments: the recipient's monitoring and audit
- **Audience:** a direct-payment recipient (or family member managing it) with a bank account rather than a prepaid card, often employing personal assistants.
- **The rule:** each council's own direct-payment agreement and audit cycle: quarterly (Haringey), about eight weeks then yearly (another council), statements, receipts, payslips or payroll reports, timesheets with holidays and sickness, HMRC and pension evidence, employer's liability certificate; breach can stop the payment and money can be recovered (*source claim*, council PDFs via search).
- **Alternatives found:** prepaid cards the council audits online, payroll providers, council spending forms.
- **Evidence of friction:** none first-hand in two searches.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** every council sets its own form and frequency, and prepaid cards remove the recipient's return.
- **Thread verdict:** reject.
