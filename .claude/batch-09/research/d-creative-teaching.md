# Thread D: creative production and teaching outside classroom planning (2026-10-14)

Bounded research thread for Batch 09 discovery round 1. Budget used: 18 of 18 searches, 10 of 10 fetches (two fetches failed: Gearspace returned 403; the Copyright Office's GRPPH tutorial transcript PDF was too large to read). Read-only: nobody contacted, nothing downloaded, saved, submitted or posted. No lead has been through a gate; the main session checks every claim before deciding.

Evidence labels: *verified* (read on an official or primary page), *source claim* (a page or search snippet says so), *inference* (judgment).

## Summary

- **One lead worth a gate check:** preparing a US group registration of published photographs (GRPPH): the numbered title list and the deposit. The rule and its penalty are verified on copyright.gov; the friction is inferred, not evidenced; the Office's next system may change the job.
- **Two weak leads**, recorded so they aren't searched again: a page-number index from a final print PDF (self-publishers), and costume sizing across vendor charts (dance studios).
- **Seven rejects**, most because a free or official incumbent already offers the Labs wedge: stem pre-flight (Stem Checker's free local browser tool), whole-book ACX checks (ACX Audio Lab), music cue sheets (EditingTools.io), drama exam-day timetables (LAMDA ExamTrack), moderation and IQA sampling (VLEs and e-portfolios; no friction found), lip-sync track reading (Papagayo), podcast chapters (existing builders and hosts).
- **Teaching outside the classroom produced nothing that clears the bar** in this thread: the rule-created jobs found are built into the exam boards' and awarding bodies' own systems, or are a sort and a filter.

## Leads

### 1. GRPPH prep: the title list and deposit for a US group registration of published photographs

- **Audience:** US professional photographers (wedding, portrait, commercial, editorial, stock) who register published work in groups, often quarterly (*inference* for the cadence); second, their studio managers and assistants.
- **The job:** Before: the period's published images exported (JPEG) from Lightroom, Capture One or Photo Mechanic. The application needs a separate, sequentially numbered list giving each photo's title, file name and month/year of publication. The Office's Excel template has "Title of Photograph", "File Name of Photograph" and "Month/Year of Publication" columns, an auto-generated "List of All Group Titles" column, a "Missing Information" warning column, and the 13-digit case number at the top; the list may be saved as Excel (.xls/.xlsx) or PDF; the camera's file name may serve as the title; the list's own file name must include the group title and the case number. Limits: up to 750 photos, all published in the same calendar year, same author and claimant. Deposit: JPEG, GIF or TIFF in one .zip with the list, at most 500 MB, and photos may be compressed to fit. Titles and months are also "strongly encouraged" on the application's Titles screen, in entries of up to 1,995 characters (*verified*, copyright.gov/eco/help/group/grpph.html, read 2026-10-14). The case number is issued only once the online application is started, so the list is finished mid-application (*source claim*, the Office's naming example via search). After: the application submitted, the certificate later, and the photographer tracking which images are registered (an Adobe Community feature request asks for a registration-status field because photographers use keywords, labels and collections as workarounds, *source claim*).
- **Frequency / importance:** recurring for diligent professionals (*inference*); legally consequential: if the file names in the list don't match the photos, "the Office may exclude the photo or refuse registration" (*verified*, GRPPH help page), and photos beyond 750 may be excluded or the claim refused (*source claim*, Circular 42 via search). Each photo in the group counts as a separate work for statutory damages (*source claim*, search snippet).
- **Evidence of friction:** none first-hand. Three searches for photographers describing the list as slow or error-prone (Reddit, DPReview, FredMiranda, ASMP terms) found only guides. Copyright Alliance's 2018 summary names the fee and judging whether work is published as photographers' difficulties, not the list (*verified*, copyrightalliance.org/new-group-registration-photographs/, read 2026-10-14). The Office "strongly encourage[s]" preparing the list before starting (*verified*). So the friction is *inference*: hundreds of rows, names that must match exactly, a size cap that may need recompression, a case number added mid-application, and the Titles screen's character limit.
- **Alternatives found:** the Office's template, filled by hand or from a pasted folder listing (*verified* template; *inference* for the paste); ImageRights, a registration service with Lightroom integration (a testimonial: it "can all be done inside my normal Adobe Lightroom Workflow"), sign-up required, price not shown (*verified*, imagerights.com homepage, read 2026-10-14; an older review gave $99 plus the Office's fee, *source claim*); Lightroom metadata plugins (Jeffrey Friedl's, PLUS for Lightroom: seen only as search results, not checked for this job); exiftool scripts, registration services and attorneys (*inference*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the Office's own template plus the photographer's catalogue export; for heavy registrants, ImageRights' Lightroom-integrated service.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** open the exported folder (up to 750 files) on the device; check the Office's limits before anything is uploaded (count, formats, one calendar year, duplicate or zip-unsafe file names, total size); set publication months quickly (default from the capture month, by range or folder, every value editable and marked as a guess); write the numbered list in the template's columns (a PDF list is accepted, which avoids an .xlsx writer) with the required file name once the case number is typed; optionally build the deposit .zip under 500 MB by recompressing JPEGs; split the Titles-screen text into entries under 1,995 characters. The photos never leave the device until the photographer uploads them to the Office.
- **Labs-fit problems:** a 500 MB zip and up to 750 recompressions in a phone browser (memory, time slices); TIFF decodes only in Safari, so elsewhere it can only be passed through (*inference*); US-only; legal-adjacent wording (it prepares files, isn't advice, the Office decides); the template's layout must be mirrored exactly if Excel is offered; capture date isn't publication date.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the friction is unproven and may be small (a pasted folder listing and a fill-down could take minutes), and the system is due to change: in 2024 the Office told the Senate that its new Enterprise Copyright System registration will "significantly expand" the photographs allowed in a group application, with an uploader designed for up to 2,000 images (*source claim*, Senate Judiciary QFR responses of 2024-11-13 via search). No announcement that the list requirement has changed was found.
- **Thread verdict:** lead, narrow. Worth a gate only if the main session finds (a) evidence that the list or its mismatches cost photographers time or claims (Office correspondence, association guides, forums), and (b) that ECS hasn't replaced, and isn't about to replace, the GRPPH list. GRUPH (unpublished photographs: title and file name only, *source claim*) would be the same tool without months.

### 2. A page-number index from a final print PDF (self-published nonfiction)

- **Audience:** self-publishing nonfiction authors and very small presses whose layout tool makes no index: Vellum doesn't support indexes, per one author's post (*source claim*, kevindangoor.substack.com via search); Scrivener needs a style-and-compile workaround (*verified*, forum thread below).
- **The job:** Before: pages final (one guide: add page numbers only at the last proofread, *source claim*, search snippet). After: an alphabetical index with page locators and merged ranges (12, 15–17) and sub-entries, pasted in as back matter; since it comes last it doesn't move earlier pages (*inference*).
- **Frequency / importance:** once per book and edition; matters for print editions of reference-style nonfiction.
- **Evidence of friction:** the Literature & Latte forum thread "Making an index?" (Feb 2021 to Jan 2024, 20+ posts): the learning curve "can be steep"; "It does look daunting" (a publisher); PDF Index Generator is "really more of a concordance" that "only gets you halfway there" (*verified*, forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/making-an-index/94742, read 2026-10-14). Nothing stronger found.
- **Alternatives found:** PDF Index Generator (Windows, Mac, Linux; $59.95 to $70 one-time; an older free version indexed only the first 10 pages; one user did 450 pages and 1,600 terms in under three minutes) (*source claim*, BetaNews, AlternativeTo, Softpedia via search); TExtract (paid, Windows, *source claim*); Word's index with a concordance file, free if the book is laid out in Word (*inference*); InDesign and Affinity Publisher index panels, if the book is laid out there (*inference*); LaTeX, LibreOffice, Pandoc routes (*verified*, the forum thread); professional indexers.
- **Strongest incumbent:** PDF Index Generator: cheap, fast, local, on every desktop platform.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** free, no install, the manuscript never uploaded; paste a term list with variants; every hit shown in context to keep or drop (a reviewed concordance, the step the forum says automatic tools skip); printed page numbers mapped from PDF pages (roman front matter, offsets); ranges merged; output in a standard style to paste back.
- **Labs-fit problems:** needs a PDF text extractor (pdf.js, a large dependency); extraction is brittle (hyphenation, ligatures, running heads and folios, footnotes, small caps); printed folios often aren't in the PDF's page labels.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** occasional, and a $60 local tool already does the mechanical part; a concordance is the easy half of an index.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

### 3. Costume sizing across vendor charts (dance studios)

- **Audience:** dance-studio owners and office managers ordering catalogue costumes for 20–80 routines a year.
- **The job:** Before: every dancer measured (girth, chest, waist, hips, height). After: one order per costume with each dancer's size from that vendor's chart, and a list for handing costumes out. Brands treat girth as the main measurement, advise sizing up between sizes, and differ by garment even within one brand (*source claim*, Kellé, Stelle and Mara pages via search).
- **Frequency / importance:** yearly or twice yearly; exchanges and reorders cost money.
- **Evidence of friction:** dated and indirect. Dance Teacher, "Costume Ordering Made Easy" (1 November 2012): one person measuring reduced exchanges; finish measuring two weeks before the order deadline; the problems described are wrong shipments, alterations, missed deadlines and parents' changes, not converting measurements to a chart (*verified*, dance-teacher.com/costume-ordering-made-easy/, read 2026-10-14).
- **Alternatives found:** Jackrabbit's costume module markets auto-sizing from girth against the vendor's chart (*source claim*, jackrabbitclass.com features page via search); CostumeManager.com (parents order and pay, sorted by class and dancer: *verified* as described in the 2012 article, current status unknown); printable measurement worksheets and Paperform order-form templates (*source claim*); spreadsheets.
- **Strongest incumbent:** studio-management suites (Jackrabbit and similar) and the vendors' own ordering.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** paste measurements once, paste or type each costume's chart, sizes assigned by the vendor's stated rule with out-of-range and conflicting measurements flagged, totals per size per costume, a dancer-by-dancer list.
- **Labs-fit problems:** charts must be entered by the user for every costume; children's measurements kept in one browser.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** no evidence the conversion is the bottleneck; paid suites already do it; it sits beside Show Order in the recital cluster the batch wants to avoid without strong evidence.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

## Screened out

- **Stem and multitrack pre-flight before a mix or master: REJECT.** The need is real (a guide calls stems that don't start and end together mastering engineers' most common complaint, *source claim*; guides tell senders to reimport into a blank session to check, *source claim*), but Stem Checker already offers a free browser tool: drop WAV or AIFF stems and it catches "clipping, silence, length mismatches, dual-mono exports and more"; "nothing is uploaded", "Everything runs locally" (*verified*, stemchecker.io, read 2026-10-14). Its Mac app (two free sessions, then $3.99 a month or $29.99 lifetime) adds duplicates, ambiguous names, mixed sample rates or bit depths, and a beta null test.
- **Whole-book ACX audiobook checks: REJECT.** ACX Audio Lab, free to any ACX account holder (which every ACX narrator has, *inference*), checks RMS, peak, bitrate, bitrate method, sample rate, mixed channels, duplicates and spacing, with an Excel download; it skips noise floor, which Audacity's free ACX Check covers per selection (*source claim*, acx.com blog and help FAQ via search; Audacity forum). Single-file browser checkers exist too (Narration Box, *source claim*; Elysia Tools: single file, processing location unclear, no room-tone, CBR or length checks, *verified*, elysiatools.com, read 2026-10-14). The remaining gap (noise floor) has no precise ACX definition, so a Labs number would be a guess.
- **Music cue sheets from an edit (EDL/XML): REJECT.** EditingTools.io reads EDL, FCP7 XML, Avid XML, Pro Tools session text, FCPXML and CSV, "merges multiple tracks and removes invisible cuts", joins clips across 1–500-frame handles, and exports CSV, XLSX, PDF and society templates (APRA, CISAC, KODA, M6) (*verified*, en.editingtools.io/cuesheet, read 2026-10-14: the sequence is uploaded; projects and presets need a login; prices not on the page). Library tools from Slipstream and Melodie (*source claim*). The hard part (titles, writers, publishers, splits, usage) is typed by hand whatever the tool.
- **Exam-day timetables for drama exam centres (LAMDA): REJECT.** LAMDA's ExamTrack holds the schedule (Manage Schedule, Alter Exam Order, break types) with at most 6 h 30 min of exams per date and room, excluding breaks and lunch; timings per grade and subject are published (*source claim*, LAMDA's ExamTrack guide and 2026–27 timings PDF via search). ABRSM, Trinity, RAD and ISTD not checked (*inference*: probably the same pattern).
- **Moderation samples (universities) and IQA sampling plans (vocational): REJECT.** The rules are explicit and vary: Brighton about the square root of the cohort with top, middle, bottom and borderlines; Chichester 20% including all fails, or at least 7; NU London 10% or 5, all borderline fails; Staffordshire 10 or 10%, 50% under ten; Cardiff Met three per band or 10%; Aberdeen every marker represented; Bristol compares first markers' distributions (*source claim*, institutional policies via search). Vocational e-portfolios build IQA plans (Tribal Maytas, Smart Assessor, OneFile, *source claim*). No complaint about the time found; choosing the sample is a sort and a filter; the per-marker comparison overlaps the main session's open lead on judges' severity.
- **Lip-sync track reading onto exposure sheets (2D animation): REJECT.** Papagayo (free, open source, desktop), Pamela (free, for Moho), Rhubarb Lip Sync (free command line), and lip-sync built into Moho, Toon Boom and Clip Studio (*source claim*); niche, no pain found.
- **Podcast chapter markers: REJECT.** Builders already turn one list into ID3 CHAP and CTOC frames, Podcasting 2.0 JSON and YouTube timestamps (Elysia Tools, though burning ID3 needs an upload, *source claim*); hosts and editors have chapter editors (*inference*, not checked); Spotify and YouTube read only description timestamps (*source claim*, a guide's table).
