## Leads (strongest first)

### 1. California HOA reserve numbers: the 5570 disclosure and AB 2050's minimum contribution
- **Audience:** the treasurer and volunteer board of a small, self-managed California condominium or HOA, especially in the two years between professional studies.
- **The rule:** Civil Code 5570 (FindLaw copy "current as of January 01, 2026", *source claim*): the Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary goes with every annual budget report (5300) and states whether reserves stay sufficient for each of the next 30 years, the extra funding needed if not, the required amount at year end with projected balance and percent funded, the same for each of the next five budget years with and without the approved plan, and the assumed interest and inflation rates; the required amount per component is "current cost of replacement or repair multiplied by the number of years the component has been in service divided by the useful life". 5550: inspection every three years where components are worth at least half the gross budget, annual board review (*source claim*). AB 2050 (Chapter 796, Statutes of 2026, chaptered 29 Sept 2026, *source claim*, Digital Democracy): every study must state the minimum reserve contribution level that keeps the projected balance above zero for 30 years; from 1 Jan 2032 associations must fund at least that level or put 15% of the gross annual budget into reserves each year.
- **The job:** before: the last study's component table (cost, useful life, remaining life), the reserve balance, this year's contribution, assumed inflation and interest. After: the reserve figures for the budget report and the 5570 form, sent to members 30 to 90 days before the year starts; from 2027, a contribution plan that meets the 2032 minimum.
- **Frequency / consequences:** yearly for every association; a required disclosure to owners and buyers; from 2032, a legal funding floor and special assessments (*inference* on liability).
- **Evidence of friction:** thin. Practitioners' percent-funded methods differ (*source claim*, reservestudy.com); a management company misused "100% funded" (*source claim*); Newsweek: the bill "could raise fees for millions of homeowners" (*source claim*). No first-hand board complaint found (Reddit unreachable).
- **Alternatives found:** reserve specialists (paid; prices not checked); PropFusion ReservePulse, free only with a PropFusion study, else USD 79 a month (*verified*); Homey Reserve (AI, reads uploaded studies, price unknown, *source claim*); reservestudy.com DIY kit (a paid service, *source claim*); a CAD 20 Google Sheet (*source claim*); spam template pages; the PropFusion "reserve-calculator" page (unread).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the association's own reserve specialist and its software (the three-yearly study is required anyway, and firms sell cheap off-year updates).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** one page, no account, data in a saved file: paste the study's component table and get the 30-year cash flow, percent funded by 5570(b)(4), the five-year table, the 30-year yes/no, and the exact minimum level contribution, printable for the board packet.
- **Labs-fit problems:** none structural (no personal data, works offline, file save); the minimum level's definition (level or inflating contributions, interest, timing) must match the chaptered text, which this thread couldn't read; California wording only, though other states have similar rules (WA, OR mentioned by CommunityPay, unread).
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the inputs come from a professional study the law already requires, boards may just pay the specialist for each update, and a Labs figure that differs from the specialist's method could confuse a legally required disclosure.
- **Thread verdict:** lead (first read the chaptered AB 2050 text, and look for evidence that boards do off-year updates themselves).

### 2. England Rent Standard: each home's maximum rent for small registered providers (April 2027, the first convergence year)
- **Audience:** the clerk, treasurer or volunteer board of a small registered provider: registered almshouse charities (weekly maintenance contributions), fully mutual housing co-ops, community land trusts, very small housing associations.
- **The rule:** *verified* (gov.uk policy statement, Direction on the Rent Standard 2026): formula rent (70% earnings, 30% Jan 1999 value, bedroom weight), CPI+1% a year to 2036, extra GBP 1 a week from 2027-28 and GBP 2 from 2028-29 for homes below formula rent (never above it), CPI only where CPI+1 would pass the flexibility level (5%, 10% supported), caps by bedrooms rising CPI+1.5 from 2027-28; almshouses, co-ops and CLTs are covered and have their own 2016-17 to 2019-20 uprating figures.
- **The job:** before: each home's rent, bedrooms, county, 1999 valuation or existing formula rent, type; September CPI (published mid-October). After: each home's maximum rent for April, a board paper, the rent notices.
- **Frequency / consequences:** yearly; errors bring regulator judgements and refunds: Rugby about 4,000 tenancies and GBP 415k from wrong valuations, East Suffolk about GBP 2.58m, Your Housing at least GBP 648k, JRHT, Mid Devon, Cambridge (*source claim*, news and council papers).
- **Evidence of friction:** the errors above are professionals'; the Almshouse Association still points members to the 2015 guidance's worked examples (*source claim*). No small-provider complaint found.
- **Alternatives found:** no official calculator (*verified* absent from the policy statement; none found by search); vendor models (Ortec Finance, *source claim*); housing software; consultants; spreadsheets; the Association's specimen budget (.docx, unread).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the provider's own spreadsheet, or its consultant or managing agent.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** the published parameters built in (uprating chain by provider type, county earnings, bedroom weights, caps), a short property list in, each home's formula rent and 2027-28 maximum out with the working shown.
- **Labs-fit problems:** yearly parameter updates (CPI each October, caps); exact transcription of the county table; England only; the 1999 valuation stays outside the tool.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** a small audience (*inference*: a few hundred tiny providers), and once formula rent is known the yearly job for most homes is one multiplication plus one comparison.
- **Thread verdict:** weak lead (narrow, but the rule and the absence of a tool are solid).

### 3. Junior fast-bowling spells under the ECB's mandatory 2025 safety regulations
- **Audience:** volunteer umpires, captains and junior team managers in recreational cricket, above all adult-league captains with under-19 fast bowlers, and long-format junior games.
- **The rule:** mandatory since the 2025 season, umpires, captains and team managers must enforce (*verified*, Cricket Yorkshire); per-spell and per-match limits by age, rest until as many overs as the spell have been bowled from the same end, the change-ends, 40-minute and spin rules (*source claim*); the 2025 limits are lower than the old directives' (*source claim*), updated again December 2025 (PDF unreadable here).
- **The job:** over by over, who bowled from which end; at each over, who may bowl next from this end and how many overs each junior has left; no formal record afterwards.
- **Frequency / consequences:** every match, weekends April to September; injury risk and a breach the umpire must stop (*inference*).
- **Evidence of friction:** objections to the limits, not to counting (quote above); no known guidance to leagues on implementation (*verified*).
- **Alternatives found:** paper Directive Cards and the scorebook (*verified*); Cricket Umpiring Counter, iPhone and Watch, free plus purchases, over limits and alerts but no same-end rest rule listed (*source claim*); Clicket Umpire USD 0.99, counts only; Play-Cricket Scorer, no spell alert found (*source claim*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the scorebook and the umpire's head; Cricket Umpiring Counter on iPhone.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** an offline phone page, one tap per over (bowler and end), showing who may bowl next from each end and when each rested bowler is free; Android and iPhone alike.
- **Labs-fit problems:** the limit table must come from the ECB's PDF and changes yearly; "fast bowler" is the umpire's judgement; near Playing Time's audience.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** most junior formats cap bowlers at 3 or 4 overs a match, so the spell rules bind mainly in longer games, where experienced umpires count from the scorebook; no evidence the counting goes wrong.
- **Thread verdict:** weak lead (needs the 2025 table and a sign that the rest rule trips people up).

### 4. US public support test: Schedule A Part II line 5 (the 2% excess)
- **Audience:** volunteer treasurers of small 501(c)(3) public charities filing 990-EZ themselves through an e-file provider.
- **The rule:** one-third (or 10% facts and circumstances) over five years (*verified*, IRS page); each donor's five-year total (with related persons) above 2% of line 11 column (f) is excluded, government units and publicly supported charities exempt; keep the list, don't file it (*source claim*, Tax990 help and search).
- **The job:** five years of gifts by donor in; line 5, the public support percentage and the pass or fail out.
- **Frequency / consequences:** yearly; e-file rejects and, after failures, private foundation status (*source claim* and *inference*).
- **Evidence of friction:** Tax990's article on how to calculate line 5 (*source claim*); preparers' forum threads on getting line 5 to flow (*source claim*).
- **Alternatives found:** CPAs; UltraTax CS calculates it from contributor entries (*source claim*); e-file providers; spreadsheets.
- **Strongest incumbent:** the CPA or the e-file provider plus a spreadsheet.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** donor names never leave the device; CSV in, per-donor excess and line 5 out.
- **Labs-fit problems:** US only; tax wording.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the arithmetic is a SUMIF and a cap; what's hard is classifying support (unusual grants, related persons, gross receipts), which is tax interpretation.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

- **Frequency / consequences:** every entry; an ineligible entry spotted before judging can be withdrawn or transferred (*source claim*); forfeiture after judging not verified.
