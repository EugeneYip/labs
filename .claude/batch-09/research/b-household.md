### Searches 1-4, fetch 1 (lay deputies; lay executors)

- **Deputy annual report (OPG, England and Wales).** OPG102A web guidance (gov.uk, "last updated 6 January 2026", *verified*, fetched): if the account doesn't balance "you need to tell us why"; unexplained, "we may not be able to accept your report"; it suggests keeping a list of money in and spending as you go, sending a copy with the form, and says a spreadsheet can't replace the report. The page itself does not list the money in / money out categories, does not mention transfers between the client's own accounts, and describes the paper form posted to a PO Box (*verified*). From search summaries (*source claim*): OPG102 is general supervision; OPG103 is minimal supervision or a first year under £21,000; the report sorts money in and out into set categories (state pension and benefits in; accommodation, rent, household bills out); OPG103 section 7 lists payments over £1,000, gifts, and deputy costs; there is an online service, complete-deputy-report.service.gov.uk; a law-firm summary says accounts must reconcile from start to end of the period.
- **Estate accounts (lay executors, England and Wales).** Searching for templates returns mostly SEO spam pages on hijacked domains (*inference* from the result URLs). One MSE thread, "signing of estate accounts" (forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/5752205), has an executor sending beneficiaries "a basic spreadsheet of income and expenditure" with an interim payment (*source claim*, search summary). Incumbents: EstateCopilot (UK app, fixed fee, creates estate accounts, *source claim*), EstateExec (US, $199 one-time, *source claim* Capterra), SwiftProbate (US, $39, *source claim*); the ICAEW publishes a guide for lay executors (icaew.com probate consumer hub, *source claim*).

### Searches 5-7, fetches 2-3 (tenancy deposits; deputies' first reports)

- **Tenancy deposit deductions, apportioned for wear and tear (tenants and small landlords).** The method is published and is one line of arithmetic: the DPS guide (2019) takes replacement cost, age, and average lifespan; tenant's share = (lifespan - age) x (cost / lifespan), e.g. £500, 2 years old, 5-year life gives £300 (*source claim*, search summary of the DPS PDF); TDS's product lifespan guide (via NRLA) gives carpet ranges by quality, 2-4, 5-8, 8-15 years (*source claim*); both say the adjudicator decides on the evidence and the formula may not apply (*source claim*). First reading: the arithmetic is trivial, the dispute turns on check-in and check-out evidence and the adjudicator's judgment, and condition reports were already rejected. Screen out (see below).
- **Deputy reports, friction (Alzheimer's Society Dementia Support Forum).** A thread search returns about ten threads on the OPG102 and first reports, including "Any Accountants who can deal with the OPG form?" (threads/93491), "Help or advice on completing annual report form opg102" (73605), "Receipts for annual report OPG102" (80402), "Financial LPA Accounts" (113826), "OPG 102 - Just when I thought things were less bad..." (99482) (*source claim*, titles and URLs in results). Search summary claims: one deputy spent about 20 hours on a first report and still could not balance it; common culprits named are interest, bank charges, and DWP payments recorded twice; the online form has a balance checker and the report can't be submitted until it balances or is explained; later years were easier when entered monthly (*source claim*).
- **Fetched (verified):** "Disheartened First Deputyship Year Report", forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/disheartened-first-deputyship-year-report.159242/, 21 February 2025. The deputy says "I've been working on it all day"; the service flagged over £3,000 "accounts not balanced", still over £1,000 after fixes; causes found: she "did have some payments twice" and wrong starting balances; she "cannot for the life of me work out where I've gone wrong"; she had uploaded all statements; another member logs DWP payments only once they show on the statement (some months showed twice); she resolves to "do it monthly" next year.
- **Not settled:** whether the online service takes one total per category or each payment, and its exact category list. The OPG102 print-form PDF did not parse in the fetch tool (binary), so the categories are *source claim* only. Next check for the main session: the categories and entry mode in complete-deputy-report.service.gov.uk (or the public ministryofjustice/opg-digideps code, if it is public: *inference*, not checked).

### Searches 8-9, fetches 4-5 (deputy incumbents; the short form; estate accounts)

- **No free OPG-specific template or lay-deputy accounts app found** in one search (results were the OPG's own guidance, SEO template pages, and nonprofit treasurer templates; *source claim*, absence from one search only).
- **OPG103A web guidance** (gov.uk, last updated 6 January 2026, *verified*, fetched): the short form asks the deputy to "Select all the relevant categories of income" (an X per category, so categories, not individual payments), to list one-off payments "over £1000", and to give the main accounts' balances at the start and end of the period; it has no reconciliation, lists "keeping financial records" as something the case manager helps with, says OPG may ask for receipts and statements, and describes posting the form to a PO Box. It does not name the categories. So the reconciliation pain is the full report's (OPG102 and the online service), not the short form's (*inference* from the two guidance pages and the forum thread).
- **Estate accounts, fetched** (*verified*): MSE "Templates for executry accounts and agreements?" (forums.moneysavingexpert.com/discussion/6242470, 16-17 February 2021, a Scottish executry). Two executors who are also the residuary beneficiaries want a template; the poster "can't find anything that clearly states how we should record payments" and says "There's so much conflicting advice out there". Replies: one reposts a layout (gross estate, liabilities, estate income and expenses, losses and gains, net estate, pecuniary legacies, residuary estate); one uses "an Excel spreadsheet, 5 columns" and says it "doesn't need to be sophisticated"; another calls that "perfectly adequate for most straightforward estates". No website, book, or software is named.
- Search summary of other MSE threads (*source claim*): only residuary beneficiaries are entitled to see estate accounts; no sign-off is needed; disputes are about co-executors not sharing accounts ("Executor refusing to provide final estate accounts", discussion/6278294) rather than about arithmetic.

### Searches 10-13 (insurance contents claims; UC self-employed reporting; deputy and attorney tools; OPG plans)

- **Contents list for a flood or theft claim.** The US flood scheme has an official worksheet: FEMA's NFIP personal property contents worksheet (FF-206-FY-21-106) asks for quantity, description, actual cash value, and amount of loss per item; the 2026 NFIP policyholder checklist asks for make, model, serial number, quantity, age, and replacement cost (*source claim*, search summary of fema.gov and agents.floodsmart.gov PDFs). An adjuster's page calls contents lists one of the most time-consuming parts of a flood claim (*source claim*). Insurers supply their own item-list templates (FMG, New Zealand, *source claim*); apps exist (Contents360, Contents Buddy, *source claim*). First reading: a long data-entry list with photos, an official worksheet already, apps already, and close to the settled home inventory. Screen out.
- **Universal Credit self-employed monthly reports.** The rule is real and official: assessment periods run from the claim date, not calendar months, with a reporting window from 7 days before to 14 days after the period's end; profit for UC is broadly cash in and out and differs from the tax cash basis (LITRG, Citizens Advice, councils, *source claim*); missing a report means the minimum income floor is assumed (*source claim*, Bromsgrove). No first-hand friction found in one search. The page could make only the date bucketing exact; what counts as a permitted expense, surplus earnings, and losses are benefits rules where a wrong figure costs money. Screen out (benefits stakes; bucketing by dates is one spreadsheet filter).
- **Tools for attorneys and deputies.** No UK app or free template for keeping a person's accounts in the OPG's categories found (two searches; *source claim*, absence only). Wrigleys (solicitors) on record keeping: a separate account for the person, keep statements, receipts, care invoices; the Court of Protection can order an attorney to give accounts (*source claim*, wrigleys.co.uk record-keeping page). **Singapore's OPG has an official "Finance Tracker" in OPG Online** for deputies to log money received and spent during the reporting period (*source claim*, msf.gov.sg): an official incumbent elsewhere, and a sign the job is recognised. No sign of open banking, statement import, or a tracker in the UK service (one search, *source claim* by absence); a 2015 OPG newsletter promised an easier form (*source claim*).

## Leads

### Lead 1. Finding where a deputy's annual account doesn't balance (working name: Deputy Ledger)

- **Audience:** lay property-and-affairs deputies appointed by the Court of Protection in England and Wales, usually an adult child or spouse running a parent's or partner's money, or a parent running a disabled adult child's, who report under general supervision (OPG102 or the online service). 64,456 deputyships were supervised at the end of 2025-26 (*verified*); how many are lay and general-supervision is not established here.
- **The job:** before, a year of statements for the client's main account or accounts (paper, PDF, or downloads), receipts and care invoices, and the reporting period from the OPG's letter. The report asks for money in and out by category, opening and closing balances, large one-off payments, gifts, and deputy expenses; the online report won't submit until it balances or the difference is explained (forum, *verified* as users' description; OPG102A: "you need to tell us why", *verified*). After, a report that balances first time, totals ready to type in, and a categorised list to send with it (OPG102A suggests sending a copy of a list kept through the year, *verified*).
- **Frequency / importance:** every year for the life of the deputyship, under supervision; an unexplained imbalance means "we may not be able to accept your report" (*verified*); the OPG may ask for statements and receipts (*verified*). Privacy: a vulnerable person's bank data.
- **Evidence of friction** (all *verified*, two threads on the Alzheimer's Society forum): "I've been working on it all day" (thread 159242, February 2025, over £3,000 out, then over £1,000, caused by payments entered twice and wrong opening balances); "I have already spent about 20 hours on it" and "I still can't get the balances to match" (thread 149395, May 2024, replies into October 2026); interest "entered twice in different categories"; missing bank charges; statements from a bank that "never turned up". About ten more first-report threads, among them "Any Accountants who can deal with the OPG form?" (*source claim*, titles).
- **Alternatives found:** the official online service, complete-deputy-report.service.gov.uk, with a built-in balance check (*source claim* plus users' descriptions); paper OPG102; a spreadsheet, which the OPG suggests; the OPG case manager; paid solicitors' lay-deputy services; the bank's own downloads. No free OPG-category template or app found in three searches (*source claim* by absence). Singapore's OPG has an official Finance Tracker for deputies (*source claim*): the job is recognised, and a UK equivalent could appear.
- **Strongest incumbent:** the OPG's own online report plus Excel. Its balance check seems to say only that the totals differ, not where (*inference* from the posts).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** reconcile account by account and statement by statement (opening + in - out = closing, checked against the bank's running balance), so the month and the line where it breaks show up; flag likely duplicates (same date, amount, payee; overlapping downloads); pair transfers between the client's own accounts; tag lines to the OPG's categories with remembered payee rules; give category totals, payments over £1,000, gifts, and deputy expenses ready to enter, and a CSV or print to attach. Everything stays on the device, saved to a file for the year. Possibly also serves attorneys who are asked for accounts (weak demand, below).
- **Labs-fit problems:** bank CSV formats vary a lot (the Off Book and Captions Up lesson); many deputies have paper or PDF statements, so line-by-line entry has to be quick; the OPG's category list and entry mode aren't verified and can change; a year of data needs file save and open, not just localStorage; England and Wales only (Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own regimes); it's practical on a phone but desktop-first in reality.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** a narrow audience (lay deputies on the full report; the short OPG103 form has no reconciliation, *verified*), with the pain concentrated in the first year (later reports "balanced first time", *source claim*). The official service already enforces the balance, and a careful user with downloads and a spreadsheet can do it. If the service wants every payment typed in, the page can't remove that typing, only make it right first time. A wrong category isn't advice and costs no money directly, but it feeds an official report, so the page must say it does the arithmetic and the user does the categorising.
- **Thread verdict:** **lead**, the strongest found. One fact to settle first: whether the online report takes one total per category or each payment, and its money-out categories.

### Lead 2. Estate accounts for a lay executor

- **Audience:** lay executors (often also the residuary beneficiaries) after probate or confirmation, accounting to the residuary beneficiaries.
- **The job:** before, date-of-death values, the executor account's statements, debts and expenses paid, legacies, interim payments. After, accounts (capital, income, distribution) showing how each residuary share was reached, net of interim payments.
- **Frequency / importance:** once per estate, but important: residuary beneficiaries are entitled to see the accounts (*source claim*, MSE threads).
- **Evidence of friction:** MSE "Templates for executry accounts and agreements?" (discussion/6242470, February 2021, *verified*): the poster "can't find anything that clearly states how we should record payments"; "There's so much conflicting advice out there". Template searches return SEO spam (*inference*). Disputes on MSE are about co-executors withholding accounts, not the arithmetic (*source claim*).
- **Alternatives found:** a simple ledger, which forum regulars call "perfectly adequate for most straightforward estates" (*verified*); layouts reposted on MSE (*verified*); EstateCopilot (UK, fixed fee, makes estate accounts, *source claim*); solicitors; EstateExec in the US, $199 (*source claim*).
- **Strongest incumbent:** a five-column spreadsheet; EstateCopilot for executors who want guidance.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** a structured page that turns the ledger into printable accounts and splits the residue by entered shares, interim payments netted, on the device.
- **Labs-fit problems:** none technical; the substance (who bears tax, interest on legacies, income against capital, partial intestacy, R185s) is legal judgment the page can't make exact.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the people who answer these questions say a plain spreadsheet is enough for most estates, and the hard parts aren't arithmetic.
- **Thread verdict:** **weak**.

### Lead 3. Attorneys' records under a financial LPA

- **Audience:** attorneys running a parent's money (10.6 million LPAs and EPAs registered, *verified*).
- **Evidence:** the 2019 forum thread (*verified*): what "reasonable accounts" means is unclear, but replies keep receipts and statements, find it not "onerous", and say nobody asked; the OPG looks if a concern is raised (*source claim*); the Court of Protection can order accounts (*source claim*, Wrigleys).
- **Thread verdict:** **weak** standalone, a secondary audience for Lead 1 at most.
