## Leads

### 1. A horse's flu vaccination record checked against an event's rule — **lead (the strongest)**

- **Name:** flu vaccination check (working titles: "Flu Jabs", "Passport Check").
- **Audience:** horse and pony owners in Great Britain who take horses to Pony Club and BHS Riding Club activities, BD, BE and BS competitions, carriage driving, agricultural and unaffiliated shows, clinics, or livery yards with a vaccination rule. Pony Club parents (companion horses count). The volunteer stewards, District Commissioners, secretaries and yard managers who check passports.
- **The job:**
  - Before: the vet has written each vaccination in the passport, and the owner is about to enter or travel to an event whose schedule states its rule, or is booking the vet.
  - After, for that event's date under that rule: does the record comply, and if not, which interval fails (V1–V2, V2–V3, a booster gap, a jab within 7 days, the show's "booster within six months"); whether the course must restart; the first day the horse may attend after a jab; and the latest day for the next booster under each rule the owner competes under.
  - For a steward: one passport checked at the ramp, quickly, often without a phone signal (*inference*).
- **Frequency / importance:** every event entered, and every booster (each 6 or 12 months). A horse that doesn't comply may not unload or compete, with no refund (BS, *verified*). A show can turn away every animal in the vehicle (New Forest Show 2026, *verified*). A missed interval means a restart and about four weeks out (*verified* rule; arithmetic *inference*). It matters most in outbreak years: 2019 and 2026 (*verified*).
- **Evidence of friction:**
  - The rule is several rules (*verified*, F3). There are two primary-course regimes by date, an amnesty some bodies accept and others don't, boosters every 12 months or within 6 months + 21 days or every 6 months, and a 7-day rule counted as the day plus the 6 days after.
  - Rules change mid-season, and shows add their own (*verified*, F8; *source claim*, BCD 2026).
  - Owners were confused about 6 or 12 months in the last outbreak (*verified*, F5).
  - Backdating cases show what a missed date costs (*source claim*).
  - Still missing: 2026 owners describing the work of checking dates.
- **Alternatives found:**
  - FEI HorseApp: FEI horses at FEI events only (*verified*).
  - Weatherbys/BHA app: racing only (*source claim*).
  - Vets' booster reminders (*inference*).
  - General horse apps with reminders typed in by hand (*source claim*).
  - The passport and the schedule, read by eye.
  - No free checker for Pony Club, BEF-body or show rules found (two searches).
- **Strongest incumbent:** the vet's booster reminder plus the owner reading the passport (*inference*).
- **Possible Labs wedge:**
  - The owner types the passport's vaccination dates once, for one horse or several; they stay in this browser, with a file to save.
  - Choose a rule set (Pony Club and BHS Riding Club, which share the amnesty clause, read here; FEI; BHA; BEF bodies once their handbooks are read) or describe a show's own rule with a few settings (booster within N months of the event; no jab within 7 days).
  - Choose the event date. Each interval is shown with the dates counted, the rule's own words and its source.
  - It explains a restart, gives the "book the next booster by" date for each rule, and prints a card to keep with the passport.
  - Steward mode: a fast yes or no, and why.
  - Works offline at a showground; nothing uploaded.
- **Labs-fit problems:** each rule set must be kept current, since bodies revise yearly and changed rules during 2026 (*verified* for the BS and New Forest notices). The page must state each rule's version and date, say that the organiser and the vet's record decide, and never call a horse "safe" or "protected" (*inference*). Date counting has edges a builder must settle from each rule's wording: "within 21–60 days" inclusive or not, "6 months + 21 days" at month ends, "7 full days". No veterinary judgment is involved: it is date arithmetic on dates a vet recorded. It fits the static limits: a few rows a horse, phone-first.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** for most owners the vet's reminder already gets the booster done in time. The hard cases (lapses, historic courses, the amnesty, a show's own wording) are where a wrong answer costs a wasted trip or an unneeded restart. Rule upkeep is ongoing. And demand may peak only in outbreak years.
- **To check next (main session):** BD, BE and BS member handbooks (the BEF bodies' current intervals), the FEI Veterinary Regulations' vaccination article, BHS Riding Club rules; 2026 owner or steward complaints (Horse & Hound forum, Facebook groups are out of reach); whether vets' practice software already prints rule-specific due dates.
- **Thread verdict:** **lead.** The rule creates the job, the bodies publish it themselves, it tightened in 2026, it's used offline at venues, and no free checker was found.

### 2. A smallholder's medicine record with withdrawal end dates — weak

- **Audience:** smallholders and hobby keepers of food-producing animals (sheep, goats, pigs, poultry, a few cattle) in the UK. Note (*inference*, not checked here): horses count as food-producing unless their passport signs them out of the food chain.
- **The job:** after each treatment, record the product, batch, date, quantity, withdrawal period and the animals treated, and keep proof of purchase. Before sending animals to slaughter or selling eggs or milk: know which animals are still within their withdrawal.
- **Frequency / importance:** each treatment. Records kept at least 5 years, even after the animal has gone, "durable, permanent" and available for inspection; they may be electronic (*source claim*, GOV.UK "Record keeping requirements for veterinary medicines" and Hampshire Trading Standards via search; not fetched). Red Tractor members also record the date the animal becomes fit for human consumption (*source claim*).
- **Evidence of friction:** none found in one search (no forum complaints surfaced).
- **Alternatives found** (*source claim*, S7):
  - GOV.UK names the AHDA Animal Medicines Record Book and a Pig Veterinary Society booklet.
  - Hampshire Trading Standards publishes a record format.
  - Red Tractor medicine administration record template (FAS Scotland download).
  - Farm Records (farmrecords.wlbp.co.uk): products picked from a VMD list with default withdrawal periods that can be overridden; who can use it wasn't checked.
  - AHDB guidance; commercial farm apps; US-style log templates.
- **Strongest incumbent:** the paper record book, or Farm Records' VMD-fed list (*source claim*).
- **Possible Labs wedge:** a withdrawal end date for each treatment, an "in withdrawal today" list, offline in the barn, an export.
- **Labs-fit problems:** five-year durable records sit badly in browser storage, so it would depend on saved files. Withdrawal periods must be typed from the label, since the VMD database would be a download.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** the only calculation is a date plus N days; free books and templates already have the column; the retention duty makes a browser page the wrong home.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

### 3. Self-check against an animal activity licence's higher standards (England) — weak

- **Audience:** home dog boarders, dog day cares, kennels, catteries, dog breeders, pet shops and riding schools applying for or renewing a licence under the 2018 Regulations.
- **The rule** (*source claim*: Cheltenham, Wyre Forest and Maidstone council pages via search; the matrix as reproduced from Defra's scoring by Calderdale):
  - To meet the higher standards, a business must meet every required higher standard and at least 50% of the optional ones.
  - Star rating: low risk gives 1, 3 or 5 stars (licences of 1, 2 or 3 years); higher risk gives 1, 2 or 4 stars (1, 1 or 2 years).
  - A business without three years' compliance history is automatically higher risk.
  - One rating covers all activities, and the lower result counts.
- **The job:** before the inspection, go through the conditions and higher standards. After: know whether minimum and higher standards are met, and so the likely band.
- **Alternatives found:** council self-assessment checklists for each activity (Kirklees PDFs for home boarding, kennels and day care; a Harborough docx; Winchester's licensee checklist; East Devon's document checklist); a SafetyCulture template (*source claim*, S8).
- **Evidence of friction:** none found.
- **Strongest incumbent:** the council's own checklist plus Defra's guidance for the activity.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** the activity's conditions as a checklist that counts required and optional higher standards and shows the possible rating band.
- **Labs-fit problems:** a lot of content (Defra guidance for each activity, OGL), revised from time to time. The risk half of the rating is the inspector's judgment.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** a once-in-one-to-three-years job, already served by free council forms; the count adds little.
- **Thread verdict:** weak.

### 4. Felling within the licence exemption, quarter by quarter (small woodland owners, England) — weak, under-researched

- **The rule** (*verified*, GOV.UK, "Tree felling licence: when you need to apply", published 8 July 2026):
  - Without a licence, an owner may fell up to "5 cubic metres within any of the following calendar quarters", of which "You can sell up to 2".
  - The licence rule covers trees of 8 cm or more over-bark diameter at 1.3 m (15 cm for coppice or underwood).
  - "Felling trees without a licence is an offence when one is required".
  - The page points to a one-page Forestry Commission "Timber Volume Calculator" (PDF) to estimate volume.
- **Audience:** owners of small woods cutting firewood or thinning through the year.
- **The job:** keep each quarter's felling under 5 m³, and under 2 m³ sold, from tree measurements. After: a running total per quarter.
- **Evidence of friction:** not searched (the search cap was hit).
- **Alternatives:** the FC's own calculator (official), a notebook, a forester.
- **Possible Labs wedge:** log each tree's measurements, total them against 5 and 2 m³ per quarter, offline in the wood.
- **Labs-fit problems:** the estimate must reproduce the FC's calculator exactly (not read). A generous estimate gives false comfort about an offence.
- **Strongest reason not to build:** an official calculator exists; no evidence of demand; the legal stakes.
- **Thread verdict:** weak until the FC calculator and owner evidence are read.
