Results and decisions follow below as they're made.

### Writing and lists (thread finished 2026-10-13)

- **Citation cross-check: REJECT.** Saturated with free tools since 2025. CiteMe's In-Text Citation Checker is free, needs no sign-up, says it runs in the browser, and handles author-date and numbered styles (*source claim*, citeme.app/tools/in-text-citation-checker). RefRunner says the same (*source claim*). Recite is paid beyond 2 uploads a day, uploads files, and needs a Google login (*verified*). Paul Beverley's free CitationAlyse macro works in Word. Labs would be about the tenth.
- **Consistency check for long documents: REJECT.** The job ends inside Word, where it's already covered: PerfectIt (offline on Windows; on Mac and iPad its cloud version uploads the document, *verified*), Paul Beverley's free macros (local, PC and Mac), and Intelligent Editing's free Google Docs add-on (*verified*). A separate browser report adds a step and fixes nothing.
- **Compare two lists of names: open (decided below with the other results).** Flookup's free Fuzzy Matching Tool runs in the browser with no sign-up but gives pair similarity scores, not "missing from each side" lists, and has no name-specific handling (*verified* from its page; not tried). molbiotools List Compare is exact matching only (*verified*). Excel's fuzzy merge in Power Query exists in Microsoft 365 and is technical (*verified*). The thread's search allowance ran out before forum evidence of how often non-technical people hit name-format mismatches.

### Files, names, tournaments (thread finished 2026-10-13)

- **Statement to spreadsheet: REJECT.** The exact tool already exists for free, several times over: free browser converters that say they process locally, need no account, and check every running balance (ExcelTool.io, Autymate, StatementToExcel; *verified* that the pages say so, local processing untested), and at least six more without the balance check. Bank CSV downloads reach back only months (HSBC about 90 days, Lloyds about 12; *source claim*), so the need is real, but Labs would be about the tenth, and accuracy needs per-bank tuning Labs can't maintain.
- **Learn students' names from photos: REJECT.** School rules, not uploading, are the barrier: policies keep pupil photos on approved systems, and "runs locally" doesn't make an unvetted site approved. Approved tools already quiz (Seating Plan's name learning, *verified*; Columbia's and UVA's photo rosters), and What Student? is free on iPhone and iPad with data on the device (*verified*).
- **Tournament day schedule: open (decided below).** Tournify is free only up to 8 teams, then €40 per event, needs an organizer login, and its planner doesn't enforce rest gaps (*verified*); MeinTurnierplan is German-only with watermarked free PDFs (*verified*); LeagueLobster's rest limits are in a $29.95/month plan (*verified*). Not yet checked (search allowance ran out): free English-language schedulers, offline phone apps, spreadsheet templates with rest gaps. Ceiling: organizers increasingly want live results for parents, which needs shared state.

### School and events (thread finished 2026-10-13)

- **Exam clock with extra time: open, the strongest so far (verified below before any decision).** The thread's evidence: JCQ's supervised rest breaks pause and restart a candidate's exam time (AARA 5.1.2), and centres track them on paper sheets with time-out, time-back, and revised-finish columns (*source claim*, school forms). JCQ's 2026 Instructions are said to require the exam-room clock to show the actual time, with countdown and count-up clocks not permitted (*to verify*). The Exams Office's Exam Room Clock Tool is compliant but needs a £395+VAT membership and a login, and pauses only the whole room (*verified* by the thread). ExamClock pauses one student, but its free plan is one session with sign-up, candidate entries are stored on its servers, and its display is a timer (*verified* by the thread).
- **Quiz night scorer: REJECT.** Adding up and sorting: free spreadsheets with jokers built in (Tes, 2013) and no-account web scoresheets (ScoreLeader) already do it (*verified* by the thread).
- **Volunteer rota: open (decided below).** Planning Center auto-schedules but is free for only 5 team members; RosterThat is free for 1 admin, 20 people, and 2 rosters, with a claimed fair auto-fill (*verified*/*source claim*); SignUp.com is self-signup, not allocation. Unfinished: whether a free, no-sign-up tool already makes a fair rota from unavailability for 20+ people.
- **Event door check-in: REJECT.** Ticket Tailor's free check-in app works offline and syncs several phones (*verified* by the thread); paper covers unticketed events; the case where software beats paper (several people at one door) needs shared live state.
- **School carpool rota: REJECT.** A fixed weekday plus a group chat covers it; swaps are live shared state; it overlaps Custody Calendar's pattern-to-calendar shape. GoKid still operates and deliberately lets families claim slots rather than auto-assigning (*verified* by the thread).

### Outdoors, clubs, health (thread finished 2026-10-13)

- **Hillwalking route card: REJECT.** The main audience already has a free official generator: eDofE Mapping fills grid references, distance, height climbed, and timing on OS maps (*source claim*; a 2026 school DofE booklet says the same). A useful card needs distance and climb along the path, so a map, and OS mapping needs a key; Open-Meteo's keyless elevation is 90 m (*verified* by the thread), too coarse for a leg's climb. Bearings are left to participants on purpose: planning is assessed.
- **Cross-country team scoring: HOLD.** Unresolved: do enough small meets score by hand at the course, and would hosts use a phone instead of a paper sheet? Cross Country Scorer (a free Google Sheets add-on, 27K+ installs, updated August 2026) covers US rules; Athletic.net scores uploaded results; the free iPhone apps have almost no ratings (*verified* by the thread). A 4-team meet is minutes by hand. Doesn't block the batch.
- **Small-club year-end accounts: HOLD.** Unresolved: can most small-club treasurers download a whole year as CSV from their bank (CAF Bank, Unity Trust, Co-op, high-street community accounts)? If not, the tool falls back to typing every line, which the free cashbooks from parent bodies and the Charity Commission's CC16a template already handle (*verified*: CC16a is free, no account, last updated 2017). Timely: for years ending on or after 30 September 2026, independent examination is needed only above £40k (*verified* by the thread, gov.uk). Yearly use makes lost browser storage a real risk.
- **Home blood pressure averages: REJECT.** Trivial arithmetic in a setting run by clinicians' own protocols and forms; protocols differ (NICE, Blood Pressure UK, BHF, AHA); any "high/normal" label drifts toward a regulated medical device (MHRA guidance, *verified* by the thread). Free record cards exist (Blood Pressure UK, BHF).
- **Raffle tickets: REJECT.** At-event raffles need nothing printed (Gambling Commission, *verified* by the thread) and use cloakroom ticket books; advance sales have Avery's free numbering or cheap perforated printing; plain paper means hours of cutting.

### Exam clock: the decisive rule, checked here

JCQ's *Instructions for conducting examinations*, effective 1 September 2026 (jcq.org.uk/knowledge-hub/intructions-for-conducting-examinations/, read 2026-10-13, *verified*): §11.7 "A reliable clock (analogue and/or digital) must be visible to each candidate in the examination room when seated… The clock must show the actual time. Countdown and 'count up' clocks are not permissible." §11.8: a board must show "the actual starting and finishing times and date of each examination." §6.1: "Candidates must always be allowed the full amount of time for the examination." §12.6: an invigilator may have a mobile phone only to summon help. So a timer-style display can't be the exam-room clock, and the board's finish times are what candidates rely on.

### Documents and privacy (thread finished 2026-10-13)

- **Redact before sending: REJECT.** Already built, free and on the device: Jasper Bernaers' PDF Redactor (search and "redact all matches", hand-drawn boxes, pages rebuilt as images, then the saved file re-checked; no account; *verified* by the thread), BentoPDF (local, AGPL), PDFGadget (local, image output). And the riskiest recipients refuse the output: the High Court held on 19 May 2026 that DWP "is entitled to require unredacted statements" (Webb v SSWP [2026] EWHC 1581 (Admin), *verified* by the thread); US rental screening through Snappt marks edited or re-rendered PDFs "Denied: Edited Docs" (*verified* by the thread). Building it could hurt users.
- **Watermark an ID copy: REJECT.** Solved where the practice is common: FiligraneFacile (French government, free, no account; files processed on its servers and deleted), DossierFacile watermarks rental files, the Dutch KopieID app; and a watermark protects little. Would be a feature of Photo Scrub, not an experiment.
- **Move-in condition report: REJECT.** Phone photos plus an email are the evidence deposit schemes want (Shelter, *verified* by the thread), and burden-of-proof rules do the rest; the move-out comparison needs a year of storage, which Safari's 7-day cap on unvisited sites undermines.
- **Anonymize interview transcripts: open, a strong survivor (checked below).** The free options are a highlight-only Word macro (UK Data Service: it "does not anonymise or make changes", *verified* by the thread), QualiAnon (free local Java desktop app, .docx only, 16 GB RAM recommended), Python libraries, and paid apps (Textwash Pro $330/yr, MAXQDA turns text into "XXX"). Researchers avoid uploading interviews (the free local transcribers noScribe and aTrain exist for that reason, and neither anonymizes). GDPR counts data as pseudonymized only with the key "kept separately" (Art. 4(5)). Unfinished: a search for browser-based transcript pseudonymizers. Risk: suggestions can falsely reassure about indirect identifiers (job titles, events).

### Music, hiring, theater (thread finished 2026-10-13)

- **Part-writing checker: open, a narrow survivor.** Free coverage is partial: MuseScore's "Check Harmony Rules" plugin (free, local, MuseScore 4.4+) checks parallel, contrary, and exposed 5ths and 8ves and leaps, but not crossing, spacing, range, doubling, or leading-tone and seventh resolution (*verified* by the thread); Musition has the full rule set at $39 a year per student through a school code (*verified*); Counterpointer is $39.99, Mac only. Three searches found no free web checker that takes SATB MusicXML. Limits: AP part-writing stays handwritten even in the 2027 hybrid exam (*verified* by the thread), so the audience is students who typeset (college courses that require notation software, Noteflight and Flat users on Chromebooks); rules differ by textbook; non-chord tones invite false positives.
- **Anonymous CVs for blind shortlisting: REJECT.** Infrequent for small organizations, often designed away with application forms, and voluntary (neither GOV.UK nor ACAS guidance asks for it, *verified* by the thread); a heuristic redactor fails silently on two-column and scanned CVs, and the harm falls on candidates.
- **Scene breakdown and rehearsal planner: HOLD.** Unresolved: does an Off Book-style parser give an accurate character-by-scene breakdown, silent entrances included, on real licensed scripts? Also overlaps Off Book's parser; the availability arithmetic is a spreadsheet job. Doesn't block the batch.

## Decisions after the gate (2026-10-13)

- **BUILD → #026 Exam Timekeeper** (`exam-timekeeper`). Checked here before numbering:
  - **The rule** (*verified*, JCQ ICE effective 1 September 2026, §11.7–11.8): the exam-room clock must show the actual time; countdown and count-up clocks aren't permitted; a board shows the actual start and finish times.
  - **The pain** (*source claim*, The Exams Office, November 2024): "a large number of candidates" have supervised rest breaks, there's "confusion in a significant number of centres about how these breaks should be managed", and breaks have increased because JCQ requires them to be trialled in mocks and timed tests before 25% extra time is applied for. A single break should be no more than 30 minutes and isn't normally needed in the first 10 minutes. Universities and professional bodies run the same "stop the clock" breaks with paper slips on the desk showing the revised finish (LSE, IFoA, Teesside; *source claim*).
  - **The alternatives** (*verified* today): The Exams Office's Exam Room Clock Tool needs a centre membership and a login, and pauses only the whole room; ExamClock needs an account, and individual rest-break pausing is in Pro at $89 a year; Mega Seating Plan's free exam clock is a countdown, which JCQ exam rooms can't use; a £5 Tes HTML timer adds fixed 5-minute breaks for up to 10 students (no reviews); keesL/ExamClock (GitHub) shows the time with a closing countdown and no provisions. No free, no-account tool found shows the actual time and the board and pauses individual candidates.
  - **Rounding:** no JCQ rule found; awarding bodies' worked examples differ (AQA uses seconds, Pearson rounds). The tool counts to the second and only ever rounds a shown finish time up.
  - **Strongest case against:** paper slips work and exams officers are cautious; a wrong finish time is maladministration; a centre may not let staff use an unapproved site; The Exams Office could add individual breaks. None is fatal: the arithmetic is small and testable, nothing leaves the laptop, and the record prints.
  - **Name:** no product called "Exam Timekeeper" found; "ExamClock" and "Exam Room Clock Tool" are other products' names, so they're avoided.
- **Next survivors, in order:** anonymize interview transcripts (after its one search: browser-based transcript pseudonymizers); part-writing checker (narrow). Each goes through its own final check after #026 ships.
- **HOLD (don't block the batch):** compare two lists of names (does Flookup's free tool already give "missing from each side"?); tournament day schedule (does a free, no-account tool schedule pitches with rest gaps offline?); volunteer rota (does a free, no-sign-up tool make a fair rota for 20+ people?); cross-country scoring (do enough meets score by hand?); small-club year-end accounts (can treasurers get a year of bank CSV?); scene breakdown (is script parsing accurate enough?).
