## 2026-10-09 · #018 Off Book

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/off-book](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/off-book/). For actors learning lines. Paste a script, or open a text file, and Off Book finds the characters and how many speeches each has. It handles NAME: and NAME. layouts, names on their own line as in screenplays, scene headings, and directions in brackets or parentheses. Pick your part (or several, for doubling) and the scene. Cue cards show the line before yours and hide yours. "Line?" gives the next few words, "Show my line" checks it, and you mark it got or missed, so you can then rerun just the misses. Read through shows the scene with your lines tinted, in full, as first letters (T b, o n t b), or hidden until tapped. The device's voice can read the cues aloud, and there are keyboard shortcuts. The script, part, and how each line went are kept on the device. A sample scene, the public-domain opening of The Importance of Being Earnest, shows how it works.
- **Problem it tests:** Every actor in every school, community, and professional show has to learn lines, usually by covering the page with a card or by recruiting a friend to run lines. The tools that help are mostly iPhone and iPad apps with paid tiers, and the few web ones are small or only take audition snippets. Is a free page that works on any device, from a pasted script, good enough to replace the index card?
- **Scope decisions:** No PDF or Word reading, since that needs heavy libraries: you copy the text and paste it. There are no recordings, no voice recognition to check what you said (you judge your own line), and no cast sharing. A mixed-case "Name:" seen only once is treated as part of a sentence ("Well: I suppose so"), which can swallow a one-line minor character written in mixed case. Cues are the previous speech by someone else plus any directions after it, up to three items. Progress is tied to the exact script text, so editing the script starts it fresh.
- **Limitations:** Scripts pasted from PDFs can arrive with broken line order, page numbers, or names split from their speeches, and those need tidying by hand. Two characters sharing a line (ALL, BOTH) count as their own character. Read-aloud cues use the device's voices, which vary by browser, and some of them work online. It was checked with the Earnest scene, made-up scripts in each layout, and scripted practice runs, not yet by actors learning a real show.
- **Revisit if:** actors use it for a production. Likely asks are recording your own cue lines in the right voices, choosing how much cue to show, a "speed run" that just shows cues, and better repair of PDF paste-ins.

## 2026-10-09 · #017 Frost Dates

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/frost-dates](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/frost-dates/). Search for a town anywhere in the world, or use the device's location. Frost Dates reads the last 30 years of overnight lows there and shows the last frost in spring and the first in autumn. Each comes as a typical date (half of years are later or earlier) and a cautious one (9 years in 10). It also shows the frost-free season, how many frost nights a year are usual, and what this season has brought so far. A chart marks every frost night of every season since 1996, with a table of each season's first and last frost. Frost can mean a light frost (36°F, 2°C), a frost (32°F, 0°C), or a hard freeze (28°F, −2°C), in °C or °F. A planting calendar for 13 common crops gives dates to start seeds indoors, sow outside, and plant out, counted from the typical or the cautious last frost. Southern-hemisphere seasons run from one summer to the next. The place and settings are remembered, and the last place's records are kept on the device for a few days.
- **Problem it tests:** Every seed packet says "sow 6 weeks before your last frost", and the frost-date lookups that answer it cover only the US and Canada, from fixed station tables. Is a page that works out the odds for any place, from records near it, useful to gardeners outside North America, and clear enough for beginners anywhere?
- **Scope decisions:** Frost is the air two meters up falling to the chosen temperature, as in official frost tables. The records are Open-Meteo's historical archive, built from the ERA5 and ERA5-Land reanalyses and ECMWF's forecast model, on a grid about 10 km across. It's free and needs no key, and it's credited as its license asks. Each season runs from the warmest month to the next, so one autumn, winter, and spring stay together in either hemisphere. The odds are simply counted over the 30 most recent complete seasons, with no fitted curve. Checked against NOAA-based dates for Boston, Denver, Minneapolis, Atlanta, and Portland (Oregon), the dates came within 8 days, except downtown Portland: 19 days later in spring and 12 days earlier in autumn, the cautious direction. The planting calendar uses common rules of thumb from seed packets and extension guides. One request of about 200 KB fetches the records, which are then kept on the device.
- **Limitations:** A 10 km grid can't see a frost pocket at the bottom of a garden or the warmth of a city, so local frost can come a week or more earlier or later, and valley sites can get many more frost nights. The light-frost setting helps there. Elevation follows the archive's terrain model, not the garden's. Crop timings vary by variety and climate, and the list is short. It needs a connection the first time for each place. The free archive limits how many requests one connection can make in a day, and the page says so when it's busy.
- **Revisit if:** gardeners outside the US use it. Natural next steps are a frost-risk forecast for the coming nights, an elevation adjustment for hillside gardens, autumn sowing dates counted back from the first frost, and more crops.

## 2026-10-09 · #016 Fluency Check

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/fluency-check](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/fluency-check/). For teachers, tutors, and reading specialists giving oral reading fluency checks. Paste the passage, or try one of three practice passages, and optionally add the student's name and a goal. Start the clock as the student begins reading aloud from their own copy, and tap each word they misread. A second tap marks a self-correction and a third clears it. After one minute, a tone sounds and you tap the last word read; there's also a mode that times the whole passage. You get words correct per minute, accuracy, errors, self-corrections, and the missed words. There's a marked record to print, a summary to copy, and a large-type copy of the passage to print for the student. Results can be saved on the device, with a progress chart for each student and a CSV download.
- **Problem it tests:** Fluency checks are a routine part of teaching reading, often weekly for students getting extra support. Most teachers still do them with a paper copy, a pencil, and a stopwatch, and then do the arithmetic. The digital options are paid apps, publisher platforms that need a school license, or spreadsheet add-ons that only do the math. Will teachers use a free page that times, marks, and scores in one place, without putting student data in anyone's cloud?
- **Scope decisions:** Words are counted by spaces, with punctuation attached to its word and a dash between two words splitting them. Scoring follows the usual rules: words read minus errors, scaled to a minute when the reading is shorter or longer. Marks after the last word read don't count. Accuracy bands follow common guidelines: 95% and up is comfortable, 90 to 94% challenging, and below 90% too hard for now. Grade-level norms aren't built in, because the widely used table may not be reproduced. Instead there's a goal field and a link to the published norms. The practice passages were written for the tool and aren't leveled. Nothing leaves the device, and the log keeps the numbers and the missed words, not the marked passage.
- **Limitations:** Languages written without spaces between words, such as Chinese, Japanese, and Thai, can't be counted. There's no audio recording, no error types beyond error and self-correction, and no scoring of expression or comprehension. The only way to share results between devices is the CSV. Saved checks live in one browser, so clearing its site data deletes them. The timer and marking were tested with scripted taps and simulated time in a desktop browser and emulated phone sizes, not yet by a teacher with a student.
- **Revisit if:** teachers use it for progress monitoring. Likely asks are error types (substitution, omission, hesitation) for running records, a recording to listen back to, importing passages from a PDF, and built-in benchmarks from programs that allow it.

## 2026-10-09 · #015 Back Row

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/back-row](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/back-row/). Choose a picture of a slide, poster, or sign (or drop it, paste a screenshot, or try the built-in sample slide). Say whether it's shown on a screen or as a poster, how wide it is, how far away the viewer is, and their eyesight: good (20/20), fair (20/40), or low vision (20/70). Back Row blurs away the detail that viewer can't resolve from there. You can see it enlarged, to look closely, or at its true apparent size for you at your device, with an optional bank-card calibration. It also states the capital-letter height that can just be read and the height that reads comfortably. For screens, it gives the matching minimum font size on a standard slide (about 30 pt for the back of a 15 m room on a 3 m screen). Meters or feet. Settings are remembered; the picture is never stored or sent.
- **Problem it tests:** Presenters, teachers, and sign makers design at arm's length and find out at the event that the back half of the room can't read the slide. Font-size rules of thumb exist, but nobody can see their own slide from the back row in advance. Is a quick, honest simulation enough to change what people put on slides?
- **Scope decisions:** Eyesight is modeled as a minimum angle of resolution (1, 2, or 3.5 arcminutes). Letters are just readable at five times that (the Snellen standard) and comfortable at three times more, and the blur removes detail finer than the resolvable size on the screen. Only blur is simulated: no glare, contrast loss, projector washout, or color. Slides are assumed to be 16:9 for the point-size advice. The true-size view uses the CSS standard of 96 pixels per inch until you calibrate.
- **Limitations:** It's a model of acuity, not of any one person's eyes or of room lighting, and contrast often matters as much as size. It can't open PowerPoint, Keynote, or PDF files directly, so it needs a screenshot. The true-size view is only as accurate as the calibration and how far you actually sit from your screen. Tested against eye-chart and slide-size references and with generated images, not with real audiences.
- **Revisit if:** presenters use it before talks. Natural next steps are opening PDFs and slide decks page by page, a contrast check for projected colors, and a "smallest text on this slide" detector that flags lines below the readable size.

## 2026-10-09 · #014 Playing Time

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/playing-time](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/playing-time/). For volunteer coaches. Enter the roster, tap anyone not here today, and set the format: players on the field at once, periods and their length, and how often to change. Presets cover soccer 7v7 and 9v9, basketball, and hockey. Optionally a goalkeeper plays each whole period, and the job rotates. You get a plan that gives everyone minutes as even as the format allows: a grid of who plays each shift, minutes per player, and a "from the sideline" list of who comes off and goes on at each break. Shuffle gives another fair plan. It prints on one page, and the roster and format are remembered on the device.
- **Problem it tests:** Fair playing time is the most common worry in youth sports and the hardest thing to manage from the sideline with a clipboard. The tools that do it are apps with paid tiers, or basketball-only. Will coaches use a free page that does the math before the game?
- **Scope decisions:** Changes happen only at set breaks, and a very short tail of a period joins the shift before it. Shifts go to whoever is furthest behind their fair share for the time they have left. That means a player due in goal later gets their field time first, and nobody sits twice in a row when the bench allows it. Keepers are chosen up front, rotating, so their time in goal counts from the start. Positions, skill balancing, and live game timing are left out on purpose.
- **Limitations:** It plans and doesn't time the game, so injuries, late arrivals, and tired legs mean adjusting on the fly. When a period in goal is longer than a fair share, as with a small number on the field, keepers end up with more minutes. Everyone else stays within one shift of each other. There's no sharing with assistant coaches or parents beyond printing.
- **Revisit if:** coaches use it on game days. Likely asks are positions or "must play" constraints, marking substitutions as they happen with a running tally, and season totals across games.

## 2026-10-09 · #013 In Tune

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/in-tune](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/in-tune/). Singing practice for intervals. Pick your voice (low, middle, or high), the intervals to work on (from the same note up to an octave), up, down, or both, and how close counts (within 50, 30, or 15 cents). In Tune plays a reference note and asks for, say, "a major third up from G3". As you sing, a meter shows your pitch against the target, with words to match: "2 semitones too low", "12 cents sharp: go a little lower", "in tune, hold it". Holding it in tune for a second counts as a match. You can replay the note, hear the answer, or skip. Settings are remembered on the device.
- **Problem it tests:** Singers in choirs and lessons need to find intervals by ear and stay in tune, but practicing alone gives no feedback. The free tools online are mostly freemium or only test your range. Can a page that listens and shows the pitch live make solo interval practice useful?
- **Scope decisions:** Pitch detection uses the YIN method, running in the browser on the live microphone signal. Nothing is recorded, stored, or sent. Voice processing for calls (echo cancellation, noise suppression, auto gain) is turned off, because it distorts pitch. Listening pauses while the reference plays, so speakers don't count as singing. Readings are smoothed over the last few frames to ignore stray octave jumps. Notes use sharps and flats in common spellings, and the meter spans a semitone either side of the target.
- **Limitations:** It needs a microphone and permission to use it. Speakers without headphones can leak into the reading in a loud room. Breathy or very low voices may read less steadily. There's no pitch history, scoring over time, or melody singing yet. It was checked with synthetic voices, both in the detector's tests (within 3 cents from E2 to G5) and by feeding a generated voice through the full page in place of the microphone. The test browser couldn't open a real microphone, so it hasn't been tried with a person singing.
- **Revisit if:** singers use it. Natural next steps are a pitch trace over time, singing short melodies or arpeggios, and a range finder that sets the voice for you.

## 2026-10-09 · #012 Glance

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/glance](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/glance/). A daily estimation game. Five fields of dots each appear for about a second: a few dots, big and small ones, dots in groups, squares among circles (count only the squares), and a crowd. After each one you guess how many you saw, then see the field again with the answer and your points. Everyone gets the same five on a given day, and a new set arrives at local midnight. Results come as a score out of 500 with a row of colored squares to share. There's an unlimited practice mode, and the device remembers your days, best, average, and streak.
- **Problem it tests:** Daily puzzles with shareable results are a popular habit, and number sense (estimating quantities at a glance) is a real skill that few games train. Will people come back for a 30-second daily estimation round?
- **Scope decisions:** Points depend on the ratio between guess and answer, so being 20% high counts the same as 20% low: 100 for exact, falling to 0 at half or double. Puzzles are generated from the date, so there's no server, no leaderboard, and no accounts. Shapes never overlap, and squares have the same area as circles so size gives nothing away. The squares round uses shape, not color, so it works for color-blind players.
- **Limitations:** The game is visual, so it doesn't work with a screen reader. Nothing stops replaying a day in another browser or reading the page's code, which is fine for a game played for fun. Display time is a timer, so a very slow device may show the dots slightly longer. Streaks live only in this browser.
- **Revisit if:** people share results. Possible next steps are a weekly "hard mode", a results calendar, and showing how far off typical guesses are (which would need a shared count, so not as a static site).

## 2026-10-09 · #011 Line Dry

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/line-dry](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/line-dry/). Search for a town or use the device's location, and choose shirts and sheets or towels and jeans. For today and each of the next six days, Line Dry says whether it's a good day to dry washing outside, when to hang it out, and when it should be dry. Otherwise it explains why not: rain coming, too damp and dull, or too late in the day. An hour-by-hour chart shows drying power (taller bars dry faster), rainy hours striped, and the suggested time out shaded; tapping an hour shows its temperature, humidity, wind, and chance of rain. The place and load type are remembered on the device.
- **Problem it tests:** Drying outside saves money and energy, but deciding is guesswork from a general weather forecast: will it rain before the towels are dry? Laundry forecasts exist as phone apps. Is a free web page that answers "when should I hang it out, and when will it be dry?" useful enough to check on wash days?
- **Scope decisions:** Drying power is the forecast's reference evapotranspiration, which already combines sun, warmth, dry air, and wind. A load needs a set amount of it (0.8 mm for light washing, 1.6 mm for heavy, calibrated against everyday drying times). Rain of 0.1 mm or more, or an even chance of it, stops drying, and a 30–49% chance is flagged as a possible shower. Drying counts until an hour after sunset. Of the start times that dry soonest, it picks the latest, so washing isn't out longer than needed. The forecast comes from Open-Meteo, which is free and needs no key, with credit given as its license asks. Only the place, rounded to about a kilometer, is sent. No notifications or accounts.
- **Limitations:** These are estimates. Shade, shelter, fabric, and how full the line is change real drying times, and the calibration comes from rules of thumb, not measured loads. Forecast showers are uncertain. There's no indoor drying advice. Times are the place's own local time. It needs a connection, and checks were made with saved real forecasts for London and Madrid plus a live check for Edinburgh.
- **Revisit if:** people use it on wash days. Next steps would be a reminder to bring washing in before rain (which needs notifications), separate settings for sun and shade, and learning from "it was dry by…" feedback stored on the device.

## 2026-10-09 · #010 Gallery Wall

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/gallery-wall](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/gallery-wall/). Enter each frame's width and height and how it hangs: a wire or a single hook (with how far below the top it hangs from) or two hooks (with how far apart they are). Arrange the frames in a row or a grid with a set gap, lined up by middles, tops, or bottoms. Choose the height of the group's middle or bottom edge (57 in, about 145 cm, is the gallery standard). Center the group on the wall (give its width) or on a pencil mark, such as over a sofa. You get a drawing of the wall to scale and the exact spot for every nail, measured up from the floor and across from the wall's edge or the mark. Searching for a gallery wall calculator turns up blog posts with worked math and spam pages, not a tool. Does a quick planner that does the math and shows the result earn a place in a move or a redecoration?
- **Scope decisions:** Rows and grids only, with no free-form salon layouts and no dragging. Frames stay in the order entered (with a move-earlier button), and a short last grid row is centered. All frames share one gap. There's no wall photo or AR, just a clean drawing. The measuring hint for wire drop is built in, since that measurement is the usual source of error.
- **Limitations:** It assumes a level floor and a wire that comes up to a single point at its middle. Frames in a grid line up within each row, not across rows of different heights. Doors, windows, and outlets aren't modeled, so check the drawing against the real wall. Not tested yet with an actual hammer and wall.
- **Revisit if:** people use it before hanging art. Natural next steps are drag-to-arrange salon layouts, a staircase layout that follows the rise of the steps, and "measure from the previous nail" steps for hanging without a long tape.

## 2026-10-09 · #008 Serve Time

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/serve-time](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/serve-time/). After a reload in cooking mode, the sound needs one tap to come back. The preheat time is a fixed 15 minutes. Times follow the device clock and time zone. The timing logic is tested with automated checks and simulated clocks; it hasn't been used to cook a real meal yet.
- **Revisit if:** someone cooks a holiday meal with it. The likely asks are a "serve later" offset per dish (dessert), burner limits, sharing a plan with a helper's phone, and saved plans for recurring meals.

## 2026-10-09 · #005 CSV Checkup

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/csv-checkup](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/csv-checkup/). Drop in a CSV, TSV, or other delimited export to get a health report. It works out the separator, encoding, and header row, then shows row and column counts and the share of empty values. Each column gets its kind of data (numbers, dates, emails, yes/no, text), how full it is, distinct values, range or most common values, and its longest value. A plain-language issue list covers repeated rows, rows with the wrong number of fields, blank lines, duplicate or missing column names, stray text in number or date columns, codes with leading zeros that spreadsheets drop, mixed date formats, and stray spaces. There's also a preview of the first 50 rows and a report to copy as Markdown. Files are streamed in pieces, about 12 MB in half a second, and are never uploaded or stored.
- **Problem it tests:** People receive exports from stores, CRMs, surveys, and data portals, and either open them in a spreadsheet that silently mangles them (leading zeros, dates) or upload customer data to random websites to check them. Is a private, instant "what's in this file and what's wrong with it" check something people return to?
- **Scope decisions:** Read-only, so it doesn't edit, clean, or convert. No Excel files: you're asked to save as CSV. Counts are exact except for columns with more than 20,000 different values (marked "+") and duplicate checks past 2 million rows. Columns of zero-padded codes like ZIPs aren't summarized as numbers. One data color, validated for both light and dark backgrounds.
- **Limitations:** Type detection is pattern-based, and dates like 03/04/2026 are assumed month-first unless the first number can't be a month. Encodings other than UTF-8 and Windows-1252 aren't detected. Very wide files (hundreds of columns) work but make a long page. Nothing is charted beyond the top-value bars.
- **Revisit if:** people use it before imports or migrations. The natural next steps are fixing what it finds (trimming spaces, removing repeats, exporting a cleaned CSV) and comparing two versions of the same export.

## 2026-10-09 · #004 Clean Paste

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/clean-paste](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/clean-paste/). Paste text copied from a PDF or an email and get readable paragraphs back, live as you type, with a copy button. It rejoins lines broken mid-sentence and words split by hyphens, keeping real compounds like "well-known" when the text writes them that way elsewhere. It also removes page numbers and headers repeated on every page, keeps bulleted and numbered lists, strips email ">" marks, and fixes ligatures (ﬁ), odd spaces, and invisible characters. Chinese and Japanese lines are joined without inserting spaces. A summary says what it changed, and the options are remembered; the text isn't.
- **Problem it tests:** Copying from PDFs is a daily annoyance for students, researchers, and office workers, and typical "remove line breaks" sites flatten everything into one block. Does a version that understands paragraphs, lists, and page clutter earn a spot in someone's routine?
- **Scope decisions:** Rule-based and instant, with no AI and no dictionary. Lines are only rejoined when the text looks hard-wrapped at a fixed width, so text with one paragraph per line, and poems, pass through untouched. Headers are only dropped when they repeat far apart, so a song's repeated chorus survives. No file upload or PDF reading, just pasting.
- **Limitations:** These are heuristics, so a paragraph whose last line happens to be nearly full width can be merged with the next one, and a hyphenated compound that appears only at a line break loses its hyphen. Columns, tables, and footnotes copied from PDFs often arrive scrambled and aren't untangled. Thai and other languages written without spaces are only partly handled.
- **Revisit if:** people paste real documents and keep coming back. The next step would be a "differences" view that highlights what changed, or reading a PDF file directly.

## 2026-10-09 · #003 Fair Share

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/fair-share](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/fair-share/). Start a group with names and a currency, add who paid for what, split equally among any of the people, and see each person's balance and the fewest payments that settle everyone up. "Mark paid" records a payment. Amounts are kept in whole cents, so shares always add up exactly, and both "12,50" and "12.50" are understood. The whole group is compressed into the page address after "#", which browsers never send to the server. Bookmarking or sharing that address is how you save and share it, and a short list of recent groups is remembered on the device.
- **Problem it tests:** Splitting costs among friends or roommates is common and fiddly. The best-known tool needs everyone to have an account, and its free plan is limited. Is a no-account version that lives entirely in a link good enough for a weekend trip or a shared dinner?
- **Scope decisions:** Equal splits among chosen people only, with no custom shares, percentages, or itemized receipts. One currency per group, with no exchange rates. There's no sync: whoever edits gets an updated link to pass on. Links are strictly validated, and decompression is capped so a crafted link can't freeze the page.
- **Limitations:** Edits on different phones don't merge, so two people adding expenses separately end up with two versions. Links grow with the number of expenses, though a realistic weekend stays under about 300 characters. The currency can't change after the group is created, and anyone with the link can see every name and amount.
- **Revisit if:** groups get passed around by link, or people ask for uneven splits or a way to merge two versions of a group. Uneven splits would be a small change, and merging would be the real test of the link-only model.

## 2026-10-09 · #002 Photo Scrub

- **What:** [labs.eugeneyip.net/photo-scrub](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/photo-scrub/). Drop in or choose JPEG, PNG, or WebP photos to see the hidden details they carry: GPS position (with a map link), place names, camera and serial numbers, dates, author and copyright, software, edit history, embedded thumbnails, and extra embedded images. It then gives you clean copies, one at a time, as a ZIP, or through the phone's share sheet. Cleaning copies the image data byte for byte and keeps only the orientation and color profile, so quality doesn't change, and each clean copy is read back to confirm nothing is left. Nothing is uploaded or stored.
- **Problem it tests:** Phone photos record exactly where they were taken, and people share them on marketplaces, forums, and by email without knowing it. Most web tools that remove this data upload the photo first. Will people use a local tool that shows the leak and fixes it in one step?
- **Scope decisions:** Everything except orientation and color profile is removed, with no "keep the date" options. No re-encoding, resizing, or editing, and no storage at all. HEIC isn't processed: iPhones convert photos to JPEG when they're chosen in a browser, and desktop HEIC files get an explanation instead.
- **Limitations:** No HEIC, AVIF, GIF, TIFF, or RAW files. It can't hide what's visible in the picture itself, such as faces, street signs, or screens. Tested with real browser-made images carrying metadata modeled on real cameras, not a library of files straight from cameras. The share button appears only where the browser supports sharing files, mostly on phones.
- **Revisit if:** people use it for marketplace listings or ask for HEIC support, resizing to fit upload limits, or an option to keep dates. Or if searches like "remove location from photo" bring steady traffic.

## 2026-10-08 · #001 Trip Board

- **What:** A private board for trip research at [labs.eugeneyip.net/trip-board](https://labs.eugeneyip.net/trip-board/). Paste links or type ideas, sort them into seven categories (Flight, Stay, Transport, Activity, Food, Research, Other), and mark each one Researching, Considering, Decided, Booked, or Skip. It has search and filters, undo for deleting, importing, and starting over, and export and import of a versioned file (`labs.trip-board`, version 1). Everything stays in the visitor's browser.
- **Problem it tests:** People who plan their own trips end up with research scattered across tabs, map links, booking pages, articles, Reddit threads, and messages. The question is whether one quiet, local list, with a decision status on every item, is enough to replace that scatter without an itinerary builder or AI. It hasn't been tried by anyone beyond its builder yet, so nothing here is validated.
- **Scope decisions:** One board per browser; export a file to keep a finished trip. Items are grouped by category with settled ones first, so each group reads from booked down to skipped. Pasted links get a title suggested from their own address and a category guess for well-known travel sites. Nothing is fetched from other sites, so there is no scraping and no cross-site requests. Pasting several links at once offers to add them all, leaving out ones already on the board. Left out on purpose: accounts, sync, sharing, maps, prices, dates, itineraries, and AI.
- **Limitations:** The board lives in one browser on one device. Clearing the browser's site data deletes it, and moving to another device means exporting and importing a file. Duplicate warnings catch only identical links, so the same hotel with different dates in the address isn't flagged. Suggested titles can be clumsy, because pages aren't fetched. Tested in desktop Chromium and in emulated phone sizes, not yet on a physical iPhone or Android phone.
- **Revisit if:** it gets used for a real trip from first idea to booking, or people ask for the same things: more than one board, a read-only copy to share with a travel companion, or a plain-text export. Archive it if a month of real trip planning passes without it being opened.
